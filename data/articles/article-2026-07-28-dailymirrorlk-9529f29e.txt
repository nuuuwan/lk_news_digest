BASL President says judges’ retirement age amendment should go to referendum

Colombo, July 28 (Daily Mirror) - President of the Bar Association of Sri Lanka (BASL) Rajeev Amarasuriya yesterday expressed his personal view that the proposed Constitutional amendment seeking to increase the retirement age of superior court judges should not only secure a two-thirds majority in Parliament but should also be approved by the people at a referendum, as it has implications for the sovereignty of the people.

Speaking during a television programme aired by a private television channel last night, Amarasuriya said that Articles 3 and 4 of the Constitution vest sovereignty in the people, with judicial power being exercised by Parliament through courts and other institutions established by law. He argued that any Constitutional amendment affecting the exercise of that sovereignty should, in his opinion, be subjected to approval by the people at a referendum in addition to obtaining the required two-thirds majority in Parliament.

Referring to the proposed amendment to Article 107(5) of the Constitution, which falls under Chapter XV titled ‘The Judiciary’, Amarasuriya said the measure directly concerns the independence of the judiciary. He said that Supreme Court judges called upon to hear Special Determination applications challenging the Bill could face a conflict of interest, as the amendment relates to extending their own tenure by two years together with the continuation of their salaries, allowances and other benefits. Therefore, he maintained that, because the proposed amendment has a bearing on the judicial power of the people and concerns the judiciary’s constitutional framework, he personally believes it requires the approval of the people at a referendum.

Amarasuriya, however, stressed that these remarks reflected only his personal opinion and not the official position of the BASL. He said the BASL has not yet reached a decision on the proposed Constitutional amendment, citing that the Bill had only been presented yesterday evening.
