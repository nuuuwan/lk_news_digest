Senanayake Samudraya fisheries anchorage proposal reviewed

Colombo, Aug. 7 (Daily Mirror) - Minister of Fisheries, Aquatic and Ocean Resources Ramalingam Chandrasekar has reviewed the long-standing request by Monaragala District fishermen to establish a fisheries anchorage at Senanayake Samudraya during a field inspection visit to the reservoir.

During the visit, the Minister held discussions with representatives of local fisheries societies, who highlighted the difficulties they have faced over several years, including transportation challenges, limited access points for fishing activities, and the absence of a proper fisheries anchorage.

The fishermen representatives renewed their appeal for the establishment of a dedicated fish landing center at a suitable location around Senanayake Samudraya, stating that such a facility would provide a lasting solution to the challenges faced by the fishing community.

The inspection and discussions were attended by senior officials and technical experts from the National Aquatic Resources Research and Development Agency (NARA) and the National Aquaculture Development Authority (NAQDA). The officials presented a scientific assessment of the environmental conditions and the current status of fish resources in the reservoir.

Technical experts noted that Senanayake Samudraya is an important ecological zone where several native fish species breed and juvenile fish mature. They explained that establishing a permanent fisheries anchorage or allowing intensive fishing activities in certain areas could pose risks to fish populations and disturb the natural balance of the aquatic ecosystem.

Officials emphasized the importance of protecting the reservoir’s biodiversity and maintaining the ecological balance of Senanayake Samudraya, one of Sri Lanka’s largest reservoirs. They stressed that sustainable management of fish resources is essential to ensure the future of the fishing industry and the livelihoods of generations to come.

Addressing the gathering, Minister Chandrasekar acknowledged that the concerns raised by fishermen represent a genuine issue that has continued for many years. However, he stressed that the matter must be addressed through a scientific approach that balances the needs of the fishing community with environmental protection.

“This is not merely an administrative issue; it is a matter that must be approached scientifically. Relevant officials have pointed out that constructing a fisheries anchorage or expanding operations at the proposed location could risk damaging fish resources. Therefore, the government is committed to finding a balanced solution that safeguards both the livelihoods of fishermen and the preservation of fish resources,” the Minister stated.
