The Opposition: NPP’s greatest asset

Colombo, September 17 (Daily Mirror)- One wonders whether the public rally organised by the Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna (SLPP) with massive publicity in Anuradhapura last Saturday — intended to create a favorable political wave across the country — has instead turned into a political disaster for the party itself.

The meeting appeared to be a chaotic blend of political contradictions, conducted without a clear realisation of who is a friend and who is an enemy. At a time when the party needed to demonstrate that they are engaged in meaningful, civilised politics superior to the ruling National People's Power (NPP), the rally degenerated into a farce filled with contradictions and lack of discipline.

Prior to the meeting, hundreds of attendees under the heavy influence of alcohol were seen behaving deplorably in videos widely shared on social media. The slogans raised by some of these individuals went so far as to openly brand the party's national organizer, Namal Rajapaksa — who is positioned to lead the party — as a "thief".

According to social media footages, several attendees explicitly stated that although they showed up at the meeting, they would not vote for SLPP in the upcoming elections. They even added that if Namal had plundered public property, he must face the full force of the law.

These individuals cannot be genuine supporters of the SLPP. Instead, it seems that the event organisers simply brought in whoever they could find to fill the venue. The disruptive behaviour of these highly intoxicated crowds did absolutely nothing to restore the party's reputation.

Ahead of the rally, on September 4, Namal Rajapaksa was arrested and remanded in connection with the infamous Airbus procurement scam. The SLPP attempted to weaponise this arrest to gain public sympathy. They aggressively spread a narrative that the government intentionally arrested Namal to sabotage the Anuradhapura rally, and seemed to be successful in that, to some extent.

In Namal’s absence, organisers brought his father, former President Mahinda Rajapaksa, to the stage. The frail, elderly leader could neither walk nor stand properly, presenting a deeply pitiful sight. Witnessing this, many citizens took to social media to urge the SLPP to let the elderly leader live out his final days in peace, heavily criticising his children for exploiting their ailing father for political survival.

The speeches delivered at the rally explicitly exposed the inherent weakness and fragmentation of the opposition while none of the problems faced by the people were discussed.

While National Freedom Front (NFF) leader Wimal Weerawansa, Pivithuru Hela Urumaya (PHU) leader Udaya Gammanpila, and Jaffna District Independent MP Ramanathan Archchuna attended the event, other groups like the Sri Lanka Freedom Party (SLFP), the New Democratic Front (NDF), and the United National Party (UNP) — all of whom had closely co-organised anti-government rallies with the SLPP in recent months—had completely been excluded.

When Namal Rajapaksa was arrested, the UNP issued an official statement condemning it as a political vendetta aimed at disrupting the SLPP rally. During the UNP's 80th annual convention on September 5, UNP leader Ranil Wickremesinghe even indicated that Namal is one of them, highlighting that the latter is the grandson of D.A. Rajapaksa, one of the UNP's founding members.

This marks a glaring contradiction: the initial investigations into the Airbus scandal were launched in 2015 under UNP-led government before being shelved. Now that the current administration has revived the case and executed the arrest, the UNP ironically decries it as political victimisation.

Despite the UNP's current alignment with the SLPP, key rally speakers such as Tissa Kuttiarachchi and D. Weerasinghe used the stage to heavily attack former ministers Mahindananda Aluthgamage, Pavithra Wanniarachchi, and S.M. Chandrasena for shifting their allegiance to Ranil Wickremesinghe.

Weerasinghe alleged that former Minister Chandrasena had defrauded farmers by mixing lake silt with tire ash to sell as organic fertiliser. Kuttiarachchi similarly pinned the blame for the infamous Gotabaya-era organic fertiliser crisis directly on Mahindananda Aluthgamage—conveniently ignoring the fact that they themselves were part of the very same government when those policies were enacted.

The most shocking moment occurred during MP Archchuna's speech, where he openly identified himself as a "Tiger" closely linked to the Tamil diaspora while launching a fierce attack on the government. Shockingly, southern nationalist leaders like Weerawansa and Gammanpila seemed to be enjoying the speech while the crowd cheered along. At one point, before addressing Tamil politics, Weerawansa went as far as expressing personal regret to Archchuna.

Had a Tamil politician stood up at any other party rally and proclaimed "I am a Tiger," the reaction from these SLPP leaders would have been radically hostile. By coddling a self-proclaimed Tiger at their rally simply to attack the current government, these leaders have proven that their long-standing brand of fiery patriotism was nothing more than a political hoax.

Furthermore, this shows that if Mahinda Rajapaksa genuinely cared about resolving the ethnic problem when he was at the peak of his power, he could have succeeded, as his supporters who cheered a man who publicly claimed to be a “Tiger” wouldn’t have opposed any of the Tamil demands.

Conversely, it remains highly doubtful whether Archchuna would dare preach his anti-separatist rhetoric to the Tamil public in the North during an actual election campaign. Since Tamils call the Mullivaikkal tragedy genocide and put the blame on Mahinda Rajapaksa, genuine Tiger supporters would have found deeply baffling to watch Archchuna praise the Rajapaksa family while claiming to be a "Tiger." Although another speaker reminded the audience that the LTTE had historically massacred hundreds of civilians at the Sri Maha Bodhi tree in Anuradhapura in 1985, the man who identify himself as “Tiger” did not respond.

Ultimately, the rally intended to spark a massive opposition wave has instead backfired, exposing an alliance riddled with catastrophic ideological flaws and contradictions.

This issue is not confined to the SLPP. The wider opposition is trapped in a loop of contradictions and lack of direction. UNP oscillates between working with the SLPP and trying to forge a coalition with the Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB). Opposition figures like Dayasiri Jayasekara argue that they must form a grand coalition against the government by welcoming absolutely anyone—corrupt individuals, financial fraudsters, or racists—while simultaneously claiming that this alliance is necessary to fight government corruption.

The opposition operates entirely without a long-term plan, merely reacting to events as they happen. These opposition leaders formed a coalition called "Maha Jana Handa" (The Great People's Voice) on October 27th last year, followed by another rally in Nugegoda on November 21. They boastfully announced a nationwide campaign claiming that these were the beginning of a long struggle against the government, yet not a single subsequent rally materialised. Wickremesinghe in November last year promised to spearhead 1,000 rallies across the island, which also failed to happen. This is the pathetic reality of Sri Lanka's opposition today.
