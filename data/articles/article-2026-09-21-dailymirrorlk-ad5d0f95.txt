Only one in five Sri Lankan households owns a computer: Census Dept.

Colombo, Sep. 21 (Daily Mirror) - Only 21.3% of households in Sri Lanka had at least one desktop or laptop computer in 2025, highlighting a significant gap in access to personal computing facilities, the  Department of Census and Statistics (DCS) said.

The finding is contained in the DCS Computer Literacy Statistics 2025 Annual Bulletin, which was based on a nationally distributed sample of 25,000 households and people aged between five and 69 years. The 2025 estimates were reweighted using data from the 2024 Census of Population and Housing.

While 37.8% of households in the urban sector had a desktop or laptop, the figure fell to 18.6% in the rural sector and just 5.2% in the estate sector.

This means that computer ownership among estate households was less than one-seventh of the level recorded in urban households.

The Western Province recorded the highest proportion of households with a desktop or laptop at 34%, followed by the Central Province at 20.3% and Sabaragamuwa at 19.4%.

The North Western and Southern provinces recorded 17.5% and 17.4%, respectively, while Uva recorded 14.1%. North Central Province recorded 12.9%, followed by the Eastern Province at 12.5%.

The Northern Province had the lowest level of household computer ownership at 10.1%.

The DCS said the proportion of households with a desktop or laptop had remained within the 19%-21% range during the 2023–2025 period.

The findings come despite a substantially higher level of digital literacy in the country. According to the bulletin, 72% of people aged 5–69 were digitally literate in 2025, compared to a computer literacy rate of 39.2%.

The gap suggests that digital access is increasingly taking place through mobile devices rather than traditional personal computers. Smartphones accounted for 80.6% of device usage recorded in the survey, while desktop and laptop computers accounted for 17.4%.

The DCS also found substantial differences in computer literacy across sectors. Computer literacy was 54% in the urban sector, compared with 37.1% in the rural sector and 19.5% in the estate sector.

At provincial level, Western Province recorded the highest computer literacy rate at 50.2%, while North Central Province recorded the lowest at 24.3%.

The overall computer literacy rate of 39.2% represented a 3.3 percentage-point increase from 2024.

The survey defines a computer-literate person as someone aged 5–69 who is able to use a computer on their own.

The DCS has conducted computer literacy surveys periodically since 2004, with a survey module attached to the Sri Lanka Labour Force Survey from 2014 onwards.
