Why re-introduce the death penalty now?

The question of whether we need the death penalty or not has once again come to the limelight. A news report read that the Welikada gallows are ready if the death penalty is going to be imposed. After 1976, Sri Lanka didn’t witness any executions taking place. The final judicial execution took place on June 23, 1976. The prisoner executed was  convicted criminal Embilipitiye Chandradasa, also known as “Honda Papuwa”, and not D.J. Siripala, widely known as “Maru Sira”, as it is widely believed. Maru Sira was hanged in 1975.  Even though there were attempts to bring back the death penalty, none of these attempts saw light of day. But the incumbent government has proposed to introduce death penalty for drug offenders. The debate about violation of human rights has taken a strong stand,  and as a result over 110 countries have abolished the death penalty. Sri Lanka predominantly follows Buddhist ideologies. Many have opposed the death penalty to support the idea that Sri Lanka should practice metta and compassion towards all beings. In this context,  it doesn’t seem appropriate to bring back this archaic law back into effect.
