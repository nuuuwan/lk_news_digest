Civilian clothing for Gnanasara Thera in court could signal State interference in Sasana: Ven. Dhammaloka Thera

Bringing a prominent and respected monk like Ven. Galagoda Aththe Gnanasara Thera before court after dressing him in civilian clothes could be perceived as conveying the message that the authorities could interfere with matters concerning the Buddhist Sasana, Founder and Convener of the Universal Buddhist Monks' Conference and Chief Incumbent of the Sri Sambuddhaloka Vihara in Japan, Ven. Thunhitiyawe Dhammaloka Thera said.

He said while the six-year prison sentence imposed on Ven. Gnanasara Thera was a judicial decision made under Sri Lankan law and should be respected, the manner in which the monk was presented before court raised serious concerns.

Ven. Dhammaloka Thera questioned whether the Commissioner General of Prisons had overstepped the authority traditionally exercised by the relevant Nikaya and Sangha Sabha in matters concerning Buddhist monks.

He explained that disciplinary matters involving a monk's conduct within a Nikaya or Sangha Sabha should be dealt with by the relevant monastic authorities, subject to the approval of the Maha Nayaka Thera.

"Once someone enters the monastic order, the authority to disrobe lies with that individual. If a monk has done something unsuitable for the Sasana or contrary to the rules of his Nikaya or Sangha Sabha, only the monks responsible for administering that body, with the approval of the Maha Nayaka Thero, have the authority to take action," he said.

He said Sri Lanka's laws should be administered while also recognising the country's long-standing Buddhist traditions, customs and cultural practices.

Ven. Dhammaloka Thera strongly criticised the decision to have Ven. Gnanasara Thera appear in court wearing white civilian clothing, noting that prisoners were generally seen appearing before courts in their own clothing.

"We have seen many people in prison custody being brought before courts in their own clothing. Therefore, we strongly condemn the act of bringing Ven. Galagodaaththe Gnanasara Thero from prison to court dressed in white or civilian clothes," he said.

Although the decision had reportedly been made by the Commissioner General of Prisons, he said responsibility ultimately rested with the Government and urged the authorities to consider the wider implications of such decisions.

He stressed that the issue should not be viewed merely as an action against one monk, but in the context of how the State deals with the Buddhist clergy and established religious institutions.

"If it was a matter of personal retaliation, other measures could have been taken. But bringing a prominent and respected monk before court after dressing him in civilian clothes sends a different message," he said.

Ven. Dhammaloka Thera warned that actions of this nature could create the impression that State authorities were attempting to interfere in matters relating to the Buddhist Sasana.

He further said such developments could influence younger generations and gradually weaken religious and cultural traditions that had been preserved in Sri Lanka for generations.

"We once again remind the President and the Government that you are governing a country with a Buddhist culture and tradition. You must understand this and conduct the administration with respect for the country's culture, traditions, customs and practices," he said.

He called on the Government to ensure that decisions affecting Buddhist monks and the Sasana were taken with due consideration for established monastic institutions, traditions and the authority of the relevant religious bodies.
