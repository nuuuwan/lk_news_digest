Govt. to evaluate BOI, SDP and Port City tax incentives

BOI non-SDP exemptions rise to Rs. 53 b from nil a year earlier

BOI firms get Rs. 28 b in VAT relief and Rs. 16 b in SSCL relief in 2025

Customs relief for Port City duty-free shop jumps to Rs. 757 m from Rs. 46 m

New laws set time limits for granting SDP and Port City incentives

The Government has begun cost-benefit reviews of tax incentives granted to Board of Investment (BOI) Strategic Development Projects (SDPs) and Colombo Port City businesses, as Corporate Income Tax (CIT) reliefs for BOI enterprises climbed 54% in 2024/25.

According to the Finance Ministry’s Tax Expenditure Statement 2025/2026 released on 12 November 2026, CIT reliefs for BOI companies rose to Rs. 65 billion in 2024/25 from Rs. 42 billion in 2023/24. Exemptions for non-SDP BOI companies, which were nil the previous year, amounted to Rs. 53 billion. Concessionary rates for these companies fell to Rs. 11.5 billion from Rs. 41.7 billion.

SDPs accounted for Rs. 84 million in concessionary-rate CIT relief in 2024/25, compared with Rs. 123 million in exemptions the previous year. Port City projects received no CIT relief in 2024/25, after Rs. 143 million in exemptions in 2023/24.

BOI companies also received Rs. 28.1 billion in Value Added Tax (VAT) relief and Rs. 15.8 billion in Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL) relief in 2025, a combined Rs. 43.9 billion. In the first half of 2026 they received Rs. 18.9 billion in VAT relief and Rs. 10.1 billion in SSCL relief. Other manufacturing activities led BOI VAT reliefs in 2025 at Rs. 15.7 billion, followed by wholesale and retail at Rs. 9.3 billion. Textiles and garments led BOI SSCL reliefs at Rs. 5.9 billion.

SDPs received Rs. 286 million in VAT relief in 2025, almost all of it in tourism, and Rs. 93 million in SSCL relief. Port City projects received Rs. 11 million in SSCL relief in 2025 and Rs. 13 million in the first half of 2026.

The Port City duty-free shop recorded the sharpest rise. Its Customs Import Duty relief rose to Rs. 757 million in 2025 from Rs. 46 million in 2024, and reached Rs. 456 million in the first half of 2026. Its excise relief, however, fell to Rs. 94 million from Rs. 250 million.

The Ministry expects these costs to grow. “Tax expenditure associated with Port City projects is expected to increase over the medium term as approved businesses commence operations and become eligible for incentives provided under the relevant legislative framework,” the statement said.

To keep the cost in check, the Ministry has introduced ex-ante cost-benefit analyses and ex-post evaluations of incentives granted to SDPs, and technical analyses and ex-post evaluations of incentives granted under the Colombo Port City Economic Commission. “The Strategic Development Projects (Amendment) Act, No. 26 of 2025, and the Colombo Port City Economic Commission (Amendment) Act, No. 1 of 2026, have strengthened the tax incentive framework by providing for specified periods for the granting of tax incentives,” it said.

The Ministry cautioned that the final bill for large projects is uncertain. “For major investment projects, including BOI projects, Strategic Development Projects, and Colombo Port City projects, the actual fiscal cost may depend on the implementation of investment commitments and the achievement of employment, production, export and other project outcomes,” it said.
