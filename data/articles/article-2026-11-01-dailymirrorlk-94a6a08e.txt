Supreme Court rules police constable violated woman’s fundamental rights through assault

Colombo, Nov. 1 (Daily Mirror) - The Supreme Court held that a Police Constable attached to the Dompe Police Station violated the fundamental rights of a woman by subjecting her to physical assault, and ordered him to pay Rs. 100,000 in compensation from his personal funds within six months.

The three-judge Supreme Court bench comprising Justices Janak De Silva, Menaka Wijesundera and Sampath Wijeratne made this determination while allowing a fundamental rights petition filed by Jayasinghage Nirmala Kanthi, who alleged that she had been assaulted by the police constable.

The Supreme Court declared that the first respondent, Police Constable Lakmal (84253), had infringed the petitioner’s fundamental right guaranteed under Article 11 of the Constitution, which prohibits torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment.

The petitioner had alleged that she was subjected to physical and verbal assault by the police constable on October 7, 2019, when she visited the Dompe Police Station to deliver a lunch packet to her husband, who had been arrested by officers of the Special Task Force over an allegation of brewing illicit liquor.

In support of her allegations, she had produced medical documents, including a medico-legal examination receipt and a diagnosis ticket, together with photographs depicting her injuries. The Court observed that the injuries shown in the photographs appeared to correspond with those recorded in the medical documents.

The petitioner had also submitted an affidavit from a three-wheeler driver who had taken her to the police station and subsequently transported her to hospital for treatment. The affidavit corroborated her account of the events surrounding the incident.

Senior Counsel Himalee Kularatne appeared for the petitioner. Counsel Udaya Bandara instructed by Kalana De Silva appeared for the first respondent while State Counsel Induni Punchihewa appeared for the Attorney General.
