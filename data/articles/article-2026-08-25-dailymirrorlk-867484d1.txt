Govt.’s Rs.95bn MSME,  agriculture credit programme nears exhaustion

Data from Finance Ministry shows Rs.75.6bn released under programme by August, equivalent to about 79% of combined allocation for MSMEs and agriculture

Uptake led by agriculture-related facilities, which disbursed Rs.38.02bn or 93% of Rs.40.8bn allocated

Two MSME facilities carrying a combined allocation of Rs.13.95bn are scheduled to be launched only in 4th quarter

FinMin says demand has already pushed 2 major schemes beyond their original allocations

The government has disbursed nearly Rs.76 billion, under a Rs.95 billion concessionary financing programme for businesses and agriculture, bringing the package close to exhaustion months before the end of 2026 and prompting the government to consider expanding similar credit schemes next year.

The data from the Finance Ministry showed that Rs.75.6 billion had been released under the programme by August, equivalent to about 79 percent of the combined allocation for the micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and agriculture.

The uptake was led by the agriculture-related facilities, which disbursed Rs.38.02 billion or 93 percent of Rs.40.8 billion allocated. The MSME programmes released Rs.37.58 billion, representing 68.5 percent of their Rs.54.89 billion allocation.

However, two MSME facilities, carrying a combined allocation of Rs.13.95 billion, are scheduled to be launched only in the fourth quarter, weighing on the overall utilisation rate.

The demand has already pushed two major schemes beyond their original allocations, according to the Finance Ministry, as the Re-MSME Plus programme disbursed Rs.25.99 billion against an allocation of Rs.25.7 billion, reaching 2,645 businesses.

The New Sapiri Rural Loan Scheme, which provides financing across 34 crop varieties, released Rs.25.94 billion to more than 50,000 beneficiaries, surpassing its Rs.25 billion annual target.

The Finance Ministry said it was examining whether the allocations for the concessionary lending could be increased in 2027, given the pace of demand. It did not indicate the amount being considered or whether additional funding would be provided under the current programme this year.

The financing package combines the government funds, with concessional funding from the Asian Development Bank (ADB). The government-funded loans carry an annual interest rate of 5 percent and are capped at Rs.25 million, while the ADB-backed facility offers loans of up to Rs.50 million at 6 percent.

The programme also includes a disaster relief facility for the businesses affected by Cyclone Ditwah. About Rs.8.06 billion of its Rs.9.3 billion allocation had been disbursed to 3,966 borrowers, more than 90 percent of whom were micro and small enterprises.

Separately, 70 entrepreneurs, whose borrowings had fallen into the non-performing category during the economic crisis, received Rs.235 million in working capital financing. Another 342 MSMEs have signed revised agreements with the banks, under the government’s loan restructuring programme and resumed operations, according to the ministry.

Agricultural lending included Rs.11.56 billion, provided to 451 small and medium-scale paddy millers and cooperative societies, under the Odapana scheme. The funding supported the purchase of about 70,000 metric tonnes of paddy.

The scheme allows the eligible borrowers to obtain up to Rs.75 million at concessionary rates for paddy purchases. A further Rs.7.5 billion was allocated for the 2026 Yala season to support the purchases at the government-guaranteed price, with loans of up to Rs.25 million offered interest-free.

The government’s collateral-free lending programme has meanwhile facilitated close to Rs.14 billion in loans to 2,143 borrowers. The Sri Lanka National Credit Guarantee Institution has issued guarantees of about Rs.9.65 billion to support the lending.

Fifteen state and private banks are participating in the financing programme, which is administered by the Finance Ministry’s Development Finance Department.

More than 70 percent of lending has reportedly been directed outside the Western province, reversing the concentration of previous credit programmes in Colombo and Gampaha. The ministry, however, did not provide a district-level breakdown of disbursements.

While the rapid utilisation indicates a strong demand for subsidised credit, the available data do not show the repayment performance, employment generated or the additional economic output created through the programme.
