Inflation rises for fourth straight month to 7.2% in July

National Consumer Price Index rose 0.5% MoM to 223.4 in July, significantly slower than 1.6% monthly increase recorded in June

Composition of inflation showed that pressure on household budgets remained concentrated outside food

Non-food inflation stood at 9.2% year-on-year while food inflation accelerated sharply to 4.9%, from 3.3%

The nationwide inflation accelerated to 7.2 percent in July, from 6.5 percent in June, as the higher non-food costs continued to account for the bulk of price pressures, the latest data from the Census and Statistics Department showed.

The National Consumer Price Index (NCPI) rose 0.5 percent month-on-month (MoM) to 223.4 in July, significantly slower than the 1.6 percent monthly increase recorded in June. The combination points to a continued acceleration in annual inflation even as fresh price pressures moderated during the month.

The composition of inflation showed that the pressure on the household budgets remained concentrated outside food. Non-food inflation stood at 9.2 percent year-on-year in July, marginally easing from 9.3 percent in June, while food inflation accelerated sharply to 4.9 percent, from 3.3 percent.

The non-food items contributed 5.04 percentage points to the 7.2 percent headline inflation rate, more than double the 2.21 percentage-point contribution from food, according to the Census and Statistics Department.

Transport was the largest source of annual non-food inflation, contributing 2.23 percentage points, followed by housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels at 1.21 percentage points. Restaurants and hotels contributed 0.50 percentage points, education 0.35 percentage points and miscellaneous goods and services 0.26 percentage points.

The figures suggest that the inflationary pressure has increasingly shifted towards relatively less discretionary household expenses such as transport, housing and utilities, even as food remains a source of renewed price pressure.

The overall NCPI increased just 0.5 percent from June, with the non-food prices accounting for 0.42 percentage points of the increase and food contributing 0.08 percentage points.

Within the non-food expenditure, the higher housing rents were a key driver, contributing 0.28 percentage points to the monthly index increase. The bus fares added 0.15 percentage points, while the secondary tuition fees contributed another 0.06 percentage points.

Those increases were partly offset by the lower fuel costs. The petrol prices reduced the monthly index by 0.19 percentage points, while the LP gas and diesel made negative contributions of 0.14 percentage points and 0.04 percentage points, respectively.

The higher prices of green chillies made the largest upward food contribution at 0.24 percentage points, followed by dried fish at 0.12 percentage points and milk powder at 0.05 percentage points. Eggs contributed another 0.04 percentage points.

However, these increases were largely countered by the falling prices of several staples. Vegetables reduced the index by 0.30 percentage points, while the coconuts lowered it by 0.11 percentage points. The prices of limes, rice and Mysore dhal each made negative contributions of 0.03 percentage points.

On a year-on-year basis, the food category nevertheless showed a clear acceleration, with inflation rising to 4.9 percent in July, from 3.3 percent in June. Non-food inflation eased only marginally to 9.2 percent, from 9.3 percent, leaving it substantially above food inflation.

The latest reading extends the sharp acceleration in headline inflation seen over the recent months. The NCPI inflation stood at 2.4 percent in January, before rising to 4.7 percent in April, 5.4 percent in May, 6.5 percent in June and 7.2 percent in July.

The 12-month moving average inflation rate also increased to 3.5 percent in July, from 2.9 percent in June, reflecting the cumulative build-up in price pressures despite the relatively modest month-on-month increase recorded in July.
