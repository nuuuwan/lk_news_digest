Janashakthi restates pre-IPO profit as finance arm slips below capital minimums

FY25 group profit restated 10.5% lower to Rs.4.69bn, against Rs.5.24bn in IPO prospectus

Parent says group strongly capitalised as finance arm’s Tier I and total capital ratios breach minimums

Janashakthi PLC has cut its group profit for 2024/25 by 10.5 percent to Rs.4.69 billion, after correcting a series of prior-period errors, revising the Rs.5.24 billion figure it presented to the investors in the prospectus for its Rs.5 billion initial public offering (IPO) in April, its audited accounts filed with the Colombo Stock Exchange showed.

The LKAS 8 restatement also cut the profit attributable to the shareholders by 15.2 percent to Rs.2.68 billion and total equity at end-March 2025 by 8.2 percent to Rs.15.87 billion.

The holding company profit fell 13.7 percent to Rs.3.02 billion, from Rs.3.50 billion in the prospectus, lowering the earnings per share to Rs.4.76, from Rs.5.52 on a pre-subdivision basis.

By Mirror Business calculations, the revision cuts the prospectus’ 36.3 percent return on equity to about 30.7 percent and lifts the implied price-to-earnings multiple at the Rs.10 issue price to about 6.0 times, from 5.15 times, although the restated book value is marginally higher.

Most of the profit revision stems from the intra-group fair value gains booked by First Capital Holdings PLC on its stakes in Janashakthi Insurance PLC and Janashakthi Finance PLC, which were not eliminated on consolidation and inflated the 2024/25 profit by Rs.413.5 million.  A 2017/18 consolidation entry had also been recorded twice, overstating the non-controlling interests by about Rs.1.4 billion in every year since, although correcting it lifted the equity attributable to the shareholders.

The remainder came from Janashakthi Finance, whose corrections went well beyond the unreconciled Rs.270.5 million debit balance behind its qualified FY26 audit opinion. It retrospectively wrote off about Rs.141.7 million in aged, unsupported other assets, found the lease liabilities understated by Rs.397 million as at March 2025 and derecognised loan collateral it had carried as investment property, cutting the group profit for 2024/25 by Rs.139.7 million. The management initiated the review during the year ended March 2026, the accounts state, without disclosing when the errors were identified.

The March 25 prospectus carried the original figures and the 500 million-share offer closed on its April 9 opening day, with the shares rising 37 percent on their April 29 debut.

KPMG signed off on the restated accounts, issuing an unmodified opinion with an emphasis of matter, only on November 16, more than seven months after the year-end and three days after the group’s interim accounts had labelled the March 2026 figures as audited.

However, Janashakthi Finance had already acknowledged in its 2025/26 annual report, released in September, that the year-end adjustments pushed its total capital ratio to 11.86 percent, below the 12.5 percent minimum, leaving a regulatory capital shortfall of about Rs.167.9 million.  By September 30, both ratios were below the minimums, with the Tier I capital at 7.99 percent of risk-weighted assets, against 8.5 percent and the total capital at 10.72 percent, the finance company disclosed on November 11.

The parent’s interim accounts, signed two days later, make no reference to the shortfall, with the executive summary instead stating that the finance company “demonstrated resilience” and that “the group and its subsidiaries are strongly capitalised to absorb these uncertainties”.

It also put the finance company’s profit after tax at Rs.162 million, a figure matching its total comprehensive income rather than its Rs.198.6 million profit after tax.

The parent’s annual accounts also disclose the Inland Revenue Department assessments on Janashakthi Finance for the corporate income tax, value added tax, nation building tax on financial services, PAYE and Social Security Contribution Levy totalling Rs.414.5 million, including the penalties of Rs.185.5 million, close to two and a half times its March capital shortfall. Janashakthi Finance’s annual report had listed additional shareholder capital among its options but its interim plan relies on Rs.350 million of subordinated debt for the Tier II capital and Rs.198 million of certified profits for Tier I, both subject to regulatory approval.

Its parent, with an effective 93.44 percent stake, has used Rs.1 billion of IPO proceeds to repay the borrowings and earmarked Rs.3.5 billion for acquiring or establishing insurance, microfinance and non-bank financial businesses, uses which the prospectus says include meeting prudential requirements and capitalising new or acquired entities, without naming Janashakthi Finance. Meanwhile, the group swung to a Rs.1.46 billion half-year loss from a Rs.3.08 billion profit a year earlier, with First Capital Holdings alone losing Rs.1.3 billion and its shares closed September at the Rs.10 IPO price.

The filings leave open when the errors were first identified, whether they were known before the prospectus was issued and whether any IPO proceeds will shore up Janashakthi Finance’s capital. (NF)
