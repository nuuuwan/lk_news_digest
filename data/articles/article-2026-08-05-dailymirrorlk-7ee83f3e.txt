CEA relaunches ’e-Kunu’ programme to boost electronic waste collection

Colombo, Aug. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The Central Environmental Authority (CEA) has relaunched the 'e-Kunu' electronic waste collection programme in a new format to strengthen the safe collection and disposal of electronic waste across Sri Lanka.

A Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) was signed between the CEA and its partner at the CEA premises, with CEA Chairman Professor Thilak Hewawasam signing on behalf of the Authority. The launch was attended by Environment Minister Dr. Dhammika Patabendi, Deputy Environment Minister Anton Jayakody, CEA Director General R.S.P. Kapila Rajapaksa and other officials.

Under the programme, members of the public can hand over electronic waste, including mobile phones, laptops, chargers, cables, lithium-ion batteries, AA/AAA batteries, hard disks, CDs/DVDs, landline phones, routers and set-top boxes, at designated collection points across the country. The collected waste will be sent to registered electronic waste recyclers for environmentally sound disposal.

The CEA said that electronic waste is one of the fastest-growing waste streams globally. According to the Global E-Waste Monitor 2024, around 62 million tonnes of e-waste were generated worldwide in 2022, but only 22.3% was formally recycled. The global volume of e-waste is projected to increase to 82 million tonnes by 2030.

The Authority warned that improper disposal of electronic waste releases hazardous substances such as mercury, lead, cadmium, arsenic and other toxic chemicals, posing serious risks to human health and the environment. It also results in the loss of valuable materials, including gold, copper, aluminium and silver, which can be recovered through recycling.

The CEA urged the public to dispose of unwanted electronic devices only through authorised collection points, saying responsible e-waste management is essential to protecting the environment and promoting a circular economy.

The relaunched programme aims to expand safe e-waste collection facilities nationwide and provide greater opportunities for the public to dispose of unwanted electronic devices responsibly.
