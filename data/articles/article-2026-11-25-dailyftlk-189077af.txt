Seizing new opportunities in China’s development, writing a new chapter in China-Sri Lanka cooperation

Guided by the principles of mutual respect, fairness and justice, we will work together to overcome obstacles and rise to the challenges, implement the common understandings reached by the two countries’ leaders in a practical manner, walk side by side on the path to modernisation, and continue to advance our strategic cooperative partnership featuring sincere mutual assistance and everlasting friendship. Together, we will work towards building a China-Sri Lanka community with a shared future

The year 2026 marks the launch of China’s 15th Five-Year Plan for economic and social development. Against the backdrop of a sluggish global economic recovery and ongoing international turbulence, China’s economy has pressed ahead under pressure, maintaining overall stability while making steady progress and breaking new ground amid global changes, showcasing a promising outlook. China’s recently released economic report for the first three quarters of the year once again delivered a stronger-than-expected performance, demonstrating the resilience and high quality of its growth. This is further proof that China’s economy enjoys bright prospects, robust growth momentum and a promising future.

First, the foundation for economic stability has continued to strengthen. In the first three quarters of 2026, China’s gross domestic product (GDP) reached 107.1 trillion yuan, up 4.6% year on year. The economy expanded by 5.7 trillion yuan, the largest increase in five years. This growth rate remains in a leading position among major global economies. Employment and prices remained generally stable, while people’s livelihoods continued to improve. China’s sound economic fundamentals and positive long-term growth trajectory has become even more evident.

Second, new growth drivers are gaining momentum. Emerging growth drivers, represented by high-end manufacturing, the digital economy and modern services, contributed more than 40% of China’s economic growth in the first three quarters of this year. China’s artificial intelligence (AI) sector has seen explosive growth. Open-source AI models developed in China have recorded more than 10 billion downloads worldwide. China accounts for over 90% of global humanoid robot shipments, while AI-related industries, including smart in-vehicle equipment, have maintained growth rates of over 30%. Embodied AI is also moving towards large-scale commercial applications. Multiple types of commercial rockets have been successfully launched, and technologies such as quantum information, integrated circuits, and nuclear fusion have been advancing at an accelerated pace. China is also stepping up its green transition. Energy consumption per unit of GDP fell by 3.1% year on year in the first three quarters. The retail penetration rate of new energy vehicles (NEVs) has remained above 60% for six consecutive months, with total sales exceeding 7.7 million units. Today, three out of every five new energy passenger vehicles sold worldwide are made in China.

Third, the benefits of deeper integration with the world economy are continuing to unfold. China remains committed to “sharing benefits with the whole world”. Through opening up at a higher level, China is embracing the broader trend of global development, injecting valuable vitality into the world economy and working with the international community to achieve shared development and prosperity. In the first three quarters of 2026, China’s total imports and exports of goods reached 39.94 trillion yuan, up 18.8% year on year, bucking the trend of sluggish global trade. Imports grew by 22.3%, significantly faster than exports, which rose by 16.4%. Trade with Belt and Road partner countries increased by 17.1%. China continues to expand institutional opening-up, extending zero-tariff treatment to 63 countries and promoting trade liberalisation and investment facilitation. The “Big Market for All: Export to China” initiative has created new channels for quality foreign goods and services to enter the Chinese market. Meanwhile, high-tech products made in China continue to support global industrial upgrading and the green transition, while making everyday life more convenient for people around the world.

These achievements clearly underscore that China remains a driving force of global economic growth and a stabiliser for the world economy, and more importantly a major engine for the development and revitalisation of the Global South. Both China and Sri Lanka are members of the Global South. The two countries have a long history of economic and trade cooperation and have achieved fruitful results, setting an example for South-South cooperation. Even before the establishment of diplomatic ties, China and Sri Lanka overcame numerous obstacles to sign the Rubber-Rice Pact, forging the spirit of “independence, self-reliance, solidarity and mutual assistance” enshrined in the Pact and laying a solid foundation for mutually-beneficial cooperation. Over nearly 70 years of diplomatic relations, the two countries have stood together through thick and thin and supported each other in times of need, while staying true to the spirit of the Rubber-Rice Pact. They have continued to deepen practical cooperation across various fields and worked together to advance high-quality Belt and Road cooperation, achieving fruitful results that promote national development, serve long-term interests and improve people's livelihoods.

Infrastructure connectivity has opened up new pathways for development. Port City Colombo, as Sri Lanka’s largest foreign direct investment (FDI) project to date, has attracted more than US$2 billion in investment and registered over 200 companies. The Colombo International Container Terminal (CICT) has become one of South Asia’s leading deep-water container terminals, handling a major share of the Port of Colombo’s total throughput. Hambantota International Port (HIP) has emerged as a world-class hub for vehicle roll-on/roll-off (Ro-Ro) operations, with its annual container handling capacity set to reach 2 million TEUs in early 2027, further strengthening Sri Lanka’s ambition to become a regional maritime hub. The Lakvijaya Power Plant in Norochcholai, Puttalam, supplies a quarter of the country’s base-load electricity, bringing light to millions of households. The Northern road network, the Southern Expressway, the Southern Railway Line and the Central Expressway under construction are steadily expanding the transport links that underpin regional economic development. The completion of the Elahera water transfer tunnel has opened up a key section of Sri Lanka’s North Central Province Canal Project. Once the entire project is completed, it is expected to provide a reliable supply of irrigation and drinking water to 74,000 hectares of farmland and 375,000 households along its route, bringing new hope for development to the drought-prone northern regions.

People-centred assistance has improved livelihoods across the whole island. Since the establishment of diplomatic relations between China and Sri Lanka, China has provided around 140 assistance projects and more than 60 batches of supplies to support people’s livelihoods in Sri Lanka. These have covered a wide range of areas, including housing, education, transport, healthcare, water supply and emergency disaster relief. Landmark buildings such as the Bandaranaike Memorial International Conference Hall (BMICH), the Nelum Pokuna Mahinda Rajapaksa Theatre and the Supreme Court Complex have become enduring symbols of China-Sri Lanka friendship. The Outpatient Department Building of the National Hospital of Sri Lanka and the National Nephrology Specialised Hospital have both been put into operation, helping improve public health and well-being. A total of 1,996 subsidised housing units for low-income families are under construction, which will fulfil the dream of a secure home for those in need. The China-Sri Lanka Joint Research and Demonstration Centre for Water Technology (JRDC) is helping more local communities gain access to safe drinking water. China has also donated school uniform fabric for four consecutive years, benefiting more than 4.4 million students across the island. The Digital Transformation Project of General Education in Sri Lanka will establish 900 smart classrooms in 500 schools nationwide, helping bridge the digital divide in education. China’s MAZU intelligent meteorological early-warning system has also been deployed in Sri Lanka, providing technical support for tackling climate change and improving disaster prevention and mitigation. Emergency reconstruction of 14 bridges has also been launched, living up to people’s expectations on road connectivity in disaster-affected areas.

Joint talent development has practiced the sharing of “fish” and “fishing”. China is dedicated to not only “giving others fish”, but also “teaching others to fish”. In addition to immediate assistance, China has also been supporting Sri Lanka in long-term capacity building and personnel training. Since 1983, China has provided training opportunities in China for nearly 14,000 Sri Lankan officials and business representatives.

Over the past six years, the China Aid Training Program has enrolled an average of 1,000 Sri Lankan participants each year. The China-Sri Lanka South-South Cooperation Technical Assistance Project has been particularly successful. Since its launch three years ago, it has trained more than 2,000 Sri Lankan farmers and agricultural workers, boosting both the yields and incomes of banana, pineapple and mango cultivation. The 6 agricultural training videos produced under the project have also been incorporated into Sri Lanka’s national training curriculum, providing long-term support for the wider adoption of improved agricultural techniques.

History and facts have proven, and will continue to prove that to stand with China is to stand with opportunity; to believe in China is to believe in tomorrow; and to invest in China is to invest in the future. China and Sri Lanka are fellow travelers on the path toward development and revitalisation, and good partners of win-win cooperation. China’s vibrant economic growth will definitely open up broader prospects for China-Sri Lanka practical cooperation and deliver greater benefits to our two peoples. China-Sri Lanka cooperation is built on the principle of mutual respect, equality and mutual benefit, and has made a significant contribution to promoting socioeconomic development and the people’s well-being of both sides. China has consistently provided support for Sri Lanka’s development in a spirit of sincerity and goodwill, with no political conditions imposed on any cooperation or assistance project. China is ready to work with Sri Lanka to foster a favourable business environment, encourage closer cooperation between enterprises of the two countries, and accelerate negotiations on a China-Sri Lanka Free Trade Agreement (FTA), upgrade China-Sri Lanka high-quality Belt and Road cooperation and jointly open up new space for win-win development of higher standard, stronger resilience and greater sustainability.

2027 will witness the 70th anniversary of China-Sri Lanka diplomatic relations and the 75th anniversary of the Rubber-Rice Pact, bringing a new milestone in China-Sri Lanka relations to build on past achievements and pave the way forward. The spirit of the Rubber-Rice Pact is a precious historical treasure of both sides in removing external irritants and lending sincere mutual support. In today’s changing world, it is more important than ever to carry forward and uphold this great spirit. China stands ready to work with Sri Lanka to draw wisdom from the history of our bilateral relations and strength from the spirit of the Rubber-Rice Pact. Guided by the principles of mutual respect, fairness and justice, we will work together to overcome obstacles and rise to the challenges, implement the common understandings reached by the two countries’ leaders in a practical manner, walk side by side on the path to modernisation, and continue to advance our strategic cooperative partnership featuring sincere mutual assistance and everlasting friendship. Together, we will work towards building a China-Sri Lanka community with a shared future.

(The author is the Ambassador of the People’s Republic of China in Sri Lanka)
