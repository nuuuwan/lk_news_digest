Access Engineering takes largest slice of Ruwanpura Expressway in Rs.68bn contract haul

Three RDA packages worth Rs.68bn, over 5 times last year’s civil engineering revenue

New wins alone exceed Rs.62.9bn order book estimated as at June

Access Engineering PLC (AEL) has secured three Road Development Authority (RDA) contracts worth a combined Rs.68 billion on the Central and Ruwanpura expressways, a haul which on its own amounts to more than five times the revenue its civil engineering arm generated last financial year, a stock filing showed.

The company received letters of acceptance dated November 19 for Package 1J of Section 4 of the Central Expressway, valued at Rs.22.39 billion and for Packages 1E and 1G of Phase 1 of the Ruwanpura Expressway, worth Rs.27.99 billion and Rs.17.62 billion, respectively. All three values are stated exclusive of VAT and will be added to its construction order book.

The letters followed the Cabinet’s approval last week of 23 packages worth Rs.494.81 billion across the two projects. These cover the Kahathuduwa-Ingiriya first phase of the Ruwanpura Expressway, including the system interchange linking it to the Southern Expressway and the 38.5-kilometre Kurunegala-Galewela stretch that forms the first phase of the Central Expressway’s fourth section.

At Rs.45.61 billion, AEL’s two Ruwanpura packages make up 18.1 percent of Rs.251.37 billion awarded for the phase. That is the largest combined share won by any contractor, ahead of Maga Engineering’s Rs.39.99 billion across two packages, although International Construction Consortium holds the single biggest package at Rs.34.94 billion.

AEL’s Central Expressway package represents 9.2 percent of Rs.243.44 billion awarded under 12 packages, giving the company 13.7 percent of the overall value approved.

The latest awards come barely six months after AEL won Package 2E of the Rambukkana-Galagedara stretch of the Central Expressway for Rs.18.70 billion in May, which lifts its expressway wins for the year to about Rs.86.7 billion. In an October research note, HNB Stockbrokers placed the company’s order book at Rs.62.9 billion as at end-June. The three new packages alone are therefore 8 percent larger than the entire backlog AEL carried at the time and would roughly double it. The brokerage observed then that “the existing order book alone is sufficient to sustain activity for nearly five years at the current revenue run rate”.

In its earnings summary for the September quarter, released on November 19, HNB Stockbrokers noted that the two Ruwanpura packages “should meaningfully expand its order book”. It also expects the year-end government capital spending, which is typically concentrated in the December quarter, to speed up the conversion of the existing orders into revenue.

The new packages also replenish a construction pipeline that had thinned as several flagship projects reached completion. According to its annual report, AEL handed over Package 1M of Section 3 of the Central Expressway to the RDA in December 2025 and the 1,000-unit Stadiumgama housing project to the Urban Development Authority in February 2026, while Phase 1 of the West Container Terminal at the Colombo Port was also handed over during the 2025/26 financial year.

Company-level revenue, which is anchored in construction, slipped 10 percent to Rs.10.89 billion in the six months to September when compared to the same period last year.

However, the civil engineering segment has grown more profitable. Its operating profit climbed 28.9 percent to Rs.1.96 billion in the first half and the operating margin widened to 23.9 percent, from 17.2 percent a year earlier.

Revenue from the external customers rose 9.8 percent to Rs.7.42 billion over the period, although the total segment revenue, which includes work for group companies, dipped 7.2 percent to Rs.8.22 billion. HNB Stockbrokers calculated that civil engineering revenue grew 7.3 percent to Rs.3.9 billion in the September quarter alone.

“Looking ahead, the civil engineering segment is expected to benefit from the opportunities arising from the next phases of the Central Expressway,” the company stated in its 2025/26 annual report.

The work should also flow through to AEL’s construction materials arm, which runs asphalt plants, quarries and crusher plants across the island. The segment’s first-half revenue fell 18.4 percent to Rs.2.67 billion, with HNB Stockbrokers linking a steeper 26 percent drop in the September quarter to the lower concrete demand after the West Container Terminal project was completed in March 2026.

In its annual report, the company had flagged the continued development of the Central Expressway network as a key driver of demand for asphalt, ready-mix concrete and aggregates.

At group level, revenue rose 63 percent to Rs.36.97 billion in the six months ended September 30, 2026, when compared to the same period last year. The handovers at the Marina Square condominium project brought in Rs.8.99 billion, while the automobile revenue grew 47.5 percent to Rs.15.46 billion.

Profit before tax climbed 67 percent to Rs.6.62 billion and the profit attributable to the shareholders rose 128 percent to Rs.5.79 billion, from Rs.2.54 billion.

Meanwhile, AEL’s interest cover improved to 4.34 times, from 3.75 times a year earlier. The operations generated Rs.2.75 billion in net cash during the half, reversing an outflow of Rs.5.51 billion in the same period last year, helped by a Rs.7.69 billion reduction in inventories since March.

The net gearing improved to 0.81 times, from 0.91 times in March, as per HNB Stockbrokers, with equity growing 13.7 percent over the period while the net debt edged up by less than 2 percent to Rs.39.5 billion.

The company also expects to recover about Rs.1.45 billion from the terminated South Asia Commercial and Logistics Hub at the Colombo Port, in which it holds a 15 percent stake. Together, these leave it better placed to fund the mobilisation of the three large packages at once.

The combined Rs.494.81 billion in the expressway contracts is equal to 24.7 percent of the Rs.2 trillion capital expenditure allocation for 2027, as the government pushes to speed up public investment. HNB Stockbrokers, which puts that allocation at about 40 percent higher than this year’s, expects it to “support new order intake”.  The budget allocates Rs.30 billion for the Kurunegala-Galewela section and Rs.27 billion for the Ruwanpura Expressway next year, together 11.5 percent of the value awarded, which means the work and revenue that comes with it, will be spread over several years.

The construction on the Ruwanpura Expressway is slated to begin on December 1. (NF)
