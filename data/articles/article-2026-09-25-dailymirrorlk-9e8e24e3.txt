Avoid taking painkillers amid rising flu cases: Expert

Colombo, Sep. 25 (Daily Mirror) - A surge in Influenza A H1N1 infections is currently being detected in Sri Lanka, with an increase in positive samples identified by the National Influenza Centre at the Medical Research Institute (MRI), Consultant Virologist Dr. Chrishan Jude Jayamaha said.

Dr. Jayamaha said laboratory surveillance at the National Influenza Centre had detected the recent increase in Influenza A H1N1 cases.

He said people should seek medical advice before taking medication for fever, body aches or other symptoms associated with influenza.

People who develop a fever that persists for more than two days should seek medical advice rather than continuing to self-medicate, Dr. Jayamaha advised.

He also advised people suffering from influenza symptoms to be cautious when taking painkillers, particularly non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).

“Do not take painkillers and other NSAIDs,” Dr. Jayamaha said, warning that complications can occur when patients with influenza take certain medicines.

He said pregnant women, elderly people and children below two years of age should be considered higher-risk groups for complications from influenza and should seek medical advice promptly if they develop symptoms.

Dr. Jayamaha also urged the public to remain vigilant as some symptoms of influenza can be similar to those of dengue, which is also prevalent in Sri Lanka.

He advised people with persistent fever or other symptoms to seek medical advice rather than attempting to diagnose or treat themselves.

He also urged those experiencing persistent or worsening symptoms to consult a doctor, particularly if they develop difficulty breathing, severe weakness, chest pain or other signs of complications.

Influenza commonly causes symptoms such as fever, cough, sore throat, runny nose, headache, body aches and fatigue. In some patients, particularly those in higher-risk groups or with underlying health conditions, the infection can lead to more serious complications, including pneumonia.
