CCC presses for legal safeguards as GSP Plus stakes rise

The Ceylon Chamber of Commerce has urged the government to safeguard investor confidence and Sri Lanka’s access to European Union trade concessions as it advances proposed security and organised crime legislation, calling for clearer definitions, judicial oversight and adequate due process.

The Chamber’s intervention comes against an explicit EU call for timely progress on Sri Lanka’s GSP Plus commitments, particularly bringing anti-terrorism legislation into line with international conventions. At the EU–Sri Lanka Joint Commission meeting in February, Sri Lanka also indicated its intention to reapply for the scheme after the current cycle ends.

The Chamber said it had submitted observations on the proposed Protection of the State from Terrorism Act (PSTA) and was finalising its views on the proposed Prevention of Organised Crime Act for submission to the relevant authorities.

While recognising the Government’s efforts to strengthen national security and address emerging challenges, it stressed that legislation must preserve legal certainty and the country’s standing as a predictable, rules-based destination for trade and investment.

“Such measures are essential to maintaining investor confidence, legal certainty, and Sri Lanka’s reputation as a predictable and rules-based destination for trade and investment,” the Chamber said.

According to the European Commission, the EU was Sri Lanka’s second-largest trading partner in 2025, accounting for 12.5 percent of its total merchandise trade. Bilateral goods trade amounted to €3.9 billion, with textiles making up more than half of EU imports from Sri Lanka.

GSP+ removes duties on 66 percent of tariff lines, covering products including textiles and fisheries. The concessions are conditional on the ratification and effective implementation of 27 international conventions covering human rights, labour rights, environmental protection and good governance, with compliance subject to regular monitoring. Sri Lanka regained access to the arrangement in May 2017.

The Chamber emphasised that continued access supports exports, employment, foreign exchange earnings and economic growth. It urged that new legislation align with Sri Lanka’s international commitments and internationally accepted standards to strengthen prospects for retaining the benefits.

The proposed counter-terrorism framework has also attracted criticism from rights groups. In an October 5 assessment, Human Rights Watch said the proposed replacement for the Prevention of Terrorism Act retained an overly broad definition of terrorism and inadequate judicial oversight, arguing that these shortcomings could jeopardise Sri Lanka’s EU trade benefits. These are the organisation’s assessments, rather than an EU determination on eligibility.

At the February Joint Commission meeting, the EU encouraged Sri Lanka to engage with the competent UN bodies on counter-terrorism reform, while Sri Lanka reaffirmed its commitment to implementing the conventions underpinning the existing GSP Plus arrangement.

The Chamber said it would continue engaging with policymakers and other stakeholders to ensure legislative reforms address national priorities while supporting economic growth and competitiveness.
