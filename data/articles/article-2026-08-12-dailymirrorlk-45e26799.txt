BASL urges Government to withdraw 22nd Amendment

Colombo, August 12 (Daily Mirror) - The Bar Association of Sri Lanka (BASL) has urged the Government to refrain from proceeding with the proposed 22nd Amendment to the Constitution in its present form, warning that the proposed extension of the retirement age of Supreme Court and Court of Appeal judges would directly affect the independence of the Judiciary.

The BASL issued the statement today (12), shortly before a scheduled meeting with President Anura Kumara Dissanayake to discuss concerns surrounding the proposed constitutional amendment.

The BASL said it had grave concerns over the Bill, which was published in the Gazette on August 7, and questioned the Government’s stated justification that the amendment was intended to expedite hearings and improve the efficiency of the judicial system.

According to the BASL, the correlation between extending the retirement age of superior court judges and achieving those objectives remained unclear.

The legal community, including the BASL and the Judicial Service Association, had unanimously resolved to oppose the proposed amendment on the basis that it constituted direct interference with judicial independence, the statement said.

The BASL said its concerns had also been echoed by religious leaders, civil society groups, and prominent overseas law and judges’ associations.

The BASL further maintained that, if the Government proceeded with the amendment despite objections from stakeholders, the proposed constitutional change could only be validly enacted with the approval of the People at a referendum.

The BASL maintained that Article 107 of the Constitution, which deals with the retirement age of judges of the superior courts, is contained under the heading “Independence of the Judiciary” in Chapter XV.

It also referred to Articles 3 and 4(c), under which sovereignty is vested in the People and judicial power is recognised as a component of that sovereignty.

The BASL cited a 2022 Special Determination of the Supreme Court in **SC SD 64-71/2022**, in which the Court observed that any constitutional amendment affecting the retirement age or period of office of incumbent judges would impinge on judicial independence and violate Article 3, thereby requiring a referendum.

The BASL said this determination made clear, in its view, that the proposed amendment was an entrenched provision that could not be validly enacted without the approval of the People at a referendum.

The BASL also raised concerns over the constitutional implications if the Bill were placed on Parliament’s Order Paper.

It said any citizen would then have the right to challenge the constitutionality of the proposed amendment before the Supreme Court. Such a challenge, the BASL warned, could place Supreme Court judges in an “embarrassing and constitutionally sensitive position”, as they would be required to determine the legality of an amendment that would extend their own tenure.

The BASL therefore said that, if the Government decided to proceed with the proposal, the Cabinet should directly submit the Bill to the People at a referendum, thereby avoiding a situation in which the Supreme Court would have to determine the constitutionality of the amendment.

The BASL also criticised the lack of consultation with principal stakeholders, saying that constitutional amendments of this nature in other jurisdictions had generally been preceded by transparent, inclusive, and lengthy consultative processes.

The BASL said it considered it its duty to safeguard the independence of the Judiciary, which it described as an indispensable cornerstone of the Rule of Law and constitutional governance.

The statement was signed by BASL President Rajeev Amarasuriya and Secretary Nalin de Silva.
