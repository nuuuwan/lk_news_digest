State to take ownership of Neville Fernando Hospital, Air Force to manage

Colombo, Sep. 22 (Daily Mirror) - The Government is set to take full ownership of the Dr. Neville Fernando Teaching Hospital (NFTH), with the Sri Lanka Air Force entrusted with its day-to-day management under a new hybrid operational framework approved by the Cabinet of Ministers.

Under the new arrangement, the hospital and its assets will be transferred to a company wholly owned by the Treasury, while the Air Force will be responsible for the hospital’s administration and daily operations.

The Cabinet approved the framework based on a joint proposal submitted by President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, in his capacity as Minister of Defence, and the Minister of Health and Mass Media.

The proposed company will be established as a state enterprise under the Companies Act, with the Treasury holding 100% of its shares.

An 11-member Board of Directors will be appointed by the Secretary to the Treasury on the recommendation of the Secretary to the Ministry of Defence.

Under the financial arrangements, the Treasury will fund staff salaries and operational expenses of the hospital from 2026 to 2028.

The hospital is thereafter expected to operate as a self-financing institution.

The hospital will provide both free and fee-based inpatient and outpatient services to the public.

To support the provision of free healthcare services, the Ministry of Health and Mass Media will provide pharmaceuticals, medical supplies and other consumables worth an estimated Rs. 125 million annually through its budgetary allocations.

The administrative operations of the hospital were transferred to the Ministry of Health with effect from August 1, 2017, following a Cabinet decision made on June 27, 2017.
