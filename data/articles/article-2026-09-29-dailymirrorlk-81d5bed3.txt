Cabinet approves committee to restore Sri Lankan heritage abroad

Colombo, Sep. 29 (Daily Mirror) - The Cabinet of Ministers has approved the appointment of a committee to facilitate the restoration of Sri Lankan cultural artefacts currently in foreign possession, particularly those removed from the country during the colonial period.

The committee, titled the ‘Committee for the Restoration of Sri Lankan Cultural Heritage in Foreign Possessions,’ will be chaired by the Additional Secretary (Cultural Promotion and Foreign Affairs) of the Ministry of Buddha Sasana, Religious and Cultural Affairs.

The committee will be responsible for maintaining direct communication with relevant foreign authorities on the return of Sri Lankan cultural artefacts and providing advice to the Government on the restoration process.

The move follows provisions contained in Articles 7 and 13 of the 1970 UNESCO Convention on the Means of Prohibiting and Preventing the Illicit Import, Export and Transfer of Ownership of Cultural Property, which provide for the return of cultural property belonging to a State that is in the possession of foreign parties.

As part of earlier efforts to restore Sri Lankan cultural heritage, the gun belonged to the Lewke Disawa, which was held at the Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam, Netherlands, was scheduled to be returned to Sri Lanka in 2023 as the first phase of the restoration programme.

The Government has now considered it necessary to establish a dedicated steering committee to coordinate further discussions with the Dutch side and other relevant parties on the restoration of Sri Lankan artefacts.

Accordingly, Cabinet approved the proposal submitted by the Minister of Buddha Sasana, Religious and Cultural Affairs to establish the committee and provide the Government with necessary recommendations on recovering cultural property removed from Sri Lanka during the colonial period.
