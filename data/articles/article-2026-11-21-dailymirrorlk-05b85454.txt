Parliamentary Committee calls for proper recycling and disposal of electric vehicle batteries

Colombo, November 21 (Daily Mirror)- The Committee on Ways and Means has stressed the importance of establishing a proper system to ensure that importers and manufacturers take responsibility for the environmentally safe disposal and recycling of electric vehicle (EV) batteries at the end of their lifespan.

The matter was raised during a recent meeting of the Committee held at Parliament under the chairmanship of MP Wijesiri Basnayake.

During the discussion, the Committee reviewed the number and value of vehicles imported into the country following the relaxation of vehicle import restrictions, along with the tax framework applicable to vehicle imports. Attention was also drawn to the complexities involved in calculating import taxes, which depend on several factors, including engine capacity, Cost, Insurance and Freight (CIF) value, and excise duty. Concerns regarding Customs valuation procedures were also discussed.

The Committee further examined delays in the clearance of imported goods arising from the need to secure approvals from multiple government institutions in addition to Sri Lanka Customs. Officials highlighted that the absence of an integrated digital system connecting relevant institutions, including the Department of Import and Export Control, has created coordination challenges and delayed the approval process.

These delays have resulted in containers being held at ports for extended periods, increasing costs for importers through additional storage and related charges. Accordingly, the Committee emphasised the need to improve coordination among government agencies and streamline procedures to facilitate faster clearance of imported goods.

Environmental and regulatory concerns surrounding the growing import and use of electric vehicles also received considerable attention. In particular, the Committee highlighted the lack of an established mechanism in Sri Lanka for the proper disposal and recycling of used EV batteries.

The importance of introducing appropriate quality standards for electric vehicles and their batteries was also emphasised. In addition, the need to establish suitable Harmonized System (HS) codes to ensure accurate Customs classification and taxation of electric vehicles was discussed.

The Committee also underscored the importance of simplifying tax policies to encourage the adoption of electric vehicles and make them more accessible to the general public. Promoting renewable energy solutions, particularly solar power and battery energy storage systems, was identified as another important measure to minimise the additional pressure that EV charging could place on the national electricity grid.

In this regard, the Committee discussed the introduction of tax concessions and other policy incentives to encourage electric vehicle imports and usage, updating Customs classifications and HS codes in line with international standards, and establishing a comprehensive system for the environmentally responsible collection, disposal and recycling of end-of-life electric vehicle batteries.
