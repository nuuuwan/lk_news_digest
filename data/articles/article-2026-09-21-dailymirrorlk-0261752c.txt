New music licensing rules introduced for weddings, parties and events

Colombo, Sep. 21 (Daily Mirror) - New regulations requiring licences for the public use of certain music at weddings, parties, hotels, banks, supermarkets, corporate functions and other events have come into effect, as part of measures to protect intellectual property rights.

The regulations, gazetted by the Association of Creators and Publishers of Sri Lanka (ACPOSL) under the National Intellectual Property Office of Sri Lanka, came into force last Tuesday.

Under the new system, hotels and other establishments have been informed that licences must be obtained for events featuring music covered by the regulations. Fees range from Rs. 20,000 to Rs. 125,000 depending on the type of event and the number of attendees.

The licensing requirement initially covers Sri Lankan musical works represented by ACPOSL, as well as Hindi and Tamil musical works represented through the Indian Performing Rights Society (IPRS).

The regulations apply to a wide range of events, including weddings and receptions, private parties, corporate events, dinner dances, award ceremonies, product launches, promotional and sponsored events, conferences featuring musical entertainment, DJ shows, live band performances, concerts and music-focused programmes.

Under the fee structure, DJ events with fewer than 100 attendees will be charged Rs. 20,000, while events with 100 to 300 attendees will cost Rs. 30,000 and those with more than 300 attendees will cost Rs. 40,000.

For live music events, the corresponding fees are Rs. 25,000, Rs. 35,000 and Rs. 50,000.

Private or corporate DJ events will carry fees of Rs. 25,000 for fewer than 100 attendees, Rs. 40,000 for 100 to 300 attendees and Rs. 50,000 for more than 300 attendees.

Private or corporate events featuring live music or hybrid performances will require fees of Rs. 50,000, Rs. 75,000 and Rs. 125,000 respectively, based on attendance.

The licence must be obtained at least 14 days before the event. The responsibility for obtaining the licence will rest with the hotel or the event organiser, including corporate clients, wedding organisers or individual organisers.

However, events that exclusively use English-language or other foreign musical works will not be required to obtain a licence under the current regulations.
