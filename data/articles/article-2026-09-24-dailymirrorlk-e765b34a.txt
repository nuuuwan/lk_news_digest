Fitch upgrade paves way for investment-led growth; DFIs urged to go beyond lending: CB Governor

Following Fitch’s sovereign rating upgrade, Sri Lanka is now strengthening its financing architecture to convert the hard-won macroeconomic stability into sustainable investment-led growth, a shift in which the development finance institutions (DFIs) must move beyond traditional lending to mobilise capital and share risk, according to Central Bank Governor Dr. Nandalal Weerasinghe.

Dr. Weerasinghe delivered the keynote address at the inauguration of the 49th Association of Development Financing Institutions in Asia and the Pacific (ADFIAP) Annual Meetings in Colombo, hosted by DFCC Bank PLC.

He noted that the restoration of stability after the economic crisis had laid a stronger foundation for the country’s next phase of development.

“The progress has been recognised by international agencies and by the upgrade of Sri Lanka’s sovereign rating by Fitch Ratings to B- yesterday. The focus is now on transforming this stability into sustainable investment-led growth,” he stressed.

Fitch on Tuesday lifted Sri Lanka’s Long-Term Issuer Default Ratings to B-, from CCC+, with a Stable Outlook, crediting the macro-stabilisation policies and structural reforms for easing the external financing risks.

However, the agency expects the government debt to stand at 92.9 percent of GDP this year. That is down from 96.7 percent in 2025 but still nearly 1.7 times the 54.7 percent median for the B-rated countries.

“In this regard, Sri Lanka is strengthening its financing architecture for a more resilient and sustainable economy,” Dr. Weerasinghe added.

He pointed to the National Climate Finance Strategy 2025 to 2030, launched last October, to integrate climate priorities into fiscal planning and investment decisions.

He also cited the Central Bank’s implementation of the Green Finance Taxonomy, which classifies environmentally sustainable activities to guide investment.

“These initiatives aim to attract both domestic and international capital into priority areas such as renewable energy, resilient infrastructure and sustainable production,” he remarked.

Turning to the institutions gathered in Colombo, Dr. Weerasinghe called on the DFIs to play a more catalytic role in channelling that capital.

“The role of the DFIs, like the members of this association, should go beyond traditional lending. They need to increasingly act as catalysts for mobilising capital, sharing risks and accelerating development impacts,” he urged.

He added that blending concessional and commercial finance could draw broader participation from development partners and private investors.

He cautioned that the geopolitical tensions, trade fragmentation, climate risks and concerns over energy and food security were testing regional resilience.

He also noted that financial integration in Asia and the Pacific remains relatively underdeveloped despite the resilient intra-regional investment. Interoperable payment systems are already advanced in ASEAN through cross-border QR payments and real-time linkages and they are now taking shape in South Asia, he observed.

Meanwhile, ADFIAP Chairman and Agricultural and Rural Development Bank of Cambodia Chief Executive Officer Dr. Kao Thach made a parallel case for the DFIs to rethink how they measure success. He argued that the region’s financing needs for climate action, SMEs and underserved communities far exceed what the public budgets or individual institutions can provide.

“The measure of a development finance institution should no longer be only how much it can lend from its own balance sheet. The more strategic question is how much additional capital it can unlock, mobilise and direct towards development priorities,” he emphasised.

“A single DFI may finance a project. A strong network of DFIs can build an investment ecosystem.”

Dr. Thach also urged the ADFIAP to evolve from a knowledge-sharing network into a regional platform where the members jointly develop project pipelines, design co-financing arrangements and connect projects with private investors.

“We should leave with new commitments to act together,” he insisted.
