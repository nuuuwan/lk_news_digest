Budget blues, worker agitation and Minister Lal Kantha

It’s just over a week since ‘good’ president Anura Kumara Dissanayake (AKD) presented his last budget on  November 12 2026. Unfortunately,  the president since he assumed power has unfailingly broken most of the promises he made prior to the presidential and general elections.

Country-wide,  he has managed to make a mess of most of what he promised the masses. To the country in general, he promised to dish the IMF deal signed by his predecessor Ranil Wickremesinghe. That promise was made so long ago, the president now seems to have forgotten about it.

During his 2027 budget presentation,  he announced a high-level Cabinet-appointed committee to evaluate whether to seek a follow-up programme after the current 17th Extended Fund Facility (EFF) ends in March. Before the presidential and general elections the president and his party promised to scrap the IMF deal and bring down the cost of living.

Lal Kantha,  the president’s Minister of Agriculture and senior member of the Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna (JVP),  on Nov. 19 2026-  acknowledged that the salary increase granted to public sector employees had not kept pace with the rising cost of living.

However, without tackling the problem, he simply announced the issue would have to be considered in the NEXT budget. No further increase to bring public sector salaries in line with the cost of living has been provided for, in the 2027 Budget he announced…

The private sector in our country is even worse off. The Ceylon United Business Alliance (CUBA) warned that the 2027 Budget lacks targeted tax, VAT, or Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL) relief. It argues this will accelerate closures in sectors like the cottage apparel industry. In fact,  today many private sector companies do not pay wages in a timely manner.

How does Minister Lalkantha expect the people of this country to live? According to the government’s own figures,  the minimum wage in the country at November 2026 stands at Rs. 30,000/- per month or R. 1,200/- per day, effective since January 1 2026. Yet according to the department of Census and Statistics as of November 2026, a balanced, nutritious meal costs an average of Rs. 400/- to Rs. 1,000/-.

A family of four (father, mother and two children) would therefore need over Rs. 30,000/- for just a single meal per day! This figure does not take into consideration cost of travel, clothes, medical costs etc. Yet in Mr. Lalkantha’s mind our people must wait until next year’s budget to expect an increase in wages.

No wonder the trade union movements are calling  out for a strike. Doctors have warned of trade union action if their demands for a salary increase is not met. Seven major principals’ associations and roughly 20 teacher trade unions plan a protest on November 23, 2026, demanding a proposed Rs. 25,000 interim allowance that was left out of the 2027 Budget.

The Government Executive Officers (20 Services) have called a one-day island-wide token strike for November 20, 2026. Following a one-day strike by non-academic university workers on November 18, 2026, Inter-University Trade Union Federation (IUTUF) has warned of a broader campaign to paralyse the university system itself.

Yet these are the more advantaged sections of our population. The weaker sections like those working in tea, rubber and coconut estates of the country are much worse off. An ordinary tea plucker on an estate receives a daily wage of Rs. 1,700/-. But does not  receive work daily.

Yet the export of tea earned US$ 1,507.2 million. Rubber and rubber-based products earned US$ 945.7 million and coconut and coconut-based products earned US$ 1,233.0 million. These workers continue to live in single-roomed attached houses dating back to the British period.

According to ‘Geo Factbook’ over 43% of our population live in city slums. A slum household is defined as lacking at least one of five essentials: Durable housing, sufficient living space, safe water, adequate sanitation, or secure tenure. Slum dwellers too,  do not receive regular work and wages range at around Rs. 2,000/- per day.

These people form the bulk of those who voted this government into power. They cannot wait until next year’s budget for an increase in wages.
