Vehicle prices increase by millions

Colombo, Sep. 3 (Daily Mirror) - Vehicle prices in Sri Lanka have begun increasing sharply, with some popular models seeing increases of up to Rs. 4.5 million, following the Government’s decision to extend a 50% surcharge on vehicle import duties until December 31.

A survey conducted by the Daily Mirror among several vehicle sales outlets, along with discussions with vehicle dealers, found that prices had already risen significantly as vehicles imported under Letters of Credit (LCs) opened after May 15 are cleared under the higher duty regime.

Among the latest price increases, a Suzuki Wagon R has gone up by around Rs. 500,000, while a Toyota Raize has increased by approximately Rs. 800,000. A Toyota Yaris has risen by around Rs. 500,000, while the price of a Honda Vezel has increased by about Rs. 900,000.

The increases are considerably steeper for higher-end vehicles. A Toyota Prado has risen by around Rs. 2.5 million, while a Toyota Land Cruiser has increased by approximately *Rs. 4.5 million. Double-cab vehicles have also seen increases of around Rs. 2–2.5 million.

Meanwhile, the price of a BYD Atto 1 has also increased by around Rs. 1 million.

Commenting on the situation, President of the Vehicle Importers Association of Sri Lanka (VIASL), Prasad Manage told the Daily Mirror that the extension of the surcharge had resulted in significant increases in showroom prices.

He said vehicles currently available in showrooms had largely been imported under LCs opened before May 15 and were therefore exempt from the surcharge. However, vehicles imported under LCs opened after May 15 are subject to the higher duty, resulting in substantial price increases.

“We expected the surcharge would not be extended, but due to various reasons it has been continued. As a result, we can expect further increases in vehicle prices,” Manage said.

He noted that around 90% of the vehicles cleared so far had been imported under LCs that were not subject to the surcharge. However, the clearance of vehicles covered by the new duty regime has now begun, resulting in the latest price increases.

Manage said the Government should consider alternative measures to stabilise vehicle prices rather than repeatedly imposing or extending surcharges. Revising tax policy, regulating LCs and introducing fixed rates could be considered as possible measures, he said.

With vehicles subject to the higher duty now entering the market, dealers warned that further price increases could be seen as more such consignments are cleared.
