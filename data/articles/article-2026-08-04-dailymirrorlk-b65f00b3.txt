Unconvicted inmates make up over 72% of prison population

Colombo, August 04 (Daily Mirror) - Amid growing concerns over overcrowding in prisons across the country, it is identified that nearly three-fourths of inmates are remand prisoners with cases pending against them.

The authorities are now implementing a series of initiatives to increase capacity and strengthen prison services, following recent incidents of unrest at Negombo and Mahara prisons.

According to the latest official statistics, the total prison population stood at 39,516 inmates as of August 1, 2026. The figures show a significant imbalance between convicted prisoners and those awaiting trial or sentencing, with unconvicted inmates accounting for more than 72 per cent of the total prison population.

Of the total number of inmates, 28,646 are classified as unconvicted prisoners, including 26,935 males and 1,711 females. Meanwhile, the convicted prisoner population stands at 10,870, comprising 10,588 males and 282 females. Overall, male inmates make up the majority of the prison population, with 37,523 males compared to 1,993 female inmates.

The issue of overcrowding came into focus following a major riot at Mahara Prison on August 1, where one inmate died, and six others were injured. The unrest, which began at around 4.00 p.m. on Saturday, continued for more than 13 hours before security forces brought the situation under control by 6.00 a.m. the following day.

The Committee for the Protection of Prisoners' Rights has attributed the incident to severe overcrowding at the facility. Chairman of the Committee, Attorney-at-Law Senaka Perera, alleged that conditions inside prisons have deteriorated due to the excessive number of inmates being held beyond the facilities' capacity.

Perera said that Mahara Prison, which has an approved capacity of 991 inmates, is currently accommodating 4,447 prisoners. He claimed that existing facilities are inadequate to meet the requirements of the inmate population, contributing to worsening living conditions.

Earlier, Cabinet Spokesman and Minister Nalinda Jayatissa said the country's prison population had increased from around 28,000 to more than 40,000 since the government assumed office in 2024. He attributed much of the increase to intensified anti-narcotics operations carried out under the "Ratama Ekata" programme.

The rapid growth in the inmate population has placed considerable pressure on the prison system, prompting the government to accelerate measures to expand capacity and improve prison conditions.

As part of these efforts, the government has begun recruiting additional prison officers and implementing several development projects. Construction work has been completed at the Pallekele Prison, while new buildings at the Pallansena Prison have also been commissioned. The Mahara Prison storage complex was completed in 2025, and temporary accommodation facilities are being completed at Welikada, Magazine, and Colombo prisons to reduce congestion.

The Minister also said that a high-security prison facility has been established at the Welisara Navy Camp to accommodate inmates requiring enhanced security arrangements.

Further prison development projects are planned between 2026 and 2029 at Pallekele, Wataraka, Kalutara, Kuruvita, and Matara.
