Justice Minister warns of attempts to incite prisoners

Colombo, Aug. 7 (Daily Mirror) - Justice and National Integration Minister Harshana Nanayakkara has alleged that certain individuals with ulterior motives are attempting to manipulate inmates to create unrest in prisons, while urging prisoners not to fall prey to the interests of a few individuals.

The Minister made these remarks during a special statement issued today (07) regarding recent incidents reported at several prisons.

Addressing the concerns of prisoners and their families, Minister Nanayakkara said one of the main issues identified over the past year was the delay in obtaining reports from the Government Analyst’s Department, which had prevented some remand prisoners from obtaining bail.

He said the Government had already taken steps to address the issue, noting that the Government Analyst’s Department had faced a shortage of 118 vacancies, which had affected the timely release of reports.

The Minister said steps had been taken to recruit around 70 staff members required to expedite the preparation of reports, while new equipment would also be introduced this month to increase efficiency.

He added that measures were being taken to increase the department’s capacity to issue between 7,500 and 9,000 reports per month in the future.

Minister Nanayakkara further said relatives of inmates had pointed out that delays in obtaining bail occurred in certain drug-related cases due to legal provisions requiring appeals to be made to the Court of Appeal when the pure weight of narcotics exceeded 10 grams.

He said a proposal to amend the relevant law would be presented to the Cabinet, enabling suspects in certain drug-related cases to seek bail from the High Court.

Meanwhile, the Minister urged inmates to remain patient, stating that the Government was taking steps to improve prison facilities and reduce overcrowding.

“Your issues will be resolved. Do not allow yourselves to be used for the interests of a few individuals,” he said.

He warned that any destruction of prison resources due to unrest would ultimately affect the inmates themselves.

“Even if the Government provides resources and facilities, if they are set on fire due to the actions of a few individuals with hidden agendas, it is the inmates who will suffer,” the Minister said.

He called on prisoners to cooperate with the Government’s efforts to expedite forensic reports, improve facilities and amend laws to provide quicker access to bail.
