Central Bank steps up digital payment push in Northern province

The Central Bank last week stepped up efforts to expand digital payment adoption in the Northern province, taking its PayDigital public awareness campaign to Jaffna.

Held under the theme ‘Shaping the Future through Digital Payments’, the programme sought to encourage businesses, government institutions and the wider public to shift towards safer, faster and more convenient digital transactions.

Jaffna became the fifth location covered by the campaign, following similar programmes in Hambantota, Nuwara Eliya, Dambulla and Trincomalee.

Northern Province Governor Nagalingam Vedanayagam attended the inauguration as the chief guest.

Addressing the gathering, Central Bank Payments and Settlements Department Director Vasantha Alwis said that the Central Bank was pleased to bring the digital payments awareness initiative to the Jaffna region, following its successful programmes in some other parts of the country.

He said the main objective of the initiative was to increase public awareness of digital payments and encourage individuals and businesses to adopt payment methods that are safe, convenient and efficient.

“Today, digital payments have become an essential part of modern life. Whether we are purchasing groceries, paying utility bills, transferring money or conducting business transactions, digital payment solutions enable us to complete these activities quickly, securely and conveniently,” he said.

Further he said, one of the key payment solutions promoted through the programme is LankaQR, Sri Lanka’s national QR-based payment mechanism. LankaQR enables the users to make instant payments by scanning a QR code using a compatible mobile payment application.

The system provides a fast, simple and secure method of making real-time payments across the participating banks and other payment service providers. For businesses, particularly the small and medium-sized enterprises, LankaQR provides an affordable and efficient means of accepting digital payments without the need to invest in expensive payment equipment.

By accepting digital payments, the merchants can serve more customers, improve operational efficiency and become part of Sri Lanka’s growing digital economy.

Alwis emphasised that the Central Bank remains committed to developing a payment system that is safe, efficient and accessible to everyone.

He stressed, however, that technology alone cannot bring about a successful transformation.

“The success of digital payments depends on public awareness, trust and confidence,” he said, highlighting the importance of awareness programmes in helping people understand the benefits of digital payments and encouraging wider adoption.

The programme also provided an opportunity for the members of the public and business community to interact directly with the participating banks, finance companies and e-money service providers.

The representatives of the participating institutions were available throughout the day to explain their digital payment products and services, answer questions and provide practical guidance.

The members of the public were also given opportunities to register for digital payment services, learn how to use them safely and gain practical experience with digital payment solutions.

Meanwhile, the merchants and business owners were provided with assistance on adopting the LankaQR payment acceptance facility, enabling them to offer the customers a modern, secure and convenient payment option.

Alwis encouraged the participants to make full use of the programme by visiting the exhibition stalls, engaging with the participating institutions and exploring the various digital payment solutions available.

He said embracing digital payment technologies would not only make everyday transactions more convenient but also contribute to Sri Lanka’s broader digital transformation.

Vedanayagam said that as the world continues to advance rapidly, amid a technological revolution, the digital payments have become the “heartbeat of the digital economy”.

He said the programme represented an important initiative connecting three key national policy priorities of the government led by President Anura Kumara Dissanayake — poverty eradication, Clean Sri Lanka and digitalisation — with the everyday lives of the people.

Vedanayagam noted that the world is rapidly moving away from traditional cash-based transactions towards cashless payment systems.

Sri Lanka, as a country aspiring to compete and progress alongside the rest of the world and the Northern province as an important region within the country, must embrace this transformation, he said.

Vedanayagam stressed that the digital payments should not be viewed merely as an alternative way of conducting financial transactions but as an important component of a modern economic framework that enhances speed, security, transparency and accountability.

He further said the digital payments could contribute significantly to the objectives of the Clean Sri Lanka initiative by helping to reduce financial fraud and corruption while strengthening tax compliance and transparency.

Highlighting the growing number of domestic and international tourists visiting Jaffna, Vedanayagam called on the traders, restaurants, accommodation providers and other service providers to adapt to the modern digital technologies.

He said the availability of international and modern digital payment systems would make it easier for the tourists to access goods and services without facing the difficulties associated with cash transactions.

Vedanayagam encouraged the businesses across the Northern province to embrace the digital payment systems as part of their efforts to modernise their services and contribute to the region’s economic development.
