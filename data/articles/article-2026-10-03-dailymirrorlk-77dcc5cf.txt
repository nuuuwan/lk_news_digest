Melwa dismisses Seeduwa plant allegations as baseless; hints at business motives

Melwa Group has rejected the allegations that its new cement plant in Seeduwa is being built in an environmentally sensitive zone.

It suggested that some of the criticism could be driven by parties with business interests rather than by people living in the area.

Melwa Group Head of Sales and Brand Malik Kannangara took a lighter view of the controversy, remarking that the critics had given the brand publicity before it had even started production.

“As the person in charge of the brand, I must say they have given us some publicity even before we started. We should be very thankful to whoever started it,” he quipped.

He added that the company intends to bring cement to the market “as strong as iron”.

On a more serious note, Kannangara insisted that the plant had obtained every approval required for a factory of its kind, including from the Urban Development Authority, Central Environmental Authority (CEA) and Civil Aviation Authority.

He maintained that the construction was proceeding under the conditions attached to those approvals and confirmed that no agency had issued any legal notice or directive to halt or modify the project.

He also noted that the CEA officials, who visit several times a month, had inspected the site as recently as the previous day.

“Allegations can be made by anyone but when we make an allegation, there should be a basis for it,” he argued.

He added that the company had invited several of its critics to visit the premises and see the technology for themselves.

Kannangara went on to claim that those making the allegations were not the residents of the area.

“At times there may be a group with business expectations behind it. But since nobody has come forward directly, we cannot name any person or institution,” he said.

However, around 1,000 residents of Katunayake staged a protest against the plant in July, according to the media reports at the time. The protesters claimed that a building plan approved by the CEA for 3,857 square metres in December 2020 had since been expanded to 12,925 square metres, more than three times the original footprint.

They also raised concerns over the cement dust near the airport runway and the plant’s proximity to three schools and the Negombo lagoon.

When a journalist from the area pointed to the other industrial facilities nearby, including a large concrete mixing operation, Kannangara acknowledged that several factories operate within a kilometre of the site but declined to name them.

“What you said is true. I don’t wish to name them but it is true,” he responded.

Melwa Cement Chief Operations Manager Chinthaka Thuduwewatta clarified that the required buffer from the adjoining lagoon had been 10 metres until 2025, when it was raised to 100 metres.

“Fortunately, we had not built anything within the 100 metres,” he noted.

Thuduwewatta further explained that the plant involves no combustion, uses no water in its process and operates entirely within the enclosed structures.

He added that it discharges nothing into the lagoon and that the noise and dust controls are in place, given the hotels in the vicinity. The existing green belt of around 40 metres will also be extended along the boundary.
