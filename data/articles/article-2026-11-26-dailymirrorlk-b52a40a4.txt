Restrictions on marriage proposal, rituals and enchantment advertisements questioned

Colombo, Nov. 26 (Daily Mirror) - The government’s decision to impose restrictions on advertisements pertaining to marriage proposals, rituals and enchantment practices was questioned in Parliament yesterday.

MP Dayasiri Jayasekera who questioned the move said restrictions should not be imposed. He was referring to a remark made by Minister of Women and Child Affairs Saroja Paulraj who has said certain advertisements including marriage proposals where the caste which a potential bride or a groom belongs to and advertisements on rituals and enchantment practices will be banned.

“The move comes from those who spoke against worshiping of idols and certain trees and Bo trees. They also said that the historic Jethawanaramaya should be filled with garbage,” the MP said.

He said people should be free to get the media to advertise anything while they should be allowed to engage in a profession of their choice.

However, the Office of the Minister of Women and Child Affairs rejected media reports claiming that Minister Saroja Savithri Paulraj had announced plans to introduce legislation banning matrimonial advertisements that specify caste.

In a clarification issued yesterday, the Minister’s Office said certain media outlets had taken the Minister’s remarks out of context and published misleading headlines.

The clarification follows a media briefing held at the Department of Government Information on November 23 to explain the 16-day national campaign to eliminate gender-based violence.

During the briefing, a journalist questioned whether the Government should intervene in matrimonial advertisements specifying social criteria such as caste, pointing out that such advertisements could subject certain sections of society to harassment and discrimination.

Responding to the question, the Minister said certain matters could be disregarded when making personal decisions.

She also drew attention to misleading advertisements promoting superstition, attraction spells and occult practices, particularly on social media, noting that such content adversely affected society, especially the younger generation.

The Minister explained that these concerns were among the factors being considered in plans to introduce legislation establishing a code of ethics for the media.

“People’s mindsets must change. Any society must move towards progress, moving forward rather than backwards,” she said.

The Minister’s Office emphasised that her remarks did not indicate any intention to regulate matrimonial advertisements or to criticise, question or prohibit any cultural identity.
