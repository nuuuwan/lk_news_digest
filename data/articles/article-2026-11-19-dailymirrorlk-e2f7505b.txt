GMOA warns of trade union action over failure to address doctors’ demands

Colombo, Nov. 19 (Daily Mirror) - The Government Medical Officers’ Association (GMOA) has warned that it may launch trade union action if the Government fails to address its demands on doctors’ salaries, allowances and other benefits.

The GMOA said its Central Committee, which met today, decided to give the Government an opportunity to resolve the issues through constructive discussions but warned that the Executive Committee would decide on future trade union action if there was no positive response.

The association has called on the Government to implement five key demands, including the full payment of the approved Disturbance, Availability and Transport (DAT) allowance and the immediate restoration of the suspended one-third of additional duty and holiday allowances.

Its other demands include exempting the DAT allowance from taxes, formally recognising doctors eligible for transport facilities under Circular 22/99 and issuing vehicle import permits with tax concessions.

The GMOA said it submitted a set of 20 proposals to the Government, including the President and the Health Minister, in July 2026, aimed at strengthening the healthcare system and preventing doctors and other healthcare professionals from leaving the country.

The proposals cover five main areas and were submitted as part of the process of preparing the 2027 Budget.

However, the association said it had been requesting discussions with the Government for more than three months without receiving a satisfactory response.

It said doctors had staged protests at provincial and national levels to highlight their concerns while avoiding trade union action that could inconvenience patients.

The GMOA also claimed that doctors had contributed to savings of more than Rs. 44 billion over the past two years following the suspension of certain allowances due to the country's economic difficulties.

It warned that continuing what it described as unfair treatment of doctors could threaten the free healthcare system, as more medical professionals might leave the country.

The association said Sri Lanka's healthcare system had achieved internationally recognised health standards and remained one of the country's most important social security services.

However, it argued that the Government had failed to provide adequate salaries and allowances for medical professionals, including specialist doctors, and had not taken sufficient steps to address staff shortages.

The GMOA said that the President, the Health Minister and the Government should take full responsibility for any difficulties faced by patients if trade union action becomes necessary due to the failure to address its demands.
