Legal action over destruction of 110 trees in Wilpattu buffer zone

Colombo, Sep.19 (Daily Mirror) - Legal action has been initiated against those responsible for the destruction of around 110 trees in the Nelum Wewa area of Parana Eluwankulama, Vanathavilluwa, located within the buffer zone of the Wilpattu National Park.

A joint field inspection was conducted on September 18 following intervention by the Ministry of Environment, with officials from the Central Environmental Authority (CEA), Forest Conservation Department, Department of Wildlife Conservation, North Western Provincial Environmental Authority and several other state institutions.

The inspection found that approximately 110 trees had fallen after being damaged by fire, including 61 large Kumbuk trees, 24 Bakmee trees and two Madan trees.

The Nelum Wewa is administered by the Irrigation Department and has been undergoing rehabilitation after its bund was breached during Cyclone Ditwah.

Officials found that soil had been excavated from the tank bed as part of the rehabilitation work. During the excavation, a fire had spread through accumulated dry vegetation, burning the bases of trees and causing them to collapse.

The inspection also revealed that the rehabilitation work had been carried out without obtaining prior approval or an environmental recommendation from the Department of Wildlife Conservation or the CEA, despite the water body being located within the ecologically sensitive buffer zone of Wilpattu National Park.

The Department of Wildlife Conservation has filed a case against the Irrigation Department before the Puttalam Magistrate's Court under the Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance.

Meanwhile, the Vanathavilluwa Divisional Secretary has lodged a complaint with the police over the incident.

The Ministry of Environment said state development projects would not be allowed to cause damage to fragile ecosystems in violation of environmental laws.

It said legal action would be pursued against those responsible under the direction of Environment Minister Dr. Dhammika Patabendi, Deputy Minister Anton Jayakody and Ministry Secretary K. R. Uduwawala.
