SheTrades Sri Lanka launches export guides in local languages to help women-led SMEs access UK market

The SheTrades Sri Lanka Hub has launched sector-specific export handbooks in Sinhala and Tamil, aimed at helping the women entrepreneurs and small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) improve their readiness to export to the UK, leveraging preferential market access available under the UK’s Developing Countries Trading Scheme (DCTS).

The handbooks, developed by the Sri Lanka Export Development Board (EDB), in collaboration with the International Trade Centre and British High Commission in Colombo, focus on the textiles and apparel sector, including women’s accessories, footwear and handicrafts as well as processed agrifood and spices.

The guides provide practical information on export procedures, regulatory requirements and market expectations in the UK, while also outlining the opportunities under the DCTS, which grants duty-free access to around 92 percent of product lines exported from Sri Lanka to the UK.

The initiative is part of the UK government-funded SheTrades Commonwealth+ Programme, which seeks to strengthen the competitiveness and international market access of women-led export-oriented businesses across the Commonwealth countries.

Speaking at the launch, Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Deputy Minister Chathuranga Abeysinghe said the UK remained an important trading partner for Sri Lanka and highlighted the opportunities available through the DCTS framework.

“The UK’s DCTS presents a golden opportunity for Sri Lankan enterprises to enter and scale within the UK market, under the preferential access arrangements,” Abeysinghe said.

The launch event was attended by UK Trade Envoy to Sri Lanka Lord Hannett of Everton, British High Commissioner Andrew Patrick, government officials, the EDB representatives and entrepreneurs. Discussions focused on expanding export opportunities for women-led businesses and addressing the barriers to international market access.

Lord Hannett said translating the handbooks into Sinhala and Tamil would make export-related information more accessible to the entrepreneurs across the country, helping a wider group of businesses better understand the UK market requirements and opportunities.

The EDB said the handbooks are intended to improve export readiness among the women entrepreneurs and SMEs while supporting broader efforts to promote inclusive, export-led growth in Sri Lanka.
