’Here’s to one more’: Lewis targets Sri Lanka scalp in final

ICC: Ireland captain Gaby Lewis believes confidence is rising for her side as they ready for the final of the ICC Women's Emerging Nations Trophy tournament against Sri Lanka in the UAE on Wednesday.

Lewis' side qualified for the one-off title decider with an impressive 44-run triumph over the Netherlands on Monday, with Player of the Match Lara Maritz (57*) and spinner Cara Murray (5/17) leading the way in what was a strong all-round team performance.

It was Ireland's third straight victory of the Super Six phase and Lewis thinks her team is getting better with every appearance at the UAE tournament.

"It was a brilliant win," Lewis said after the win over the Netherlands.

"It was always touch and go coming in here as T20 can be a crazy game, especially this tournament.

"I'm really proud of the performance today. Everyone stood up."

While Sri Lanka have yet to taste defeat during the tournament, Ireland had to earn their place in the title decider via a somewhat different route given they failed to take any points across to the Super Six phase from the initial group stage.

Ireland split four matches during that first group stage - with victories against the UAE and Papua New Guinea offset by losses to Sri Lanka in the tournament opener and Scotland -  leaving them with a trio of must-win contests across the Super Sixes that culminated in a winner-take-all clash with the Netherlands on Monday.

"I think we knew coming in that we could beat the three teams ahead (in the Super Six phase)," Lewis noted.

"I think we had a lot of confidence with parts of our performance in the group stages, but we just didn't have that whole team performance.

"We just took it game by game and I think we're on a bit of a streak now. So here's to one more (win)."

And while Lewis looms as Ireland's key player heading into the final against Sri Lanka, it's Murray that could well prove the wildcard in the eagerly-awaited contest given the right-armer has 15 scalps for her side across the tournament to be the second leading wicket-taker so far at the event.

"She's been unbelievable, not even in this game (against the Netherlands) but this whole tournament," Lewis said.

"She's been putting in (good) performances for many years, but I think in recent times she's just been unbelievable.

"Her confidence and she's really guiding those other two spinners as well, which is really great to see as captain."
