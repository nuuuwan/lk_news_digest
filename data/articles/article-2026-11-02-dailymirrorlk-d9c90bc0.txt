Pan Asia Bank 3Q profit rises 25%

Pan Asia Banking Corporation PLC’s September-quarter net profit rose 25 percent from a year earlier, supported by an impairment reversal, while rising funding costs and operating expenses weighed on income growth.

The bank reported earnings of Rs. 1.09 billion for the three months ended September 30, 2026, against Rs. 872.5 million a year earlier. Earnings per share increased to Rs. 2.47 from Rs. 1.97.

An impairment reversal of Rs. 367.2 million, compared with a charge of Rs. 193.4 million a year earlier, helped pre-tax profit rise 25 percent to Rs. 1.78 billion.

Quarterly net interest income grew 11 percent to Rs. 3.61 billion. Interest income increased 36 percent to Rs. 10.41 billion, but interest expenses climbed faster, rising 55 percent to Rs. 6.81 billion.

Net fee and commission income edged up 1 percent to Rs. 717.1 million. Total operating income increased 3 percent to Rs. 4.25 billion, while operating expenses rose 12 percent to Rs. 2.29 billion.

For the first nine months, net profit increased 19 percent to Rs. 3.59 billion, with earnings per share rising to Rs. 8.12.

Cumulative net interest income grew 12 percent to Rs. 10.68 billion, while net fee and commission income increased 17 percent to Rs. 2.18 billion. The bank attributed fee income growth to lending, trade finance, card usage and higher transaction volumes.

However, its nine-month net interest margin narrowed to 4.16 percent from 4.55 percent. The cost-to-income ratio increased to 51.49 percent from 48.94 percent, while a reversal of excess tax provisions from previous years also supported cumulative earnings.

The bank said lower impairment charges reflected improved credit quality, recovery efforts and favourable changes in economic assumptions. Refinements to its expected credit loss models also affected impairment assessments.

Treasury income faced pressure from rising government securities yields, which reduced gains from trading and investment securities sales.

Total assets increased by Rs. 66.43 billion, or 22 percent, from end-December to Rs. 374.45 billion at September 30. Net loans and advances expanded 17 percent to Rs. 243.45 billion, while customer deposits grew 19 percent to Rs. 274.89 billion over the same period.

The gross Stage 3 loan ratio, a measure of impaired lending, declined to 3.67 percent from 4.62 percent at end-2025. The corresponding net ratio fell to 1.44 percent from 1.73 percent, with provision coverage at 60.87 percent.

Return on equity stood at 15.25 percent, while pre-tax return on assets was 1.83 percent.

The bank’s Common Equity Tier 1 capital ratio was 14.34 percent, while its total capital ratio stood at 15.67 percent, above the respective regulatory minimums of 7 percent and 12.5 percent. Its all-currency liquidity coverage ratio was 138.98 percent, against the minimum requirement of 100 percent.
