Short-to-belly end of yield curve nudges up,while long end remains steady

Stemming from the cautious investor sentiment due to the geopolitical escalations, the short-to-belly end of the yield curve saw some upward movement by approximately 10-20bps.

However, the long end of the yield curve held broadly unchanged. Meanwhile, the trading volumes were high in the secondary market.

Over the short end, 15.03.2028 traded at 10.10 percent, while 01.09.2028, 15.10.2028 and 15.12.2028 traded within the range of 10.25 percent-10.40 percent.

At the 2029 segment, both the 15.09.2029 and 15.12.2029 maturities changed hands at 10.70 percent. Moving ahead, 15.05.2030 and 01.07.2030 traded at 10.90 percent and 10.95 percent, respectively, while 01.08.2030 saw trading between 10.95 percent-11.10 percent and 15.10.2030 was dealt between 11.05 percent-11.20 percent.

Furthermore, 01.12.2031 traded between 11.52 percent-11.60 percent and the 01.11.2033 bond was quoted at 11.85 percent. Towards the belly end, 15.06.2034, 15.09.2034 and 15.10.2034 traded within the range of 11.96 percent-12.00 percent. Finally, 15.08.2036 bond traded at 12.00 percent

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee depreciated against the US dollar, standing at Rs.328.68/US dollar, compared to Rs.328.60/US dollar seen earlier.

Market liquidity in the banking system expanded to Rs.381.40 billion, compared to Rs.366.11 billion recorded previously.
