Family doctor system must not threaten free healthcare

Colombo, Sep. 8 (Daily Mirror) - The proposed introduction of a family doctor system in Sri Lanka should be implemented in a manner that strengthens, rather than undermines, the country’s free healthcare system, Medical and Civil Rights Doctors' Trade Union Alliance Chairman and Specialist Dr Chamal Sanjeewa said.

He said the family doctor concept could play an important role in reducing overcrowding at the outpatient departments of major government hospitals and improving the management of patients within the public healthcare system.

However, he said that the initiative should not eventually create a system in which patients are required to bear additional costs to access essential medical services.

According to Dr Sanjeewa, the concept of family doctors had been discussed in Sri Lanka for many years, but successive governments had been reluctant to implement it due to political considerations and resistance from sections of the health sector.

He said the previous Good Governance Government had taken initial steps towards introducing the concept in 2015, although the socio-political environment at the time prevented it from being implemented successfully.

Dr Sanjeewa said the current Government’s efforts to introduce the family doctor system were positive, but warned that several fundamental weaknesses within the health sector needed to be addressed alongside the reform.

He questioned whether the country’s existing health expenditure was producing sufficient practical benefits for the public and pointed to concerns over the implementation of projects funded through both domestic allocations and international assistance.

He also highlighted concerns over overlapping health programmes, bureaucratic procedures, delays in implementing projects and difficulties in maintaining a consistent supply of medicines and medical equipment.

Dr Sanjeewa said health sector reforms should also address weaknesses in human resource management, recruitment, specialist training, disease prevention, pharmaceutical policy and coordination among healthcare institutions.

He said that both the public and private sectors should be considered when developing a comprehensive national healthcare strategy.

The health expert further warned that the responsibilities placed on specialist doctors and other healthcare workers should also be taken into consideration when introducing the family doctor system.

He said the Government should implement the concept gradually, with the support and participation of healthcare professionals and other relevant stakeholders.

Dr Sanjeewa cautioned that if the system was introduced without proper planning and adequate resources, it could create another barrier to healthcare access and potentially push more patients towards private hospitals.

He said the primary objective of the reform should be to improve access, reduce pressure on major hospitals and provide patients with better continuity of care while safeguarding the principle of free healthcare.

He said that the family doctor system should therefore become part of a broader effort to modernise Sri Lanka’s healthcare system, rather than being implemented as an isolated project.
