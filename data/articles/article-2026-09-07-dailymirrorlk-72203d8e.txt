’’US$10 Billion in Exports” — Tell the People the Full Story

Colombo, Sep. 7 (Daily Mirror) - The Government proudly promotes the fact that Sri Lanka’s total export earnings exceeded US$10 billion during the first seven months of 2026. An increase in exports is certainly a positive development, and there is no reason to deny that. However, highlighting the US$10 billion figure alone and creating the impression that the country’s external trade position has significantly improved does not present the complete picture.

From January to July 2026, merchandise exports amounted to approximately US$8.19 billion, while services exports were around US$2.30 billion, bringing total exports to approximately US$10.49 billion. But during the same period, merchandise imports were approximately US$14.6 billion.

As a result, Sri Lanka’s merchandise trade deficit for the first seven months of 2026 increased to approximately US$6.5 billion. During the same period in 2025, the trade deficit was around US$3.9 billion. In other words, the trade deficit has increased by roughly 67% within a year. Exports have grown, which is good, but imports have been growing much faster. That part of the story also needs to be told.

There is another important distinction. The US$10.49 billion export figure includes both merchandise and services exports, whereas the merchandise trade deficit is calculated by comparing merchandise exports with merchandise imports. These figures should therefore not be mixed or presented in a way that gives people a misleading picture of the country’s trade position.

There is another warning sign as well. During January–July 2026, Sri Lanka recorded a current-account deficit of approximately US$387 million, compared with a surplus during the corresponding period last year.

So, saying “Sri Lanka’s exports have exceeded US$10 billion” is not a lie. But it is not the whole truth either. Using one positive figure to suggest that the country’s overall external trade position is performing exceptionally well can be misleading.

Celebrate the growth in exports. But at the same time, tell the people that the trade deficit has increased from around US$3.9 billion to US$6.5 billion.

When you tell the people about US$10.49 billion in exports, tell them about the US$6.5 billion trade deficit too. Then it becomes the complete economic picture, rather than political propaganda.
