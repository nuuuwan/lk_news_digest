CAA conducts over 1,000 raids on electrical, electronic goods

Colombo, Sep. 14 (Daily Mirror) - The Consumer Affairs Authority (CAA) has conducted more than 1,000 island-wide raids on electrical and electronic goods so far this year, including 204 raids carried out under a recent special programme.

The special raids were conducted from August 25 to 29 and were resumed on September 8 and 9 based on information gathered during the initial operations.

The CAA said more than 1,150 electrical and electronic items were seized as case material or taken into official custody during the 204 raids, with legal action being initiated against those responsible.

The raids focused on products including extension cords, multi-sockets, plug tops, switch sockets, power cords, LED bulbs, chargers, electrical adapters, torches, electric scales, kettles, irons and power tools.

Officials also inspected whether traders maintained proper bills, receipts and invoices, could establish the source of their products and complied with labelling, product information and warranty requirements.

The CAA particularly stressed the requirement for a minimum six-month warranty for relevant electrical and electronic goods and said consumers should be provided with a written warranty certificate clearly specifying the warranty period, conditions and responsible party.

The authority said investigations would not necessarily stop at the retailer level. Under relevant laws, including Order No. 83, officials would trace the supply chain from retailer to supplier, distributor, importer or manufacturer where necessary.

Those found to have been involved in offences could face legal action, including suppliers, distributors, importers and manufacturers, in addition to retailers.

The CAA urged traders to maintain proper invoices and documents identifying suppliers and importers, sell only goods complying with mandatory standards and labelling requirements, and provide the required warranty certificate.

Consumers have also been advised to obtain a bill when purchasing electrical or electronic goods, check manufacturer or importer details and relevant standards and labels, and ensure that a valid minimum six-month warranty and written warranty certificate are provided.

The CAA said it would continue targeted raids and supply-chain investigations island-wide to identify unsafe, substandard and non-compliant products entering the market.

Consumers can report market irregularities to the CAA on its 1977 hotline during office hours.
