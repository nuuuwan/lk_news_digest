PUCSL proposes 1.46% electricity tariff increase for fourth quarter

Colombo, Nov. 4 (Daily Mirror) - The Public Utilities Commission of Sri Lanka (PUCSL) has proposed a 1.46% increase in electricity tariffs for the fourth quarter of 2026, following a preliminary review of cost and revenue forecasts submitted by electricity sector licensees.

The proposal will be discussed at a stakeholder consultation scheduled to be held at the Bandaranaike Memorial International Conference Hall (BMICH) in Colombo tomorrow (November 5).

According to the PUCSL, the electricity sector's total forecasted cost of supply for the October-December quarter stands at Rs. 157.3 billion, while the expected revenue from electricity sales is Rs. 154 billion. This includes a revenue deficit of Rs. 1.1 billion brought forward from the previous period.

Based on submissions by the National System Operator (NSO) and Electricity Distribution Lanka (Pvt) Ltd. (EDL), the required revenue increase was initially estimated at 2.88%.

However, following its preliminary assessment, the PUCSL has reduced the proposed increase to 1.46% by removing an increase in generation capacity costs attributed to Electricity Generation Lanka (Pvt) Ltd. (EGL) and revising the estimated additional costs arising from the use of substandard coal during the second quarter of 2026.

The NSO has forecast electricity demand of 4,796 million units for the fourth quarter. Major hydro power is expected to contribute 32.34% of generation, while coal power is expected to account for 25.68%. Other renewable energy sources are forecast to contribute 28.81%.

The forecast also includes 8.94% from furnace oil, 3.65% from naphtha and 0.58% from diesel.

The estimated generation cost submitted by the NSO is Rs. 117.29 billion, while the energy cost component alone has been estimated at Rs. 98.3 billion.

The PUCSL has identified several areas for further examination, including the adequacy of hydro power generation forecasts in comparison with rainfall predictions and the impact of introducing battery energy storage systems (BESS) on renewable energy generation.

The Commission has also raised concerns over fuel pricing arrangements. It noted that EGL has yet to enter into a Fuel Supply Agreement, while transparent pricing provisions have not been included in the agreements covering the Westcoast and Sobadhanavi power plants. As a result, liquid fuel generation cost forecasts remain exposed to pricing assumptions made by the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation.

Meanwhile, the NSO has projected generation capacity costs of Rs. 19 billion for the quarter, an increase of Rs. 1.1 billion compared with the Commission-approved costs for the third quarter. Of this increase, Rs. 700 million is attributed to EGL plants, which operate without Power Purchase Agreements.

The NSO has also included Rs. 703 million as capacity costs for battery energy storage systems, anticipating the phased connection of 160 MW of battery storage capacity.

Another issue under review is EDL's request for Rs. 2.1 billion in additional costs outside the multi-year tariff framework. Of this amount, Rs. 1.1 billion relates to loan repayments under the Supporting Electricity Supply Reliability Improvement Project (SESRIP).

The PUCSL has excluded this amount from its preliminary assessment, stating that costs relating to capital work in progress should be recovered through tariffs only after the relevant assets are completed and capitalised.

The Commission has also considered the revenue position for the second quarter of 2026, which is relevant to the current tariff review under the tariff methodology. The NSO reported a provisional revenue deficit of Rs. 1.1 billion after removing Rs. 2.9 billion in estimated additional costs linked to substandard coal usage during that period.

The PUCSL said the stakeholder consultation would provide an opportunity for interested parties to submit their views on the generation mix, fuel prices, generation and capacity costs, battery storage costs and other matters relating to the proposed tariff revision.

Stakeholders have been requested to limit their presentations to the specified areas. Each speaker will be given approximately 10 minutes to make a presentation in Sinhala, Tamil or English.

The Commission has also instructed participants to refrain from promotional activities, disruptive behaviour and criticism deemed unrelated to the consultation, while cross-examination of representations will not be permitted.
