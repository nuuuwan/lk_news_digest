Sri Lanka connects first grid-scale battery storage facility

Developed by Vidullanka PLC and WindForce PLC, through project company StoreX Ltd

It is first of 13 battery energy storage system projects planned across country

Sri Lanka yesterday connected its first grid-scale standalone battery energy storage system (BESS) to the national grid, marking a key step towards storing the surplus solar power and reducing the dependence on the costly fossil fuel-based generation during the peak demand hours.

The 10 MW/40 MWh facility in Anuradhapura was commissioned under the patronage of President Anura Kumara Dissanayake.

Developed by Vidullanka PLC and WindForce PLC, through project company StoreX Ltd, the facility is the first of 13 BESS projects planned across the country, with a combined capacity of 130 MW/520 MWh.

Of the total, 12 facilities are being developed under StoreX, while the partners are developing the 13th project in Vavunathivu through Solar Universe Ltd.

The remaining facilities are expected to be connected to the national grid within the next two to three months.

The battery storage systems capture the excess solar electricity generated during the daytime and release it to the grid during the periods of higher demand at night, helping balance the supply, improve grid stability and reduce the use of fossil fuel-based power.

Energy Minister Anura Karunathilake said Sri Lanka currently generates close to 3,000 MW of solar power during the day, compared with the average daytime electricity demand of about 2,800 MW.

The night-time demand, however, rises to around 3,100 MW and could increase further by February, under the forecast El Niño conditions.

“Our challenge has been that, until now, we have not had the capacity to store the excess solar power generated during the daytime,” Karunathilake said.

The government is implementing battery storage projects with a combined capacity of 160 MW, including 130 MW being developed by WindForce and its partners.

The full 160 MW is expected to be connected to the grid before the end of the year, he said.

The authorities have also initiated procurement for several additional battery storage projects, including facilities with capacities of 400 MW, 250 MW and 150 MW. Some are under evaluation and are expected to enter the system by the first half of 2027.

Karunathilake said expanding the storage capacity would allow the grid to absorb more renewable energy while reducing the high cost of fossil fuel-based electricity generation at night.

The Anuradhapura project also marks Vidullanka’s entry into grid-scale energy storage, expanding its operations beyond electricity generation.

The company is preparing to commission its eighth ground-mounted solar facility in Hambantota, which will raise its total installed capacity to 96 MW across 27 projects.
