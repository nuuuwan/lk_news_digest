CA Sri Lanka brings together leading experts to analyse Budget 2027

The Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka (CA Sri Lanka) hosted a comprehensive post-Budget seminar last week to provide members, business leaders, tax professionals and policymakers with an in-depth analysis of the key economic, fiscal and taxation proposals in National Budget 2027.

Held at CA Sri Lanka, the seminar attracted over 700 participants, both in person and virtually. It featured leading experts from the public and private sectors, who shared insights on the Budget’s potential impact on economic growth, investment, taxation, exports, public finances and tax administration.

Speaking at the event, CA Sri Lanka President Tishan Subasinghe said Budget 2027 marks a significant phase in Sri Lanka’s economic journey as the country moves from a period of stabilisation towards sustainable growth and development. He also spoke on the performance of the country’s revenue-generating agencies and highlighted the importance of maintaining fiscal discipline while strengthening foreign exchange earnings, investment and economic resilience. He further stressed the importance of effective implementation in achieving the Budget’s growth objectives.

CA Sri Lanka Faculty of Taxation Chairman Saman Srilal observed that the Budget places considerable emphasis on productive sectors of the economy through investments in export-oriented manufacturing, tourism, agriculture, technology, digitalisation and SMEs. He welcomed several tax measures, including personal income tax relief, investment incentives to encourage export-oriented projects, and initiatives to strengthen tax administration and improve compliance processes.

Delivering the Budget highlights, EY Sri Lanka Partner and Head of Tax Sulaiman Nishtar gave a detailed overview of the Budget proposals and the legislative process. He highlighted the Government’s strategic focus on investment promotion, export development, digital transformation, infrastructure development, energy security and public sector modernisation. He also outlined several tax and regulatory measures designed to enhance Sri Lanka’s competitiveness and improve the overall business environment.

Offering an independent economic perspective, Verité Research Executive Director and Head of Research Dr. Nishan de Mel discussed the broader fiscal and economic implications of the Budget. He acknowledged the emphasis on the digital economy, renewable energy and export growth, while drawing attention to areas requiring continued policy focus, including labour force participation, higher education and public sector reform.

Representing the Government’s policy perspective, Finance Ministry Tax Policy Advisor Tanuja Perera provided insights into several tax proposals announced in the Budget, including revisions to personal income taxation, withholding tax measures and reforms relating to digital and cross-border taxation. She explained the policy rationale behind these proposals and how they are intended to help maintain fiscal sustainability while providing targeted relief to taxpayers.

Addressing developments in tax administration, Inland Revenue Department Commissioner P. Nandan Kumara outlined ongoing efforts to modernise the tax system through digitalisation, improved taxpayer services and greater inter-agency integration. He highlighted plans to advance the Department’s transition towards a more efficient, technology-driven tax administration framework.

The panel discussion also featured John Keells Holdings PLC Vice President and Head of Corporate Finance, Group Tax and Social Entrepreneurship Nisreen Rehmanjee, who shared practical perspectives from the corporate sector on tax compliance, taxpayer facilitation and broadening the tax base. The discussion was moderated by BDO Partners Partner and Head of Tax Sarah Afker.
