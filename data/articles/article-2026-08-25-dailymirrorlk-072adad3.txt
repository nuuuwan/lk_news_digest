Man held over illegal road construction

Colombo, Aug. 25 (Daily Mirror) - A man has been arrested for allegedly entering the Panilkanda Reserve in the Deniyaya area of Matara District without authorization and constructing a road through the forest to gain access to private land.

Officers of the Deniyaya Kekundeniya Forest Field Assistant Division inspected the area on August 17 following a tip-off and subsequently arrested the suspect.

Matara District Forest Officer Chandana Dayaratne said another person from the same area is also due to be arrested for allegedly aiding and abetting the forest crime.

He said the suspects will be charged under the Forest Conservation Ordinance and produced before the Magistrate’s Court.

According to the Forest Conservation Ordinance, forests are declared under several categories, including reserved forests and village forests, while the Forest Conservation Department has the authority to intervene in activities carried out within such areas.

The Department also has powers to investigate forest crimes committed in government-owned protected areas that have not been formally gazetted, and to arrest suspects and produce them before court.

Unauthorized entry into a gazetted forest area is an offence under the law. Other activities considered forest crimes include setting fires, encroachment, hunting wildlife, obstructing waterways, setting traps, poisoning water sources, transporting timber without a licence and engaging in unauthorized timber-related businesses.

Meanwhile, Conservator of Forests (Protection and Law Enforcement) Wasantha Gajanayake said the number of forest crimes reported during the first eight months of this year has decreased compared to the corresponding period last year.

He attributed the decline mainly to the Forest Protection Unit established in 1995 and said members of the public have continued to provide information about forest crimes.

Based on such information, raids and night patrols have been intensified, he said.

Gajanayake also warned of an increase in forest fires amid dry conditions linked to the prevailing El Niño-related weather pattern.

He said some fires had spread into forests after being deliberately set to clear farmland, while others had allegedly been started for hunting or for entertainment.

Strict legal action is being taken against those responsible for setting forest fires and committing other forest offences under the Forest Conservation Ordinance, he said.

The Ministry of Environment has also launched several programmes aimed at strengthening environmental protection and conserving Sri Lanka’s ecosystems for future generations.
