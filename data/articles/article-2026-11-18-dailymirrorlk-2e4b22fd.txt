eBird exhibit to introduce public to global citizen science

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Visitors to the P. B. Karunaratne Memorial Exhibition at the University of Colombo will have an opportunity to learn how they can contribute to global bird research through eBird, an online platform that allows people to record bird sightings and share observations with scientists.

The exhibit is part of the 50th anniversary celebrations of the Field Ornithology Group of Sri Lanka (FOGSL), which is based in the Department of Zoology and Environment Sciences at the University of Colombo.

The exhibition aims to introduce schoolchildren and the general public to ornithology and scientific research while encouraging them to become citizen scientists and actively participate in nature conservation.

Through eBird, people can record birds they observe in their surroundings and contribute valuable information to a global database used by researchers to study bird distribution, population changes and migration patterns.

The exhibition will explain how ordinary citizens, including birdwatchers, students and nature enthusiasts, can support scientific research by submitting their observations.

Such public contributions help researchers collect information from different locations and monitor changes in bird populations and their habitats.

FOGSL has been involved in scientific research on Sri Lanka's birdlife, operating bird-banding stations, studying migratory patterns and training generations of citizen scientists over the past five decades.

The eBird display will be among several interactive exhibits at the P. B. Karunaratne Memorial Exhibition, which will also feature a simulated rainforest, wetland biodiversity displays, bird songs and a live bird-banding station.

Visitors will also be able to learn how researchers use modern technologies, including satellite tagging, to track bird movements across continents.

Other attractions include exhibits on the evolutionary history of birds, nocturnal species such as owls and nightjars, marine ecosystems and seabirds.

The National Zoological Gardens and the National Museum will contribute special displays, while university undergraduates will present interactive models explaining Sri Lanka's terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems.

The exhibition will also feature expert seminars, children's activities and an art corner, providing opportunities for visitors of all ages to explore the world of birds and nature conservation.

The four-day exhibition will be held from November 19 to 22 at the Faculty of Science premises of the University of Colombo, from 8.30 a.m. to 7.00 p.m. daily.

Under the theme of encouraging the next generation of conservationists, FOGSL hopes to inspire more people to take an interest in bird research and environmental protection as it marks five decades of work.

"The birds you meet today need you tomorrow," is the message behind the Golden Jubilee celebrations.
