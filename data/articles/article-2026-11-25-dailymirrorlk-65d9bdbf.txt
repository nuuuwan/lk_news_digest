Tokyo Cement profit falls 13% despite 28% sales jump

The Tokyo Cement group’s profit fell 13 percent in the September quarter, as the higher raw material costs and currency volatility squeezed the margins, even as the rising cement demand and price increase lifted the sales.

The group profit after tax fell to Rs.870 million in the three months ended September 30, from Rs.1.003 billion a year earlier. Revenue rose 28 percent to Rs.20.94 billion, from Rs.16.32 billion, while the net profit margin narrowed to 4.2 percent, from 6.1 percent.

Tokyo Cement attributed the rise in revenue to the higher sales volumes and price increase from the preceding quarter. The group said it gained the market share as the coastal shipping improved its access to the customers in the Western province and beyond, helping it make a greater use of its expanded production capacity.

Across the industry, the cement supply remained uninterrupted and the market prices held steady during the quarter, despite the rising raw material, freight, insurance and fuel costs, the company said.

The cement consumption increased, helped by the favourable weather and availability of construction materials.

The public development projects generated new orders, supporting the continued expansion in the Construction Purchasing Managers’ Index. The Middle East supply disruptions, however, constrained the supplies of bitumen used in roadbuilding.

Tokyo Cement said the rupee depreciation and higher global costs pushed up the prices for the locally produced and imported inputs, making the private developers and individual investors more cautious and threatening to slow the public projects.

The group said the volatility in the global fuel prices and risk of diesel supply disruptions dented the consumer confidence during the quarter. The higher energy costs, a global fertiliser shortage and the potential impact of severe El Niño conditions on agriculture added to the uncertainty.

The national consumer price inflation reached 8.1 percent year-on-year in August, while the Central Bank held its overnight policy rate at 8.75 percent throughout the quarter.

The company pointed to the rising gross official reserves and continued foreign exchange inflows from the exports and worker remittances. The tourism earnings showed signs of improving ahead of the peak season, although the recovery was slower than expected.

Fitch Ratings’ upgrade of Sri Lanka’s sovereign rating to ‘B-’, with a stable outlook, could help attract a wider range of foreign currency inflows, Tokyo Cement said.

The group maintained a conservative short to medium-term outlook, citing uncertainty over the Middle East conflict, energy prices and supply chains, alongside the expectations of the slower private-sector credit growth in the coming quarters.

It said the regional infrastructure projects already underway were supporting the construction activity but sustaining that momentum would depend on getting larger public investments off the ground and encouraging private-sector participation.
