Court throws out shareholder bid to sue NDB board, auditors over Rs.13.6bn fraud

The Commercial High Court on Friday dismissed a minority shareholder’s bid to bring a derivative action over the Rs.13.6 billion fraud at National Development Bank PLC (NDB), after concluding that the threshold requirements under the Companies Act had not been satisfied, the bank announced in a stock filing.

The application, filed by M. Thiyagaraja, sought leave to proceed on the bank’s behalf against its directors and external auditors, Ernst & Young. As per the filing, the court held that the requirements of Section 234 (3) of the Companies Act, No. 07 of 2007 had not been met and dismissed the application subject to taxed costs. The order, delivered by High Court Judge Viraj Ranatunge, is understood to have noted that NDB had already commissioned a forensic audit and lodged a complaint with the Criminal Investigation Department, leaving insufficient grounds for the petitioner to pursue the matter independently of the directors, according to a statement issued by NDB.

The petitioner had argued that weak oversight, and not the fraud alone, caused the bank’s losses.

However, the dismissal leaves the wider accountability question open. The fraud, initially estimated at Rs.13.2 billion in April, was confirmed at Rs.13.64 billion in the final forensic report by Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu India LLP, disclosed on September 29.

Meanwhile, Parliament’s Committee on Public Finance days earlier questioned why no board member, senior official or auditor had been held responsible. The Sri Lanka Accounting and Auditing Standards Monitoring Board informed the committee that its findings on NDB’s external auditors are due within two weeks.
