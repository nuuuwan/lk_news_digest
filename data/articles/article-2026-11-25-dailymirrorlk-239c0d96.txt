Sri Lanka tea industry performance – January to September 2026

During the period January to September 2026, Sri Lanka’s tea industry recorded declines in both production and export volumes compared with the corresponding period in 2025, while the national tea sale average increased.

Total tea production declined by 6.54 million kilogrammes (Mn/Kgs), falling from 199.16 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 192.62 Mn/Kgs in 2026.

Total tea export volume also decreased by 8.91 Mn/Kgs, from 198.33 Mn/Kgs to 189.41 Mn/Kgs.

Meanwhile, the national tea sale average increased by Rs.9.64 per kilogramme, rising from Rs.1,160.56 in 2025 to Rs.1,170.20 in 2026.

In terms of export destinations, Türkiye emerged as the largest importer of Ceylon Tea during the first nine months of 2026, recording a substantial increase in imports from 15.26 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 35.71 Mn/Kgs in 2026, an increase of 20.44 Mn/Kgs.

In contrast, Iraq recorded a significant decline of 11.64 Mn/Kgs, with the imports falling from 30.07 Mn/Kgs to 18.43 Mn/Kgs. Russia maintained a relatively stable demand, with the imports increasing marginally by 0.22 Mn/Kgs to 16.92 Mn/Kgs.

Azerbaijan and Jordan also recorded a notable growth, with the imports increasing by 2.29 Mn/Kgs and 2.15 Mn/Kgs, reaching 9.03 Mn/Kgs and 6.36 Mn/Kgs, respectively.

However, several other key markets experienced declines. Libya recorded a reduction of 9.33 Mn/Kgs, while Chile and China declined by 2.28 Mn/Kgs and 0.80 Mn/Kgs, respectively. Saudi Arabia also recorded a marginal decrease of 0.16 Mn/Kgs.

Overall, Sri Lanka’s tea industry experienced lower production and export volumes during January to September 2026, despite the improvements in the national tea sale average.

The export destination trends were mixed, with a strong growth in Türkiye, Azerbaijan and Jordan, partially offset by the substantial declines in Iraq and Libya. These developments highlight the importance of monitoring production trends, export market performance and international price movements in assessing the overall performance of Sri Lanka’s tea industry.

The total tea production for the January to September 2026 period recorded a decline compared with the corresponding period in 2025, with the production decreasing across all three elevations. Total tea production declined by 6.54 Mn/Kgs, falling to 192.62 Mn/Kgs in 2026, from 199.16 Mn/Kgs in 2025.

The high-grown tea production declined by 1.21 Mn/Kgs, decreasing to 41.89 Mn/Kgs during January to September 2026, compared to 43.11 Mn/Kgs recorded during the corresponding period of 2025. The medium-grown teas experienced a more significant reduction of 3.07 Mn/Kgs, with the production falling to 33.55 Mn/Kgs, from 36.62 Mn/Kgs.

The low-grown teas, which continued to account for the largest share of national tea production at approximately 60.83 percent, recorded a decline of 2.26 Mn/Kgs. Production decreased to 117.17 Mn/Kgs during January to September 2026, compared with 119.43 Mn/Kgs in the corresponding period of 2025.

The decline in tea production during the period January to September 2026 reflects reduced output across all three elevations. The medium- grown teas experienced the most significant decline in volume, followed by the low-grown and high-grown teas. Despite the low-grown teas continuing to account for the largest share of total production, the combined reductions across all elevations contributed to the overall decline in Sri Lanka’s tea production compared with the corresponding period of 2025.

The comparison of the CTC tea production for the January to September 2026 period against the corresponding period in 2025 reflects an overall improvement, with the total production increasing by 0.69 Mn/Kgs, from 18.15 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 18.84 Mn/Kgs in 2026.

The high-grown CTC production recorded a decline of 0.17 Mn/Kgs, decreasing from 3.84 Mn/Kgs in January to September 2025 to 3.67 Mn/Kgs in the corresponding period of 2026.

In contrast, the medium-grown CTC production recorded a significant increase of 0.91 Mn/Kgs, rising from 6.67 Mn/Kgs to 7.58 Mn/Kgs. This category was the main contributor to the overall growth in the CTC tea production.

The low-grown CTC production recorded a marginal decline of 0.05 Mn/Kgs, decreasing from 7.63 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 7.58 Mn/Kgs in 2026.

Overall, the CTC tea production increased from 18.15 Mn/Kgs during January to September 2025 to 18.84 Mn/Kgs in the corresponding period of 2026. The increase was primarily driven by the strong growth in the medium- grown CTC production, which more than offset the declines recorded in the high-grown and low-grown categories.

The comparison of Sri Lanka tea sale averages for the period January to September 2026 against the corresponding period in 2025 indicates a marginal improvement in the overall average price in Sri Lankan rupee terms, while the US dollar values declined across all elevation categories.

In the high-grown category, the average price increased by Rs.21.33, rising from Rs.1,083.91 in 2025 to Rs.1,105.24 in 2026. However, in US dollar terms, the average declined by US $ 0.20, from US $ 3.62 to US $ 3.42.

The medium-grown category recorded a decline in both rupee and US dollar values. The average price decreased by Rs.45.13, falling from Rs.1,022.69 in 2025 to Rs.977.56 in 2026. Similarly, the US dollar average declined by US $ 0.39, from US $ 3.42 to US $ 3.03.

In the low-grown category, which continued to command the highest average price among the elevation categories, the rupee value increased by Rs.19.16, from Rs.1,230.94 in 2025 to Rs.1,250.10 in 2026.

However, the US dollar average declined by US $ 0.25, from US $ 4.12 to US $ 3.87.

Overall, the national tea sale average for January to September 2026 increased by Rs.9.64, rising from Rs.1,160.56 in 2025 to Rs.1,170.20 in 2026. In contrast, the average in US dollar terms declined by US $ 0.25, from US $ 3.88 to US $ 3.63. This reflects an improvement in the average sale price in rupee terms, despite a decline in the corresponding US dollar value across all elevation categories.

The comparison of Sri Lanka’s tea export performance for the period January to September 2026 against the corresponding period in 2025 indicates a decline in total export volume, alongside an increase in total export value in Sri Lankan rupee terms.

Total tea export volume decreased by 8.91 Mn/Kgs, from 198.33 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 189.41 Mn/Kgs in 2026.

Despite this reduction, the total export value increased by approximately Rs.2.23 billion, rising from Rs.347.96 billion in 2025 to Rs.350.18 billion in 2026. Consequently, the average FOB price per kilogramme increased from Rs.1,754.46 to Rs.1,848.78, reflecting an improvement of Rs.94.32 per kilogramme.

Among the different export categories, tea in packets recorded the highest export volume at 84.55 Mn/Kgs, followed by tea in bulk at 79.16 Mn/Kgs.

Tea in packets generated approximately Rs.144.80 billion in export earnings, while tea in bulk contributed Rs.121.25 billion. The average FOB price for tea in packets increased from Rs.1,622.64 per kilogramme in 2025 to Rs.1,712.59 per kilogramme in 2026. Similarly, tea in bulk recorded an increase from Rs.1,494.44 to Rs.1,531.78 per kilogramme.

Tea in bags recorded a decline in export volume, decreasing from 19.67 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 19.05 Mn/Kgs in 2026. However, its export value increased from Rs.54.81 billion to Rs.57.79 billion, while the average FOB price rose from Rs.2,786.73 to Rs.3,033.91 per kilogramme.

Instant tea exports increased in volume from 2.54 Mn/Kgs to 3.12 Mn/Kgs, with the export value rising from Rs.9.26 billion to Rs.10.97 billion. However, its average FOB price declined from Rs.3,646.05 to Rs.3,512.32 per kilogramme. The green tea exports recorded a decrease in volume from 3.74 Mn/Kgs to 3.54 Mn/Kgs, while the export value increased from Rs.14.81 billion to Rs.15.38 billion. Its average FOB price also increased from Rs.3,959.64 to Rs.4,347.30 per kilogramme.

In US dollar terms, the approximate total export value declined from US $ 1.16 billion in the January to September 2025 period to US $ 1.08 billion in the corresponding period of 2026. The average FOB price in US dollar terms also decreased from US $ 5.87 per kilogramme to US $ 5.73 per kilogramme.

Overall, Sri Lanka’s tea exports during January to September 2026 generated higher earnings in rupee terms, despite a reduction in export volume. The increase in average FOB prices across most export categories, particularly tea in bags and green tea, contributed to the growth in the export earnings.

During the period January to September 2026, Türkiye emerged as the largest importer of Sri Lankan tea, recording a significant increase in imports to 35.71 Mn/Kgs, compared with 15.26 Mn/Kgs during the corresponding period of 2025. In contrast, Iraq, one of Sri Lanka’s major tea export destinations, recorded a substantial decline in imports to 18.43 Mn/Kgs, from 30.07 Mn/Kgs. Russia maintained a relatively stable demand, with the imports increasing marginally to 16.92 Mn/Kgs, from 16.70 Mn/Kgs.

Among the other major destinations, Azerbaijan recorded an increase in imports to 9.03 Mn/Kgs from 6.74 Mn/Kgs, while Jordan also registered a notable growth, with the imports rising to 6.36 Mn/Kgs, from 4.21 Mn/Kgs. Syria recorded a marginal increase in imports to 6.47 Mn/Kgs, compared with 6.19 Mn/Kgs during the corresponding period of the previous year.

Conversely, China experienced a decline in imports, decreasing to 7.13 Mn/Kgs, from 7.93 Mn/Kgs. Libya recorded a substantial contraction, with the imports falling to 6.62 Mn/Kgs, from 15.95 Mn/Kgs. Saudi Arabia registered a marginal decline to 6.33 Mn/Kgs, from 6.49 Mn/Kgs, while Chile also recorded a reduction in imports to 6.32 Mn/Kgs, from 8.60 Mn/Kgs.

Overall, Sri Lanka’s tea exports during January to September 2026 reflected a notable shift among its leading export destinations. The significant growth in exports to Türkiye, together with the increases in Azerbaijan, Jordan and Syria, was accompanied by the substantial declines in the traditional markets such as Iraq and Libya. Despite these mixed trends, Türkiye, Iraq and Russia continued with a strong demand for Sri Lankan tea and were the top three importers for the period January to September 2026.

The comparative analysis of Sri Lanka’s average FOB prices for the period January to September 2026 against the corresponding period in 2025 indicates an overall improvement in the export prices, with the FOB prices remaining higher in every month of 2026.

The year commenced with a strong increase in January, when the average FOB price rose from Rs.1,722.78 in 2025 to Rs.1,821.71 in 2026, reflecting a gain of Rs.98.93/kilogramme.

In February, the FOB price increased by Rs.48.24, reaching Rs.1,785.76, compared with Rs.1,737.52 in the corresponding month of 2025.

During March, the average FOB price continued to improve, reaching Rs.1,813.84, an increase of Rs.60.68 over Rs.1,753.16 recorded in March 2025. In April, the prices remained relatively stable, rising marginally by Rs.0.64, from Rs.1,760.31 in 2025 to Rs.1,760.95 in 2026.

In May, the average FOB price increased by Rs.17.52, from Rs.1,783.18 in 2025 to Rs.1,800.70 in 2026.

The upward trend strengthened in June, with the FOB price rising to Rs.1,871.14, from Rs.1,714.08, representing an increase of Rs.157.06/kilogramme.

The positive trend continued during the third quarter. In July, the average FOB price increased to Rs.1,921.95, from Rs.1,766.51 in 2025, recording a gain of Rs.155.44. In August, the price rose to Rs.1,910.27, compared with Rs.1,805.35, an increase of Rs.104.92. September recorded the highest monthly FOB price of the period at Rs.1,928.62, compared with Rs.1,741.45 in 2025, reflecting the largest year-on-year increase of Rs.187.17.

Overall, the January to September 2026 period demonstrated a consistently stronger FOB price performance compared with the corresponding period in 2025. The most significant year-on-year increases occurred in September, June and July, highlighting the strengthening of the average export prices during the latter part of the period. These higher FOB prices indicate improved export price realisation in Sri Lankan rupee terms.

The comparison of the tea sold quantities and national tea sale average for the period January to September 2026 against the corresponding period in 2025 indicates a decline in the volume of tea sold, while the average auction prices recorded a moderate improvement during the nine-month period.

During the period under review, a total of 193.01 Mn/Kgs of tea was sold from January to September 2026, compared with 204.96 Mn/Kgs during the corresponding period in 2025, reflecting a decline of approximately 11.95 Mn/Kgs.

On a monthly basis, the average auction prices during the first quarter of 2026 remained below the corresponding levels in 2025, with the largest year-on-year decline recorded in March, when the average price decreased by approximately Rs.39.53/kilogramme.

However, the prices recovered during the second quarter, with April, May and June recording year-on-year increases of Rs.12.95/kilogramme, Rs.33.24/kilogramme and Rs.50.22/kilogramme, respectively. This positive price trend continued into July, when the average increased by Rs.48.50/kilogramme compared with the corresponding month in 2025.

Although August recorded a marginal decline of Rs.7.81/kilogramme, the prices improved again in September, reaching Rs.1,202.74/kilogramme, an increase of Rs.11.12/kilogramme year-on-year.

The contrasting movement between the lower auction volumes and improved average prices suggests that the relatively tighter tea supplies helped support the market prices despite the reduction in the quantities sold. The supply constraints, variations in production levels and sustained demand for Ceylon Tea may have contributed to the overall price resilience observed during the period.

Overall, the January to September 2026 period was characterised by a contraction in tea sold quantities alongside a moderate strengthening of average auction prices. While the decline in the sales volumes reflects the prevailing supply-side challenges in the tea industry, the improvement in auction prices indicates continued resilience in market demand. The ability of the Colombo Tea Auction to maintain relatively firm price levels despite the lower volumes remains an encouraging development for the industry.

The comparison of the monthly average exchange rates for the period January to September 2026 against the corresponding period in 2025 indicates an initial depreciation of the Sri Lankan rupee against the US dollar, followed by a gradual recovery during the third quarter.

The average exchange rate increased from Rs.296.18/US dollar in January 2025 to Rs.309.65/US dollar in January 2026. Although the rate marginally declined to Rs.309.36/US dollar in February 2026, it resumed its upward trend in March, reaching Rs.311.91/US dollar and continued to increase steadily throughout the second quarter, reaching Rs.334.43/US dollar in June 2026, compared with Rs.299.90/US dollar in June 2025.

The exchange rate remained relatively stable during the first nine months of 2025, fluctuating within a narrow range of Rs.295.91 to Rs.302.20 per US dollar. In contrast, 2026 experienced considerable exchange rate volatility, with the Sri Lankan rupee weakening progressively during the first half of the year.

The year-on-year difference widened from Rs.13.47 in January to a peak of Rs.34.90 in July 2026, indicating increased depreciation pressure during the second quarter.

However, the exchange rate recorded a gradual appreciation during the third quarter, declining from Rs.336.02/US dollar in July to Rs.332.91/US dollar in August and further to Rs.329.54/US dollar in September 2026. Despite this improvement, the exchange rate remained considerably higher than the corresponding 2025 levels, with the year-on-year difference standing at Rs.27.34 in September.

Overall, the exchange rate movements during January to September 2026 reflect significant depreciation of the Sri Lankan rupee during the first half of the year, followed by a moderate recovery in the third quarter. Nevertheless, the rupee remained weaker against the US dollar compared with the corresponding period in 2025.

Sri Lanka’s tea industry experienced a decline in both production and exports during January to September 2026. Although the Sri Lankan rupee prices and earnings increased, US dollar earnings declined due to the rupee depreciation.

The lower auction volumes helped support the tea prices but also highlighted the supply constraints. The export performance was mixed, with growth in Türkiye, Azerbaijan and Jordan, while the exports to Iraq and Libya declined sharply.

The industry should focus on increasing productivity, ensuring a stable tea supply, managing exchange rate risks and expanding value-added exports to overcome the impact of the lower volumes.
