More than half of households burn polythene and plastic waste in Sri Lanka

Colombo, Nov. 21 (Daily Mirror) - More than half of Sri Lankan households dispose of their polythene and plastic waste by burning it, despite open burning being prohibited under the National Environmental Act, highlighting continuing challenges in waste management and enforcement.

According to data from the Department of Census and Statistics, 52.5% of households burn polythene and plastic waste, while 25.3% dispose of it through local authorities. A further 12.3% dispose of such waste within their premises, 9% hand it over to waste collectors and 0.9% use other methods.

An official from the National Solid Waste Management Support Centre (NSWMSC) said that although the burning of plastic waste is prohibited under the National Environmental Act No. 47 of 1980, the practice continues, particularly in rural areas where monitoring and enforcement remain challenging. The Act prohibits the open burning of refuse or other combustible matter, including plastics, and makes those who violate the regulations liable to prosecution under Section 31.

He said the practice is monitored more closely in urban areas and that plastic waste burning had declined over the years, although it continues to occur. He pointed to limitations in local authority waste collection services, including inadequate staffing and outdated or insufficient vehicles, affecting the ability to collect waste effectively. Enforcement is further hampered by the limited availability of Environmental Police personnel, making it difficult to take action against those violating the regulations.

Meanwhile, local authorities conduct awareness programmes across the country to encourage proper waste disposal, separation and recycling. Programmes are carried out in all nine provinces to promote the collection and segregation of plastic waste, while social media is being used to broaden public awareness. Local authorities also collect residual solid waste, including plastic waste left behind by informal collectors who remove recyclable materials for sale, the NSWMSC official said.

As part of efforts to improve plastic waste management, the Ministry of Public Administration, Home Affairs, Provincial Councils and Local Government has introduced a Material Recovery Facility (MRF) plan covering the 2025–2027 period. The initiative aims to bring polythene and plastic waste to designated facilities for separation and recycling, reducing the amount released into the environment, as means of a more systematic way of managing waste disposal and discouraging burning as a disposal method.
