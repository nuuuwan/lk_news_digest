Supreme Court rules judges liable for income tax

Colombo, September 17 (Daily Mirror)- The Supreme Court has dismissed three appeals filed by associations representing judicial officers, holding that judges of the High Court, District Courts, Magistrates’ Courts and Labour Tribunals are liable to pay income tax and that Advance Personal Income Tax (APIT) could be deducted from their remuneration under the Inland Revenue Act.

The five-member Supreme Court Bench comprising Chief Justice Preethi Padman Surasena and Justices Shiran Gooneratne, Janak De Silva, Mahinda Samayawardhana and Arjuna Obeyesekere delivered the judgment. The appeals were dismissed without costs.

The appeals were filed against a November 2, 2023 judgment of a divisional bench of the Court of Appeal, which had dismissed three writ applications filed by associations representing judicial officers. The associations represented High Court Judges, District Judges, Additional District Judges, Magistrates, Additional Magistrates and Presidents of Labour Tribunals.

Justice Janak De Silva, delivering the principal judgment, held that the application of the Inland Revenue Act to the judicial officers represented in the appeals did not violate the Constitution.

He further held that the Act did not create an employer-employee relationship between judicial officers and the relevant respondents. Instead, it recognised, for taxation purposes, the factual role of the Secretary to the Ministry in paying the salaries of judicial officers and identifying the person responsible for deducting APIT.

Justice De Silva observed that although the independence of the judiciary was a foundational constitutional principle, judicial officers remained subject to taxation imposed by law where such taxation was of general application, non-discriminatory and not specifically directed at judges or the judiciary.

The principal issue before the Supreme Court was whether judicial officers were liable to income tax under the Inland Revenue Act No. 24 of 2017, as amended, and whether APIT could lawfully be deducted from their remuneration.

The appellants had argued that judicial officers could not be regarded as employees for the purposes of the Inland Revenue Act because they did not serve under an employer-employee relationship. They contended that treating judges as employees would be inconsistent with the constitutional principle of judicial independence.

They further argued that the remuneration of judicial officers was constitutionally protected from reduction during their tenure and that the deduction of APIT effectively reduced their remuneration. They sought orders quashing the decision to subject judicial officers to APIT, preventing future deductions and requiring reimbursement of monies already deducted.

The respondents, however, maintained that judicial officers were liable to income tax under the Inland Revenue Act and that APIT was merely a mechanism for collecting income tax in advance. They argued that the tax was a general, non-discriminatory tax applicable to citizens and did not undermine judicial independence.

Justice Shiran Gooneratne, while agreeing with the conclusions concerning the liability of judicial officers to income tax and the operation of APIT, expressed a different view on certain aspects of the constitutional reasoning.
