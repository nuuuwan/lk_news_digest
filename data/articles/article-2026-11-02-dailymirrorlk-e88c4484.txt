‘Public likes it or not, necessary policies must go ahead’: CB Governor

Colombo, Nov. 2 (Daily Mirror) - Central Bank Governor Dr Nandalal Weerasinghe said that the financial policies necessary for the country must be implemented decisively, regardless of whether the public favours or opposes them.

He said that avoiding necessary economic policies simply because they are unpopular could cause serious harm to the country in the long run.

Speaking at a lecture at the University of Kelaniya, he said that with the Central Bank now operating as a more independent institution, it must implement whatever monetary and regulatory policies are required to safeguard the country's long-term economic stability, irrespective of whether such measures are popular with the public.

“In the past, we managed to sustain things in certain ways. The Central Bank has implemented strict policies and will continue to do so. If a particular policy is fundamentally required by the country, it must be implemented. Failing to enforce it simply because the public likes or dislikes it will only cause harm to the nation,” he said.

Referring to taxation, Dr Weerasinghe said tax rates sometimes had to be increased according to the country's needs, even if the majority of the public opposed the hikes.

He acknowledged that such decisions could trigger protests and create a degree of social instability, stressing that the Government had a responsibility to explain the economic implications of increasing or reducing taxes.

He also said that the public should be kept informed of policy decisions and their implications through effective communication.

“Before implementing a policy, we think about it and make a significant effort to communicate it to the public through our various communication channels. We work closely with the public and the media to ensure that when a policy is introduced, it is communicated in a way that the entire country can understand,” he said.

He said that public understanding was essential for the successful implementation of economic policies and for achieving the intended results.

Referring to the Central Bank's independence, Dr Weerasinghe said it would implement whatever measures were most appropriate for the prevailing economic conditions. “Whatever policy is needed for the country, whether it involves reducing or increasing interest rates, allowing the exchange rate to fluctuate or taking any other measure suited to the current economic conditions, will undoubtedly be implemented,” he said.

He said that properly explaining the reasons behind such decisions would help the public understand why they were necessary and make their implementation easier.
