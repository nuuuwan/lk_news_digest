Cancer patients in limbo as vital treatment disrupted

Colombo, Sep. 9 (Daily Mirror) - Cancer patients awaiting radiation therapy are facing further delays as the CT simulation service required for treatment planning at a major cancer hospital has reportedly not been functioning properly since April 28, leaving more than 1,000 patients on the waiting list.

CT simulation is a specialised planning procedure that uses a computed tomography (CT) scanner to map the precise area to be treated before radiation therapy begins. The procedure is essential for patients undergoing treatment with a linear accelerator, as it enables doctors to accurately plan the radiation fields while minimising exposure to surrounding healthy tissue.

However, the service has reportedly been disrupted since April 28, forcing the authorities to rely on PET scans for treatment planning. Medical sources said this is not considered the preferred method for CT simulation and could further complicate the timely provision of radiation therapy.

Sources said more than 1,000 cancer patients are currently waiting for the necessary treatment plan, raising concerns over potentially life-threatening delays in their care.

Patients who seek to undergo the procedure through private facilities could face costs ranging from around Rs. 500,000 to Rs. 1.7 million, depending on the services required, placing the option beyond the reach of many families.

The Daily Mirror had previously highlighted the challenges faced by cancer patients, including disruptions to radiation therapy caused by malfunctioning equipment.

According to informed sources, waiting lists for radiation treatment had increased significantly due to problems with linear accelerator machines. Of the five machines currently in operation, two had reportedly been out of service, severely reducing the capacity to provide timely treatment.

Cancer patients at the Apeksha Hospital in Maharagama have also recenlty appealed to authorities over shortages of essential medicines, including Neupogen, which is required by patients undergoing chemotherapy.
