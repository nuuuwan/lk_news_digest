Over 8,000 bottles of drinking water with fake SLS mark seized

Colombo, Aug. 23 (Daily Mirror) - The Consumer Affairs Authority (CAA) has seized nearly 8,500 bottles of drinking water bearing a counterfeit Sri Lanka Standards (SLS) mark during a special raid in Avissawella.

The raid was conducted on Friday (21) by officers of the CAA’s Competition Promotion Division, who found the stock stored at a warehouse and being prepared for release to the market.

Preliminary investigations revealed that the water had been manufactured at a facility in Amithirigala, Ruwanwella, and transported to the Avissawella warehouse.

The bottles, ranging from 500 millilitres to 19 litres, were found to display a purported SLS mark along with certificate number 894. The CAA said the mark and certificate number had been falsely displayed.

Officials said the stock had not yet been released to the market. The nearly 8,500 bottles have been sealed and taken into custody, while further investigations are underway before legal action is taken against those responsible.

The CAA warned that the use of counterfeit quality marks not only misleads consumers but also creates unfair competition against businesses that comply with required standards, taxes and regulatory requirements.

The Authority urged wholesalers and retailers to verify the authenticity of quality certificates, manufacturers, suppliers and supply chains before purchasing products, particularly those carrying mandatory quality certifications.

Consumers have also been advised to check product labels, manufacturer details and standard marks when purchasing bottled drinking water, as the presence of an SLS mark alone does not guarantee its authenticity.

Anyone with information about the production, storage or sale of products bearing fake or suspicious quality marks can inform the Consumer Affairs Authority through its hotline 1977 during office hours.
