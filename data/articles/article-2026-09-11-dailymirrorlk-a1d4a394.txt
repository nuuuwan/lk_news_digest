Nelum Wewa fire did not destroy 2,000 kumbuk trees

Colombo, Sep. 11 (Daily Mirror) - The Forest Conservation Department has denied reports that more than 2,000 kumbuk trees were destroyed in a fire at the old Eluwakkulam Nelum Wewa bordering the Wilpattu National Park.

The Department said reports claiming that over 500 kumbuk trees had been destroyed in an alleged arson attack were baseless.

Recent reports had said that a fire that initially affected the roots of a century-old kumbuk tree had spread across the area, destroying more than 2,000 kumbuk trees, while nearly 300 trees were reportedly still burning.

However, the Puttalam Additional District Forest Officer, who inspected the area, confirmed that the fire had mainly affected grassland surrounding the tank and several large, decaying trees that had remained in the tank.

The officer said there were a large number of mature trees of various species around the tank and that, apart from branches of a few young trees, no other trees had been affected by the fire.

The Forest Officer of the Wanathavilluwa Beat also inspected the area and confirmed that the fire had been brought under control.

The old Eluwakkulam Nelum Wewa has a water spread of more than 100 acres and provides water to nearly 700 acres of paddy fields, with the tank receiving water from the Kala Oya.

The tank is also an important ecosystem and a habitat for a large number of local and migratory birds. It is considered a popular attraction for both local and foreign tourists.

The Forest Conservation Department said investigations were underway into the suspected arson, which occurred while renovation work was being carried out on the tank bund.

Those responsible will be dealt with under the Forest Conservation Act, the Department said.

It said that the fire had not affected the Wilpattu National Park.
