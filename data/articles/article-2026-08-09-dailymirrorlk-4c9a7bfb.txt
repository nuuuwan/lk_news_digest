Indigenous communities to receive legal identification under new measures

Colombo, Aug. 9 (Daily Mirror) - Legal identification for Sri Lanka’s Indigenous communities will be introduced under new measures announced by Prime Minister Dr. Harini Amarasuriya, as the Government moves ahead with long-awaited legislation to protect their rights and traditional way of life.

Speaking at the World Indigenous Peoples’ Day 2026 celebrations held at the Indigenous Heritage Museum premises in Dambana today (9), Amarasuriya said an extraordinary Gazette notification addressing several longstanding issues affecting Indigenous communities had been signed under the leadership of the Minister in charge of the environment.

The measures include provisions for the registration of Indigenous communities, the issuance of legal identification and permission for Indigenous people to enter forests as part of their daily lives, she said.

The Prime Minister said the measures were expected to provide lasting solutions to several longstanding issues and enable Indigenous communities to protect their rights and culture while continuing their traditional way of life.

Meanwhile, Amarasuriya said the long-awaited Indigenous Peoples Bill had been submitted to the Cabinet, with discussions on the proposed legislation expected to begin this week.

She said Indigenous leader Uruwarige Wannila Aththo and members of the Indigenous community had held discussions with successive governments for years seeking legislation specifically addressing their rights, but the process had not previously been completed.

According to the Prime Minister, the proposed Bill was referred to the Cabinet following the intervention of Minister Sunil Senevi.
