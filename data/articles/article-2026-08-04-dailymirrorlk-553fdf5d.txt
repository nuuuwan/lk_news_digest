International Association of Judges urges Sri Lanka to reconsider judges’ retirement age amendment

Colombo, August 04 (Daily Mirror) - The International Association of Judges (IAJ), the world’s oldest and largest global organisation representing judges through national judicial associations, has expressed strong concern over the Sri Lankan Government’s proposed constitutional amendment to extend the retirement age of judges by two years, warning that the manner in which the proposal has been introduced could undermine judicial independence.

In a statement, the Rome-headquartered organisation called on the Government to reconsider the proposed legislation, stressing that any constitutional changes affecting the judiciary should be introduced through a principled, transparent and consultative process, rather than through what it described as an arbitrary and ad hoc approach.

Founded in 1953 in Salzburg, Austria, the IAJ said its primary mission is to safeguard judicial independence, strengthen the rule of law, and protect fundamental rights and freedoms. It observed that it speaks out whenever potential threats to judicial independence arise around the world and said the current developments in Sri Lanka warranted such intervention.

The IAJ said it shared the concerns already raised by the Judicial Service Association of Sri Lanka, the Commonwealth Lawyers Association, and other observers regarding the proposed amendment.

According to the IAJ, its concerns do not stem from the general concept of revising the retirement age of judges, but from the timing, manner of introduction, and the widespread perception surrounding the proposal.

It said constitutional amendments affecting the judiciary should be based on a reasoned, principled, and robust consultative process, warning that introducing such changes through an arbitrary and peremptory announcement could threaten judicial independence.

The IAJ also noted that where legislation is widely perceived to benefit a particular judicial officer or a small group of judges, regardless of the Government’s actual intention, public confidence in the independence of the judiciary could be undermined.

The organisation urged the Government to reconsider the proposal in its present form and said it would continue to closely monitor developments in Sri Lanka.
