Referendum needed to extend judges’ retirement age, SC determined earlier

Colombo, Aug. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The Supreme Court had determined earlier that any Constitutional amendment to the retirement age or the period of office impacting on incumbent judges, whether directly or indirectly, will impinge on the independence of the judiciary and violate Article 3 ,which requires a Referendum.

The Court, in its previous determination regarding the Inland Revenue Bill, had mentioned it.

The matter has been brought to the fore in political circles in the wake of the Government insisting that there is no need for a referendum for the latest move by it to extend the retirement age of superior court judges. However, the Opposition insists that it should be referred for public approval through a referendum.

Pivithuri Hela Urumaya (PHU) leader Udaya Gammanpila held a press conference and challenged the views expressed by Health Minister Nalinda Jayatissa that a referendum was no longer required.

Besides, Opposition Leader Sajith Premadasa said the Supreme Court has already ruled that a referendum was required to extend the retirement age of judges. He said this while tabling a copy of the judgement delivered by three eminent judges in 2023.

“Those in the Government are speechless when questioned about a referendum. However the judgment given by justices Murudu Fernando, Janak De Silva and Buwaneka Aluwihare clearly calls for a referendum,” Premadasa said.

He questioned as to whether the Government was ready to confer the matter with all stakeholders and if so when it could take place. Furthermore he said the Law Asia and Commonwealth Bar Association and a few other international organisations have expressed concern regarding the issue.
