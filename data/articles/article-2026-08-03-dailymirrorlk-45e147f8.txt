Security tightened across all prisons following Mahara Prison unrest

Colombo, Aug. 3 (Daily Mirror) - Authorities have stepped up security at prisons across the country after the violent unrest at the Mahara Prison left one inmate dead, several others injured, and caused extensive damage to prison property.

Senior Deputy Inspector General of Police (SDIG) Sajeewa Medawatta said special security measures and close monitoring have been implemented at all prisons nationwide.

He said that additional attention has been directed towards key facilities, including Welikada, Magazine, Kalutara and Negombo prisons, to prevent similar incidents.

The unrest, which began on Saturday (01) afternoon at the Mahara Priso continued until the early hours of yesterday before security forces succeeded in restoring order.

A joint operation involving the Department of Prisons, Sri Lanka Police, the Special Task Force (STF), and personnel from the Sri Lanka Air Force and Navy brought the situation under control at around 6.00 a.m.

One inmate was killed and six others were injured during the incident. Prison Department Spokesperson A.C. Gajanayake said an investigation has been launched to determine the cause of the unrest. He also said that inmates caused extensive damage to prison property during the violence.

According to authorities, the disturbance began when a group of inmates hurled stones from inside the prison while relatives were visiting.

Several inmates reportedly attempted to escape by breaking through a temporary wall under construction, while another group set fire to a prison building. Prison officers positioned near the main entrance opened fire to contain the escalating situation. During the unrest, some inmates also allegedly assaulted fellow prisoners who refused to join the violence.

The six injured inmates were admitted to the Ragama Teaching Hospital for treatment, where one later succumbed to his injuries. The deceased was identified as a 33-year-old remand prisoner from Hendala, Wattala, who had been held on charges related to motorcycle theft and possession of 20 grams of crystal methamphetamine, commonly known as "ice."

Meanwhile, tension also mounted outside the prison on Saturday as worried relatives gathered after hearing sounds resembling gunfire from inside the facility. Police said some members of the crowd attempted to breach security barriers and threw stones towards the prison before being dispersed by police and STF personnel. The Sri Lanka Air Force also deployed drones and a Bell 412 helicopter to conduct aerial surveillance of the prison complex.

To maintain public order, police imposed an overnight curfew in three Grama Niladhari divisions surrounding the Mahara Prison within the Ragama Police Division—Kendaliyaddapaluwa West (246), Kendaliyaddapaluwa North (246/C), and Dambuwe (181/G). The curfew was lifted earlier yesterday after authorities confirmed that the situation had been brought fully under control.

The violent disturbance caused extensive damage to several prison facilities, including the rehabilitation division, visitors' area, prison hospital, kitchen, industrial section, and a number of prison buildings, according to the Department of Prisons.

The Department said a number of inmates from Mahara Prison are being transferred to other prisons following the unrest.
