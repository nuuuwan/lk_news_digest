Mobile hospital service planned to provide emergency care for wild elephants

Colombo, Aug. 11 (Daily Mirror) - A dedicated mobile hospital service for wild elephants is being established by the Department of Wildlife Conservation to provide faster medical treatment to injured and sick elephants in remote areas.

The Environment Ministry said the programme is being developed with the necessary institutional, technical and resource support from the Ministry.

The initiative is aimed at addressing one of the major challenges faced by wildlife authorities when treating injured wild elephants, particularly those located far from established veterinary facilities.

Under the proposed mobile service, medical teams will be able to reach affected elephants more quickly and provide treatment at or near the locations where they are found.

The initiative forms part of a wider Government programme aimed at reducing human-elephant conflict and strengthening wildlife protection.

Authorities are also working to improve elephant habitats by restoring natural food and water sources inside forest reserves.

Invasive vegetation around the Kalawewa and Tabbowa reservoirs has been removed to establish elephant enrichment zones and grasslands, while similar restoration work is being carried out around other reservoirs.

The Government has said these measures are intended to encourage elephants to remain within protected forest areas and reduce their movement into human settlements.

Meanwhile, the Government has also expanded field-level resources through the recruitment of Elephant Fence Assistant Officers and the deployment of Civil Defence Force personnel and Multi-Purpose Development Officers.

New electric fences are being constructed, while measures are also being taken to reduce elephant-train collisions.

The Ministry said the combined initiatives are aimed at protecting both human lives and wildlife while developing a sustainable approach to human-elephant coexistence.
