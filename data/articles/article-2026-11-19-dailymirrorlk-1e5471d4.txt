Influenza sweeps across Sri Lanka as doctors urge caution

Colombo, Nov. 19 (Daily Mirror) - Influenza infections are spreading across Sri Lanka, with the public urged to remain cautious as more people experience fever, cough, sore throat, body aches and other respiratory symptoms, particularly among children, older adults and those with underlying health conditions.

Senior Consultant Physician Dr Ananda Wijewickrama told the Daily Mirror that influenza, commonly known as the flu, is a viral respiratory infection that spreads mainly through droplets released when an infected person coughs, sneezes or talks.

He said the infection could also spread when people touch contaminated surfaces and subsequently touch their eyes, nose or mouth.

The most common symptoms include fever, chills, persistent cough, sore throat, runny or blocked nose, headaches, muscle aches and extreme tiredness. Some patients may also experience changes in their sense of taste or smell, although these are not typical symptoms of influenza.

Unlike the common cold, influenza symptoms often develop suddenly and can leave patients feeling considerably unwell.

Although most people recover within a week, the infection can sometimes lead to serious complications, including pneumonia and other respiratory problems, Dr Wijewickrama cautioned.

He said children below two years of age, adults aged 65 and above, pregnant women and people with underlying medical conditions such as diabetes, heart disease, asthma and kidney disease face a greater risk of developing severe complications.

People with weakened immune systems, including those undergoing cancer treatment or taking immunosuppressive medication, are also particularly vulnerable.

Dr Wijewickrama advised the public to follow simple preventive measures, including washing hands frequently with soap and water, covering the mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing, wearing face masks when experiencing respiratory symptoms and avoiding close contact with infected individuals.

People who develop symptoms should also stay at home, get adequate rest and avoid crowded places to prevent spreading the infection to others.

The public has further been urged to avoid self-medication and seek medical advice, particularly before taking antiviral medication.

Sri Lanka generally experiences two seasonal peaks in influenza infections, from May to July and November to January, although the virus circulates throughout the year.
