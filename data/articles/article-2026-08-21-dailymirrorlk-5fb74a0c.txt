Korea-backed mineral processing research moves to second phase

Sri Lanka will move ahead with the second phase of a Korea-backed research project aimed at developing processing technology for quartz and coastal silica sand, as the government looks to strengthen domestic research capabilities around the country’s mineral resources.

The Cabinet this week approved the signing of a Research Outsourcing Contract Agreement between Sri Lanka’s Industrial Technology Institute (ITI) and the Korean Ceramic Engineering and Technology Institute for the second stage of the project titled “Development of a Processing Technology for the Quartz and Silica Sand in the Coastal Area of Sri Lanka.”

The initiative originated from a research concept submitted by an ITI research team, which was selected by the Korea Invention Promotion Association in 2025 as an Intellectual Property Partnership Project. The programme is structured in two phases.

Cabinet had previously approved an agreement for the first phase in April this year between the Korea Invention Promotion Association and ITI.

Under that arrangement, Korea is expected to provide funding, materials, research equipment and technical assistance required by ITI, with support from the Korean Ceramic Engineering and Technology Institute.

The latest approval, granted on a proposal by the Minister of Science and Technology, provides for the research outsourcing agreement covering work under the project’s second phase.

The Cabinet decision did not disclose the value of the Korean assistance, the duration of the second phase or the expected commercial applications of the processing technology
