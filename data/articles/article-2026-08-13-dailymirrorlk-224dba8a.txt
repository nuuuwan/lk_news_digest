Ready to risk office for change, says President

Colombo, Aug. 13 (Daily Mirror) - President Anura Kumara Dissanayake has said meaningful political and constitutional change requires taking risks, stressing that he is prepared to face the consequences of pursuing reforms.

The President made the remarks during a meeting with a delegation from the Bar Association of Sri Lanka (BASL) at the Presidential Secretariat to discuss the Government’s proposed 22nd Amendment to the Constitution.

The meeting ended without an agreement, with the BASL maintaining its objections to the proposal to extend the retirement age of judges of the Supreme Court and Court of Appeal.

BASL President Rajeev Amarasuriya, along with former presidents of the Association, senior academics and President’s Counsel, expressed concerns that the proposed amendment could undermine judicial independence.

The BASL called on the President to halt the process and conduct wider consultations with legal experts before proceeding with the proposed constitutional changes.

President Dissanayake, however, said the proposed amendment was not intended to benefit or target any particular individual. He also assured the delegation that he had no intention of exceeding his executive powers and that state power would be exercised within the existing constitutional framework.

Emphasizing his commitment to political change, the President said he was prepared to leave office if necessary.

“I am a person who is ready to leave. I did not come here intending to remain seated,” he said.

The President also referred to the ongoing investigation into the Easter Sunday attacks as an example of his willingness to take risks in pursuing what he described as necessary change.

He said the investigation involved intelligence agencies that are also responsible for his security but maintained that this would not deter him from pursuing the truth.
