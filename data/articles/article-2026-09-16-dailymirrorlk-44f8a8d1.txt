Services surge as manufacturing loses steam in August

Economic activity displayed mixed momentum in August, with the services sector accelerating sharply while manufacturing growth slowed as production contracted and new orders stagnated, the latest Central Bank Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) showed.

The Services PMI rose to 65.6 in August from 61.4 in July, signalling a faster expansion in business activity, while the Manufacturing PMI slipped to 53.0 from 55.0, though remaining above the 50-point threshold that separates expansion from contraction.

The manufacturing reading masked greater weakness within the sector. Production fell into contraction territory, with the sub-index dropping to 48.4 from 54.5, while New Orders declined to 50.0 from 55.9, indicating broadly unchanged new business during the month.

The Central Bank said the moderation in production was driven by the textiles and wearing apparel sector.

However, some manufacturing indicators remained encouraging. Employment strengthened to 54.8 from 51.0, while stocks of purchases increased to 54.8 from 51.5, reflecting continued recruitment and a gradual inventory build-up ahead of year-end seasonal production.

Supply conditions remained a concern, with the Suppliers’ Delivery Times index rising to 63.0 from 61.5, reflecting what the Central Bank described as “persistent supply disruptions”.

Despite the slowdown, manufacturers remained positive about the months ahead, largely on expectations of the customary year-end seasonal pick-up, though the Central Bank said near-term challenges continued to temper the outlook.

In contrast, services gathered considerable momentum during August, with stronger activity recorded across several key segments of the economy.

The Central Bank said the expansion was primarily powered by transportation, wholesale and retail trade, and professional services, with financial and other personal services also making notable contributions.

New business continued to increase, particularly in wholesale and retail trade, financial and other personal services, while professional services also contributed positively. Employment improved on fresh recruitment. Backlogs of work, however, fell to 48.3 from 51.2, reversing the increase recorded in July.

Sentiment towards the coming quarter remained upbeat, supported by expectations of improving economic conditions and higher tourist arrivals. Nevertheless, the Expectations for Activity index moderated to 71.7 from 75.0 in July.

The Central Bank cautioned that broader global economic uncertainties remained a downside risk to the services sector outlook.
