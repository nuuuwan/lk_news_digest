64% of pre-schoolers get 30+ minutes of daily screen time in SL, raising concerns - Survey

Colombo, Sep. 26 (Daily Mirror) - Excessive screen use among preschool children is raising concerns over their brain development, language and social skills, physical activity and overall health, with 64% of children spending more than 30 minutes a day on screens, according to a recent survey conducted by the Health Promotion Bureau in collaboration with the University of Colombo and UNICEF.

The figures were highlighted by Dr Anil Jasinghe, Secretary to the Ministry of Health and Mass Media, at a media briefing. The survey, conducted across four provinces - Southern, Western, Central and Northern - involved around 1,100 children, their parents and preschool teachers from 55 preschools.

According to the survey, 64% of children spend more than 30 minutes a day on screens, while approximately 43% consume their meals while using a screen. Around 32% of the children also sleep for less than the recommended 10 to 11 hours a day.

According to World Health Organisation recommendations, children below two years should not be exposed to screens, including televisions, mobile phones, tablets and computers. For children between two and five years, screen time should not exceed 60 minutes a day and should preferably be limited to 30 to 45 minutes per day, according to Dr Enoka Wickramasinghe, Consultant Community Physician and Head of the National Preschool Health Promotion Programme at the Health Promotion Bureau of the Ministry of Health and Mass Media.

Dr Wickramasinghe said excessive passive screen use could affect early childhood development, particularly as the first few years of life are a period of rapid brain growth and development. She explained that when children passively watch screens, they do not receive the same stimulation through their five senses as they would through active play and interaction.

“When a child is not stimulated through the five senses, neuronal connections will not be developed in the brain,” Dr Wickramasinghe said.

She said this could affect areas of development, including cognitive, language, motor, emotional and social development, as children require active experiences and interaction to develop these skills.

Dr Wickramasinghe also pointed to concerns raised by psychologists and psychiatrists over language development and behavioural problems among children who spend excessive amounts of time in front of screens.

“Language abilities can be poor, and language development can be reduced remarkably. There can also be behaviour problems, such as aggressive behaviour,” she said.

She stressed, however, that screen use does not cause autism, while noting that children who spend excessive periods in front of screens may have fewer opportunities to develop social connections and social skills.

Another concern is the impact of prolonged screen use on children's physical activity. Dr Wickramasinghe said spending extended periods seated in front of screens contributes to sedentary behaviour, which may have longer-term consequences for children's health. According to her, children should engage in at least 180 minutes of physical activity a day, including around 60 minutes of vigorous activity. She noted that remaining seated for more than one hour is considered sedentary or inactive.

Further, reduced physical activity can increase the risk of overweight and obesity and contribute to non-communicable diseases such as diabetes, high blood pressure and heart diseases later in life, she said.

She also identified eye-related problems as another possible concern associated with excessive screen exposure among young children.

Meanwhile, the survey also found that approximately 43% of children consume their meals while using screens, highlighting concerns over eating habits, as children become distracted from eating when using a screen during meals.

The survey has now formed the baseline for a new social and behaviour change communication programme under the Health Promotion Bureau’s National Preschool Health Promotion Programme. Dr Wickramasinghe said the programme aims to improve eight key health behaviours among preschool children and their caregivers, including parents, teachers and other family members, with reduced screen time identified as one of the eight behaviours requiring attention. She further added that parents and caregivers are encouraged to provide children with toys, storybooks and other age-appropriate activities to keep them engaged and entertained without relying on screens, while spending more time playing and interacting with children themselves.
