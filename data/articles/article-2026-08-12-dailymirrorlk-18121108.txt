What Sri Lanka’s executioners do when no execution is scheduled

Colombo, Aug. 12 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka currently has two members serving as executioners. However, the executioners do not remain on standby solely for capital punishment duties. They are regular employees of the Prisons Department, carrying out other assigned duties and receiving standard government salaries until they are called upon for an execution.

When an execution date is officially determined, the executioners report to the prison on the previous day and begin preparations. These include inspecting the gallows and execution equipment and ensuring that all arrangements are in working order.

According to the sources, the executioners also assess the condemned prisoner’s physical measurements to determine the required rope length and other technical arrangements. A test is conducted using a weighted sandbag to ensure the equipment is functioning properly before the scheduled execution.

On the day of an execution, the responsibilities are divided between the two executioners. One assists with the prisoner’s final preparations and accompanies the prisoner to the gallows, while the other remains responsible for operating the execution equipment.

The position has historically been associated with a hereditary tradition in Sri Lanka, with the role reportedly passed from father to son. However, this practice has since ended, with executioners now recruited through the Prisons Department’s regular employment process.

Despite the nature of their duties, the executioners reported to have remain ordinary employees at the department and perform routine prison-related duties when no execution has been scheduled.
