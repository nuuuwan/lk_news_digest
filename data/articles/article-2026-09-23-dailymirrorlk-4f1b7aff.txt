22A to be approved by Parliament on Friday

Colombo, Sep. 23 (Daily Mirror) - Following the Supreme Court’s determination that a referendum is not required for the proposed 22nd Amendment to the Constitution, Parliament is set to approve the Bill on Friday (25) with a two-thirds majority, following a two-day debate.

Announcing the much-awaited determination of the Supreme Court, Speaker Jagath Wickramaratne said the Supreme Court had ruled that the provisions of the “Twenty-Second Amendment to the Constitution” Bill do not require approval by the people at a referendum under Article 83 of the Constitution.

The Speaker announced that as per the determination, in the proviso appearing in Clause 2 of the “Twenty Second Amendment to the Constitution” Bill, the phrase “හැට හතට එලඹෙන දිනය” appearing therein shall be replaced with the phrase “හැට හත සම්පූර්ණ කළ දිනය”.

He said the determination has observed that the part “Provided further that the term limit of six years set out in the first proviso will not apply to any person holding the office of the Chief Justice as at the date of coming into operation of this Act” should be added as the second proviso to Clause 2 of the “Twenty Second Amendment To The Constitution” Bill.

The Speaker said the Supreme Court has further determined that the provisions of the “Judicature” (Amendment)” Bill are not inconsistent with the Constitution and may be passed by the simple majority of the Parliament.

A record 67 petitions seeking a Special Determination were filed before the Supreme Court in relation to the proposed 22nd Amendment. The petitions were considered by a five-judge bench of the Supreme Court.

Opposition parties had urged the Government to hold a referendum on the proposed amendment, arguing that it could have implications for democracy.
