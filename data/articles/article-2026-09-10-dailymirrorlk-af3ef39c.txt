Sri Lanka calls for end to ‘indefinite’ UN human rights mandates

Sep. 10 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka has urged UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk to ensure that country-specific human rights mandates are not allowed to continue indefinitely, calling for periodic reviews and clear mechanisms to bring them to an end when countries demonstrate progress.

Sri Lanka’s Permanent Representative to the UN in Geneva, Ambassador Sumith Dassanayake, made the call during the General Debate under Agenda Item 2 at the 63rd Session of the UN Human Rights Council in Geneva on Tuesday (8).

Dassanayake told the Council that no mandate should be regarded as indefinite, and that country-specific mandates should be periodically reviewed against clearly defined objectives and measurable outcomes.

He also called for the inclusion of sunset clauses and phase-out provisions in country-specific resolutions where appropriate, arguing that countries demonstrating progress through national processes, dialogue, cooperation and technical assistance should have a credible pathway towards the conclusion of such mandates.

“The continuation of mandates should be justified by demonstrable need and results, not institutional perpetuation,” the Ambassador said.

He further urged the Council to consolidate or rationalise mandates where there are overlaps, stressing the need for greater efficiency in the use of Council resources.

The Sri Lankan delegation said the Human Rights Council should focus its limited resources on the most pressing global human rights challenges, including armed conflicts, humanitarian crises and displacement.

Dassanayake also maintained that Sri Lanka’s national processes had made progress in protecting and promoting human rights, and said such progress should be recognised by the Council.

His remarks came as the written update on the human rights situation in Sri Lanka was presented to the Council, with High Commissioner Volker Türk in attendance.

Sri Lanka also called for a more focused, objective and mandate-based approach to country-specific reporting, particularly in the context of ongoing UN reforms aimed at improving efficiency and prioritisation across the UN system.
