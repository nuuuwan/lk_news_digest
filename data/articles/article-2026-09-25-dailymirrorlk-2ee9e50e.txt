Counsellors warn of growing sexual withdrawal among married men

Colombo, Sep. 26 (Daily Mirror) - Psychological counsellors have raised concerns about a growing pattern of sexual withdrawal among married men in Sri Lanka, saying a lack of intimacy is increasingly contributing to tension and conflict in relationships.

“Men in Sri Lanka are increasingly withdrawing from sexual relationships. Around 80% of those who seek counselling are women, and they report that their husbands gradually distance themselves from intimacy after marriage,” a counsellor said.

Counsellors said the issue is particularly observed among men aged 35 to 45. Financial difficulties, work-related stress, family responsibilities and emotional exhaustion were identified as factors that can affect sexual desire and intimacy.

Prolonged financial and economic pressures can also affect mental wellbeing and, in turn, a person's interest in intimacy, they said.

“In the past, physical exhaustion from demanding work was often a major factor affecting men. Today, however, many face greater psychological and emotional stress. Mental pressure can negatively affect sexual desire in men, just as it can in women,” the counsellor said.

The increasing use of mobile phones and other digital devices has also become a concern, with counsellors saying excessive screen time can reduce communication between spouses and contribute to emotional distance.

They also pointed to excessive pornography consumption as a factor that can create unrealistic expectations about sexual relationships. In some cases, embarrassment or discomfort in discussing such expectations with a partner can further affect communication and intimacy.

“Online sex is easy, convenient and simple. There is no responsibility towards anyone, and it takes only a few minutes. Men may naturally choose the easier option, which can be a psychological tendency rather than a personal flaw,” the counsellor said.

Counsellors said some couples gradually begin to view intimacy as another responsibility rather than as part of their emotional connection. Work pressures, childcare, household responsibilities and unresolved disagreements can further contribute to the problem, particularly when couples do not communicate openly.

They stressed that sexual withdrawal should not automatically be interpreted as a lack of affection or attraction, as it may be associated with stress, emotional difficulties, relationship problems or other personal factors.

Counsellors encouraged couples experiencing persistent difficulties with intimacy to communicate openly and seek professional support when necessary.
