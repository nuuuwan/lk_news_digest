Millions bleed into dormant state enterprises as liquidation delays persist

Sri Lanka continues to bleed millions in public funds to sustain defunct state enterprises, driven by severe bureaucratic inertia that has stalled formal winding-up procedures, the Auditor General has revealed.

Despite a binding Cabinet directive issued on September 3, 2025, explicitly instructing the Ministry of Industry and Entrepreneurship Development to appoint liquidators for inactive state-owned institutions, no substantive action had been taken as of May 31, 2026. According to the National Audit Office, eight state enterprises and statutory bodies remain in institutional limbo. Stripped of commercial production or service delivery, these entities continue to drain public resources while locking up valuable state assets. The entities marked for delayed liquidation include Sri Lanka Cement Corporation, Lanka Cement PLC, Hingurana Sugar Industries Company Limited, Kantale Sugar Industries Limited, the Small and Medium Enterprises Authority, the Wood-based Design Centre, Kahatagaha Engineering Services Limited, and Lanka Textile Mills Embilipitiya Limited.

The most glaring fiscal hemorrhage highlighted in the audit is Kantale Sugar Industries Limited, where manufacturing operations ceased in 1994. The state audit uncovered that 32 workers—all of whom have exceeded the mandatory retirement age of 60—remain firmly on the payroll of the shuttered facility. The Auditor General observed that while operational activities at the Kantale Sugar institution were brought to an end over three decades ago, the entity is maintained at a fruitless expenditure of approximately Rs. 1.7 million per month. This sum covers salaries, allowances, administration, and overheads for its aging workforce, with no concrete liquidation steps taken by the end of May 2026.

Beyond the Kantale facility, the audit flagged severe accountability lapses across other non-operational units, notably Lanka Cement PLC and Hingurana Sugar Industries Company Limited. Both entities failed to submit essential information to the Auditor General regarding the status of tabling their statutory Annual Reports in Parliament, leaving their financial standing and liabilities entirely unscrutinised. The audit further exposed a wider systemic governance failure across the Ministry, revealing that 9 statutory institutions in 2022, 14 in 2023, and 24 in 2024 failed to table their Annual Reports in Parliament within the mandatory 180-day statutory window.

The persistence of defunct commercial arms such as Kahatagaha Engineering Services Limited and Lanka Textile Mills Embilipitiya Limited reflects the state’s prolonged inability to amputate non-viable corporate appendages from operating entities. While legacy institutions like the Wood-based Design Centre and the Small and Medium Enterprises Authority remain dormant corporate shells without an operational mandate, the Ministry is simultaneously struggling to deal with shuttered production assets within the Ceylon Ceramics Corporation. Although the Cabinet granted policy clearance in May 2025 to hand over four defunct ceramic factories in Eragama, Mahiyanganaya, Uswewa, and Oddusudan to private investors under public-private partnerships, progress remains gridlocked.

Meanwhile, another four inactive ceramic factory sites—located in Bangadeniya, Yatiyana, Weuda, and Elayapattu—are being excised from the corporation’s books to be converted into industrial estates.

The Auditor General warned that these prolonged liquidation delays trigger severe downstream economic costs, locking up hundreds of acres of prime land and decaying factory infrastructure while unresolved liabilities drain the Treasury.

The National Audit Office has urged the Ministry to urgently coordinate with the Department of Public Enterprises and the Treasury to appoint liquidators, formalize terminal settlements for aging staff, and systematically dissolve all eight inactive commercial entities to finally halt the bleeding of public funds.  (NF)
