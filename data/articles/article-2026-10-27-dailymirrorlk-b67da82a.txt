SJB urges speaker to resign or allow no-confidence debate

Colombo, Oct. 27 (Daily Mirror) - The Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB) and its allies in Parliament have urged Speaker Jagath Wickramaratne to resign or allow a debate on the no-confidence motion against him to proceed, accusing the Government of undermining democratic principles.

In a special statement, the Opposition parties said the Speaker should step down if the Government was committed to good governance or face the allegations levelled against him.

They warned that the Government's decision to reject the proposed debate posed a serious threat to democracy and the Opposition's parliamentary rights.

The parties criticised the Committee on Parliamentary Business, chaired by the Speaker, for rejecting the no-confidence motion over allegations of personal misconduct.

They also rejected the Government's argument that the matters raised in the motion were *sub judice*, stating that legal experts had described the claim as unfounded.

According to the statement, the allegations were based on a parliamentary report titled *Report of the Inquiry into the Leak of Confidential Information Regarding the Speaker*, which was prepared following a letter from the Speaker's Private Secretary.

The Opposition further maintained that it was unethical for the Speaker to remain in office while the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption was conducting an investigation against him. It called on him to step down at least until the investigation was concluded.

The no-confidence motion was handed over to Parliament on October 9, 2026, and included in Parliament's Order Book on October 12, the statement said.

At a meeting of the Committee on Parliamentary Business held on October 12, while the Speaker was overseas, it was proposed that the Attorney General be consulted to determine whether the allegations in the motion could conflict with ongoing legal proceedings.

However, the Opposition claimed that the motion had not been referred to the Attorney General despite the agreement reached at the meeting.

At a party leaders' meeting on October 23, chaired by the Speaker, the Government and the Speaker had maintained that there was no need to seek the Attorney General's opinion and that the Opposition should amend and resubmit the allegations before further action could be taken, according to the statement.

The Opposition accused the Speaker of using the process to prevent a debate on the allegations against him.

It said it would not withdraw or amend the allegations in line with the Government's demands and remained determined to pursue the no-confidence motion.
