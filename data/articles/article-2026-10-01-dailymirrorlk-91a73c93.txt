CDS account openings retreat to 12-month low as market momentum fades

The new account openings at the Central Depository Systems (CDS) retreated to a 12-month low in July, as a slump in trading and a Rs.321.5 billion erosion in the value of securities held in custody underscored a market steadily shedding the momentum it carried into 2026, as per the CDS data.

The depository registered 4,329 new accounts during the month, 12.8 percent fewer than in June and 36 percent below this year’s high of 6,748 in February.

Equity trades, meanwhile, contracted 29 percent year-on-year to 687,210, with both months spanning 22 market days.

Custody value slipped for a third straight month to Rs.7.62 trillion, 5.5 percent below April’s Rs.8.06 trillion, while a marginal rise in securities held suggests that the prices, rather than the withdrawals, drove the decline. However, the slowdown comes off an exceptional base, with the openings of 38,146 in the first seven months running 46 percent ahead of a year earlier.

Foreign investors have also lifted their share of custody value to 18 percent, from 16.8 percent over the year, despite holding 1.4 percent fewer securities, reflecting stronger gains in the counters they favour.

The depository’s latest annual report counts just 60,516 active investors in 2025, barely 6 percent of the 1,006,499 accounts on its books at end-2025.

As at July, the retail and high-net-worth investors accounted for 96.7 percent of unique accounts, yet commanded only 19.5 percent of custody value, against 62.5 percent for local institutions.
