DFCC scales up retail banking with StanChart portfolio buyout

DFCC Bank PLC has completed the acquisition of Standard Chartered Bank Sri Lanka’s wealth and retail banking business, bringing approximately 50,000 customers and employees attached to the operation into the bank.

The transaction covers customer relationships across priority banking, credit cards, retail lending, deposits, wealth management and small and medium enterprise (SME) portfolios, along with selected operating assets.

The acquisition expands the scale of DFCC’s retail operations and strengthens its wealth management and SME businesses, forming part of the bank’s longer-term strategy to broaden its customer base across Sri Lanka.

DFCC said the migration was structured to minimise disruption, following several months of preparation and collaboration between the two banks.

The customers can continue using their existing Standard Chartered account numbers alongside their newly issued DFCC account numbers. The existing credit and debit cards, card PINs, standing instructions, direct debits, recurring payments and instalment arrangements will also remain operational.

The saved payees and beneficiaries have been transferred to DFCC’s digital banking platforms, DFCC ONE and DFCC iConnect. The customers can also continue using their existing branches and dealing with their current relationship managers.

The newly transferred customers will gain access to DFCC’s personal banking, wealth management, SME, card and lending products, along with a network of more than 130 branches and over 5,000 ATMs through LankaPay. The eligible customers will also have access to DFCC Pinnacle, the bank’s premium banking proposition.“From the very beginning, our focus has been on making this transition as seamless and familiar as possible for the customers. Behind that experience has been an exceptionally complex technology programme, supported by months of detailed planning, close collaboration and an extraordinary effort by the teams across both organisations,” DFCC Bank CEO Thimal Perera said.

“Success meant ensuring that the customers experienced as little of that complexity as possible. Their existing Standard Chartered account numbers remain usable alongside their new DFCC Bank account numbers. They can continue using the same credit and debit cards and the same card PINs, while their standing instructions, direct debits, recurring payments, instalment arrangements and other everyday banking arrangements continue as before,” he added.

Perera said transferring the customers’ saved payees and beneficiaries to DFCC’s digital platforms would remove one of the most common inconveniences associated with changing banks.

“I extend my sincere appreciation to my colleagues at DFCC Bank and our colleagues at Standard Chartered Bank, the Central Bank of Sri Lanka, our technology and implementation partners and everyone whose expertise and commitment made this possible. It is an honour to welcome our new customers and colleagues to DFCC Bank. We look forward to earning their trust and supporting them through every stage of their financial lives,” he said.

The customers requiring assistance can contact DFCC Bank’s 24-hour Customer Care Centre on +94 11 235 0000 or continue using the existing Standard Chartered customer hotline on +94 11 248 0480. Calls to this number are now routed to DFCC Bank.

The customers may also visit any DFCC Bank branch or access information and support through www.dfcc.lk/welcome.
