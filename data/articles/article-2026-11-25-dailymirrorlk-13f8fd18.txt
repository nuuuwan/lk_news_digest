Israel’s Eisenkot secures first coalition partner in bid to form government

ALJAZEERA - The party of Israeli opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot has announced an agreement to unite with a right-wing party in a bid to form the next government following October’s elections.

The centrist Yashar party said on Tuesday that it agreed to sign a coalition pact with Israel Our Home party after a meeting with its leader Avigdor Lieberman, the first alliance of its kind since the election.

After no bloc managed to secure a 61-seat majority, Israel’s parliament was sworn in on November 10 without a new government.

President Isaac Herzog tasked Eisenkot with forming ⁠a government, while Likud party leader Benjamin Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister despite the October 27 election stripping him of his majority.

Eisenkot has until December 6 to form a government under a 28-day mandate, though he could ask for a 14-day extension. If Eisenkot fails, Netanyahu would have an opportunity to do so. If no one is able to form a government, another election will be held next year.

The right-wing Likud’s alliance with far-right and ultra-Orthodox parties holds 53 of the Knesset’s 120 seats.

Eisenkot’s centre-right Yashar party and patchwork coalition partners, including Lieberman, hold 48 seats. But they have potential allies in the parties representing Israel’s Palestinian minority, which hold 19 seats and ⁠are aligned with Eisenkot in their vehement opposition to Netanyahu.

Lieberman had once served as foreign minister under a previous Netanyahu government but has since become a strong critic.

Talks are also ongoing between Eisenkot, some of his “change bloc” partners and Likud on forming a unity government.

The left-wing Democrats party, which won eight parliament seats, separately said it was suspending talks with Eisenkot after the Yashar party leader’s talks with Netanyahu’s Likud on a possible unity government.

Yair Golan, head of the Democrats, said he prefers a government without Netanyahu but with the Arab parties.
