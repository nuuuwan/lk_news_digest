People’s Struggle Alliance warns Government against introducing ‘repressive’ laws under new names

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The People’s Struggle Alliance has warned the Government against introducing legislation that could reproduce the repressive powers of the Prevention of Terrorism Act (PTA), saying a change of government does not make oppressive laws democratic.

The alliance made the remarks during a protest against the existing PTA, the proposed Protection of the State from Terrorism Act (PSTA) and proposed legislation aimed at preventing organised crime.

Addressing the issue, alliance Executive Committee member Rajkumar Rajeevkanth said the opposition was based on the need to protect human rights, democratic freedoms and the rule of law.

He said the proposed legislation could weaken judicial oversight and grant excessive powers to the police and security forces, creating opportunities for arbitrary arrests, prolonged detention and human rights violations.

Recalling the consequences of the PTA, Rajeevkanth said many people detained under the law had spent years in prison without being convicted of any offence. Some were eventually released after losing valuable years of their lives, while their families, livelihoods and futures had been severely affected.

He acknowledged that national security and the prevention of organised crime were legitimate responsibilities of the State but stressed that such objectives should never be pursued at the expense of fundamental rights or judicial independence.

He also expressed concern that political groups which had previously opposed repressive legislation were now attempting to introduce alternative laws that could grant similar powers to the authorities.

The alliance called on the Government to repeal the PTA without replacing it with legislation containing similar provisions that allow arbitrary detention or excessive executive powers.

It also demanded the withdrawal of the proposed PSTA in its current form, calling for any future counterterrorism framework to be developed through transparent public consultation and an independent human rights review.

Regarding the proposed organised crime legislation, the alliance urged the Government to remove provisions that could enable arbitrary detention, disproportionate investigative powers or restrictions on fundamental freedoms without adequate judicial safeguards.

The alliance further called for guarantees of due process and independent judicial oversight, including ensuring that every arrested person is promptly produced before a judge, informed of the allegations and given access to legal representation.

It also demanded an urgent review of cases involving people held under the PTA for prolonged periods, calling for the release of those against whom there was no lawful basis for continued detention and timely judicial proceedings for others.

Rajeevkanth said independent and impartial investigations should also be conducted into allegations of torture, enforced disappearances, unlawful detention and other human rights violations to ensure accountability through due legal process.

The alliance further called for national security laws to be applied without discrimination based on ethnicity, religion, language or political affiliation, while insisting that proposed legislation be made available for public scrutiny and comply with the Constitution and Sri Lanka's international human rights obligations.

Delivering a message to the Government, Rajeevkanth said those who had previously opposed oppressive laws should remember the principles they had defended before coming to power.

" A law does not become democratic simply because it is introduced by a government that once opposed repression," he said, adding that legislation should be judged by the powers it grants, the safeguards it provides and its consequences for ordinary citizens, rather than the political identity of those introducing it.

He stressed that the alliance's opposition was not directed at any particular government or political party but against the misuse of State power, regardless of who held office.

"We are not demanding special privileges. We are demanding justice, accountability, due process, judicial independence and equal protection under the law," he said.

"Governments may change. Political leaders may change their positions. But our commitment to human rights and democratic freedoms will remain unchanged. We opposed repression yesterday. We oppose it today. And we will oppose it tomorrow," he added.
