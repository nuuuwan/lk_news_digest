John Keells Foundation celebrates purpose-driven volunteerism

The John Keells Foundation (JKF), the dedicated Corporate Social Responsibility arm of the John Keells Group, recently hosted its annual Volunteer Recognition Day 2026 at Cinnamon Lakeside Colombo. Held under the patronage of Group Chairperson Krishan Balendra and senior leadership, the event celebrated the exceptional contributions of staff and business teams who dedicated their time, skills, and efforts toward fostering positive social impact across Sri Lanka during the 2025/26 financial year.

Presenting the milestones of the past year, Head of Group CSR Carmeline Jayasuriya highlighted a remarkable growth in community engagement. Over the year, 2,500 volunteers contributed across 4,842 instances, accumulating a staggering 105,096 hours of service. Environment-based initiatives, disaster relief efforts such as Ditwah, and the iconic annual art fair Kala Pola saw the highest levels of participation. Commemorating JKF’s 21st anniversary, the foundation also launched a custom-built Volunteer App designed to digitize the entire engagement process across the group, which has already gained traction among more than 1,100 staff members, including the Group Executive Committee.

A central feature of the 2026 ceremony was the inauguration of a special award designed to recognize strategic, sustainable community development initiatives executed in partnership with JKF. The inaugural honor was awarded to the John Keells Praja Shakthi Habarana Beekeeper Project team, based at the Habarana hotel complex, for their success in creating shared value. Wasantha Bandara, a local Community Project Coordinator, shared how ongoing guidance and training from the John Keells and Cinnamon teams transformed local beekeeping into a sustainable enterprise, boosting community income while protecting bee populations. As a testament to its success, the pilot consignment of honey from the project was launched during the event and recorded strong pre-market sales.

Addressing the gathering, Group Chairperson Krishan Balendra commended the growing volunteer numbers and praised the unwavering spirit of the staff, noting their visible dedication at public events like Kala Pola. He also pointed to the Group’s broader national contributions, including a LKR 500 million allocation toward rebuilding Sri Lanka—the largest collective corporate contribution by any Sri Lankan entity. Emphasizing the success of sustainable models like the Habarana beekeeping initiative, Hikka Batiks in Hikkaduwa, and the Ja-Ela mushroom project, Balendra encouraged further expansion of such community-centric programs and urged staff to actively utilize the new volunteer application.
