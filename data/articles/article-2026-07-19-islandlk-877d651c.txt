First Capital sees 70% chance Central Bank will hold rates on July 21

Sri Lanka’s Central Bank is likely to keep interest rates unchanged at its monetary policy review on July 21, with the effects of its surprise tightening in May still working through the economy, according to First Capital Research.

In a pre-policy report released yesterday, the research firm assigned a 70% probability to the Central Bank maintaining the Overnight Policy Rate at 8.75%, while leaving open a 30% chance of a modest 25-basis-point increase if policymakers judge external and inflation risks to have intensified.

First Capital argues that the Central Bank’s unexpected 100-basis-point rate hike in May has yet to fully transmit through the economy, noting that monetary policy typically takes several months to produce its full effects. It says another increase at this stage could unnecessarily restrain economic activity before the impact of the previous tightening becomes evident.

The report also points to easing inflation pressures following declines in global oil prices and recent domestic reductions in fuel and cooking gas prices. It expects private sector credit growth to moderate under existing monetary conditions, while liquidity in the banking system stabilises naturally without further intervention. Higher market lending rates and already restrictive financial conditions further reduce the case for another immediate increase, it says.

However, the research house cautions that policymakers may still opt for a precautionary rate hike if external risks worsen. It identifies renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, the possibility of higher global oil prices, softer worker remittance inflows, pressure on foreign reserves and the need to support the Sri Lankan rupee as key factors that could justify further tightening.

The report concludes that, on balance, the Central Bank is more likely to pause and assess the effects of its earlier policy action while closely monitoring inflation, exchange rate movements and global developments before deciding whether additional tightening is warranted later in the year.
