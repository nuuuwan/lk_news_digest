Iconic Galadari reborn as Radisson Blu Hotel Galadari Colombo

Foreign Affairs and Tourism Minister Vijitha Herath leading a ceremonial cake-cutting to mark the occasion

Landmark hotel unveils Radisson Blu identity after US$ 48.5mn transformation

Targets December opening as island heads for a record tourism year

Colombo Fort’s Galadari Hotel ushered in a new era on Sunday evening as it celebrated its grand opening as Radisson Blu Hotel Galadari Colombo, unveiling a landmark reimagined from its foundations to blend four decades of local heritage with the standards of a global hospitality brand.

The ceremony at the hotel’s Lotus Road premises was held under the patronage of Minister of Foreign Affairs, Foreign Employment and Tourism Vijitha Herath. Guests included Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) Chairman Suranjith Wevita, diplomats, senior principals of the Galadari Brothers group, hoteliers and business leaders. Managing Director for South East Asia and Pacific Bert Fol represented Radisson Hotel Group.

The highlight of the evening was the on-stage unveiling of the new brand, in which Minister Herath, senior Galadari representatives and Director of Operations Bilal Shafi took part. The Minister also led a ceremonial cake-cutting with a traditional sword to mark the occasion.

First opened in 1984 as the Galadari Meridien, the hotel has spent more than four decades as a fixture of Colombo’s commercial and social life, a legacy General Manager Marko Janssen placed at the heart of his video address to guests. “Over the years, this hotel has been part of Colombo’s story.

It hosted weddings, business deals, farewell dinners, and first dates,” he noted, adding that a hotel “is only as alive as the memories it creates. And this one has plenty.”

The transformation, which got under way in February 2024, went well beyond a cosmetic facelift. Existing guest room interiors were stripped out to make way for fully refurbished rooms as part of a significant structural overhaul aligning the property with Radisson Blu’s global standards. The first phase, with the overall design overseen by a Hong Kong-based architectural firm, covered 196 rooms, the restaurant outlets, the guest lobby and common areas, as well as a fully revamped facade.

Accommodation ranges from Deluxe Rooms of 32 square metres with city or sea views to Executive Rooms and Suites, a two-bedroom suite of 128 square metres and a sea-view Presidential Suite, with connected rooms available for families. Guests in the Executive Rooms and Suites also enjoy an in-room espresso machine and access to the Executive Lounge.

The dining line-up has been rebuilt around an all-day dining restaurant, specialty Indian and Lebanese restaurants, a lobby bar and café, a pool bar and a separate café with outdoor access. The leisure offering includes an outdoor pool and a spa with five treatment rooms for massages, facials, body wraps and aromatherapy, along with a fitness centre, sauna and steam room.

The hotel is also making a strong play for the meetings and weddings market. It has seven event venues, led by the Blu Grand Ballroom, which spans 580 square metres and can seat up to 800 guests theatre-style, and the 520-square-metre Blu Sapphire, which accommodates up to 500, complemented by five smaller rooms including the ballroom’s VIP lounge.

Reflecting on the journey, Janssen admitted that “a closed hotel is a quiet, strange place”, but maintained that “a building can be closed while its spirit is not”. He acknowledged that the road to the grand opening brought delays, difficult decisions and plans that had to be rewritten more than once. Each setback, he argued, pushed the team to ask what the hotel could become rather than how to restore what it once was.

“We keep the warmth, the pride, and the heritage of 40 years, and we add world-class design, modern comfort, international standards, and the reach of a global brand,” he remarked, promising guests “the authentic Sri Lankan hospitality that no brand can manufacture, and the consistency and quality that travellers around the world trust”.

Galadari Hotels (Lanka) PLC, 63.57 percent owned by Dubai-based Galadari Brothers Co. (LLC), raised its total investment in the project to US$ 48.5 million last year from an initial US$ 33.5 million, an increase of nearly 45 percent. Janssen thanked the owners for their vision and patience, telling them, “You stayed committed when it would have been easier not to.” Chairman M. A. I. Galadari has likewise expressed confidence in the company’s latest annual report that the repositioned hotel will “emerge as a strong contender within Colombo’s 5-star hospitality landscape”.

Janssen kept his warmest words for the hotel’s team, a blend of long-serving staff and recruits who joined during the transition, including through the ‘Race to the Blu Future’ careers drive launched in January. “Walls and furniture create a building, but you create experience. Your pride, your smile, and your care are what guests will remember long after they check out,” he told them.

The hotel joins Radisson Hotel Group’s Sri Lankan portfolio, which already includes properties in Colombo, Kandy and Galle. Its grand opening comes as the island’s tourism industry gathers momentum. Sri Lanka crossed two million tourist arrivals for the third consecutive year this month, and authorities expect 2026 to surpass last year’s record of 2.36 million arrivals. The sector is also shifting its focus from volume to higher-yielding corporate, MICE and leisure travellers. With the hotel targeting a December opening as the winter season sets in, Radisson Blu Hotel Galadari Colombo is well placed to capture that demand from its vantage point overlooking Galle Face Green and the Indian Ocean.

“Tonight is not an ending. It’s a beginning,” Janssen concluded. “Our promise to every guest who walks through these doors is simple: you will feel welcome, you will feel looked after, and you will want to come back.”
