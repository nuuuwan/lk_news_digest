Govt. plans social ethics framework to curb misleading advertisements

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - The Government is planning to crack down on advertisements promoting superstition, caste discrimination and other harmful social practices through a proposed social ethics framework, Minister of Women and Child Affairs Saroja Savithri Paulraj said.

The Minister made these remarks in response to a journalist's question about newspaper advertisements that identify individuals by caste and promote discriminatory attitudes.

She pointed out that numerous advertisements also mislead both men and women by promoting superstitious beliefs, including spells, attraction charms and other occult practices.

“These are advertisements of the lowest level through which society and the next generation of children are misled and distorted,” she said, referring to promotions of various oils and other products circulating on social media.

The Minister said the proposed framework would seek to ensure that society moves forward rather than regresses.

She added that the principles of social ethics should extend beyond advertising to encompass art, media and literature, with the aim of discouraging content that reinforces harmful beliefs and attitude.
