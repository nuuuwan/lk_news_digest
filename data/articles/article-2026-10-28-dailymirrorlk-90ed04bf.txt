Two high-speed trains collide in Spain, at least 10 dead

Reuters - Two high-speed trains have collided in Spain ​on the line connecting Madrid to Spain's southern Andalusia region, rail ‌operator Adif said on Wednesday, killing at least 10 people.

The collision comes nine months after an accident between high-speed trains belonging to the same operators on the same line that ​killed 46 people.

That crash in January, which occurred after a train ​derailed and smashed into an oncoming train near Adamuz, in ⁠Cordoba province, triggered a debate about the level of investment in railway ​maintenance in recent years.

"Traffic on the Madrid-Andalusia high-speed rail line has been suspended ​following a rear-end collision between two trains," Adif said in a message posted on X on Wednesday.

A Renfe and an Iryo train crashed just after 9 a.m. (0800 GMT), causing two ​Iryo wagons to derail, a government spokesperson for the Castilla La Mancha ​region said, adding that the cause and number of injuries were still being determined.

At least ‌10 ⁠people died in the collision, a regional government source said.

Footage by regional Castilla La Mancha Television showed a derailed train with at least one carriage remaining on an elevated section of track while twisted metal, broken panels and ​wreckage could be ​seen strewn across ⁠an embankment beneath the line.

Rail operator Adif said the incident occurred on the line from Madrid to Seville.

Manuel Galan, ​mayor of nearby town of Urda, told Cadena SER that ​emergency ⁠services had asked the town council to prepare emergency facilities, including a sports hall, to accommodate passengers and the injured.

Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez's agenda, published on the ⁠Spanish ​government's website, had been cleared for the day. ​The opposition People's Party also cancelled all campaign events, a spokesperson said.
