Rainy weather drives vegetable prices higher

Oct 26 (Daily Mirror) - Prices of several upcountry and low-country vegetables have increased amid prevailing rainy weather, with tomatoes, capsicum and cabbage recording sharp rises in wholesale prices.

The wholesale price of a kilogram of tomatoes, which ranged between Rs. 350 and Rs. 400 last week, has increased to between Rs. 600 and Rs. 650.

The price of a kilogram of capsicum has risen from Rs. 250 to between Rs. 400 and Rs. 450, while the wholesale price of cabbage has increased from Rs. 150 to Rs. 350 per kilogram.

Former Chief Organiser of the Manning Market Traders Union Anil Indrajith said vegetable prices could increase further as the festive season approaches.

He attributed the recent price increases mainly to prevailing weather conditions in the country, noting that tomatoes had become the most expensive vegetable, with wholesale prices reaching around Rs. 600 per kilogram.

According to Indrajith, beans are currently priced between Rs. 400 and Rs. 500 per kilogram, while carrots are selling at between Rs. 200 and Rs. 250.

Leeks are priced at around Rs. 200 per kilogram, while green chillies are selling at between Rs. 350 and Rs. 400 per kilogram.

He warned that vegetable prices could rise further in the coming weeks as demand increases ahead of the festive season.
