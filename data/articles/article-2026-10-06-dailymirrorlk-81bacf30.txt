Tea’s rupee gains outpace dollar performance

The national tea sale average rose in September, with the low-grown teas posting the largest monthly gain, although the prices in US dollar terms remained below the year-earlier levels across all elevations.

The national average reached Rs.1,202.74 per kilogramme, up Rs.28.28 or 2.4 percent from August, according to the Sri Lanka Tea Board data compiled by Forbes & Walker Research.

Compared with September 2025, the average increased by Rs.11.12 or 0.9 percent. Its dollar equivalent, however, fell to US $ 3.65 per kilogramme, from US $ 3.94 a year earlier, a decline of 7.4 percent.

The figures show a modest improvement in the rupee prices alongside a weaker dollar equivalent. The report uses the Central Bank spot rates for currency conversion.

The low-grown teas recorded an average of Rs.1,310.05 per kilogramme in September, gaining Rs.33.22 from August and Rs.58.21 from a year earlier. It was the only elevation to record a year-on-year increase in rupee terms during the month.

The low-grown dollar average rose to US $ 3.98 per kilogramme, from US $ 3.84 in August, although it remained below US $ 4.14 recorded in September 2025.

The high-grown teas averaged Rs.1,070.05 per kilogramme, up Rs.12.99 from August, while falling Rs.85.71 from a year earlier. The dollar equivalent stood at US $ 3.25, compared with US $ 3.82 in September 2025. The medium-grown teas registered the smallest monthly gain, rising Rs.2.14 to Rs.956.96 per kilogramme. The average was Rs.70.58 below its year-earlier level, while the dollar equivalent declined to US $ 2.90, from US $ 3.40.

For the first nine months of 2026, the national sale average stood at Rs.1,170.20 per kilogramme, an increase of Rs.9.64 or 0.8 percent, over the corresponding period of 2025. In dollar terms, it fell to US $ 3.63, from US $ 3.88, a decline of 6.4 percent.

The cumulative rupee averages increased for high-grown and low-grown teas, while the medium-grown teas remained below last year’s level. All three elevations recorded lower dollar averages over the nine-month period.
