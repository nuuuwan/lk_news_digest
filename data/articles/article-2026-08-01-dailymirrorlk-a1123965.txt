Station Masters warn of safety risks over unresolved signal failures

Colombo, August 1 (Daily Mirror) - The Sri Lanka Railway Station Masters' Association (SLRSMA) has raised serious concerns over unresolved signaling failures across several railway sections, warning that continued train operations under temporary arrangements could jeopardize passenger safety.

Association Chairman Sumedha Somaratne said train services on the Talaimannar railway line were resumed last week despite major defects in the signaling system, adding that the damaged equipment has yet to be properly restored.

He alleged that trains on the single-track section between Medawachchiya and Talaimannar Pier are currently operating under the "pilot method," a procedure intended only for temporary use during exceptional situations such as signal failures. However, he claimed the method has now become a routine operating practice.

Somaratne further said signaling problems on the Maho–Anuradhapura section also remain unresolved, with train movements continuing under temporary signaling arrangements.

He alleged that railway lines reconstructed at a cost of billions of rupees in public funds are gradually deteriorating due to inadequate maintenance and the failure to implement necessary operational improvements.

The Association said it has already informed the relevant authorities in writing and warned that it would resort to trade union action if immediate measures are not taken to resolve the signaling failures and operational issues affecting railway stations.
