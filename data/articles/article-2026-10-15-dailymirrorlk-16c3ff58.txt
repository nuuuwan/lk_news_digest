Sri Lanka targets commercialisation of research to boost economic growth

Sri Lanka is planning to align research and development (R&D) with the national economic priorities and promote the commercialisation of research to improve productivity and generate business opportunities, Sri Lanka Ports Authority Chairman Dr. Mahinda Parakrama Dissanayake said.

Addressing Sri Lanka Economic and Investment Summit 2026, organised by the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce, in Colombo, Dr. Dissanayake said the country needed a coordinated approach to research, with greater emphasis on turning the scientific findings into commercially viable products, services and technologies. Sri Lanka currently spends less than one percent of its gross domestic product on R&D, while research activities are often carried out independently by the ministries, institutions and academics without a common national economic focus.

Under the proposed reforms, the research priorities and funding would be linked to the national development objectives, allowing the research institutions to focus on areas with the potential to deliver practical economic and social benefits.

The Cabinet approval has been granted to establish a National Commission for R&D to coordinate policy development, identify research priorities, allocate funding, monitor progress and promote commercialisation.

The proposed framework is expected to cover 15 ministries, with the priority areas including agriculture, livestock, fisheries, food security, energy, digital technology, artificial intelligence, tourism, healthcare, education and social development.

The government is also considering a funding model, under which the public funds would support early-stage research, while the private-sector investors would be encouraged to participate once the projects demonstrate commercial potential.

The funding for research commercialisation could reach Rs.50 million per project, subject to the technical, financial, marketing and administrative assessments.

Dr. Dissanayake highlighted the need to strengthen partnerships between the universities and businesses to help the research institutions develop products and technologies that can compete in domestic and international markets.

The proposed approach is intended to bridge the gap between research and industry by encouraging businesses to identify practical challenges that the researchers can address.

The success of the initiative will depend on effective coordination among the government institutions, universities and private sector as well as the ability to turn the research outcomes into sustainable commercial ventures. (CPS)
