EDB appoints 24 advisory committees to support US$ 36bn export drive

The Sri Lanka Export Development Board (EDB) this week appointed 24 advisory committees for 2026–2029 to help shape export policy, address constraints facing businesses and support the country’s ambition to achieve US$36 billion in exports by 2030.

The committees bring together exporters, small and medium enterprises, industry associations, government institutions and sector experts. Their inputs will inform the EDB’s annual action plans, the National Export Development Plan (NEDP) and the National Export Policy.

Speaking at the presentation of appointment letters in Colombo, Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Minister Sunil Handunnetti called for changes to outdated laws and regulations that hinder exports.

“Building on the growth achieved by all export sectors over the past two years, we must move towards new targets that surpass our own past records,” he said.

“By amending outdated laws and regulations that hinder exports, and with the collective strength and technical expertise of the public and private sectors, everyone must join hands to achieve these export targets.”

The committee structure covers both individual industries and issues affecting businesses across the export sector.

Six committees will focus on logistics and hub operations; trade facilitation; trade finance and reforms to the business and investment environment; trade promotion and market links; quality, standards and environmental, social and governance requirements; and skills, entrepreneurship and innovation.

A further eight will cover the NEDP’s priority sectors: auto components and engineering, mineral-based products, rubber-based industries, marine industries including boat and shipbuilding, spices and concentrates, digital products and services, electrical and electronic components, and processed food and beverages.

The remaining ten cover apparel, Ayurveda and herbal products and cosmetics, coconut products, construction and power energy services, gems and jewellery, organic products, ornamental fish, pharmaceuticals, seafood and aquaculture, and SME export development.

The Ministry and EDB developed the NEDP 2026–2030 with public and private sector stakeholders and technical assistance from the Asian Development Bank under the Ministry’s policy-based lending programme. The plan identifies strategies to strengthen the business environment, expand exports and diversify the product basket.

The appointments were made under Section 10 (1) of the Sri Lanka Export Development Act No. 40 of 1979, which empowers the EDB to establish advisory committees for products, services and functional aspects of export trade.
