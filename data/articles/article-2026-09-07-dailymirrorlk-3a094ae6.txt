Metro bus fleet to reach 172 as service expands nationwide

Colombo, Sep. 7 (Daily Mirror) - The government plans to expand the Metro Bus Service to 172 buses by the end of this year or early next year, with the programme also expected to extend beyond the Western Province to Galle, Kandy and Jaffna.

Transport, Highways and Urban Development Minister Bimal Ratnayake said the Metro bus fleet would increase to 122 buses this month as part of efforts to provide more efficient and comfortable public transport services to the urban population.

He said a Cabinet paper had already been submitted seeking approval to purchase a further 50 buses for the Metro service. Once these buses are added, the fleet is expected to reach 172.

The Minister made these remarks while attending the official commencement of operations of the newly established Kadawatha Metro Depot yesterday (7).

The Metro Bus Service is being developed jointly by the Ministry of Transport, Highways and Urban Development, the Clean Sri Lanka Secretariat, the Sri Lanka Transport Board (SLTB) and Lanka Metro Transit (Pvt.) Ltd.

The service was initially launched with a focus on the Western Province as part of the Government's programme to modernise the country's urban public transport system.

Under the first phase of the programme, Lanka Metro Transit plans to establish four new depots in Thalangama, Kadawatha, Ekala and Ratmalana to strengthen the operational, administrative and maintenance capacity of the service.

The newly opened Kadawatha depot is located near the SLTB depot along the Kadawatha-Ganemulla main road in the Mahara electoral division of the Gampaha District.

The Metro service currently includes Route No. 01, operating between Makumbura and Kadawatha via Maharagama. Fifteen new Metro buses are planned to be deployed on this route through the establishment of the new depot facilities.

The project also includes the development of bus stops, integrated payment systems, depot facilities, parking areas and other supporting infrastructure aimed at improving the quality and efficiency of public transport.

The Metro Bus project was initiated through the 2025 Budget, with modern buses, technological facilities and digital platforms being introduced through Lanka Metro Transit.

The Government has allocated Rs. 800 million for the Metro operation process in 2026, while Rs. 267.5 million has been allocated as share capital for 2027.

Minister Ratnayake said the expansion of the Metro service would not be limited to the Western Province, with plans to introduce the service in Galle, Kandy and Jaffna.

He also said the private sector could be given opportunities to operate Metro bus services in the future under contracts with the Metro institution.

However, the Minister stressed the continuing importance of the SLTB in Sri Lanka's public transport system.

Describing the SLTB as the “lifeblood” of public transport in the country, he said the Government was also taking steps to strengthen the state-owned bus service.

The Government is working to import 600 new air-conditioned buses for the SLTB by the end of this year, while a further 200 buses are expected to be brought in using SLTB funds.

The expansion of both the Metro Bus Service and the SLTB fleet is expected to increase public transport capacity and provide commuters with more modern and efficient transport options.
