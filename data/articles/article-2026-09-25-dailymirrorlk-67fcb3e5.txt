After 22A, NPP Govt to begin work on new Constitution

Colombo, Sep. 25 (Daily Mirror) - Despite the Government’s sudden move to enact the 22nd Amendment to the Constitution, which provides for an increase in the retirement age of superior court judges, the introduction of a new Constitution, a key election promise of the ruling National People’s Power (NPP), will come under formal discussion within the party next year, an official said.

The NPP, in its election manifesto, 'A Thriving Nation, A Beautiful Life, promised to repeal the current Constitution and replace it with a new Constitution, scrapping the executive presidential system.

However, the "Government opted to amend the Constitution first through a piecemeal approach by introducing the 22nd Amendment, a move that drew flak from many quarters, including the Mahanayake Theras.

Opposition parties have also resisted the enactment of the new Bill.

The vote on the Bill is due to be taken this evening in Parliament, with wide anticipation that the Government, which commands a two-thirds majority, will secure its passage through Parliament conveniently.

Asked about the current status of Constitution-making, NPP Secretary Dr. Nihal Abeysinghe said a serious discussion on a new Constitution would begin next year, with a focus on the fate of the Provincial Council system.

Dr. Abeysinghe said the discussion would begin with a plan to conclude the process as early as possible.

“We have to discuss it among ourselves first. The new Constitution and the Provincial Council system are the two matters which are yet to be touched upon,” he said.

He said past constitution-making proposals would be studied as part of this exercise to explore whether they could be incorporated into the NPP’s thinking.

The JVP, the key constituent of the NPP, has been advocating the abolition of the executive presidency for decades.
