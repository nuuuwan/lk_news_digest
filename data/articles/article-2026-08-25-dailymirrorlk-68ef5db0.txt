WindForce eyes South Asia engineering services expansion as generation curtailments squeeze profit

WindForce PLC is moving to commercialise its in-house engineering and turbine repair capabilities into a standalone business line, with plans to deploy specialised technical services across South Asia, as Sri Lanka’s largest renewable independent power producer (IPP) looks to build revenue streams beyond generation.

The push comes as the group revenue rose 16 percent year-on-year to Rs.8.04 billion in the financial year 2025/26, driven by the newly commissioned capacity and full-year contributions from the recent assets.

Profit after tax, however, moderated by 4 percent to Rs.2.15 billion, weighed down by the industry-wide generation curtailments imposed by the National System Operator (NSO), formerly the Ceylon Electricity Board, during the low-demand weekend periods — the restrictions that cost the company an estimated 6.75 GWh in wind power generation alone. That squeeze is sharpening the case for diversifying into higher-margin technical services.

According to the group’s latest annual report disclosures, WindForce operates a dedicated engineering workshop backed by an in-house technical team of over 100 professionals, handling complex component overhauls, including wind turbine generators and gearboxes.

Having built end-to-end operational capabilities across its domestic fleet, the company is evaluating plans to offer these repair and maintenance services to the third-party operators locally, before expanding into the wider South Asian corridor — alongside its civil engineering and plant lifecycle management expertise, leveraging its existing overseas footprint to capture the cross-border opportunities.

The engineering push complements WindForce’s broader international growth strategy. The group maintains an operating footprint across Pakistan, Uganda and Ukraine, alongside the recent solar EPC projects in the Maldives and Zanzibar, with its overseas assets accounting for 38 percent of its total 253 MW installed capacity.

The IPP is looking to build hard-currency, US dollar-denominated revenue through further geographic expansion, reducing its reliance on a single off-taker and mitigating the domestic foreign exchange exposure.

Domestically, the group is also formulating repowering programmes across its pioneer utility-scale wind farms, under which the ageing turbines in the proven high-density wind corridors will be replaced with the next-generation, higher-capacity models to lift the energy output per site footprint.

WindForce Managing Director Manjula Perera said long-term value creation requires continuous technological diversification alongside operational agility.

“As a renewable energy company, our mission extends beyond generating electricity; we are committed to creating lasting value for our stakeholders while contributing to a cleaner, more resilient and energy-secure future for Sri Lanka,” Perera said, adding that the group’s development pipeline spanning solar, wind, floating solar and battery storage positions it to capture the emerging opportunities.

The focus on engineering commercialisation and repowering comes as the private renewable developers navigate the finite lifecycles governing Sri Lanka’s power purchase agreements.

WindForce’s generation portfolio operates under 20-year standard PPAs signed with the NSO and with the early installations advancing through their contract tenures, the group has identified fixed-term PPAs as a transition risk requiring proactive lifecycle planning to prevent asset stranding and manage long-term decommissioning liabilities.

By rebuilding electro-mechanical systems in-house and upgrading key generation corridors, the company aims to secure contract extensions and preserve generation productivity beyond the original 20-year agreements.

WindForce Chairman Ranil Pathirana said disciplined capital allocation remains essential to navigating the sector’s structural reforms.

“Guided by operational excellence, prudent capital allocation, innovation and sustainable growth, we continue to strengthen our portfolio and support Sri Lanka’s goal of generating 70 percent of electricity from renewable sources by 2030,” Pathirana said in his annual review. (NF)
