Sri Lanka’s Budget 2027 faces a test of investment delivery: Fitch

Primary surplus could exceed 2.3% of GDP target, if capital spending falls short again

Budget carries forward Rs.260bn in unspent Cyclone Ditwah reconstruction funds

Fitch says wider deficit is consistent with falling debt and budget broadly aligns with IMF programme

Sri Lanka could again beat its primary surplus target in 2027, if it falls short on public investment, Fitch Ratings said yesterday, pointing to a risk hanging over the government’s spending plans after Rs.260 billion earmarked for cyclone reconstruction went unspent in 2026.

Fitch said Budget 2027 kept Sri Lanka on a debt reduction path, despite a wider deficit. It forecast that the interest payments would still absorb about 41 percent of government revenue this year, more than three times the median for the sovereigns in the ‘B’ rating category.

The government is targeting a primary surplus of 2.3 percent of gross domestic product (GDP) in 2027, down from an estimated 3.6 percent this year, as revenue moderates and capital spending rises. The overall deficit is projected to widen to 4.5 percent of GDP, from 3.7 percent.

Stronger revenue put the 2026 primary surplus well ahead of the government’s 2.5 percent target and Fitch’s earlier forecast of 2.6 percent. Fitch said the outperformance and the government’s focus on reducing debt were key factors behind its September decision to upgrade Sri Lanka’s sovereign rating to ‘B-’, from ‘CCC+’, with a stable outlook.

Fitch said the budget assumptions appeared credible and were broadly in line with its September sovereign review. The government’s primary surplus target is below Fitch’s forecast of 2.8 percent of GDP because the planned spending is higher than the agency had assumed. Another shortfall in capital spending could push the surplus above the budget target, Fitch said.

The primary expenditure is budgeted at 13.6 percent of GDP, above the 13 percent ceiling set by the 2024 Public Finance Management Act. The government is seeking a temporary deviation for a second year, mainly to accommodate the Cyclone Ditwah reconstruction and higher public investment. The spending fell short of plans in 2026, keeping the actual expenditure within the ceiling.

The 2027 spending plans include Rs.260 billion for the cyclone reconstruction from a supplementary budget approved in December 2025. The allocation was carried forward after it went unspent in 2026.

Revenue is projected to ease to 15.8 percent of GDP in 2027, from 16.6 percent in 2026 and 16.7 percent in 2025. Fitch said the boost to the import duties from the pent-up demand for vehicles was fading as the imports normalised, while the efforts to widen the tax base and improve compliance were supporting the gains across other sources of revenue.

Fitch said the budget was broadly aligned with the parameters of Sri Lanka’s International Monetary Fund (IMF) programme, which should support the seventh review of the Extended Fund Facility. The current programme is due to end in March 2027. The government has announced a committee to assess a possible follow-on arrangement, which Fitch said could help sustain policy continuity.

Over the medium term, Fitch said maintaining the recent revenue gains and restraining the spending would be central to further debt reduction and the outlook for the sovereign rating. The government’s aim of maintaining a primary surplus of 3 percent of GDP would require politically difficult spending choices to stay within the 13 percent expenditure ceiling.

Higher public investment could help lift potential growth, which Fitch forecasts at 4.2 percent and support more stable public finances. The agency said those gains would depend on the government’s ability to carry out and properly target capital spending as well as progress on broader structural reforms.

Fitch forecasts government debt will fall to about 92 percent of GDP in 2026, from 115 percent in 2022, still well above the 55 percent median for the ‘B’-rated sovereigns. The interest payments are expected to decline to about 41 percent of revenue, from nearly 80 percent in 2023, against a peer median of 12.7 percent.

Fitch said a longer record of credible fiscal policy and sustained primary surpluses would be critical to keeping debt sustainable and supporting any further improvement in the sovereign rating.
