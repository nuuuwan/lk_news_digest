Apparel, tea declines pull Sri Lanka’s July merchandise exports lower

Services exports expanded an estimated 3.8% to US $ 351.92mn during month

Combined merchandise and services exports for July stood at US $ 1.64bn

Cumulative analysis shows merchandise exports rose 5.1% to US $ 8.19bn during January-July, while services exports grew at a faster 7% to US $ 2.30bn

The lower earnings from the apparel, tea, seafood and coconut-based products outweighed the strong growth in electronics and spices, pulling the merchandise exports down 1.3 percent year-on-year (YoY) to US $ 1.29 billion in July.

The services exports, meanwhile, expanded an estimated 3.8 percent to US $ 351.92 million during the month, led by the information and communication technology (ICT) and business process management (BPM) services, according to the provisional data released by the Export Development Board (EDB).

The EDB estimated the combined merchandise and services exports at US $ 1.64 billion in July.

The apparel and textile export earnings, the country’s largest merchandise export category, fell 8.8 percent YoY to US $ 437.59 million. The apparel shipments to all three major markets declined, with the exports to the United States falling 6.3 percent, the European Union 8.6 percent and United Kingdom 5.7 percent. The tea export earnings dropped 17.2 percent to US $ 116.73 million, reflecting a 27 percent contraction in bulk tea and a 10.1 percent decline in tea packets. The sector was hit by a 47.9 percent fall in shipments to the Middle East. The tea exports to the United Arab Emirates declined 57.5 percent, while the shipments to Iran and Iraq plunged 73.1 percent and 78.5 percent, respectively.

The seafood exports recorded the steepest sectoral decline, falling 60.3 percent to US $ 15.58 million. The frozen fish exports dropped 54.5 percent, while the fresh fish shipments contracted 81.2 percent.

The coconut-based product exports declined 9.9 percent to US $ 111.71 million, mainly due to the weaker earnings from coconut oil and liquid coconut milk. The food and beverage exports also fell 4.7 percent to US $ 58.08 million.

These declines were partly offset by a near doubling of the electrical and electronic component exports to US $ 76.34 million. The insulated wire and cable shipments surged 150.2 percent, while the exports of boilers, piston engines, pumps and vacuum pumps rose to US $ 5.87 million, from US$ 0.21 million a year earlier.

The spice and concentrate exports increased 96 percent to US $ 72.14 million, driven by a more than fivefold increase in pepper shipments.

The rubber and rubber-based product exports rose 3.2 percent to US $ 87.24 million. The industrial and surgical glove exports increased 19 percent, while the shipments of gaskets, washers, seals and other hard-rubber products grew 47.6 percent.

Services provided further support, with the ICT and BPM exports estimated at US $ 143.24 million in July.

Despite the weaker monthly merchandise performance, the combined exports increased 5.5 percent YoY to US $ 10.49 billion during the first seven months of 2026.

The merchandise exports rose 5.1 percent to US $ 8.19 billion during January-July, while the services exports grew at a faster 7 percent to US $ 2.30 billion.

The growth during the period was supported by a 57.7 percent increase in the electrical and electronic component exports to US $ 377.91 million. The ICT/BPM exports rose 14.5 percent to US $ 1.02 billion, while the food and beverage exports grew 19.2 percent to US $ 405.22 million.

The United States remained Sri Lanka’s largest single export destination, accounting for about 22 percent of the merchandise exports. The shipments to the market declined 0.6 percent to US $ 250.87 million in July and fell 1.1 percent to US $ 1.71 billion during the seven-month period.

The exports to the European Union, which accounted for about 25.5 percent of the merchandise shipments, fell 14.6 percent in July. The cumulative exports to the bloc were down a marginal 0.5 percent.

India strengthened its position as Sri Lanka’s second-largest export destination, overtaking the United Kingdom, while Turkey recorded a strong growth during both July and the seven-month period.

“Our priority now is to build on this momentum by strengthening the competitiveness of the existing export sectors while accelerating the development of new products, services and markets,” EDB Chairman and CEO Mangala Wijesinghe said.
