Sri Lanka launches Marine Industries Advisory Committee to position country as regional maritime hub

Holds inaugural Marine-Based Services Advisory Committee meeting, appoints Kaushal Rajapaksa as Chairman and Ricky Barnett as Co-Chairman

EDB brings public and private leaders together to tackle space shortages, boost marine tourism and drive export-led growth

The inaugural meeting of the Advisory Committee on Marine-Based Industries was held on 16 November 2026 at the Sri Lanka Export Development Board (EDB), bringing together key public and private sector stakeholders to discuss strategic priorities, industry challenges and emerging opportunities for the development of Sri Lanka’s marine-based industries.

The meeting was attended by the EDB Chairman, Director General, and the Additional Director General (Development), underscoring the importance placed on the sector as a key component of Sri Lanka’s export diversification and economic growth agenda.

During the meeting, Kaushal Rajapaksa was appointed as Chairman of the Marine-Based Services Advisory Committee, while Ricky Barnett was appointed as Co-Chairman. Members expressed confidence in their leadership to drive the Committee’s future initiatives and strengthen collaboration among industry stakeholders.

EDB Chairman Mangala Wijesinghe delivered the main presentation, outlining the current status of the sector, emerging global opportunities, and the strategic priorities required to position Sri Lanka as a competitive regional hub for marine-based services. The presentation highlighted the sector’s growth potential and the need for coordinated efforts to enhance value addition, innovation, skills development, and international market access.

The Committee members actively contributed to the discussions by sharing ideas, perspectives, and strategic recommendations for sector development. A key area of discussion was the need to address infrastructure and space constraints affecting the expansion of shipbuilding, ship repair and vessel conversion activities. Industry representatives highlighted the limited availability of suitable industrial space within the Colombo Port for fabrication, vessel construction, assembly, storage, equipment installation and sea trials. The importance of identifying suitable alternative locations and developing dedicated industrial facilities to accommodate the industry’s future requirements was emphasised.

The Committee also discussed opportunities to strengthen marine infrastructure and tourism-related activities, including the development of marinas, sheltered anchorages, breakwaters, boatyards and other marine tourism facilities. The potential to develop existing harbors, including Galle and other coastal locations, as marine tourism and boating hubs was highlighted. In this regard, the progress of the proposed Kapparathota Marina Development Project was discussed.

Another important area of discussion was the taxation structure and HS Code classification applicable to locally manufactured commercial boats supplied to domestic tourism operators. The Committee also emphasised the importance of fostering stronger public-private partnerships (PPPs) and creating an enabling environment that supports sustainable growth and increased export earnings from marine-based services.

Speaking at the meeting, Committee Chair Rajapaksa noted the significant opportunities available within the sector and stressed the importance of collective action to unlock its full potential. Some members of the committee highlighted the need for continuous engagement with industry stakeholders to ensure that the Committee’s recommendations translate into tangible outcomes.

To facilitate focused discussions and effective implementation of proposed initiatives, the establishment of specialised subcommittees covering Boat Building and Manufacturing, Shipbuilding and Ship Repair, Regulatory Affairs, Training and Skills Development, Marketing and Export Promotion, Nautical and Marine Tourism, and Offshore Services was discussed. These subcommittees are expected to develop practical proposals and recommendations addressing specific industry requirements.

The Committee also highlighted the opportunities presented by Sri Lanka Expo 2027, particularly in promoting marine-related investment opportunities, showcasing industry capabilities and connecting local businesses with international buyers and investors. Members were encouraged to identify potential buyers, investors and investment-ready marine-related projects for promotion at the event. The importance of aligning the Committee’s activities with the National Export Development Plan (2026–2030) and strengthening coordination among relevant government institutions was also emphasised.

The inaugural meeting concluded with a shared commitment to translate the discussions and recommendations into practical initiatives, with an emphasis on measurable outcomes, effective implementation and collaborative actions among industry stakeholders and government institutions.

The establishment of the Marine-Based Services Advisory Committee marks an important step towards strengthening Sri Lanka’s blue economy and leveraging the country’s strategic maritime location to generate sustainable economic growth and export-led development.
