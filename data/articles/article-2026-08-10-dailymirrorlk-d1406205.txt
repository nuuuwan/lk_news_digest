Sunshine Holdings’ 1Q margin narrows as healthcare, agribusiness face pressure

Sunshine Holdings PLC reported a 7.8 percent year-on-year decline in operating profit for the first quarter of FY2026/27, as the regulatory pricing pressure in healthcare and higher agribusiness costs weighed on the margins, despite a double-digit increase in revenue.

Group earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) fell to Rs.2.4 billion during the quarter, while the EBIT margin narrowed to 13.2 percent.

Gross profit increased by a modest 3.2 percent to Rs.5.2 billion, significantly trailing revenue growth, as the gross margin contracted by 366 basis points to 28.1 percent.

Consolidated revenue rose 16.6 percent to Rs.18.5 billion, supported substantially by the inclusion of Joint Agri Products Ceylon (Pvt.) Ltd (JAPC) in the group accounts.

Profit after tax nevertheless increased 6 percent to Rs.1.4 billion.

The consumer sector recorded the strongest top-line growth, with revenue rising 38 percent to Rs.6.6 billion. However, excluding the impact of consolidating JAPC, revenue growth from the segment was limited to 2.1 percent.

Branded tea revenue grew 8.3 percent, supported by Watawala Thei and Ran Kahata, while increased competition affected the premium tea category. Confectionery revenue rose 6.9 percent, driven by gums and wafers.

Tea export revenue declined 6.3 percent on lower volumes from key customers. JAPC’s spice export business recorded an 11.5 percent increase in revenue from its cinnamon, coconut, black pepper and clove portfolio.

Healthcare, which accounted for 49.2 percent of group revenue, posted a 5.9 percent increase in revenue to Rs.9.1 billion. Growth in medical devices and pharmaceutical manufacturing helped offset the price reductions imposed by the National Medicines Regulatory Authority and supply constraints. The pharmaceutical agency business contracted 18.2 percent, due to the lower volumes and wider industry challenges. Revenue from Healthguard Distribution and Healthguard Pharmacy declined 0.3 percent and 0.9 percent, respectively.

Medical-device revenue rose 8.8 percent, while Lina Manufacturing recorded 20.5 percent growth, aided by deliveries to the government during the quarter.

Agribusiness revenue increased 12.7 percent to Rs.2.8 billion, led by the oil palm operation, where revenue rose 17.9 percent to Rs.2.5 billion on higher market prices, sales volumes and crop availability.

The dairy business remained under pressure, with revenue falling 7.1 percent to Rs.258.9 million because of lower milk volumes and higher input costs. The sector’s EBIT margin consequently narrowed to 47.2 percent, from 48.8 percent a year earlier.

Sunshine Holdings Group Chief Executive Officer Shyam Sathasivam said the regulatory pricing and supply constraints in healthcare, together with the higher agribusiness operating costs, had affected profitability during the quarter.

“Our priority remains to protect the earnings quality, deepen local manufacturing capabilities and pursue disciplined growth opportunities,” he said.

During the quarter, Sunshine Healthcare Lanka and India’s Zydus Lifesciences established Zydus Sunshine Lifesciences (Pvt.) Ltd. The joint venture plans to develop an oral solid dosage pharmaceutical manufacturing facility at the Board of Investment’s Horana Export Processing Zone.
