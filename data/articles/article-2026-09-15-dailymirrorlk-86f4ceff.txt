Sampath Bank acquires Anilana’s last 2 hotels under parate execution

Board contests takeover; tells court unnamed party has offered Rs.1.2bn for Nilaweli, Pasikudah

June quarter loss of Rs.88.5mn on zero revenue; negative net assets widen to Rs.615.7mn

Anilana Hotels and Properties PLC’s two remaining hotels in Nilaweli and Passikudah have been acquired by Sampath Bank PLC, under parate execution, closing off the strategic investor search the loss-making group launched a year ago, to restart its dormant properties, according to a filing with the Colombo Stock Exchange.

The properties were taken over under Recovery of Loans by Banks (Special Provisions) Act No. 04 of 1990, which allows the licensed commercial banks to sell the mortgaged property on a board resolution, without going to court.

The company annexed a letter from the bank dated August 31, 2026, which it received on September 01, confirming the acquisition. The disclosure was made to the bourse on September 11, ten days later.

The board is resisting the takeover on three fronts. Two cases have been filed in the Commercial High Court “challenging the resolution adopted by Sampath Bank PLC”, both of which are now pending in appeal before the Supreme Court.

Separately, the company has applied under Companies Act No. 07 of 2007, informing the court of “a prospective investor who had offered a sum of Rs.1.2 billion” for the hotels, in an effort to compromise with the bank. The Commercial High Court has issued notice on Sampath Bank and the company said it would disclose the outcome “in the event a compromise is reached between the bank and us”.

The offer, if it holds, would cover only about 47 percent of the Rs.2.55 billion in borrowings carried on the group balance sheet as at end-June and a little over half the Rs.2.23 billion at which the group’s property, plant and equipment are stated. The investor has not been identified in the filing.

Nilaweli and Passikudah were the last income-generating assets of any scale left in the group. The Panichchankerni beachfront, earmarked for an eco-resort, was acquired by a bank at auction in April 2021, following parate execution, while the 39-acre Dambulla land was sold to Riverside Serenade (Pvt.) Ltd for Rs.240 million in June 2024, in the unauthorised transaction that triggered the boardroom rupture and required the retrospective shareholder ratification four months later.

The interim accounts for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, released last month, show a group with no operations to defend. Revenue was nil for a second consecutive year, with both hotels shut since November 2022 and the group posted a net loss of Rs.88.5 million or close to Rs.1 million a day, against Rs.190.6 million in the corresponding quarter, which carried a Rs.98.8 million loss on the disposal of assets.

The finance costs of Rs.55.9 million accounted for the bulk of the deficit and were met almost entirely by Rs.55.4 million in fresh borrowings drawn during the quarter.

The current liabilities of Rs.2.82 billion now exceed the current assets of Rs.410.4 million by Rs.2.41 billion, widening from the Rs.1.86 billion shortfall the auditors flagged as a going concern risk in March 2024, the last year for which the audited accounts exist.

The negative net assets deepened to Rs.615.7 million, from Rs.527.2 million three months earlier, while the accumulated losses of Rs.6.35 billion have overtaken the Rs.5.08 billion in stated capital raised since the 2013 listing by Rs.1.27 billion. Cash at the end of the quarter stood at Rs.3.8 million.

However, the interim accounts, signed on August 13, record that “no events have occurred since the reporting date which would require any adjustment” and that there has been “no significant change in the nature and value of the contingencies”, despite the bank’s acquisition resolution having been under legal challenge since 2025.

Meanwhile, the regulatory action against the board has continued in parallel. The CSE imposed penalties on every director serving at the date of non-compliance on June 25 and again on July 24, in both instances for failing to rectify the non-submission of interim accounts, for the quarters ended June 30, 2025 and September 30, 2025, respectively, within three months of the securities being transferred to the watch list.

On September 7, the bourse added “Non-Submission of Annual Report for the year ended March 31, 2026” as a further reason for the shares to remain on the list.

The stock has not traded in either the June 2026 or June 2025 quarters. The float adjusted market capitalisation of Rs.242.6 million disclosed for end-June implies a price of Rs.0.90 across the 21.04 percent public holding, against the Rs.12 at which shares were issued at the initial public offering.
