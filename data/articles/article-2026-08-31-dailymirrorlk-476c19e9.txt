Colombo Port breaks into global top 20 amid Middle East shipping shake-up

The Port of Colombo has climbed to 20th place in the world’s busiest container port rankings at the half-year mark of 2026, up from 26th at the end of last year, according to Alphaliner’s mid-2026 top-30 global container port table.

The rise coincides with an 11.9 percent jump in the port’s own first-half throughput. Colombo handled 4,444,034 TEUs between January and June 2026, up from 3,972,053 TEUs in the same period last year, Central Bank data show.

Transhipment, which continues to dominate the port’s business, accounted for 84.7 percent of total container volumes and grew 12.6 percent year-on-year, while domestic container traffic rose 8.3 percent. Among the port’s terminals, Colombo International Container Terminals handled 1,642,647 TEUs, or 37 percent of the total.

Vessel traffic also strengthened. A total of 2,066 vessels called at the port in the first half, up 2.5 percent from 2,016 a year earlier, with break-bulk arrivals up 55.6 percent and calls for bunkering, ship repair and other value-added maritime services up 24.4 percent.

Colombo’s growth rate outpaces nearly every port ranked above it in the Alphaliner table. Only India’s Nhava Sheva, which grew 13.6 percent and climbed from 28th to 21st over the same period, matched the pace among Colombo’s immediate neighbours in the rankings. The gains were not shared evenly across the region: Mundra, also in India, slipped from 24th to 26th despite modest growth of its own.

Part of the reshuffle reflects turmoil far from South Asia. Jebel Ali, the Middle East’s largest container port, fell from 10th to 32nd after the Strait of Hormuz disruption cut second-quarter volumes at the Dubai hub by more than 90 percent, while Abu Dhabi’s Khalifa Seaport dropped out of the top 50 entirely. Both exits opened space in the rankings for ports below them.

Sri Lanka Ports Authority Chairman Dr. Parakrama Dissanayake said the sustained growth reflected shipping lines’ continued confidence in the port, adding that “our focus remains on delivering world-class port services with greater efficiency, reliability, and sustainability.”

John Keells Holdings, whose consortium with India’s Adani Ports operates the Colombo West International Terminal, said the terminal reached full capacity utilisation within its first year of operation.

Whether the climb continues into the world’s top 15, as the port has said it is working towards, is less certain on current numbers. Colombo’s FY2025 volume of 8.3 million TEUs trails 15th-placed Xiamen’s 12.5 million by more than four million TEUs, and even the nearest target, 19th-placed Ho Chi Minh City, holds a lead of roughly 600,000 TEUs. Projected forward at its current growth rate, Colombo would end 2026 near 9.3 million TEUs, enough to threaten 19th place, but short of the ground needed to break into the top 15 by year-end.
