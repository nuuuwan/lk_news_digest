China flies 19-seat electric passenger plane for first time

Commercial Aircraft Corporation of China (COMAC) has flown its 19-seat CE-25A electric passenger aircraft with a crew onboard for the first time, bringing battery-powered aviation closer to regional passenger travel.

The test flight took place on 14 November, 2026 in Binzhou, Shandong Province, with two pilots and a flight-test engineer onboard. COMAC said the aircraft performed as expected and met the required targets on all key performance indicators.

The CE-25A is COMAC’s first electric commercial aircraft. It was developed with battery manufacturer CATL, Shanghai Jiao Tong University and other partners for short-distance commercial routes between cities. The aircraft has a maximum takeoff weight of 10 tonnes, a cruising speed of 300 km per hour and a range of 400 km. It cruises at an altitude of approximately 2,400 metres.

The piloted flight follows the aircraft’s first autonomous flight, without a crew, on 2 February, 2026. Since then it has completed 19 unmanned flight validations before moving on to piloted operations. The project has also entered certification. The Civil Aviation Administration of China has accepted COMAC’s application for a type certificate, which is the regulator’s approval of an aircraft’s design. The aircraft, however, is yet to be cleared for commercial operations.
