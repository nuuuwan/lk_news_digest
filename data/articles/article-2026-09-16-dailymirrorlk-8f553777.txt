30,000 families at risk of landslides after cyclone Ditwah

Colombo, Sep. 16 (Daily Mirror) - More than 30,000 families have been identified as being at risk of landslides following Cyclone Ditwah with the National Building Research Institute (NBRO) warning that even areas previously considered safe could now be vulnerable.

NBRO Senior Scientist Dr. Wasantha Senadheera said preliminary signs of landslides had already emerged in several areas affected by the cyclone amid continued heavy rainfall.

He said areas where soil and rocks had already shifted, even without an actual landslide occurring, could face renewed landslide activity if heavy rains continue.

According to Dr. Senadheera, 16,793 high-risk families have been identified following the Ditva-related situation, in addition to around 15,000 families identified as being at high risk over the past 15 years. Of those previously identified, around 5,000 families are currently being resettled.

He warned that the number could rise further as monitoring operations are still underway.

“After the cyclone, even places we considered safe have become unsafe,” Dr. Senadheera said, urging people living in landslide-prone areas to move to safer locations immediately when heavy rains occur.

He also cautioned the public against approaching landslide-prone areas to watch or record landslides, stressing that doing so could put lives at risk.

Dr. Senadheera further urged the public to follow official warnings issued by Government institutions and avoid acting on unverified information circulating on social media.
