Death row prisoner saved, police exposed

Colombo, Nov. 17 (Daily Mirror) - The Court of Appeal has set aside the death sentence imposed on a man convicted of possessing and trafficking 236.3 grammes of heroin, holding that serious lapses in the police investigation had rendered the conviction unsafe and observing that the raid conducted by the officer-in-charge of the Kandy Police Narcotics Division appeared to have been carried out “for mockery”, ultimately leading to the suspect’s acquittal.

A two-judge Bench comprising Justices Amal Ranaraja and Dr. Sumudu Premachandra, allowing the appeal, held that the prosecution had failed to establish the essential elements of the case beyond reasonable doubt.

The accused, Wasantha Wijaya Kumara Yatawara, alias Bunga, had been convicted by the Kandy High Court on two counts relating to the possession and trafficking of 236.3 grammes of heroin following a raid conducted near a leading school in Kandy, on May 5, 2021. He was sentenced to death on September 9, 2024.

In its judgement, the High Court highlighted serious shortcomings in the conduct of the raid, particularly the failure of the principal investigating officer, Sub-Inspector H. H. Madushan, to maintain contemporaneous records of the operation.

The officer, who had reportedly conducted around 400 drug-related raids, admitted that he had not taken his Police Pocket Notebook (Sakku Satahan Potha) when leaving for the operation and had failed to record several crucial events, including the handling and custody of the seized heroin.

The Court observed that maintaining contemporaneous notes was a fundamental requirement of police duties and that the failure to record important events raised serious doubts about whether the raid had actually taken place in the manner described by the prosecution.

The Court also identified significant contradictions between the evidence of the two principal police witnesses regarding the circumstances of the arrest and the search of the suspect.

The Court questioned why the seized heroin had not been weighed and sealed immediately at the scene, despite the police team having the necessary sealing equipment.

The Court held that the unexplained delay, coupled with the failure to maintain proper records and other investigative deficiencies, created serious doubts regarding the integrity of the production and the prosecution's version of events.
