Liquidator files preliminary report on Daya Group’s Bimputh; seeks directors’ statements of affairs

Bimputh Lanka Investments PLC, formerly Bimputh Finance and part of former Minister Daya Gamage’s Daya Group, has disclosed that its liquidator has filed a preliminary report on the winding up and called for statements of affairs from its directors, with the liabilities exceeding the assets by close to Rs.2.2 billion, according to a stock filing.

G.K. Sudath Kumar, who was appointed as liquidator on June 26, 2025, “has since called for statements of affairs from the company’s directors and has filed his preliminary report on the conduct of the liquidation”, the filing noted.  The company disclosed the appointment to the market only this week, some 15 months after it took effect.

Bimputh’s appeal against the Commercial High Court’s winding-up order of February 14, 2025, remains pending before the Supreme Court.

HDFC Bank had filed the petition after Bimputh defaulted on the loans exceeding Rs.350 million.

As at December 31, 2024, the liabilities of Rs.4.64 billion exceeded the assets of Rs.2.46 billion, leaving the shareholders’ funds at minus Rs.2.18 billion. This deficit was nearly 16 times the Rs.136.7 million recorded in March 2022.

The accumulated losses more than tripled over the same period to Rs.2.92 billion, a figure equivalent to 4.7 times the company’s stated capital. According to its 2021/22 annual report, Bimputh’s capital adequacy ratio stood at 7.5 percent, against the regulatory minimum of 10.5 percent, while a promised Rs.1.5 billion capital infusion never materialised.

The Central Bank froze deposit-taking and lending and then cancelled the licence, with effect from September 01, 2023, citing deficient capital, poor asset quality and continuous losses.

The external auditor qualified the 2021/22 accounts, stating that it could not obtain sufficient evidence on the recoverability of Rs.2.1 billion in loans and advances.  Meanwhile, the same report disclosed that Bimputh had bought property worth Rs.450 million from its affiliate Olympus Construction (Pvt.) Ltd. Of this amount, Rs.270.9 million was paid in cash at a time when the company was already short of capital.

Daya Group (Pvt.) Ltd, Olympus Construction and Daya Apparel Export (Pvt.) Ltd together held 48.2 percent of Bimputh as at December 31, 2024.

Gamage, who rejoined the board in March 2024 and his wife Anoma Gamage held 12.15 percent each. C.K. Gamage and P.M.K. Gamage held a combined 10.81 percent, taking the stake held by the group and family to 83.3 percent. The remaining 16.67 percent is held by 1,205 public shareholders, whose shares have been suspended from trading since January 2022 and who will rank last for any recovery.

The Central Bank noted that the company had repaid a major portion of its deposits during 2022. The lenders, however, face a larger exposure. They include Sampath Bank, NDB, People’s Bank and Bank of Ceylon and most of their facilities were secured on a loan book that has since shrunk 72 percent to Rs.580.5 million.

The company stated that it “will make further announcements to the market as and when there are material developments in this matter”.
