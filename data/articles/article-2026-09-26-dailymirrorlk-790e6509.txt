The pros and cons of mandatory seat belt law

It is common knowledge that the driver and the passenger or passengers sitting in the front of a vehicle should wear  seat belts. This is because they are vulnerable to be thrown towards the windscreen due to an unexpected slamming on the footbrakes to avoid a head on collision. In such instances,  the air bags too are supposed to work automatically. The passengers sitting on rear seats get thrown towards the back which is not as dangerous as most seats are cushioned. There is no urgent need of belts for passengers at the back. They must be free to get out in an emergency.

Instead of saving the lives of the passengers they are made vulnerable to be exposed to danger when kept tied to seats with belts. They are unable to jump out in difficult circumstance such as a vehicle rolling over, catching fire, rolling down a precipice, skidding into a river/canal or reservoir,  a tree falling on the vehicle body or caught up in a whirlwind.

It is up to the authorities to reconsider the futility of enforcing this law for the back seat passengers in buses. The previous position where the front seat rider/s and the driver requiring to wear the belt is fair enough. Why is this rush to implement this law when the government has to address other burning issues of the day?
