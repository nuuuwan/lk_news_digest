The Indian Defence Minister’s visit -- what happened  in-between

What is working for the regime is its anti-corruption campaign

On  September 8, Indian Defence Minister Ranjith Singh arrived in Colombo. It was the first visit of an Indian Defence Minister to our country in 38 years. It is also the first visit of an Indian Defence Minister to Sri Lanka since the controversial deployment of the Indian Peace Keeping Force (IPKF) during the late 1980s.

The Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna (JVP) at that time vociferously decried the deployment of Indian troops here and what it referred to as Indian interference in Sri Lanka’s internal problems. It accused the then government of traitorously selling the country to India.

The JVP took up arms against the then government and even went so far as to call for the death of then president. Posters with the slogan “JR Maramu” (‘Let’s Kill JR’) were plastered countrywide. Our current President Anura Kumara Dissanayake was probably an active member of the JVP at that time.

This time around, President Dissanayake hosted the Indian Defence Minister and signed three key Memoranda of Understanding (MoUs) on strengthening defence co-operation between our two countries.  However,  the full text and legal clauses of the documents have not been published in full detail.

According to EBSCO (a research organisation), an estimated 40,000 to 60,000 people were killed or went missing during the second JVP insurrection, which peaked fiercely in 1989. An exact tally however is very difficult to  get; but human rights organisations generally agree on these figures.

Around   550 Sri Lanka Transport Board (SLTB) buses and 15 bus depots were destroyed. Two hundred and ten government offices and equipment were damaged. Twelve trains and eight railway stations were set alight. Seventy-five tea factories and 40 estate bungalows were also burnt and damaged,  and so were 30Ceylon Electricity Board (CEB) transformers. Central Bank[L1]  of Sri Lanka 1989 Annual Report notes that the total revenue and asset losses incurred by the regional transport boards due to civil disturbances during this  period amounted to approximately Rs. 400 million.

It seems the wheel has turned full circle. The JVP/NPP government  today apparently  understands India has no ulterior designs on little Lanka. Big brother India is really our best friend. How sad… so many thousands of young men and woman died in vain,  and so much material damage was caused to our country.

Since coming into power,  the present government has also made a ‘few’ mistakes. Before being elected into power at the presidential and parliamentary elections, the NPP/JVP coalition promised to bring down the cost of living. Unfortunately,  they have not been able to live up to expectations.

According to the Department of Census and Statistics July 2026 report, between 20% to 25% of the population remains affected by poverty. Since January 2026 the national minimum wage stands at Rs. 30,000/- per month. But a family of four (father mother and two children) needs  a minimum over Rs. 100,000/- to have two square meals a day!

Even if both parents worked, their take home wage will not be able to cover the cost of meals alone. Not surprisingly,  More than 20% (nearly one in five) of schoolchildren in Sri Lanka suffer from malnutrition and there is no indication wages will increase. In fact wages of employees have not been paid by a number of employers.

However the parliamentary Opposition has not been able to capitalise on suffering of the people. Even today,  while malnutrition stalks the land and wages are not keeping pace with the rising cost of living, parties in opposition are taking up the issue of the retirement age of judges in the country!

What is working for the regime is its anti-corruption campaign. A large number of corrupt politicians ranging from past presidents to low ranking government servants have been taken into custody on corruption charges. Even as this column is being written, a past president of the country is appearing before the Bribery Commission.

However,  this situation could change overnight if the government does not take meaningful steps to control the cost of living. The signs of things to come are plainly to be seen. Groups of workers are threatening strike action. The overnment needs to take corrective action urgently.
