NATO ties: A gain with one, a loss  with others

NATO was formed in 1949 with the signing of the Washington Treaty.

Sri Lanka has sought to deepen defence cooperation with NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organisation), with discussions focusing on military modernisation, advanced technology, cyber capabilities and disaster response.

Secretary to the Ministry of Defence, Air Vice Marshal Sampath Thuyacontha (Retd), held bilateral talks with NATO Military Committee Chairman Admiral Giuseppe Cavo Dragone on the sidelines of the Chiefs of Defence Conference in Victoria, Canada, according to a press release from the Ministry.

Sri Lanka’s decision has sparked a debate in the country. Many an eyebrow has been raised as to why Sri Lanka, given its neutral posture, sought to deepen ties with NATO.

NATO was formed in 1949 with the signing of the Washington Treaty. It is a security alliance of 32 countries from North America and Europe, with safeguarding the Allies’ freedom and security through political and military means being its fundamental goal.

Article 5 of the treaty says: “The Parties agree that an armed attack against one or more of them in Europe or North America shall be considered an attack against them all...” It provides for collective defence, under which an armed attack against one NATO member is considered an attack against all.

During the meeting, Secretary Thuyacontha, according to the Ministry and reports published in the media, outlined Sri Lanka’s defence requirements and areas of interest, including the modernisation of military hardware, acquisition of advanced technologies, and strengthening cyber capabilities and surveillance platforms, the Ministry of Defence said in a statement.

The two sides also discussed opportunities for greater technical cooperation, including the exchange of military personnel, expanded education and training programmes, and increased sharing of expertise and information.

Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief (HADR) was also identified as an area for potential cooperation. The discussions included the exchange of expertise and the effective use of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in disaster response and related operations.

Defence Secretary Thuyacontha invited NATO to further strengthen its engagement with Sri Lanka, emphasising sustained dialogue, information sharing and practical cooperation in areas of mutual interest. Modernisation of security capabilities and keeping abreast of the latest advancements in military technology to be used in modern warfare are necessities for Sri Lanka as a country with a strategic position in the Indian Ocean.

However, in the execution of its foreign policy related to defence matters, multiple factors have to be taken into account. First and foremost, any decision should not be an affront to the trademark of the country’s foreign policy — non-alignment.

Sri Lanka matters greatly to India as far as its security is concerned. With India’s northern borders being volatile due to tensions with Pakistan and China, Sri Lanka matters even more from a security perspective as its closest maritime neighbour. One wonders whether the government considered how ties with NATO will have a bearing on security relations with India.

NATO was established in the early years of the Cold War in response to the perceived threat from the Soviet Union. Its subsequent expansion and its role in European security have made it an important actor in the current geopolitical tensions involving Russia and the West. Ukraine’s aspiration for NATO membership has been one of the issues cited in the broader dispute between Russia and the West.

Sri Lanka is a country that has maintained friendly ties with Russia over the decades. Russian support was crucial in defeating the separatist LTTE. NATO’s increased involvement in Sri Lanka could, therefore, have implications for Sri Lanka’s relations with Russia. Likewise, other friendly countries such as China may not be in favour of Sri Lanka getting close to NATO at a volatile time like this. It is all right for Sri Lanka to enhance defence ties with individual member states of NATO, such as the United States, France and Germany, which have genuine interests in the region.

Yet, it is questionable why the government sought engagement with NATO  despite its implications for the country. No one can belittle the importance of the Western world for Sri Lanka. Military modernisation is a legitimate duty. Yet, it has to be done by taking all aspects into account and upholding a foreign policy grounded in practical realities.
