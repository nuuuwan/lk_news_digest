July tea exports slide 15% as key categories weaken

Sri Lanka’s tea export volumes fell sharply in July 2026, extending the decline seen so far this year, even as rupee-denominated export prices improved from a year earlier.

Tea exports declined 15.1 percent year-on-year to 20.41 million kilograms in July, from 24.04 million kilograms a year earlier, a reduction of 3.63 million kilograms, according to data compiled by Forbes & Walker Research based on Sri Lanka Customs figures.

The weakness was broad-based, with all major export categories other than instant tea recording lower volumes during the month.

Packeted tea, the largest export category in July, fell to 8.92 million kilograms from 10.77 million kilograms a year earlier, while bulk tea shipments declined to 8.59 million kilograms from 10.16 million kilograms.

Tea bag exports eased to 2.16 million kilograms from 2.24 million kilograms, while green tea exports dropped to 0.39 million kilograms from 0.56 million kilograms. Instant tea was the only category to buck the trend, rising to 0.35 million kilograms from 0.30 million kilograms.

Despite the volume contraction, the average FOB value increased to Rs. 1,921.95 per kilogram in July from Rs. 1,766.51 a year earlier, an increase of Rs. 155.44.

In US dollar terms, however, the average FOB value slipped to US$5.72 per kilogram from US$5.87, highlighting the impact of currency movements on the rupee-denominated improvement. Green tea was the only category to record an increase in its dollar FOB value during the month.

The July decline also pulled down the year-to-date performance, with tea exports during the first seven months of 2026 falling 4.9 percent to 143.51 million kilograms from 150.85 million kilograms in the corresponding period of 2025.

Packeted tea accounted for much of the decline, with shipments falling by 4.69 million kilograms to 63.89 million kilograms. Bulk tea exports dropped by 2.73 million kilograms to 59.92 million kilograms.

Tea bag and green tea exports also declined, while instant tea exports increased by 479,020 kilograms to 2.43 million kilograms.

The cumulative average FOB value rose to Rs. 1,826.16 per kilogram during January-July from Rs. 1,748.51 a year earlier. In dollar terms, however, the average fell to US$5.70 from US$5.86.

All export categories except instant tea recorded higher FOB values in rupee terms during the seven-month period. In dollar terms, green tea was the only segment to post an increase.

Türkiye remained Sri Lanka’s largest tea export destination, with shipments reaching 24.76 million kilograms, up 138 percent year-on-year from 10.40 million kilograms.

Iraq retained second place despite imports falling 36 percent to 14.42 million kilograms, while Russia ranked third with 13.35 million kilograms, up 2.6 percent. Azerbaijan recorded a 49 percent increase to 7.14 million kilograms, placing it fourth. China followed with 5.59 million kilograms, down 8 percent, while exports to Libya plunged 58 percent to 5.41 million kilograms.

Jordan, Chile, Saudi Arabia and Syria completed the top 10 export destinations.
