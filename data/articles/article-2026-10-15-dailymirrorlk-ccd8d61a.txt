Commercial High Court upholds travel restrictions in Hela winding-up proceedings

The Commercial High Court has rejected a challenge by the directors of Hela Clothing (Private) Limited to its authority to restrict their overseas travel in the winding-up proceedings, while allowing travel for specified periods with prior court approval. Commercial High Court Judge Chamath Madanayake held that the court had jurisdiction to impose such restrictions where necessary, to conduct the statutory inquiries, under the Companies Act and ensure the effective administration of justice. The proceedings involve Hela Apparel Holdings PLC, Hela Clothing (Private) Limited and Foundation Garments (Private) Limited.

The directors of Hela Clothing had argued that overseas travel restrictions could be imposed only by a court exercising criminal jurisdiction or under the Immigration and Emigration Act. The court rejected that argument, citing its powers under the statutory framework governing the winding-up proceedings. It observed that the powers to require the directors to attend inquiries and produce company records could be ineffective without the measures to ensure their availability within the jurisdiction. The court also varied an earlier order to allow the directors to travel overseas for specified periods, subject to prior approval and conditions imposed by the court.
