Preventing stroke: The role of physiotherapy in building a healthier Sri Lanka

Colombo, Sep. 8 (Daily Mirror) - Every year on 8 September, the global physiotherapy community celebrates World Physiotherapy Day, recognizing the contribution of physiotherapists to health, wellbeing and quality of life. In 2026, the theme focuses on the role of physiotherapy and physical activity in the prevention, management and rehabilitation of cardiovascular disease and stroke.

This year's message is particularly relevant to Sri Lanka, where stroke continues to place a considerable burden on individuals, families and the healthcare system. According to the World Health Organization, Sri Lanka records approximately 60,000 stroke admissions to state hospitals each year, with around 4,000 hospital deaths. Major risk factors include high blood pressure, physical inactivity, unhealthy diet, tobacco use, alcohol consumption, diabetes, obesity and raised blood lipids.

Stroke is often perceived as an unpredictable medical emergency. However, a large proportion of strokes are preventable by addressing modifiable risk factors. The World Stroke Organization estimates that up to 90% of strokes are linked to modifiable risk factors, including hypertension, physical inactivity, unhealthy diet, smoking, alcohol consumption and obesity.

This places prevention at the centre of the fight against stroke.

Regular physical activity is one of the simplest and most accessible ways to improve health. The World Stroke Organization states that 30 minutes of exercise five times a week can reduce stroke risk by around 25%, while regular activity also helps manage blood pressure, diabetes, cholesterol, stress and body weight.

For Sri Lankans, being physically active does not necessarily mean going to a gym. Walking, cycling, gardening, household activities, taking the stairs and reducing prolonged sitting can all contribute to a more active lifestyle.

Physiotherapy is sometimes still misunderstood as simply massage, exercise or the use of treatment machines. Physiotherapy is a professional healthcare discipline requiring university-level education, clinical training and specialized knowledge of human movement, physical function, exercise and rehabilitation.

Physiotherapists can contribute to health promotion and disease prevention by encouraging physical activity, assessing physical function, designing safe and appropriate exercise programmes, and helping individuals manage conditions that may increase their risk of cardiovascular disease and stroke.

This is particularly important for people who are physically inactive, older adults and those living with long-term health conditions. Physiotherapists can work with individuals to make physical activity safe, appropriate and achievable according to their health status and functional ability. World Physiotherapy specifically identifies physiotherapists as professionals who can support people to become more physically active and manage long-term health conditions that contribute to cardiovascular and stroke risk.

When stroke occurs, physiotherapy becomes an important part of rehabilitation. Stroke can affect walking, balance, strength, coordination, mobility and the ability to perform everyday activities.

A person recovering from stroke requires a multidisciplinary team involving doctors, nurses, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech and language therapists, dietitians, psychologists and other healthcare professionals.

Each professional contributes a different area of expertise, while the patient remains at the centre of care.

Physiotherapy can help stroke survivors improve mobility, balance, strength, physical function and independence. Exercise-based physiotherapy can also contribute to improved quality of life throughout the recovery journey, from hospital care to longer￾term community rehabilitation.

World Physiotherapy Day 2026 should therefore be more than a celebration of a profession. It should be an opportunity to create greater public awareness about stroke prevention, physical activity and the role of physiotherapy in healthcare.

Every Sri Lankan can take simple steps: know and control your blood pressure, maintain a healthy diet, remain physically active, avoid tobacco, limit excessive alcohol consumption, manage diabetes and cholesterol, and seek appropriate professional advice when needed.

Most importantly, we should recognize that prevention begins long before a person enters a hospital.

Physiotherapy has a valuable role to play, not only after illness or disability occurs, but also in promoting movement, preventing functional decline and supporting healthier lifestyles.

As Sri Lanka continues to address the growing burden of non-communicable diseases, strengthening multidisciplinary healthcare and improving public understanding of physiotherapy can contribute to a healthier and more independent population.

Move more. Know your risks. Prevent what can be prevented. Act quickly when stroke occurs. And work together for a healthier Sri Lanka.
