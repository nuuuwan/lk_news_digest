Two remanded over alleged multi-million rupee Customs fraud involving luxury vehicles

Colombo, August 4 (Daily Mirror) - Two suspects, including the son of the owner of a well-known gate manufacturing and motor vehicle importing company, were ordered to be remanded till August 5 by Colombo Additional Magistrate Oshadha Migara Maharachchi over allegations of evading taxes amounting to millions of rupees through the import of luxury vehicles and raw materials for the construction sector.

The suspects, Achini Tharaka Mihirani Dias, an employee of the company and Athila Ravinaka Weeraratne, were remanded.

The order was made after the Magistrate considered submissions made by officials of the Sri Lanka Customs Department, who appeared before court on behalf of the investigation.

Customs officials informed court that an investigation was being conducted into allegations that goods had been imported by declaring lower values, causing a loss of revenue to the government.

The officials told court that investigations had revealed that a Mercedes-Benz vehicle had been imported by declaring an undervalued price, resulting in a loss of Rs. 4.1 million in government taxes.

They further stated that an examination of invoices belonging to the company had revealed information relating to the import of goods valued at USD 49,815.75 through an invoice, the issuing of letters of credit amounting to USD 19,926.30 and the import of goods valued at USD 29,889.45.

The Customs officials also requested the court to impose overseas travel bans on three directors of the company in connection with the investigation.

Appearing for the suspect, President’s Counsel Nalinda Indatissa informed court that the investigating officers had failed to submit a proper summary of evidence against the suspects.

He argued that under Section 127A of the Customs Ordinance, the decision on whether a suspect should be further remanded was a matter within the discretion of the Magistrate, and that continued remanding was not necessary as claimed by the investigating officers.

The President’s Counsel further submitted that according to previous Supreme Court decisions, proper evidence must be presented before court, and that no such summary of evidence had been submitted to court in relation to the ongoing investigation.

Meanwhile, the investigating officers informed court that further suspects were expected to be arrested in connection with the incident.

They opposed bail, stating that if the suspects were released, there was a possibility of evidence being destroyed or interfered with.

After considering submissions from both parties, the Magistrate ordered that the suspects be remanded till August 5 and directed the investigators to submit a progress report on the investigation on that date.
