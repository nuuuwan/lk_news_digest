FBI team to visit Sri Lanka to assist probe into online scam centers

Colombo, Nov. 16 (Daily Mirror) - A team from the Federal Bureau of Investigation’s (FBI) Charlotte Field Office will visit Sri Lanka this week to work alongside Sri Lankan law enforcement officials investigating scam centers operating in the country, the US Embassy in Colombo said.

The US Embassy said the United States is strengthening cooperation with Sri Lankan law enforcement to combat cyber-enabled fraud and disrupt transnational criminal networks.

"At the request of Sri Lankan authorities, a team from the Federal Bureau of Investigation’s (FBI) Charlotte Field Office will visit Colombo November 16–22 to work alongside Sri Lankan law enforcement officials investigating scam centers operating in the country," it said.

The FBI has established a dedicated team to support ongoing investigations, provide specialized technical expertise, and strengthen the ability of U.S. and Sri Lankan authorities to identify and disrupt transnational fraud networks.

During the visit, FBI personnel will work with Sri Lankan law enforcement partners on investigative techniques, digital forensics, and case development. This cooperation will help investigators identify victims, trace criminal activity, and build stronger cases against those responsible for online scams. It will also strengthen Sri Lanka’s ability to prevent transnational criminal networks from exploiting its territory to carry out cyber-enabled criminal operations.

“Criminal networks operating overseas are stealing billions of dollars and we are committed to stopping them,” said U.S. Ambassador to Sri Lanka Eric Meyer. “By bringing FBI investigative expertise and advanced technology together with Sri Lankan law enforcement, we can identify victims, pursue criminals, and prevent more Americans and Sri Lankans from falling prey to these schemes.”

The visit marks the beginning of sustained FBI engagement with Sri Lankan authorities to strengthen investigative capabilities and combat scam center operations. The initiative is supported by the U.S. Department of State, working in coordination with the FBI.

"The United States remains committed to pursuing transnational criminal networks wherever they operate, protecting American citizens from financial exploitation, and working with international law enforcement partners to hold perpetrators accountable," the Embassy said.
