Bank lending to real estate tops Rs.2tn as bad loans retreat

The banking sector’s lending to real estate grew 16.9 percent to Rs.2,075.7 billion in the 12 months to end-June 2026, while the nonperforming loans in the segment contracted 13.4 percent, pointing to the improving credit quality in an area where the bad loans still run above the industry average, the latest Central Bank data showed.

The loans extended to the real estate sector increased by Rs.300.2 billion, from Rs.1,775.5 billion a year earlier, with the growth accelerating from 11.3 percent at end-March, when the portfolio stood at Rs.1,981.4 billion.

The banks added Rs.94.3 billion in real estate loans during the June quarter alone, a period in which the Central Bank raised the Overnight Policy Rate by 100 basis points to 8.75 percent in late May. The market lending rates have since adjusted, with the weekly prime lending rate rising by 136 basis points.

However, the pace of real estate lending trailed the 23.6 percent expansion in overall bank credit, leaving the segment with a smaller share of the banking sector’s loan book, at under 14 percent, when compared to the same period last year.

The stage three loans pertaining to real estate fell to Rs.215.9 billion, from Rs.249.2 billion, a decline more than twice as fast as the 5.8 percent drop in the banking sector’s overall stage three loans.

The segment’s stage three ratio consequently improved to 10.4 percent, from 14 percent a year ago and from 11.3 percent at end-March, as per the Central Bank data.

Despite the improvement, the ratio remains above 9.1 percent recorded for the banking sector as a whole, with real estate accounting for over 15 percent of the industry’s stage three loans.

Meanwhile, the condominium sales volumes in Colombo and other major cities fell 6.7 percent in the June quarter when compared to the same period last year, easing from the 15.2 percent decline in the March quarter, as per the Central Bank’s condominium market survey.

The year-on-year increase in the prices of new condominiums in the Colombo district slowed to 3.2 percent, from 18.5 percent in the March quarter, although the asking prices for land, houses and condominiums in the district were still up 15.2 percent, 9 percent and 12.6 percent, respectively.

The bank borrowings remained among the main sources of funding for the condominium developments, alongside the pre-sale deposits and equity, whereas the buyers, who were predominantly Sri Lankan residents purchasing for immediate occupancy, relied mainly on their own funds.

About 55 percent of the units in the ongoing projects were reserved by the end of the quarter. (NF)
