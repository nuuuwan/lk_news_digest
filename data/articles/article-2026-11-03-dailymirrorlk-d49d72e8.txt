Supermarkets collecting polythene bag levies without environmental benefits, SC told

Colombo, Nov. 3 (Daily Mirror) - The Centre for Environmental Justice today moved the Supreme Court seeking an order directing the authorities to formulate a mechanism to establish a government-administered levy on single-use plastic and polythene bags, in a bid to curb increasing environmental pollution.

Senior Counsel Ravindranath Dabare, appearing for the petitioners, told Court that the levies imposed on polythene bags by supermarkets are being directly credited to their respective accounts, without serving the intended purpose of protecting the environment.

Meanwhile, the Attorney General informed Court that the respondents are in the process of formulating a mechanism to establish a government-administered levy on single-use plastic and polythene bags.

A three-judge bench of the Supreme Court, comprising Chief Justice Preethi Padman Surasena, Justices Achala Wengappuli and Gihan Kulatunga, directed the respondents to submit a report on the progress made in formulating the proposed mechanism on March 5 next year.

The petitioners are seeking an order directing the relevant authorities to immediately establish and implement a government-administered levy on single-use plastic and polythene bags. They further requested that the revenue collected through such a levy be credited to a designated public fund exclusively dedicated to environmental protection, waste management, recycling initiatives and the implementation of the internationally recognised “polluter pays” principle.
