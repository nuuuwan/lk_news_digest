Dialog sells 20% of Doc990 operator for Rs.374.9mn

Dialog Axiata PLC has sold a 20 percent stake in Digital Health (Private) Limited, the operator of the Doc990 platform, for Rs.374.9 million, bringing in Sunshine Healthcare Lanka Limited as a strategic investor and reducing the digital healthcare business from a subsidiary to an associate.

The transaction reduced the Dialog group’s ownership in Digital Health to 44.66 percent, from 64.66 percent, according to a stock exchange filing yesterday.

Dialog’s wholly-owned subsidiary Digital Holdings Lanka (Private) Limited sold approximately 16.65 percent of Digital Health to Sunshine Healthcare Lanka, a subsidiary of Sunshine Holdings PLC, for Rs.312.17 million. It sold a further 3.35 percent to Nawaloka Hospitals PLC for Rs.62.72 million, after the hospital operator exercised its right of first refusal, under the existing shareholders’ agreement.

Sunshine Healthcare also acquired Ceylon Hospitals PLC’s entire 5.26 percent holding in Digital Health, taking its total stake to 21.92 percent. Ceylon Hospitals, which operates Durdans Hospital, exited through the exercise of its tag-along rights. The filing did not disclose the consideration for that sale.

“The transaction enables the company to partially monetise its investment in Digital Health while introducing Sunshine Health as a strategic healthcare partner,” Dialog said. The company said the partnership was expected to support Doc990’s continued growth by combining the platform’s digital healthcare capabilities with Sunshine Healthcare’s presence across pharmaceuticals, healthcare distribution, manufacturing and retail.

Following the transaction, Digital Holdings Lanka retains 27.57 percent of Digital Health, while Dialog Axiata Digital Innovation Fund (Private) Limited continues to hold 17.09 percent.

Nawaloka Hospitals increased its holding to 12.12 percent, from 8.77 percent. Asiri Hospital Holdings PLC’s stake remains at 13.33 percent, while Aartiz Technologies (Private) Limited retains 7.98 percent.

Dialog said Digital Health would cease to be a group subsidiary upon completion of the transaction, with its retained interest subsequently accounted for as an associate in the group’s consolidated financial statements.
