Sri Lanka ranks third globally for most public holidays

Colombo, Aug. 31 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka has the third-highest number of public holidays of any country in the world, according to a new analysis from the Pew Research Center, behind only Myanmar and Bangladesh.

The report, published February 12, found that Sri Lanka will observe 25 public holidays in 2026, well above the global median of 13 across the 190 UN member states surveyed. Myanmar topped the list with 30 holidays, followed by Bangladesh with 29. Cambodia, Iran and Lebanon also passed 20.

At the other end, Switzerland recognizes just one federal holiday, though its cantons observe at least eight each. Bosnia and Herzegovina and Uruguay were close behind, with four and five holidays respectively.

Pew researchers attributed Sri Lanka's high count mainly to its practice of declaring every full moon day, or "Poya," a public holiday. As a predominantly Buddhist nation with a calendar built around lunar cycles, Sri Lanka is grouped with Myanmar as one of two Buddhist-majority countries with many holidays tied to the lunar calendar, though Myanmar's total comes from a broader mix of Buddhist, ethnic and state holidays rather than the same every-full-moon rule. Countries with large Buddhist or Muslim populations, the researchers noted, tend to observe more public holidays overall.

The findings draw on data from Time and Date's global calendar database, checked against national labour codes, government notices and regional news reports.

Pew counted only nationally recognized, non-working public holidays, leaving out optional days, half-days and anything observed only at the state, provincial or local level.

A number of Sri Lanka's holidays are shared with much of the world rather than being unique to the island. Christmas and Good Friday fall on the same dates observed across the Christian world, while Eid ul-Fitr and Eid ul-Adha follow the Islamic calendar kept by Muslim-majority countries from Indonesia to Saudi Arabia

Deepavali coincides with the festival as celebrated in India, Nepal and other countries with large Hindu communities. Vesak, marking the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and passing, is also a public holiday in Myanmar, Thailand and Cambodia, though the exact date can shift by a day depending on which lunar calendar a country follows.

It is the Poya days that set Sri Lanka apart. Most Buddhist-majority countries mark Vesak, but few besides Sri Lanka treat every full moon as a nonworking holiday, which is the main reason its total climbs so far above what most other nations, Buddhist-majority or otherwise, observe.

Nine long weekends already on the 2027 calendar for Sri Lanka

Next year's calendar is already locked in, and it rewards planning ahead. The Public Administration, Provincial Councils and Local Government Ministry gazetted the 2027 schedule this month under the Holidays Act No. 29 of 1971, listing 25 public and bank holidays, matching Sri Lanka's count in the Pew analysis for 2026.

Nine of those holidays fall on a Friday or a Monday, stretching an ordinary weekend into three days off. It happens twice in January alone, on the 15th and the 22nd, and again in March, May, June, August, October and December.

The best-placed cluster comes in May. The Eid ul-Adha (Hajj Festival) lands on a Monday and Vesak spills into Wednesday and Thursday, leaving just two working days in the whole third week of the month.

Not every holiday works in the public's favour. Six others, including May Day and Christmas, fall on a Saturday or Sunday that was already free, adding nothing extra to the calendar unless the government later announces a day off in lieu.
