Sri Lanka’s fish harvest in steady decline since 2014

Colombo, August 22 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka’s fish harvest has been steadily declining since 2014, raising concerns over the long-term sustainability of the country’s marine resources, the National Aquatic Resources Research and Development Institute (NARA) says.

NARA Chairman Dr. Sanath Hettiarachchi said the decline was not a sudden development, but part of a wider threat affecting marine ecosystems in Sri Lanka and many other countries.

He said no single cause had so far been identified for the reduction in fish harvest, although changes in the biological systems of the marine environment were considered a major factor.

Dr. Hettiarachchi also said it had not yet been established whether the El Niño phenomenon was contributing to the decline, adding that there was no confirmation of a direct impact on Sri Lankan waters.

He warned that if the situation continues over the long term, measures may have to be taken to help fishing communities shift towards alternative livelihoods.

A special programme has already been launched to promote fish cultivation as a possible solution, but its progress so far has not reached a satisfactory level, he said.

Meanwhile, National Fisheries Cooperation Movement Convener Harman Kumara attributed part of the decline to the failure to properly enforce laws against prohibited fishing nets and destructive fishing gear.

He alleged that the continued use of such practices was contributing significantly to the depletion of Sri Lanka’s fish resources.
