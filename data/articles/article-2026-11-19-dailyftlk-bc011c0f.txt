Sri Lanka’s young people most optimistic in world: Oxford Scholastica Academy study

A new global survey of young people has ranked youth optimism about the future across 100 countries, with students from 41 countries providing sufficient responses, measuring how hopeful students feel about their personal futures, the economy, the environment, politics, and the future of society as a whole.

The study by Oxford summer school provider, Oxford Scholastica Academy, received responses from 1,433 students to discover where Gen Z are the most and least optimistic about their future.

Sri Lankan students are the most optimistic in the world, scoring 4.32 out of 5 overall, while young people in Kuwait are the least hopeful, with an overall score of just 2.92.

Sri Lanka leads the study with young people reporting the highest rates of optimism, with the highest environmental optimism score of any country in the study (4.62) and strong personal optimism (4.75). Despite significant economic turbulence in recent years, Sri Lankan students remain deeply hopeful about the future of their society (4.25).

The second most optimistic country is Kenya, with students surveyed scoring 4.31 overall. Kenyan students marked feeling optimistic about the future of society (4.43, joint highest in the study alongside Cyprus) and record the highest political optimism of any country surveyed (3.96).

Nepal achieves a perfect personal optimism score of 5.00 – the only country to do so and ranked third overall. Nepal also has the highest economic optimism (4.80), suggesting that despite its lower-income status, students feel strongly positive about their individual prospects.

Kazakhstan is the fourth most optimistic nation according to the students polled, students report high economic confidence (4.56) along with the third-highest political optimism in the study (3.76).

Indonesia scores 4.07 while Nigeria scores 4.08, with Nigerian students scoring highly for environmental optimism (3.92) and Indonesian students reporting economic confidence (4.41).

Among Western nations, Canada ranks highest at sixth (4.08), comfortably ahead of the US (3.99), the UK (3.61), Germany (3.73), and France (3.22).

Oxford Scholastica Academy Co-Director Lavinia Abell said: “In every aspect of society, there are things to be anxious and pessimistic about. This could be the rise in Artificial Intelligence (AI), war, the cost-of-living increasing, and much more. This will be especially worrisome for young people, facing challenges as they enter the world of work. What this study highlights is the attitudes of each nationality towards these uncertainties. What is particularly striking is how low Italy’s political optimism scores are compared to other European nations. Even France and Poland, which also rank near the bottom, show slightly different patterns.”
