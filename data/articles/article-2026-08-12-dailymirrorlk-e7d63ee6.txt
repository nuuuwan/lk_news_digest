Court finds favouritism, illegality in Peradeniya lecturer selection

Colombo, Aug. 12 (Daily Mirror) - The Court of Appeal ordered the University of Peradeniya to appoint a Senior Lecturer Grade II in Buddhist Studies to the post of Senior Lecturer Grade I, after finding that the selection process had been tainted by illegality, procedural unfairness, favouritism and mala fides.

The petitioner Dr. Iromi Nisansala Manike Ariyaratne had filed a writ petition challenging the selection process for the post of Senior Lecturer Grade I/II in the Department of Pali and Buddhist Studies at the University of Peradeniya.

A two-member bench comprising Justices R. Gurusinghe and Dr. Sumudu Premachandra issued Writs of Certiorari and Mandamus quashing the decision not to appoint Dr. Iromi Nisansala Manike Ariyaratne to the Senior Lecturer Grade I position.

The Court directed the University to appoint Dr. Ariyaratne to the post with effect from September 22, 2022, the date on which she filed the writ application.

The Court also quashed the appointment of the 33rd respondent to the post of Senior Lecturer Grade II. However, it permitted the appointment of Ven. Dr. Divulapellesse Wimalananda, the 32nd respondent, to the Grade II post to remain in force, while directing that his appointment also be backdated to September 22, 2022.

Dr. Ariyaratne, a Senior Lecturer Grade II at the Postgraduate Institute of Pali and Buddhist Studies of the University of Kelaniya, had challenged the recruitment process conducted by the University of Peradeniya for Senior Lecturer Grade I and Grade II positions.

In considering the application, the Court observed that Dr. Ariyaratne had been the sole candidate shortlisted for the Grade I position and possessed specialised academic qualifications in Buddhist Studies.

The Court further took into consideration that she held a First-Class Honours degree and a Master's degree in Buddhist Studies and had more than 12 years of academic experience.

The Court, having examined the circumstances surrounding the selection process, concluded that the decision-making process was affected by illegality, procedural unfairness, favouritism and mala fides, warranting judicial intervention.

Sanjeeva Jayawardena, PC, with Ranmalee Meepagala appeared for the petitioner.
