Solar sector at risk, Chamber of Commerce tells president

Colombo, Sep. 25 (Daily Mirror) - The National Chamber of Commerce of Sri Lanka has urged President Anura Kumara Dissanayake to intervene urgently over policy concerns affecting the country’s rooftop solar energy sector, warning that the changes could undermine energy security and economic recovery.

In a letter titled “The Threat to Sri Lanka’s Rooftop Solar Energy Sector,” the Chamber said Sri Lanka could face an electricity shortfall of up to 450 MWh by the first half of 2027, citing forecasts and statements by the Public Utilities Commission of Sri Lanka and the National System Operator.

The Chamber said the rapid expansion of rooftop solar and battery energy storage systems could help address the anticipated shortage. According to the Chamber, the sector currently contributes around 2,600 MW of capacity to the national grid and helps save approximately Rs. 20.4 billion a month in imported fossil-fuel costs.

However, the Chamber warned that recent policy directives could threaten the continued growth of the sector. Industry representatives have raised concerns over changes to existing rooftop solar arrangements, including the reported restriction of consumers to the Net Plus scheme and the removal of Net Metering and Net Accounting under recent directives.

The Chamber has argued that policy uncertainty could affect investment in the renewable energy sector at a time when rooftop solar is becoming increasingly important to the national power supply. Earlier this year, industry groups also raised concerns that proposed electricity policy changes could weaken incentives for rooftop solar and make renewable energy projects more difficult to finance.

The Chamber has therefore called on President Dissanayake to address the policy concerns and safeguard the rooftop solar industry, stressing its contribution to electricity generation, fossil-fuel savings and Sri Lanka’s broader energy security.
