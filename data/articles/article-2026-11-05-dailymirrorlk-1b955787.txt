Bad food? Colombo residents can now complain with a QR scan

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - In a move to strengthen monitoring and accountability in the city’s food service sector, the Colombo Municipal Council (CMC) has introduced a QR code-based complaint system to enable the public to report food safety and hygiene violations at restaurants and food outlets.

The initiative, launched today (5) by the CMC’s Public Health Department, allows consumers to submit complaints directly to the relevant Medical Officers of Health (MOHs) and Public Health Inspectors (PHIs) for investigation and necessary action.

The system was initially introduced in Wellawatte and at the Majestic City food court, with plans to expand it across Colombo 1 to 15 in the coming stages.

Under the new mechanism, consumers can scan a QR code, complete a short online form and submit complaints without having to visit municipal offices or make a formal written submission.

The CMC has also enabled anonymous complaints, assuring that members of the public can report food safety concerns without disclosing their names or contact details.

Consumers lodging complaints regarding food they have purchased have been advised to retain both the food and the receipt, as these may be required by health officials during investigations. They have also been requested not to return the food to the restaurant concerned.

The public can submit complaints by scanning the QR code made available by the CMC or by contacting its Public Health Department on 011 267 6161 or via email at [email protected].

The CMC said the programme would be expanded to cover all areas from Colombo 1 to Colombo 15, reaffirming its commitment to ensuring access to clean and safe food for the public.
