ASG says medical report found no torture, Sallay’s counsel disputes prosecution’s interpretation of findings

Colombo, Nov. 26 (Daily Mirror) - While Additional Solicitor General Dileepa Peeris told the Fort Magistrate’s Court that a psychiatric report had not established that former SIS Director Suresh Sallay had been subjected to torture, Sallay’s Counsel Bhanuka Malawiarachchi disputed the prosecution's interpretation of the medical findings, saying the report had not reached a final conclusion and had instead recommended further investigations.

The submissions were made when the case against three suspects, including retired Major General Suresh Sallay, over allegedly misleading investigations into the 2019 Easter Sunday terror attacks was taken up before Fort Magistrate Pasan Amarasena yesterday.

Meanwhile, the Magistrate Amarasena directed the Criminal Investigation Department to expedite investigations into whether former President Gotabaya Rajapaksa should be named as a suspect in connection with the 2019 Easter Sunday terror attacks and report its findings to court.

The court also rejected a request made by former President Gotabaya Rajapaksa, through his lawyers, seeking the temporary lifting of the overseas travel ban imposed on him in connection with the case.

ASG Peeris, submitting a further report to court that the report, comprising 311 pages and 1,185 paragraphs, had not established that Sallay had been subjected to torture.

Referring to the medical report, the Additional Solicitor General stated,

"Your Honour, the psychiatric report prepared by the medical specialists concerning the third suspect, Suresh Sallay, has been received. It consists of 311 pages and 1,185 paragraphs. There is no finding in the report that the third suspect had been subjected to torture. Sallay himself has made 95 statements, while approximately 30 statements have been provided by his wife.

I believe this is one of the largest psychiatric reports presented in the judicial field. During the process of obtaining this report, the third suspect had even stated that the country's best forensic experts had been assigned to investigate his case.

The Additional Solicitor General further informed court that an informant had alerted the State Intelligence Service (SIS) about Zahran Hashim's activities approximately six months before the Easter Sunday attacks.

According to statements recorded from SIS officers by the CID, an intelligence officer identified as Waris had received information through an informant named Thambi Annam that Zahran Hashim was undergoing training in India with a group in October 2018 and was preparing to carry out an attack in 2019.

He had reportedly conveyed the information to senior SIS officials, following which it was referred to the desk responsible for monitoring extremist activities within the intelligence service.

ASG further stated that an intelligence file had been maintained in relation to the information and that, following the Easter Sunday attacks, Assistant Superintendent of Police Nirosha Gunawardena had handed the file over to Suresh Sallay. However, investigations had subsequently revealed that the file had gone missing.

Appearing for the third suspect, Suresh Sallay, Counsel Bhanuka Malawiarachchi disputed the prosecution's interpretation of the medical report.

He submitted that the medical reports had not reached a final conclusion regarding the allegations made by his client and had instead recommended further investigations.

"Your Honour, the medical reports state that officers of the Criminal Investigation Department had lowered my client's trousers and examined him. However, the medical panel has not reached a final conclusion regarding the incident and has recommended further investigations.

The panel has made four recommendations, with the fourth recommending further investigations into the indecent act allegedly committed during the period of detention.

The report also states that CCTV footage from the location where my client was detained at the CID is retained for only around 30 days. I do not understand why CCTV footage from such a location is deleted after just 30 days."

At this stage, the Magistrate questioned whether the medical report had specifically described the alleged incident as an indecent act.

In response, the defence counsel clarified that the report had not used the word "disgraceful" but had referred to an indecent act and recommended further investigations into the incident.

The Additional Solicitor General, however, maintained that the medical report had not indicated that the suspect had suffered any psychological distress.
