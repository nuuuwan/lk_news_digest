Dengue epidemic warning: 140 MOH divisions on red alert

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - Dengue could surge to epidemic levels within the next two to three weeks, with around 140 Medical Officer of Health (MOH) divisions across 13 districts showing a significant increase in mosquito larval density, the National Dengue Control Unit warned.

Dr. Kapila Kannangara, Acting Director of the National Dengue Control Unit, said recent larval surveys had revealed a worrying increase in mosquito breeding, raising fears of a possible escalation in dengue infections.

“This is a red alert. The increase in mosquito larval density indicates that the dengue situation could escalate to a high-risk level within the next two to three weeks, potentially reaching epidemic levels,” he warned.

He called on the public to take immediate action to destroy mosquito breeding sites, warning that collective efforts were essential to prevent the situation from worsening.

Colombo is among the areas raising serious concern, with the Colombo Municipal Council limits, Nugegoda and Dehiwala recording increases in both dengue patients and mosquito larval density.

The Gampaha and Kalutara districts have also reported significant increases, while Galle and Matara are showing worrying trends, particularly within their respective municipal council limits.

The situation is also concerning in Kandy, where an increase in mosquito larval density has been observed across all MOH divisions.

In the Eastern Province, Thirukkovil, Batticaloa and Trincomalee have recorded rising trends, while all MOH divisions in the Jaffna district have also reported increases.

Ratnapura has shown an increase in the Sabaragamuwa Province, while Kegalle has also recorded a rise.

The warning comes as Sri Lanka has already reported 109,722 dengue cases and 81 deaths so far this year, raising concerns over a possible further escalation in the coming weeks.
