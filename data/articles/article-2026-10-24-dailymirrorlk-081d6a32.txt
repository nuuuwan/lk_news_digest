The Gnanasara affair, ethno-religious divisions and political opportunism

The NPP government is probably facing the biggest challenge since it was elected to power in 2023. The present imbroglio over the disrobing of a convicted monk       (Gnanasara Thero) led by political opportunists and sections of the Buddhist clergy shows no sign of disappearing.

The challenge posed by the backers of Gnanasara is serious. Their slogan ‘Free Gnanasara immediately, or go home’ is a threat, a direct challenge aimed at President Dissanayake. Their demand is non-negotiable and calls on the president to cave into the demand of a section of particular religious leaders.

The protestors also demand that Buddhist clergy -- even those who break the law -- be granted immunity, that they should  be treated differently from ordinary citizens of this country. For years,  a number of rogue members of the clergy have been granted immunity by past leaders. This government treats them differently,  hence the backlash.

Whether the prison guards were right to disrobe a convicted monk prior to producing him in court is not the question. The monk publicly displayed his utter contempt of the judiciary by disrupting a court in session. He also threatened judicial officers.

Government on the other hand has emphasised its position is non-negotiable and that the monk –Gnanasara -- will not receive a Presidential pardon simply to please particular groups of political opportunists who are attempting to sow seeds of ethno-religious division in the country to gain political mileage.

However, government should also realise the sensitivity of today’s situation. Religion as in most South Asian countries is close to the hearts of our people. In our country, especially since the beginning of the three-decade long civil war, Sinhalese people were continuously fed a staple of ethno-religious propaganda built on a belief the Sinhala language and Buddhist culture were endangered.

The arrest and disrobing of convicted prisoner Gnanasara is being portrayed by political opportunists and sections of the clergy in the light of an attempt to please minority communities. The proponents of the ‘free Gnanasara’ movement are playing on the heart-strings of ordinary folk not in an attempt to protect the Buddhist religion or the dignity of members of the Buddhist clergy.

Gnanasara Thero after all has been convicted for contempt of court. He has been sentenced to a four-year term of rigorous imprisonment. Again, he has a number of other cases for which he awaits trial. The  attempt to hold him up as a hero standing up for Buddhist values and principles therefore rings hollow, especially in light of the many ‘misdemeanours’ he has committed.

It appears more an insidious attempt to rouse ethno-religious passions to achieve cheap political goals.

The National People’s Power government must remember however, the majority of our people, be they middle class, working class or the peasantry are crushed under tremendous economic burdens which they have borne for the past five or more. Government has not fulfilled its pre-election promises to ease these burdens.

Thus far,  the people have tolerated untold misery as they have seen government somewhat successfully fulfil one of its key pre-election promises –to crack down on corrupt politicians who bankrupted the country. But there is a limit to patience. It is these sections of our people who are being targeted today with claims of an attack on the Buddhist culture and faith.

Sadly sections of the media especially social media are sensationaling this. Government needs to counter these lies with a propaganda blitz of its own based on factual events. Firstly, the government  needs to offer economic relief to the people.

Secondly it needs to come up with strategies to counter attempts to rouse ethno-religious extremism by using civil organisations and moderate Buddhist monks to expose the unsavoury purveyors of hate-speech.  It should enable such persons and organisations by providing them space in the state media.

Most importantly to secure support of the public and non-government sector in its stated aim of creating a society where all citizens are equal, government needs to keep to its pledges of doing away with draconian legislation such as the PTA. Wihout replacing it with even more draconian legislation.
