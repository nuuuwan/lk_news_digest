Over half of NBFI household borrowers in high-risk bands as Stage 2 loans nearly double

Some 52.4% of household borrowers at finance companies fall in high or very high risk bands, compared with 24.1% at banks

Stage 2 loans at finance companies rose 92.1% in a year, while gold-backed Stage 2 loans jumped 279.2%

More than half of the households borrowing from finance companies fall in the high or very high risk categories based on their Credit Information Bureau (CRIB) scores, over twice the share among bank borrowers, as early signs of stress build up in the sector’s vehicle and gold-backed loan books, as per CBSL data.

As at end June 2026, 52.4 percent of household borrowers of non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs), comprising finance companies and a specialised leasing company whose registration was cancelled in June, fell within the high or very high risk categories, compared with 24.1 percent at banks. Nearly one in three, or 30.4 percent, sat in the very high risk band, more than double the 14.3 percent at banks, while only 31.6 percent were rated low or very low risk.

NBFI household borrowers recorded an average CRIB score of 497.4, inside the very high risk band of 250 to 500 and 59.8 points below the bank average of 557.2, which falls in the average risk band. The overall household score improved to 538.2 from 511.6 a year earlier, although it remained in the high risk category.

The Central Bank pointed out that NBFI scores had improved in recent quarters. The same divide held for businesses, with 53.9 percent of NBFI institutional borrowers in the two highest risk bands, against 21.6 percent at banks.

“Nevertheless, the relatively larger share of higher risk in the NBFI sector remains an area warranting continued attention from a financial stability perspective,” the Central Bank cautioned.

NBFIs are also gaining ground in household lending. Household credit grew 25.4 percent year-on-year at end June, up from 14.8 percent a year earlier, while NBFIs’ share of that lending rose to 37.5 percent from 35.5 percent. Repayment, however, improved, with loans carrying zero days in arrears rising to 67.7 percent of household exposures from 65.0 percent.

Meanwhile, finance companies’ gross loans expanded 45.0 percent year-on-year, driven by vehicle-backed loans, which grew 46.3 percent, and gold-backed loans, which rose 59.8 percent. Together, the two accounted for 85.2 percent of the sector’s credit, up from 82.6 percent, a reliance the Central Bank flagged as a significant collateral concentration risk.

However, the sector’s gross Stage 2 loans, which signal a significant rise in credit risk, jumped 92.1 percent, lifting the Stage 2 ratio to 15.4 percent from 11.6 percent. Stage 2 vehicle loans rose 75.5 percent, while Stage 2 gold-backed loans climbed 279.2 percent. Although the Stage 3 ratio fell to 5.1 percent from 8.3 percent, Stage 3 loans rose by Rs.23.6 billion in the second quarter alone, while Stage 3 impairment coverage slipped to 41.0 percent from 46.1 percent.

The Central Bank attributed part of the Stage 2 increase to weaker repayment capacity among some borrowers following Cyclone Ditwah and the Middle East conflict.

“The current uptick in Stage 2 loans reflects a potential buildup of credit risk within the sector and requires continuous monitoring and proactive risk mitigation by (finance companies),” it warned.

Notably, the CRIB risk scores exclude gold-backed loans altogether, as CRIB is still incorporating pawning data and such data are not yet consistently reported by all financial institutions. This leaves out a segment that makes up 21.4 percent of finance company credit, and one in which Stage 2 loans have nearly quadrupled in a year.

The Central Bank tightened loan-to-value limits on vehicle loans and introduced limits on gold-backed lending in May 2026, alongside a policy rate increase, and raised risk weights on gold-backed loans from September, measures it expects to slow credit growth in the sector in the coming quarters.
