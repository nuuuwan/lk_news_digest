LMFN chief urges microfinance sector to embrace regulation as ally of growth

Sri Lanka’s microfinance institutions must view regulation and growth as two expressions of the same commitment rather than competing forces, as the industry braces for a new era of formal oversight, Lanka Microfinance Network (LMFN) Chairman Danushka Udugama told sector leaders gathered in Bolgoda last week.

He made these remarks at the Microfinance Leaders’ Forum 2026, the network’s flagship annual gathering held under the theme “An Evening Gathering: Leading Through a Regulated Future” on 11 September at Hotel Clio, Bolgoda.

The forum drew Chief Executive Officers, board members and senior management from microfinance institutions across the island, in what organisers described as one of the sector’s most significant gatherings in recent years.

“As our industry moves into a new regulatory era, evenings like this remind us that regulation and growth are not competing forces, they are two expressions of the same commitment: to protect our clients and strengthen the institutions that serve them,” Udugama noted.

Thanking those in attendance, he added, “I want to thank every CEO, board member and partner who set aside their evening to stand together as one microfinance family. Tonight, we didn’t just talk about a regulated future, we started building it, together.”

Delivering the keynote address on “The Role of CEOs in Leading Institutional Transformation,” Dr. Kishu Gomes called on industry leaders to approach the coming regulatory transition as an opportunity to strengthen governance and rebuild public trust in the sector, rather than treating it as a constraint on growth.

Credit Information Bureau of Sri Lanka (CRIB) Deputy General Manager Janaka Lakmal, meanwhile, addressed the growing importance of credit information sharing as the sector moves towards tighter regulation. LMFN Secretary Imran Nafeer traced the evolution of Sri Lanka’s microfinance sector from its early history to the present day, before outlining his view of how the industry’s next phase would unfold and the role that CEOs, board members, regulators and other stakeholders would need to play in charting a responsible and sustainable path forward.

In recognition of his long-standing service to the country’s microfinance movement, the forum conferred Honorary Membership of LMFN upon G. Victor Rathnayake.

The event was supported by LCB Finance PLC as Finance Partner, represented by its Chief Executive Officer Leelananda, and M-CRIL Limited as LMFN’s International Knowledge Partner, represented by Chief Executive Officer Sudhir Narayan and AVP – Inclusive Finance Abhinav Ashesh. Evoke Labs served as IT Partner, while Liyasi TV was the Television Partner.

Formal proceedings were followed by a networking dinner, which offered participants an informal setting to continue discussions on collaboration and the sector’s direction.

LMFN is the largest and most inclusive microfinance network in Sri Lanka, works to strengthen the sector through advocacy, capacity building and engagement with policymakers and regulators.
