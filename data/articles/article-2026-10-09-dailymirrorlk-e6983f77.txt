Police launch probe into attacks on Metro buses

Colombo, Oct. 9 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Police have launched an investigation into a series of alleged deliberate attacks and acts of vandalism targeting the newly introduced Metro bus service.

According to Deputy Inspector General of Police in charge of the Traffic and Road Safety Range, W. P. J. Senadheera, investigators suspect that the incidents may be part of a coordinated effort to disrupt public transportation services and damage state property.

The Metro bus service was expanded by the government to ease traffic congestion in and around Colombo while providing commuters with a comfortable, reliable and convenient travel option. The service benefits passengers travelling from areas including Makumbura, Kadawatha and Kottawa, as well as persons with disabilities.

However, several incidents involving deliberate collisions and vandalism have been reported in Homagama, Athurugiriya, Piliyandala, Rajagiriya and Borella. The DIG also alleged that some private bus operators were preventing Metro buses from stopping to pick up passengers.

Police investigations have reportedly found that some private bus drivers and owners allegedly involved in the incidents were operating without valid public transport permits or proper driving licences. In one incident in Homagama, a driver involved in a collision was found to be under the influence of alcohol, according to police.

Authorities have also identified incidents in which private buses were allegedly reversed into Metro buses intentionally, causing damage and disrupting services.

In response, the Inspector General of Police has issued strict instructions to police officers,  OICs of the relevent police areas operating along the relevant bus routes to take firm action against those responsible.

Police have warned that legal action will be taken under the relevant provisions of criminal law, the Public Property Act and Motor Traffic regulations against individuals involved in the alleged attacks, including drivers, vehicle owners and anyone suspected of assisting or organising the incidents.

Several suspects, including bus drivers and vehicle owners, have already been arrested and are due to be produced before the courts.
