SLTPB to get acting chairman this week amid conflicting reasons of Hewawasam’s exit

The government is expected to appoint an Acting Chairman to the Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau (SLTPB) this week, as the agency faces a narrow window to finalise its budget for next year following the resignation of Chairman Buddhika Hewawasam, Mirror Business learns.

Hewawasam, who had headed the bureau since October 2024, stepped down upon completing his two-year term, with his last day in office understood to have been October 30. No successor or acting head had been formally named at the time of writing.

Industry sources indicated that the urgency stems from the bureau’s annual budget for 2027, which must be finalised within the next few weeks. This leaves little room for a prolonged leadership vacuum at an agency that receives 70 percent of the Tourism Development Fund (TDF), the statutory levy paid by tourism businesses.

However, accounts of why Hewawasam left differ sharply. While some within the industry maintain that he stepped down on personal grounds, one source familiar with the matter pointed to friction with the ministry. “From what I hear, there is a disagreement between him and the foreign minister—a clash,” the source told Mirror Business. Foreign Affairs, Foreign Employment and Tourism Minister Vijitha Herath oversees both the SLTPB and the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA). Attempts to reach Hewawasam for comment were unsuccessful.

His departure from the SLTPB comes less than two and a half months after he resigned as SLTDA Chairman on August 17, citing personal and professional reasons. He held both posts under a dual-chairmanship arrangement introduced on the instructions of President Anura Kumara Dissanayake. At the time, he indicated that he had originally agreed to serve for only a year but stayed on at the President’s request. Suranjith Wevita was subsequently appointed SLTDA Chairman, while some reports linked that exit to differences over a proposed separation of responsibilities between the two institutions.

Meanwhile, the change in leadership comes at a delicate juncture for Sri Lanka’s destination marketing. In August, the bureau launched a Rs.1.5 billion interim digital and public relations campaign across six primary source markets, intended to bridge the gap until a Rs.5 billion global branding campaign then slated for March 2027. Less than two weeks later, however, Tourism Deputy Minister Prof. Ruwan Ranasinghe confirmed that the global campaign would be pushed back by nearly a year, with the government opting to complete a World Bank-supported roadmap consultancy, led by Skift Inc. and MTI Consulting, before awarding the main campaign.

The bureau has also struggled to deploy its funds. It utilised only Rs.2.5 billion of the Rs.8 billion allocated for promotions in 2025, roughly 31 percent, a shortfall Hewawasam had previously attributed to procurement bottlenecks. Its leadership has been similarly unsettled, with 13 chairmen since 2007 and an average tenure of under 18 months, which places his two-year stint above the norm. (NF)
