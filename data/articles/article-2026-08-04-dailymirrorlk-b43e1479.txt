The only way for government organizations to be efficient is by making their task simpler and bringing in technology

The Deputy Minister of Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Chathuranga Abeysinghe in an interview with Daily Mirror said that the Government is weak in communicating its long-term objectives to the public and he believes that the coordination between Ministries should improve. He further said that the Government will be introducing Strategic Investment Act, SOE Management Act and Private Public Partnership Act. The Minister added that they will abide by their Manifesto and will take steps to abolish the Executive Presidency. Excerpts of the interview:

QIrrespective of the opposition’s claims that the government has failed to attract new investors, you stated a few weeks ago that the government plans to enact key reforms within the next three months to attract investment and boost exports. How do you intend to move forward with these reforms?

The reasons for not having direct investment have been historic. Number one is the country not having a clear economic plan and not being able to connect with wider markets that is like free trade agreements etc. The bureaucracy and the issues related to corruption and not having a clear industrial policy. As per my view, these are some fundamental reasons. And apart from that we had two issues stemming from the economic crisis. That is recent and the other ones are inherent. The one related to our sovereign rating has been low. We are a B minus I think by last week Earlier we were CCC plus. So that is one reason. The other reason is the global uncertainty. At this point of time people are reluctant to invest.

This year we will bring two more Acts related to investment. One is Investment Protection Act which has been drafted already. The other one is SOE Management Act which will also allow certain SOEs to have private public partnerships and manage these investments

There are reforms we have made. One is the Strategic Investment Act, we have already brought into Parliament. It clearly states for which category of investments and what the type of incentives are. Port City Act is amended to clearly articulate types of incentives. So now anybody investing in Sri Lanka it is not ambiguous.

This year we will bring two more Acts related to investment. One is Investment Protection Act which has been drafted already. The other one is SOE Management Act which will also allow certain SOEs to have private public partnerships and manage these investments.

Thirdly the most important one is Private Public Partnership Act. This will allow foreign investments to partner with the government in public infrastructure development. So these are the three changes that will be taking place within this year. Further these Acts will put us in line with the global best practices.

QAccording to the National SME Strategy published by the Ministry of Industry and Entrepreneurship Development in May 2026, one of the key strategic actions is to leverage digital tools to improve enterprise support and service responsiveness. Given the country’s current resource constraints and the fact that public sector digitalization is still at an early stage, do you believe this objective is achievable?

Yes, this is a journey that we are stepping in to. Therefore, as a first step we will be opening a help desk for industries which they don’t have now. So that will at least coordinate the services among our institutions and rest of the institutions as a single point of contact. That is step one. We have already launched the national industry database, which we are populating now.

We are focusing on journey incentivizing and educating industries and telling them why they should join. And we are also in discussion with the Digital Ministry on of a particular platform that can do end to end servicing which will be part of the entity that is going to be called EITA- Entrepreneurship and Industry Transformation Agency. That will be digital first organization. With that we are confident.

QDo you think creating infrastructure itself is sufficient? How about the reception of new technologies by the state sector employees are they trained?

Technological adaptation is anyway a difficult task irrespective of whether it is private or public sector. It is difficult with the Public sector because we are lagging. Therefore, we have already started an employee up skilling programme especially for our Development Officers, who are now around 1200 in numbers. This programme is done with Sri Lanka Institute of Marketing, and they are investing around 300 million rupees for the programme. That is one side. We also feel that today’s technology is simpler and believe that people can learn how to use it better.

The strategy itself is having a technological platform. Because of that you could train people while on the job. What we feel is that the only way for government organizations to be efficient is by making their task simpler and bringing in technology. If other countries have done it we should also be able to do it too.

QIn your opinion, in which areas do you believe this government has underperformed? If there have been shortcomings, what corrective measures has the government taken to address them?

I think the government has been a little weak in communicating its long-term objectives and reforms to the public. That is a weakness that needs to be corrected. At the same time we could have done better in inter-ministerial coordination and getting a cohesive strategy or a game plan which again we could have done better. Also, we could have done better in terms of industrial land and readiness for industries for infrastructure, which we are struggling but we could have done better. There needs to be more coordination and speed in industrial, land preparation and infrastructure readiness. This is from my Ministry’s perspective.

Government is trying to improve communication in each Ministry. Staff members are being trained and there is a cohesive communication plan that’s been implemented and various methods of reaching to public both physically and digitally. We would see improvement by the completion of the second year. We are planning to communicate the substantial changes the government has brought into every sector, so that people can be clear.

QThere is an ongoing debate that increasing the retirement age of judges is an attempt by the government to influence the composition of the judiciary. How do you respond to these concerns?

It’s right for people to hold different opinions in the actions that government is taking. But we are very much focused on continuing the strong process that we have been following during the past one and half years in ensuring rule of law. Nobody is above the law. And we have shown results. At the same time, we see the need for stability and experienced professionals in the legal systems not only for High Court and higher ranks. Our policy is to improve the tenure of most of the professionals, which will add value to the country’s public service. In the same light we know that public servants age of retirement has been increased previously. Even the retirement age of doctors has been extended earlier. So this is another process to increase judges’ retirement age. It is only for the High Courts and the higher ranks that we need the Constitution. For everything else, we could do at a Ministerial level.

The other fact is that I will not agree with the argument that they bring saying that this is an ad hoc thing. I say this because we can to do this change at any point that we wish; whether now or next year even afterwards. The same logic would be presented by the opposition.

We are also in discussion with the Digital Ministry on of a particular platform that can do end to end servicing which will be part of the entity that is going to be called EITA- Entrepreneurship and Industry Transformation Agency. That will be digital first organization. With that we are confident

Anyway, the existing judges’ retirement will increase at any point. We don’t see a rationale behind why such a resistance is coming. If there is a proper case, we would like to listen to it.

QThere are allegations that politically sensitive investigations progress more slowly than investigations involving ordinary members of the public. Do you agree with this? If not, why?

We don’t agree with that as we do not discriminate between the cases we can take first or which ‘name’ to take. We have only asked the investigative units to fast track. When they prioritise it, is a professional judgement on which case to prioritise based on complexity and the resources they have. So what we see now are the cases which are low hanging fruits which you term are the ones being proceeded much faster. The complex ones take time and resources. All the allegations and all the files that have been lodged are been investigated and proceeded. As a government, we have no influence over the process. We have strengthened the CID and the relevant institutions including the judiciary and legal processes. That process includes testing the bottlenecks and speeding up the process. This is the mandate people gave us and we have a limited number of years to prove that we are true to what we promised.

There is no reason for us to pick and choose among cases. Just because of complexity and time lapses of some of these cases, some may observe that the recent ones (Cases) are being brought faster than the older cases.

QThe NPP’s election manifesto included a pledge to abolish the executive presidency. It has now been two years since the party assumed power, yet no concrete action has been taken on this issue. Does the NPP intend to contest the 2029 Presidential Election with the executive presidency still in place, or will it be abolished before that?

Our current understanding still is that we will abide by the manifesto. This has been clearly articulated by the Prime Minister in Parliament. The intention is to adhere to the majority of the demands made regarding the manifesto. And we also know that the Constitution and the changes to be made are complex and take time. In our manifesto itself we have mentioned that we will not re-invent the wheel, but we will start from where we have left. Therefore, we still believe that there are three more years to start this debate and bring reforms that are in line with our promises to the people.
