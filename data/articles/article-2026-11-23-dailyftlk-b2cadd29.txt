Proposals for development of tourism infrastructure and facilities in Eastern coastal region

Under the current practice, a 1% levy is allocated to the SLTD and 1% to the relevant Local Authorities. In this regard, the new Budget proposal to remove the SLTD’s 1% levy is a welcome and commendable initiative, as it will provide further support to the tourism sector.

The Eastern coastal region, home to popular destinations such as Pasikuda, Arugam Bay, Nilaveli and Trincomalee, has significant potential to contribute further to the country’s tourism industry. However, gaps in infrastructure, public amenities, safety and environmental management continue to hold the region back. The following 10 proposals set out the key areas that require attention. A proper and sustainable beach cleaning and maintenance mechanism should be established in popular tourist destinations along the Eastern Coast, particularly Pasikuda, Arugam Bay and Nilaveli. These areas are among the country’s prominent tourism destinations and attract a considerable number of local and foreign tourists. The relevant Local Government Authorities should be given responsibility and adequate resources to ensure regular beach cleaning, proper waste management and the maintenance of a clean and attractive coastal environment.

China Bay Airport and Batticaloa Airport should be further developed and upgraded to improve domestic air connectivity and, where feasible, facilitate international tourism. Such development would improve accessibility to the Eastern Province, encourage more tourists to visit the region and contribute to the overall growth of the country’s tourism industry.

Development of Trincomalee Harbour as a passenger and tourism harbour

Trincomalee Harbour, recognised as one of the world’s finest natural harbours, has significant potential to become an important destination for passenger and tourism vessels. Facilities should be developed to accommodate passenger ships, cruise vessels and other tourism-related maritime activities. This would create new opportunities to attract international tourists, promote coastal tourism and strengthen the tourism economy of the Eastern Province.

Allocation of a portion of Pigeon Island Boat Service revenue for local environmental facilities

At present, the Department of Wildlife Conservation operates boat services for tourists visiting Pigeon Island National Park in Trincomalee. However, adequate sanitary facilities and other basic public amenities are lacking in the surrounding area. It is therefore proposed that a portion of the revenue collected from these boat services be allocated to the relevant Local Government Authority to establish and maintain sanitary facilities, improve waste management and maintain clean beaches. This would ensure better environmental protection while improving the facilities available to tourists and local communities.

Adequate Tourist Police facilities should be established in major tourist destinations across the Eastern Province. At present, there is a lack of properly equipped Tourist Police posts with even basic facilities. Therefore, the necessary infrastructure, accommodation, communication facilities and other essential requirements should be provided to enable Tourist Police officers to ensure the safety and security of tourists effectively.

Arugam Bay is a major international tourist destination with a considerable number of hotels, resorts and other tourism-related businesses. However, the area lacks adequate fire and emergency response facilities, creating significant concerns regarding the safety of tourists, hotel establishments and local residents. It is therefore proposed that a dedicated fire brigade unit, equipped with the necessary personnel, vehicles and equipment, be established in Arugam Bay to ensure an effective emergency response and enhance the safety of the area.

Establishment of Tourist Information Centres in Nilaveli, Arugam Bay and Pasikuda

Tourist Information Centres should be established in Nilaveli, Arugam Bay and Pasikuda to provide information, guidance and assistance to both local and foreign tourists. These centres would help visitors obtain reliable information about tourist attractions, accommodation, transportation, local services and other essential facilities. Considering the significant contribution of tourism in these areas to the country’s foreign exchange earnings, the establishment of such centres would improve the quality of tourist services, enhance visitor experiences and further promote these destinations as attractive international tourism hubs.

Declaration of Pottuvil, Lahugala and Thirukkovil Divisional Secretariat Divisions as Tourism Zones

The Pottuvil, Lahugala and Thirukkovil Divisional Secretariat Divisions should be officially identified, developed and declared as Tourism Zones, considering their tourism potential, natural attractions, coastal resources and cultural heritage. This initiative would help attract public and private sector investment, encourage the development of tourism-related infrastructure and facilities, create employment opportunities for local communities and promote new tourism destinations in the Eastern Province. It would also facilitate better coordination among relevant Government institutions in planning and implementing tourism development activities in these areas.

Implementation of measures to prevent coastal erosion in Arugam Bay and Whisky Point

Appropriate coastal protection measures should be implemented in Arugam Bay and Whisky Point to address the increasing threat of sea erosion. Geo-bags have already been installed along a 150-metre stretch in the area. However, it is urgently necessary to extend this protection by a further 450 metres along the vulnerable coastal stretches. This would help minimise further coastal erosion, protect tourism-related infrastructure and safeguard the coastal environment, which is essential for sustaining tourism activities in the area.

A suitable seaplane landing facility should be developed in Arugam Bay to facilitate emergency and urgent air transportation between Arugam Bay and Colombo. This facility would be particularly beneficial for tourists who require urgent transportation due to medical emergencies or other unforeseen circumstances. It would also improve regional accessibility, enhance tourist safety and contribute to the further development of Arugam Bay as an international tourism destination.

(The author is President of the Chamber of Tourism and Industry)
