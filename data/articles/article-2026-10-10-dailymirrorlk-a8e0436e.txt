Ragging in universities: Make students accountable from day one

With reference to the Daily Mirror editorial published yesterday on university ragging, “Mild punishments may not break the cycle of sadistic university ragging”, I wish to propose a firm and practical approach to eradicating this menace from our State universities.

Every student admitted to a State university should be required to sign a formal undertaking acknowledging that ragging is strictly prohibited and that proven involvement in, or instigation of, such acts will attract severe disciplinary action, including expulsion from the university.

As an initial measure, students found guilty following a fair and impartial inquiry could face suspension for one or two years. If such measures fail to eliminate this degrading practice, permanent expulsion should be imposed in serious cases, subject to clearly defined regulations and due process.

Ragging cannot be eradicated through condemnations and mild punishments alone. A firm, consistent and credible disciplinary framework is essential to protect the dignity, safety and fundamental rights of new entrants and to ensure that universities remain places of learning rather than intimidation.

I earnestly hope that the Prime Minister and Minister of Education, Higher Education and Vocational Education, Dr.  Ms. Harini Amarasuriya will consider this proposal and initiate appropriate action. Alternatively, the Government should invite constructive recommendations from university academics, the University Grants Commission (UGC) and the wider public to formulate an effective, legally enforceable policy to end ragging once and for all.

If necessary, the UGC Act and other relevant legislation should be amended to provide the legal framework required to enforce appropriate penalties.
