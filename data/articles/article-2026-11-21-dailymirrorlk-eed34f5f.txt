CBSL springs rate surprise

Overnight Policy Rate raised by 25 basis points to 9%, first increase since May

Governor sees inflation nearing 9% this month, with a return to 5% target projected for second half of 2027

Bond yields climb and Colombo stocks open lower after markets had expected a hold

The Central Bank sprang a surprise yesterday, raising its policy rate by 25 basis points to 9 percent, after the earlier increases in energy and food costs spread to other prices and Sri Lankans began to expect higher inflation over the coming year.

The move ended a six-month pause, following May’s full percentage-point increase.

In September, the Monetary Policy Board had been prepared to hold at 8.75 percent, even though it expected inflation to remain in high single digits through the first quarter of 2027.

Yesterday’s decision reflected a growing risk that the price pressures would persist, even after the initial energy shock eased.

“Inflation is going to remain elevated in the near term, at higher than expected levels compared to the last review,” Central Bank Governor Dr. Nandalal Weerasinghe said at a briefing yesterday.

He said Colombo inflation could approach 9 percent by end-November, from 8.3 percent in October and stay around that level in the near term.

The Central Bank now expects inflation to return to its 5 percent target in the second half of 2027.

The rise in core inflation helps explain why the board moved, even though the global energy prices had eased somewhat. The earlier cost increases were still working their way through the economy and higher expectations for inflation over the next year raised the risk of further price increases.

The medium-term expectations, however, stayed around the 5 percent target. That gave the board room to act in quarter-point increments while May’s larger increase continued to feed through to the borrowing costs.

The bank lending also complicated the case for waiting. The annual private-sector credit growth slowed to 23.6 percent in September, from 24.5 percent in August but the net credit added during the month rebounded to Rs.212.4 billion.

The board also raised the banks’ statutory reserve requirement from 2 percent to 3 percent, from December 9, a move Dr. Weerasinghe estimated would absorb about Rs.150 billion of the excess rupee liquidity built up through the Central Bank’s dollar purchases.

The government bond yields moved higher in early trade, with the dealers quoting the August 2030 bond at 11.20-11.40 percent, against 11.00-11.05 percent the previous day.

For the businesses, the impact will become clearer when the banks reprice the working-capital lines and loans due for refinancing.
