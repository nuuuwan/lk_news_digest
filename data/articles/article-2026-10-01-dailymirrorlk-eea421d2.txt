Johnston: Government targeting opponents through legal action

Colombo, Oct. 1 (Daily Mirror) - Former Minister and Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna (SLPP) Head of Operations Johnston Fernando alleged that the Government was targeting those who oppose or challenge it through legal action, including remanding suspects and filing new cases.

Speaking to journalists near the Fort Magistrate’s Court, Fernando said the SLPP had expected such developments and claimed that remanding and imprisoning government opponents had become a policy of the present administration.

“We expected things like this. Remanding and imprisoning those who oppose or challenge the government is this administration’s policy,” he said.

Fernando alleged that the Government was using such cases as a means of generating publicity, while claiming that it had little to show in terms of development projects or measures to reduce the cost of living.

“They have no news to share about building roads or bridges. They have no news about constructing a port or an airport. So, the only news they have is about remanding people,” he said.

He also alleged that individuals who obtained bail were subsequently faced with new cases.

Fernando said the SLPP would hold its next rally in Matara and indicated that the party would continue its political activities despite what he described as pressure from the Government.

He further alleged that certain officials were abusing the law and fabricating evidence to serve the Government’s agenda.

However, Fernando said his criticism was not directed at the judiciary, stressing that he had confidence in the courts and an independent judicial system.

“We have never had a conflict with the courts. The problem lies with certain officials,” he said.

Fernando also criticised what he described as political interference in law enforcement, saying politicians should not take the enforcement of laws into their own hands.

“The country has laws, and the relevant legal authorities enforce them—not politicians,” he said, adding that independent institutions should be allowed to function independently.
