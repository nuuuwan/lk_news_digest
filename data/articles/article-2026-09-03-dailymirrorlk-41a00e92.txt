Elephant participation in processions

Mr Upali weerasinghe’s opinion (28/08/2026) about the tradition of elephant attendance in the Kandy Esala perehera is an appropriate point for discussion. while agreeing with his views in his letter,  I would like to add one more point regarding the vulnerability of animal presence in a procession where thousands of spectators are likely to face terrible consequences from any unpredictable elephant rampage.

Although tamed, animal behaviour is unpredictable. Elephants get agitated, run wild or go berserk at the sight of fireworks displays, loud music or drum beating, dense crowds and the elephant costume with decorations.

In the recent past, there have been so many instances where domesticated elephants have killed the mahouts, the owners, Buddhist priests who were nursing or feeding them. In many past processions, the elephants have rampaged and injured the crowds.

There is another critical issue with the condition known as musth-(intoxicated like state) marked by behavioural shifts with restlessness, aggression, irritability etc.

In the face of these realities, it is the responsibility of the organisers to ensure the safety of the viewers from elephant rampage and the proper control of the elephants’ behaviour during the procession.
