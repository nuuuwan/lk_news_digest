Week opens on a quiet note in secondary market

The week got off to a quiet start in the secondary market, with some slight buying interest and moderate trading volumes.

Additionally, the PDMO concluded the weekly T-bill auction yesterday, due to the shortened trading week.

Over the short-end, 01.07.2028 traded at 10.15 percent and 15.09.2029 traded between 10.35 percent and 10.30 percent.

Moving ahead, 15.12.2032 changed hands at 11.30 percent, while the long-dated 15.08.2036 maturity was dealt within a narrow range of 12.00 percent and 11.97 percent.

At the weekly T-bill auction, the PDMO successfully raised the full initial offer of Rs.120.0 billion.

The three-month bills raised Rs.50.0 billion, while the six-month and 12-month bills raised Rs.35.0 billion each, on par with the amounts initially offered.

The weighted average yields on the three-month and six-month bills declined by 16bps to 9.06 percent and 9.44 percent. Meanwhile, the 12-month T-bill yield declined by 2bps to 9.89 percent.

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee appreciated against the US dollar, standing at Rs.329.45/US dollar, compared to Rs.330.17/US dollar seen earlier. Market liquidity in the banking system expanded to Rs.333.21 billion, from Rs.278.51 billion recorded previously.
