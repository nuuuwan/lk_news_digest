Sri Lanka clash headlines big sports day

England will look to complete a 3-0 T20I series sweep when they face Sri Lanka in the third and final match at Old Trafford, Manchester, on September 19.

Harry Brook’s side have already secured an unassailable 2-0 lead after winning the second T20I by six wickets, leaving Sri Lanka with one final opportunity to finish the series with a victory.

England head into the series finale in winning form, with Harry Brook leading their batting charts with 137 runs across the series. Liam Dawson has been their leading wicket-taker with five wickets.

Sri Lanka, meanwhile, will need a stronger all-round performance after struggling in the second encounter. Pathum Nissanka remains their leading run-scorer with 49 runs, while Wanindu Hasaranga has taken two wickets.

Batting first in the second T20I, Sri Lanka managed 145 for nine in their 20 overs. Nissanka top-scored with 37, while Lahiru Udara contributed 21 off 20 balls.

Charith Asalanka also made a start but failed to convert it into a substantial score. England’s Liam Dawson claimed three wickets as Sri Lanka’s lower order struggled to accelerate, leaving them with a below-par total.

England’s chase got off to a brisk start, with Jos Buttler scoring 30 off 16 balls and Aneurin Donald adding 21 from 13 deliveries.

Brook chipped in with a quickfire 23 before Tom Banton took control of the chase, smashing an unbeaten 52 off just 24 balls.

England reached the target in the 13th over, sealing a six-wicket victory and taking a 2-0 lead in the series.

England: Harry Brook (c), Ben Duckett, James Coles, Will Jacks, Gus Atkinson, Liam Dawson, Jamie Overton, Tom Banton, Jos Buttler, Jordan Cox, Aneurin Donald, Jofra Archer, Sonny Baker, Saqib Mahmood, Josh Tongue, Adil Rashid.

Sri Lanka: Charith Asalanka (c), Pathum Nissanka, Pavan Rathnayake, Kamindu Mendis, Janith Liyanage, Dasun Shanaka, Dunith Wellalage, Wanindu Hasaranga, Tharindu Ratnayake, Kamil Mishara, Lahiru Udara, Maheesh Theekshana, Dushmantha Chameera.

With England chasing a series clean sweep and Sri Lanka aiming to avoid a whitewash, the final T20I presents both sides with contrasting objectives at Old Trafford.

England fast bowler Brydon Carse will face no further police action after an investigation into an alleged assault in Derby was closed.

Derbyshire Police confirmed that the investigation into an alleged assault in Derby city centre was dropped following an incident in the early hours of August 23. Carse was initially arrested on suspicion of being drunk and disorderly before being de-arrested after officers took his details.

However, Carse will remain subject to an investigation by the Cricket Regulator. The 31-year-old was previously left out of England’s squad for the third Test against Pakistan and the white-ball series against Sri Lanka following the incident.

The England and Wales Cricket Board confirmed that Carse remains available for selection, while head coach Brendon McCullum said the fast bowler needs to “get his house in order” before returning to international cricket.

The BCCI has authorised its office-bearers to decide whether to extend Ajit Agarkar’s tenure as chairman of selectors or appoint a replacement following its 95th Annual General Meeting (AGM) on Friday.

Agarkar’s current contract is set to expire on October 4, with the board also looking to fill five vacancies on the junior selection committee.

“The office-bearers have been authorised to take decisions on matters concerning the selection committees,” a senior BCCI official said after the meeting.

The AGM was attended by representatives of affiliated state associations, including Sourav Ganguly of the Cricket Association of Bengal and former India pacer Venkatesh Prasad of the Karnataka State Cricket Association.

Meanwhile, the Mumbai Cricket Association requested consideration for hosting prominent matches, while Jharkhand State Cricket Association secretary Saurabh Tiwary denied any knowledge of reports suggesting that an India-Australia Test would be moved away from Ranchi.

UEFA President Aleksander Ceferin and CONCACAF President Victor Montagliani have called for an independent review of FIFA’s financial reserves and proposed a $2.1 billion payout to its 211 member associations.

In a letter to FIFA President Gianni Infantino, the two FIFA vice-presidents proposed making at least $10 million available to each association during the 2027–30 cycle, in addition to existing FIFA Forward Programme funding.

They said FIFA was on course to end the 2023–26 cycle with reserves of around $6 billion, raising questions about the need for the abandoned FIFA Forward Enterprise project, which sought investment from outside parties.

Ceferin and Montagliani said an independent review should assess FIFA’s financial position and determine how much additional funding could be distributed. They also proposed considering a similar payout for the 2031–34 cycle.
