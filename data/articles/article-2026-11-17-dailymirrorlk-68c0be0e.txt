Sri Lanka records premature births in one in 10 deliveries

Colombo, November 17 (Daily Mirror) - Around 10 per cent of all births in Sri Lanka are premature, highlighting the need for continued medical care and long-term follow-up services for babies born before completing their full term.

Although advances in medical science have significantly improved the ability to save premature babies, experts stress that survival alone is not enough to ensure their healthy development.

Premature babies may face complications affecting their overall growth and development, including vision and respiratory problems. Such complications can also emerge after they are discharged from Neonatal Intensive Care Units (NICUs).

Continuous medical supervision and developmental assessments are therefore essential to identify potential health issues early and provide the necessary treatment and support.

The importance of long-term care for premature babies was highlighted at a special awareness programme organised by the Perinatal Society of Sri Lanka to mark World Prematurity Day on November 15.

Deputy Health Minister Dr. Hansaka Wijemuni said the responsibility of the healthcare system should not end with saving the life of a premature infant.

He said that babies discharged from NICUs must receive proper follow-up care to ensure healthy growth and development.

The Deputy Minister also called for stronger cooperation between the state healthcare system, professional associations, the private sector and other stakeholders to improve care for premature babies.

A set of special guidelines aimed at improving the health and well-being of premature infants and their future health outcomes was unveiled during the programme.

The Perinatal Society of Sri Lanka, which brings together specialists in obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics and neonatal care, works to improve maternal, fetal and newborn healthcare through research, professional development and the promotion of scientific knowledge.

Improving care for premature babies is also important in minimising the long-term social and economic consequences of premature births for families and society.
