Melwa readies Rs.6bn Seeduwa cement plant, secures key ticket to European steel market

Melwa Group’s Rs.6bn cement plant in Seeduwa is 90 percent complete, with first bags due within two to three months.

The plant can produce around 30,000 bags a day, and the company expects to recover its investment in about five years.

Melwa’s steel arm has secured CARES certification, which opens the door to European exports.

Melwa Group will begin rolling out cement from its Rs.6 billion grinding and blending plant in Seeduwa within the next two to three months, extending its building materials portfolio beyond steel as it positions itself for a pipeline of state infrastructure projects, company officials said.

According to Melwa Cement Chief Operations Manager Chinthaka Thuduwewatta, civil structures at the plant have been completed, leaving only the remaining mechanical, process and laboratory work before production begins.

The company expects to recover the investment within about five years, which implies average annual returns in the region of Rs.1.2 billion. However, officials declined to disclose how the project was financed, noting that the matter rested with top management.

Daily output is expected to be around 30,000 bags, depending on the product mix. At 50 kg a bag, this works out to roughly 1,500 tonnes a day, or close to 550,000 tonnes over a full year. That would be equivalent to nearly 12 percent of the 4.71 million tonnes of domestic cement demand reported by Tokyo Cement last year.

Unlike integrated cement plants, the Seeduwa facility will neither produce clinker nor operate a kiln.

Raw materials imported in containers through the Colombo Port will be stored, proportioned into a mixer, separated by particle size and held in three vertical silos before being packed. Thuduwewatta explained that the plant handles only the final stage of the conventional cement process, which accounts for about 20 percent of the full cycle.

“There is no melting of stone here. What we do is only blend imported raw materials. There is no high temperature, because we don’t burn anything,” he pointed out.

In the initial phase, the company plans to release three product categories, including blended hydraulic cement under SLS 1247 and Portland composite cement under SLS 1697. These will be sold both in 50 kg bags and in bulk through bowsers of 40 to 60 tonnes. The process is aligned with ISO 14001 and ISO 9001, while the plant has been designed to European best available standards with a working life of around 40 years in mind.

The site was chosen mainly for its proximity to the Colombo Port and the Katunayake Expressway, which the company believes will considerably ease the movement of its imported materials.

Melwa Group Head of Sales and Brand Malik Kannangara was asked why the company was entering a market already dominated by established international players. In response, he drew a parallel with the group’s steel business.

“When we came to the market in 2002, there were so many players who had been running for over 40 to 50 years. But within a short period, when we came out with our 2018 steel mill, we became Sri Lanka’s market leader and the biggest manufacturer,” he recalled.

Kannangara sees demand building on the back of the government’s move to award tenders to local construction companies. He pointed to projects such as the Kandy tunnel, multimodal transport hubs, water projects and the long-awaited Central Expressway, as well as the group’s own construction requirements at Port City Colombo.

“The opportunities are coming. The industry is getting opportunities as well as we are getting them. So we are ready for that, and this is something targeting all these projects,” he stressed.

On distribution, Kannangara claimed that the group operates the largest transport fleet in the country and can reach any part of the island within a day, a network the new cement business will tap into. Looking further ahead, Thuduwewatta indicated that having both steel and cement under one roof would allow the group to offer certified high-strength concrete as a value-added product.

Meanwhile, Kannangara revealed that Melwa’s steel arm has obtained certification from the UK Certification Authority for Reinforcing Steels (CARES), a requirement for supplying reinforcing steel to European construction projects. The certification followed a process that took three to five years. He claimed that only three manufacturers in South Asia hold it, two of them in India, which makes Melwa the only Sri Lankan company to have it.

“This is the first time we are coming out to the media with this. We have got the CARES certificate, which is a necessary certificate to export steel to the European market,” he disclosed.

The certification builds on an export record that already includes shipments to neighbouring countries and Melwa’s maiden 15,000-tonne consignment of reinforcement steel to Canada. Kannangara described that shipment as a first for a Sri Lankan company in the Canadian construction market.

Founded in 2002 under the Melwire Group, Melwa has grown from a steel bar maker into a diversified group spanning construction steel, galvanised products and welding rods, and in 2021 it had explored a 1.3 million tonne cement plant near the Hambantota International Port. More recently, the group has partnered with Prime Group to amass around 16 acres in the Port City Colombo Marina District, making the partnership the largest property investor in the Port City.
