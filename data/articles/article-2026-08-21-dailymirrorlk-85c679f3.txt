Emergency airlift planned as Filgrastim shortage hits hospitals

Colombo, August 21 (Daily Mirror)- A Daily Mirror report on the shortage of the cancer drug Filgrastim, published on August 18 under the headline “Drug shortage puts cancer patients’ lives at stake,” came under scrutiny in Parliament yesterday, with MP Shanakiyan Rasamanickam questioning Health Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa over the issue.

Responding to Rasamanickam’s question, Jayatissa acknowledged that the country was facing a temporary shortage of Filgrastim injections but assured Parliament that the situation was expected to be resolved within about a week.

The Minister said the shortage arose after a consignment of 160,000 vials supplied on July 28, 2026, had to be rejected for failing to meet the specifications of the National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA).

He explained that the prefilled syringes were rejected because they did not carry the labels required under NMRA specifications.

The Health Ministry is now taking steps to address the shortage, with the supplier being requested to airfreight an additional stock of the drug to meet the immediate demand.

“We have also asked the supplier who was awarded the contract for 2027 to provide stocks as soon as possible. We cannot release the existing stocks without the labels required under NMRA specifications,” the Minister said.

Responding to a question raised by MP Rasamanickam, the Minister said the country’s annual requirement of Filgrastim is around 300,000 vials, while the daily requirement stands at approximately 900 vials.

He said the Maharagama Apeksha Hospital alone requires about 325 vials a day, amounting to a monthly requirement of around 9,750 vials.

The Minister stressed that the shortage was not caused by any delay on the part of either the Health Ministry or the supplier. He said the Medical Supplies Division (MSD) had placed the order for the 2026 requirement more than nine months in advance, in January 2025.

“The supplier delivered 160,000 vials, sufficient for approximately six months, on July 28,” he said.

Responding to a question as to whether the Ministry could allow the use of the drug despite the absence of labels, given the urgent needs of patients, the Minister said discussions were underway to determine whether this could be permitted based on recommendations from the College of Oncologists and the College of Haematologists.

He added that relabeling the rejected stock would take at least six weeks.
