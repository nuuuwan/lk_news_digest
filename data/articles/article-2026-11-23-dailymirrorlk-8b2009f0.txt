Smartphones linked to concerns over sleep and productivity

Colombo, Nov. 23 (Daily Mirror) - Smartphones have become an increasingly unavoidable part of everyday life, but spending too much time on these devices is emerging as a growing concern, particularly among younger generations, with many adults admitting they struggle to cut back on their screen time.

According to a survey conducted by the Pew Research Center in May and June 2026, just over half of adults in the United States said they spend too much time on their smartphones.

Around one-third said their usage was about right, while only 3% believed they spent too little time using the devices.

Younger adults were more likely to express concerns about their smartphone habits, with 70% of those under 30 saying they spent too much time on their devices. The figure was around two-thirds among adults aged 30 to 49, approximately half among those aged 50 to 64 and a quarter among those aged 65 and above.

The findings also showed that 45% of adults had attempted to reduce their smartphone use over the previous 12 months, while 46% had not made any such effort.

Among those who felt they spent too much time on their smartphones, 62% said they had tried to cut back.

However, reducing screen time has not been easy for everyone. Only 25% of those who attempted to limit their smartphone use said they were extremely or very successful. Another 52% described their efforts as somewhat successful, while 23% said they had been unsuccessful.

Adults under 50 were considerably more likely to have attempted to limit their smartphone use than older age groups.

Despite concerns over excessive usage, smartphones remain a constant presence in daily life, with around one in four adults saying they use their devices almost constantly. Another 60% said they use their smartphones several times a day.

Among adults under 30, 42% reported using their smartphones almost constantly. This compared with 33% of those aged 30 to 49, 21% of those aged 50 to 64 and 9% of adults aged 65 and above.

The survey also examined how smartphone use affects different aspects of people’s lives, including their ability to stay informed, maintain relationships, productivity, mood and sleep.

Around three-quarters of adults said smartphones helped them stay informed, while approximately half said the devices helped them feel connected to others.

Views were more divided on productivity and mood, with respondents reporting both positive and negative effects.

Sleep, however, emerged as the area most negatively affected by smartphone use. Around 41% said their smartphones had a negative impact on the amount of sleep they received, compared with just 5% who said the devices helped their sleep. Nearly half said smartphones had no impact.

The impact on sleep was particularly evident among younger adults, with 62% of those under 30 saying smartphones negatively affected their sleep. Around half of those aged 30 to 49 also reported a similar effect.

Younger adults were also more likely to say their smartphone use negatively affected their productivity, while those under 50 were more likely than older adults to report an adverse impact on their mood.
