SLPP to strengthen grassroots campaign ahead of elections: Namal

Colombo, Aug. 24 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna (SLPP) National Organiser and Parliamentarian Namal Rajapaksa said the party would strengthen its grassroots engagement by visiting communities across the country as it prepares for future elections.

Speaking to journalists at the SLPP headquarters on Nelum Mawatha today, Namal said the party had contributed to bringing the President closer to the people and would continue to focus on engaging directly with communities.

“We have to go from village to village for the presidency today. We have been able to take the President from the AC room in Colombo 7 to the main road and bring him closer to the people,” he said.

Commenting on the proposed 22nd Amendment to the Constitution, Rajapaksa expressed concern over statements made by Government representatives regarding matters that could come before the judiciary.

He said that public comments appearing to anticipate court decisions could raise concerns about judicial independence.

Namal also said that recent discussions on judicial independence had highlighted the importance of allowing courts to carry out their duties without political influence.

Turning to several recent controversies, he said the Government should give greater attention to issues directly affecting the public, including the timely provision of fertiliser, the purchase of paddy, fuel concessions for fishermen, support for graduates and assistance to industries facing difficulties.

He said the Government should focus on fulfilling the commitments outlined in its policy programme while ensuring that investigations into allegations of wrongdoing were conducted fairly and transparently.

Referring to recent investigation reports, Namal questioned whether some investigations may have focused on selected individuals and called for a comprehensive review of the findings.

He said the SLPP was continuing to study the reports and that a proper assessment and investigation would be necessary before drawing conclusions.

Namal also expressed concerns regarding the proposed 22nd Amendment, saying any constitutional changes should be considered carefully and should not undermine public confidence in democratic institutions or the rule of law.

Meanwhile, commenting on the potential impact of El Niño conditions, Namal said successive governments had previously prepared contingency plans based on forecasts of adverse weather conditions.

He urged the Government to take timely preventive measures and ensure that relevant institutions were adequately prepared to respond to potential weather-related challenges.
