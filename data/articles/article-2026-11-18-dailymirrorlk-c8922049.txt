Bancroft sandpaper claims heap pressure on Cummins

BBC - In a detailed testimony, published by the West Australian,, external batter Bancroft - the player caught with the sandpaper during the Test match against South Africa - has given arguably the most complete account of the events in Cape Town eight years ago.

"In 2018 not the entire truth was told," said Bancroft. "I have never been completely honest about what happened."

For the past two weeks, Australian cricket has been embroiled in a debate over the ball-tampering saga following the publication of Usman Khawaja's autobiography.

Khawaja wrote that the whole Australia team knew of the plot, claims supported by David Warner and Chadd Sayers. Cummins described Khawaja's account as "wrong" and "nonsense".

On the day Cricket Australia announced a forum into the sandpaper episode, Bancroft published his account, explaining he was motivated by the news conference given by Cummins.

"The final straw for me to write this account was Australian cricket captain Pat Cummins' press conference," said Bancroft.

"What I saw, experienced and lived with - not only him, but everyone else on that tour in South Africa - is not the public account he gave in his press conference and in the years previously when he has spoken on this time."

Opener Bancroft was given a nine-month ban for his part in the plot.

Steve Smith was stripped of the Australia captaincy and given a year-long ban, the same suspension given to vice-captain Warner. No other players or coaches were punished.

Khawaja's autobiography and the subsequent claims made by Warner and seamer Sayers have reopened old wounds related to the sandpaper saga.

Still, those connected have remained true to the accounts they have presented during the intervening eight years.

The most significant denial came from Cummins, who rebuffed Khawaja's account in an extraordinary 68-minute address to the media in Sydney. Lehmann and Starc have also reiterated their denials.

Now, it is the extensive detail provided by Bancroft that could shed a whole new light on the controversy and lead to further questions for Cummins, Lehmann and Cricket Australia.

Cummins is due to lead Australia in the first Test against New Zealand on 9 December.

A statement released on behalf of Cummins said: "Whilst we feel for Cam and everything he's been through there's nothing in the statement that hasn't been answered by Pat in his press conference."

A Cricket Australia statement said: "We respect that Cameron Bancroft has told his story, and we will take time to consider his comments.

"We expect that there will be many dimensions to the events of 2018, which is why we have established the forum for individuals to give their version of events.

"We are finalising the terms of reference and will commence the process as soon as possible."

In his account, Bancroft said: "I have spent eight years speaking about accountability, honesty and the lessons I learned from one of Australian cricket's darkest moments.

"However, in 2018 not the entire truth was told. I have never been completely honest about what happened.

"It was not just the three of us — Steve Smith, David Warner and I — who knew about me using sandpaper on the ball in South Africa. It was the whole team.

"When I was questioned during Cricket Australia's investigation, I did not give a complete and truthful account.

"I withheld important details, including when the use of sandpaper began, how long it went on for, and the involvement and awareness of others.

"I did this to protect Cricket Australia's investigation and not implicate others around me."

Bancroft said his actions were "discussed" and "acknowledged" by the entire playing group at the end of the second day and before the beginning of the third day. He said he was encouraged by Saker, who has since worked as a consultant with England.

The only person within the camp to express unease at the ball-tampering, according to Bancroft, was media manager Kate Hutchison.

"I received wraps and encouragement from members of the playing and coaching staff, especially David Saker," said Bancroft.

"Our media manager, Kate Hutchison, expressed her objection to what was happening.

"I recall her making it clear that she believed it was wrong, that she wanted nothing to do with it, and walking away from the discussion.

"Somebody had demonstrated that being part of the team did not mean having to accept everything the team was doing. And I ignored it."

Bancroft identifies Saker as the coach Khawaja claimed encouraged him to work harder on the ball during the third Test in Cape Town. The 33-year-old said he took "feedback" from all the bowlers and wicketkeeper Tim Paine, who took over the captaincy when Smith was sacked.

In Cape Town, Bancroft was confronted by the umpires when TV cameras showed him concealing the sandpaper in his trousers. He claimed Lehmann sent a message to him via Handscomb.

"Peter Handscomb ran out at the drinks break after I'd been caught and shoved the sandpaper down my pants," wrote Bancroft.

"He said to me: 'I think it's time I take that off you now.' And Pete was doing his job after Boof [Lehmanm] shouted to him on the walkie talkie "get the sandpaper off Bangers [Bancroft] now".

"If he didn't know he wouldn't have said that. And if it wasn't clear enough before, then it was obvious now."

In 2021, Bancroft was playing county cricket with Durham and gave an interview to The Guardian in which he said the bowlers' involvement in the plot was "self-explantory".

In response, Cummins, Starc, Hazlewood and spinner Nathan Lyon released a joint-statement denying any knowledge that ball-tampering had taken place.

"I had memories of conversations on the field in Port Elizabeth and Newlands, feedback about the ball and shared celebrations that made the statement difficult for me to digest."
