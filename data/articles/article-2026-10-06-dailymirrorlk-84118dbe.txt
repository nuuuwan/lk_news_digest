Gnanasara Thera’s prison attire: What the rules say

Colombo, Oct. 6 (Daily Mirror) - Prison Media Spokesman and Commissioner of Prisons (Operations) Senaka Pallethenna has clarified the regulations governing the attire of inmates, following concerns over Bodu Bala Sena (BBS) General Secretary Ven. Galagodaaththe Gnanasara Thera being brought before court in prison attire instead of his robes.

Pallethenna said under prison regulations, anyone serving a prison sentence is required to wear standard prison attire. Inmates sentenced to more than three years for serious offences are required to wear prison attire while serving their sentences.

"According to prison rules and regulations, if a venerable Buddhist monk has been sentenced to simple imprisonment by a respected court, they may remain in their monastic robes while serving the relevant prison sentence within the correctional facility. If the sentence involves rigorous imprisonment, any individual must wear the prison uniform. When producing individuals sentenced by a court to prison, if their prison sentence is less than three years, the prison superintendent may permit them to wear the civil clothing they were wearing prior to the sentence when being presented to court.

However, any person considered under any circumstance to have been sentenced to imprisonment exceeding three years must be dressed in the attire issued by the prison when being presented to court," he said.

Pallethenna also said more than 63 senior clergy members have served prison sentences at various times, with their detention and treatment being managed in accordance with established administrative and legal procedures.
