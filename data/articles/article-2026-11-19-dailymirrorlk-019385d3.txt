CEJ sends letter of demand to CEA over Divulapitiya e-waste facility fire

Colombo, Nov. 19 (Daily Mirror) - The Centre for Environmental Justice (CEJ) has sent a letter of demand to the Central Environmental Authority (CEA), calling for an immediate investigation into a fire that broke out at an electronic waste collection and storage facility in Wewagedara, Divulapitiya, on November 15.

In its letter dated November 18, the CEJ has urged the CEA to take immediate legal and regulatory action over the incident, citing serious concerns about possible air pollution and environmental contamination caused by the fire.

The letter, addressed to the Director General of the CEA, states that the uncontrolled burning of electronic waste could release highly toxic substances, including dioxins, furans, lead and cadmium, into the atmosphere and surrounding environment.

It warns that such emissions could expose nearby residents to serious public health risks and threaten the soil, water, vegetation and surrounding ecosystems.

The CEJ has called for a comprehensive investigation to identify all individuals responsible for collecting, handling and storing electronic and hazardous waste at the premises and to initiate appropriate legal proceedings against those found responsible.

It has also urged the CEA to verify the legality of the facility's operations, including the validity of licences, permits and approvals issued by the Authority or any other competent institution.

The organisation has further called for appropriate legal, administrative and disciplinary action against officers or individuals found to have violated their statutory duties.

The letter warns that if the CEA fails to respond within 14 days of receiving it, specifying the action taken or proposed, the organisation's clients will consider such failure a refusal to discharge the Authority's statutory and constitutional obligations.

The CEJ has also reserved the right to initiate legal proceedings against the CEA and other responsible parties before the competent courts and tribunals, seeking judicial review, constitutional remedies, public law relief and enforcement of statutory duties.
