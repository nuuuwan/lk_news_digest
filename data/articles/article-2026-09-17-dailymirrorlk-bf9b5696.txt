T-bill auction yields edge higher across the board

The secondary market activity remained active, with the high trading volumes and moderate activity amidst continued selling pressure concentrated around the 2030 to 2033 maturity bracket at the belly of the curve.

Consequently, the yields moved higher across the curve, with the belly witnessing a relatively sharper upward movement.

Starting at the short end, the 15.09.2029 and 01.03.2030 maturities traded at 11.00 percent. Moving on to the 2030 segment, the 01.07.2030, 01.08.2030 and 15.10.2030 maturities traded from 11.40 percent to 11.55 percent.

Moving along the curve, the 01.02.2031 maturity traded from 11.60 percent to 11.65 percent. The 15.01.2033 and 01.11.2033 bonds traded from 11.95 percent to 11.96 percent. The 15.09.2034 and 15.10.2034 maturities traded at 12.16 percent and 12.10 percent, respectively, whilst 15.03.2035 and 15.08.2036 traded at 12.20 percent and 12.16 percent, respectively. Furthermore, the 01.07.2037 maturity traded at 12.20 percent.

Yesterday, the PDMO concluded its weekly T-bill auction, raising Rs.70.0 billion, in line with the total amount initially offered. For the three-month T-bill, Rs.25.0 billion was offered, against the bids totalling approximately Rs.63.4 billion.

The weighted average yield increased by 15bps to 9.18 percent for the three-month maturity. The PDMO accepted the full Rs.25.0 billion offered for the six-month T-bill, with the weighted average yield rising by 12bps to 9.36 percent. For the 12-month T-bill, the full Rs.20.0 billion offered was accepted, while the weighted average yield increased by 11bps to 9.88 percent.

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee depreciated against the US dollar, standing at Rs.330.18/US dollar, compared to Rs.329.04/US dollar seen earlier.

Market liquidity in the banking system contracted to Rs.336.44 billion, compared to Rs.347.55 billion recorded previously.
