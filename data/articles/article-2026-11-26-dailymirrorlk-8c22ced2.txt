Software takes centre stage as Sungrow wires AI into every layer of energy storage

Point Sungrow’s artificial intelligence (AI) assistant at a factory on a satellite map and, within 30 seconds, it returns a tailor-made design for a solar and battery storage plant, including the technical layout, installation plan and financial calculations. Once the equipment reaches the site, Sungrow claims the same assistant can complete commissioning in three minutes, a process that has long relied on manual parameter setting and painstaking checks.

The assistant, nicknamed Bubbly, was one of the clearest signals at Sungrow’s Global Energy Pioneer Summit (GEPS) 2026 in Hefei that the Chinese inverter and storage major sees the next leap in energy storage coming as much from software as from hardware. Across its new residential and commercial and industrial (C&I) systems, the company is embedding AI into design, installation, dispatch and maintenance, betting that smarter software will decide how much value each kilowatt-hour delivers.

Bubbly runs five intelligent agents covering plant design, commissioning, energy management, operation and maintenance, and comes built into the newly launched PowerStack 3.0 C&I storage series. Its design agent combines satellite imagery with 3D modelling to produce a tailor-made proposal, which Sungrow says can reduce on-site surveys and help developers assess a project’s feasibility before committing capital.

Introducing the system on stage, Sungrow C&I Energy Storage Product Line General Manager Dr. Cao Wei pointed out that installers previously had to visit each site to work out its configuration, which often involved a huge amount of work. The commissioning agent now sets grid parameters automatically, runs communication checks and validates settings, cutting the risk of errors that come with manual verification and easing the training burden on service teams.

“Today, we have entered the 3.0 era, the scenario-driven era. Sungrow brings you more than a new product. We bring a complete ecosystem solution, designed for every scenario, powered by intelligence and connected across the entire value chain,” Dr. Cao declared.

Bubbly’s energy management agent goes further by changing how a battery earns its keep. Commercial storage plants have typically relied on a single revenue mode, such as storing solar power for self-consumption or shaving peak demand. The agent instead draws on solar generation and load forecasts to coordinate self-consumption, demand control, energy arbitrage and frequency regulation, continuously reshaping the dispatch plan to lift returns by more than 12 percent when compared to a conventional self-consumption mode.

The same logic runs through the company’s smaller systems. For the PowerKeeper C&I battery, an AI-driven operating mode on Sungrow’s iSolarCloud platform optimises energy scheduling to deliver 10 to 30 percent higher returns along with longer backup, Sungrow C&I Energy Storage Product Line Director Jim Hu told delegates. For homes, the flagship PowerHarbor applies similar AI scheduling around weather, solar output, battery levels and electricity prices. It also integrates EnergyBridge, which Sungrow bills as the industry’s first mini backup solution, while Bubbly simplifies its installation and commissioning by up to 30 percent, according to the company.

In Sri Lanka, where there is no market yet for grid services such as frequency regulation, the immediate gains would come from self-consumption and demand control. These carry clear value for industrial consumers whose electricity bills include maximum demand charges, and for businesses that have been asked to disconnect large solar systems on Sundays.

C&I storage systems are often scattered across remote locations, and after-sales teams typically drive long distances with testing equipment to check each system component by component, Dr. Cao noted. Bubbly’s maintenance agent instead monitors system conditions around the clock and identifies more than 30 potential risks, generating a fault diagnosis report with maintenance recommendations for a 10 MWh plant within a minute. Sungrow has also upgraded iSolarCloud and introduced 24/7 AI-powered multilingual support for its partners.

That capability carries particular weight in emerging markets such as Sri Lanka, where specialised technical skills are not evenly spread. Sungrow told Mirror Business that Bubbly supports initial diagnostics and technical information searches for users and installers, while iSolarCloud allows service teams to track system performance and equipment health remotely. “We see digital innovation and local service capabilities as complementary rather than interchangeable,” the company stressed, adding that combining AI-assisted diagnostics and digital operations and maintenance platforms with professional local service teams could deliver more timely and reliable support to end users.

Behind the software sits a physical network of more than 170 local warehouses, seven central warehouses spanning 80,000 square metres, or an average of over 11,000 square metres each, and a 20,000 square metre headquarters spare parts warehouse, holding inventory worth over US$ 220 million, as per a company presentation at the summit. Sungrow claims an on-site parts first-fill rate of over 98 percent, backed by more than 20 refurbishment centres.

Sensing and automated controls also underpin Sungrow’s approach to battery safety. The PowerKeeper system is built on a patented three-layer architecture of proactive detection, instant interception and physical containment, Hu explained at a media briefing.

The first layer detects arc faults on both the solar and battery sides while monitoring temperature and other abnormal conditions. Once a fault is detected, an intelligent switch inside the inverter shuts down the array, removing the arc before it develops into a failure. The final layer, designed for extreme events such as thermal runaway, uses guided pressure relief to vent any explosion upwards so that nothing is ejected towards nearby people or buildings.

“Mostly it’s stopped by the first and second stage. The last stage is like the final extreme situation,” Hu observed, adding that such an event had never occurred across the company’s global shipments.

PowerKeeper has passed more than 700 tests from cell to system level. Hu quipped that he could hold his breath underwater for about two minutes, against a world record of about 12 minutes, while the battery kept running for 24 hours under 50 centimetres of water in a flood simulation, 120 times that record. In other tests shown at the summit, it withstood a drop from 5 metres and stopped fire spreading between stacks even with its fire suppression kit removed. PowerStack 3.0 adds three-level fuse protection and a four-layer fire safety design.

Underneath the software, PowerStack 3.0 marks what Sungrow describes as a shift from all-in-one physical integration to “Scene-in-One” design. Its multi-scenario dock accepts four modules covering power conversion, solar, EV charging and microgrid functions, allowing a single platform to serve as standalone storage, a solar-plus-storage system without a separate inverter, a solar-storage-charging hub or a microgrid with a zero-millisecond switchover to off-grid operation that needs no separate static transfer switch cabinet. A business that starts with solar and storage can later add EV charging by fitting the relevant module on site without replacing the battery, a Sungrow official explained at the media briefing.

Each cabinet delivers 125 kW or 250 kW with 551 kWh of storage, giving roughly four-hour and two-hour configurations respectively, and up to 20 units can run in parallel for about 11 MWh. Its self-developed silicon carbide (SiC) power conversion system achieves a maximum conversion efficiency of 99.2 percent and, combined with 684 Ah stacked cells, delivers a round-trip efficiency of over 92 percent, with an energy density of up to 256 kWh per square metre.

Rated IP55 for ingress protection and C5 for corrosion resistance, the system operates between -30°C and 50°C and tolerates up to 30 percent grid harmonics. Sungrow told Mirror Business that the C5 rating helps systems withstand salt mist in coastal industrial areas, while the IP55 rating protects against dust and water jets, a consideration for factories along Sri Lanka’s coastline. The company says a new PowerStack 3.0 unit rolls off its smart production line every 25 minutes, or more than 50 units a day on a round-the-clock schedule. Each unit undergoes over 500 tests, and factory pre-installation allows grid connection within two hours of arrival on site.

Sungrow is also pushing intelligence down to individual solar panels through its Smart Module Solutions, which are designed to enhance module-level protection, optimise energy yields and safeguard long-term returns.

Data also shapes how Sungrow manages the end of a battery’s life. Through a battery passport system, the company records the source, materials and component-level data of each battery, keeping the information until the end of its life and passing it on to recyclers, the company noted at the media briefing. Such traceability will become increasingly relevant as Sri Lanka scales up battery storage at both grid and household level.

Sungrow PV and Storage Business Group Vice President Leo Li described the company’s product philosophy as “Simplicity, Evolved”, built around simple choice, simple installation, simple returns and simple safety. “We go one step further, so our customers can take one step less. We engineer for simplicity, so your experience is truly effortless,” he remarked.

For an industry long judged on cell chemistry and capacity, Sungrow’s message in Hefei was that the intelligence wrapped around the battery may matter just as much.
