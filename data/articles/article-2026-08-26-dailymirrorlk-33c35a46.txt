Closing the Stable Door After the Horse Has Bolted

We Sri Lankans have become notorious for closing the stable door after the horse has bolted. The past few decades provide ample evidence of our failure to act despite having warnings in advance.

The 2004 tsunami, the Easter Sunday attacks in 2019, COVID-19 in 2020 and Cyclone Ditwah in 2024 all demonstrated the devastating consequences of delayed action. In each case, there were warnings and indications of what was coming, yet we failed to respond with the urgency required. The result was the needless loss of many lives.

Today, we are hearing repeated warnings about the possible impact of El Niño on Sri Lanka’s weather patterns. Some areas are already facing threats to water resources, while others are experiencing heavy rains and flooding. Meteorological experts have been warning of possible repercussions, yet there appears to be little visible preparedness.

We cannot afford to wait for disaster to strike before taking action. The Sinhala saying, “Kotiya aawo, Kotiya aawo”—“The tiger is coming”—aptly describes our pathetic habit of ignoring repeated warnings.

When the tiger finally comes, we discover that we are not ready.

This time, let us prove that we have learned from the past. The time to prepare is before the disaster—not after it.
