BOI puts first-half realised FDI at US$ 450mn, confident of meeting US$ 1.5bn target

Realised FDI of approximately US$ 450mn in first six months, equal to 30% of 2026 target

Second half needs US$ 1.05bn, more than twice the first-half pace

Sri Lanka’s Board of Investment (BOI) received approximately US$ 450 million in realised foreign direct investment (FDI) in the first six months of 2026 and is confident of meeting its US$ 1.5 billion target for the year, BOI Director General Dr. Sulakshana Jayawardena told Mirror Business.

Jayawardena stressed that the figure represented money actually received rather than commitments, explaining that an investment is counted as realised once the investor has brought in at least 10 percent of the project value, which he described as a basic requirement.

He noted that domestic  investments during the period exceeded the foreign inflows.

The first-half figure amounts to roughly 43 percent of the US$ 1,057 million in FDI the BOI reported for the whole of 2025 and 30 percent of this year’s target. At an average of about US$ 75 million a month, inflows have so far trailed 2025’s monthly average of close to US$ 88 million by around 15 percent, which leaves the agency needing to secure some US$ 1.05 billion in the remaining six months, or about US$ 175 million a month, more than twice its first-half pace.

However, Jayawardena remained upbeat on the outlook, pointing to a steady flow of applications that includes several large and sizeable investments alongside a number of smaller projects. The BOI has also compiled a country-wise breakdown of investment progress, he noted.

The latest figure is considerably higher than the approximately US$ 250 million in realised FDI he publicly cited for the period to June in early July, when he defined realised investments as those remitted into the country through Inward Investment Accounts.

Meanwhile, the Sinopec refinery project in Hambantota, the country’s largest ever FDI commitment at US$ 3.7 billion, is yet to move forward, as the Ministry of Finance has not finalised the term sheet and the matter remains with the Ministry of Power and Energy, according to Jayawardena.

The BOI is yet to formally publish its first-half figures, with the Director General indicating that data, including export figures, is still being collected and combined zone by zone ahead of release.

In early July, Jayawardena outlined a reform agenda aimed at lifting investment, including a digital single-window approval platform targeted for launch before the end of this year, an Investment Protection Act already cleared by the Attorney General’s Department, and a review of land allocation rules, given that transferring State land for investment projects currently takes between 18 and 24 months.

FDI inflows in 2025 rose 72 percent from a year earlier, with Singapore, India, France, the Netherlands and Luxembourg the top five sources. (NF)
