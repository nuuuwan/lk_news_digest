Sri Lanka seeks stronger China-South Asia Ocean cooperation

Colombo, Sep. 20 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka is fully prepared to further strengthen long-term, practical cooperation with China and South Asian nations across key sectors, including marine science, fisheries, aquaculture, the blue economy, technology, investments, and human resource development, Minister of Fisheries, Aquatic, and Ocean Resources Ramalingam Chandrasekar announced.

Delivering the keynote address at the opening of the "China–South Asian Countries Marine Cooperation Forum" held on September 17 and 18 in the Qingdao West Coast New Area, the Minister emphasized Sri Lanka’s commitment to sustainable ocean governance. Minister Chandrasekar attended the event during a three-day official visit to China following a formal invitation from Zexun Wei, Director General of the First Institute of Oceanography under China's Ministry of Natural Resources.

The high-level forum brought together over 100 government officials, experts, and academics from China, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, the Maldives, and Pakistan. Key discussions centered on climate change, marine environmental protection, ocean disaster monitoring, early warning systems, disaster risk reduction, and capacity building. The Sri Lankan delegation accompanying the Minister included Ministry Secretary Dr. B.K. Kolitha Kamal Jinadasa, National Aquatic Resources Research and Development Agency (NARA) Chairman Dr. Sanath Hettiarachchi, NARA Director General Dr. K.H.M.L. Amaralal, and Senior Oceanography Researcher Dr. W.N.C. Priyadarshani.

Highlighting China's leadership as a top global producer in fisheries and aquaculture, Minister Chandrasekar noted that China's scientific advancements, policy frameworks, and systematic planning offer valuable models for Sri Lanka and the wider region. He stressed that Sri Lanka's future fisheries strategy focuses on alleviating pressure on existing wild fish stocks by advancing aquaculture, ocean-based industries, value-added products, and modern technology. Reaffirming Sri Lanka's diplomatic stance, the Minister added that the nation will engage in regional fisheries and maritime affairs while upholding its non-aligned policy.

Emphasizing the importance of regional collaboration, the Minister noted that shared threats such as climate change, marine pollution, resource depletion, and Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) fishing extend beyond national borders. Consequently, he called for enhanced joint research, data sharing, marine observation, and disaster early warning mechanisms among South Asian countries and China to safeguard ocean resources while expanding livelihoods for coastal communities.

Addressing ongoing legislative changes, Minister Chandrasekar pointed out that both Sri Lanka and China are actively reforming their respective fisheries management frameworks. China’s broad revisions to its Fisheries Law are scheduled to come into effect on May 1, 2026, while Sri Lanka is currently drafting a comprehensive new Fisheries Act to replace the Fisheries and Aquatic Resources Act No. 2 of 1996. Expressing gratitude for China’s ongoing technical assistance and training programs, he urged the expansion of these initiatives into emerging fields such as blue finance, artificial intelligence (AI) in marine technology, and ocean science.

During his visit, Minister Chandrasekar also attended the "2026 Global Ocean Development Forum" in Qingdao on September 17, which focused on sustainable ocean economic growth and global governance. Underscoring the vision that "the ocean does not divide us, but connects us," the Minister concluded that future marine cooperation must balance resource conservation with creating sustainable economic opportunities for coastal communities dependent on the sea.
