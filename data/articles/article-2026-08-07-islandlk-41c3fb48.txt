99x rebuilds flood-damaged IT labs restoring digital learning for rural schools in Mahiyanganaya

Last December, floods across Sri Lanka left many communities having to rebuild their lives. Homes and livelihoods were the immediate focus, but many schools were also impacted. Several lost the facilities that students relied on every day, including IT laboratories where they learnt digital skills, completed practical lessons and, for many in rural communities, had their first experience using a computer. Without these spaces, learning was interrupted, widening a digital gap that many rural schools already face.

As the country shifted its focus from immediate relief to long-term recovery, 99x, a global product engineering company rooted in Sri Lanka, launched several recovery initiatives under its Corporate Relief Fund, bringing together employees, customers and partners to support communities affected by the disaster. One of its largest initiatives came through the Prime Minister’s Office, in collaboration with SLASSCOM, to help restore technological infrastructure in areas affected by the floods.

Recognising how important access to technology is for students, 99x partnered in the initiative to rebuild IT laboratories in schools that had lost them during the disaster. Over a few months, teams worked closely with the Prime Minister’s Office, SLASSCOM and school communities, selecting three schools in Mahiyanganaya to establish IT laboratories that build the digital skills becoming increasingly important in today’s world.

In July, volunteers from 99x travelled to Mahiyanganaya to complete the restoration of IT laboratories at Hebarawa Maha Vidyalaya, Bathalayaya Maha Vidyalaya and Keselpotha Maha Vidyalaya. The project included setting up laptops, classroom furniture and the supporting infrastructure needed to bring the laboratories back into use. Students gathered outside with excitement, waiting to see the new labs. The principal and teachers spoke about finally being able to restart practical IT lessons after months without the facilities they depended on.
