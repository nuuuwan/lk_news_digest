Committee proposes community service, work camps to ease prison overcrowding

Colombo, Sep. 22 (Daily Mirror) - Officials have proposed referring minor offenders serving sentences of less than four years, or those unable to pay relatively small fines, to community service or work camps through expedited judicial procedures as part of measures to ease severe overcrowding in Sri Lankan prisons.

The proposal was discussed at a meeting of the Sectoral Oversight Committee on Governance, Justice and Civil Protection, chaired by MP Dr Najith Indika, at the Parliamentary complex. It was proposed that such cases be dealt with through expedited judicial procedures.

The committee examined a range of legal, administrative and infrastructure measures aimed at addressing the growing pressure on the country’s prison system.

Officials said the high number of suspects held in remand custody, compared to convicted prisoners, was a major factor contributing to prison overcrowding.

They also pointed to delays in obtaining Government Analyst reports in drug-related cases as a major issue, with some suspects reportedly remaining in remand custody for periods ranging from 18 months to two years while awaiting the reports.

The committee discussed amending existing laws to allow persons arrested for minor drug offences or drug use to be referred to rehabilitation centres following a medical assessment, instead of being incarcerated.

The committee also called for the rapid introduction of remote video conferencing facilities for court proceedings involving inmates.

Officials noted that the daily transportation of prisoners to courts places a significant burden on prison staff and creates additional security risks. They said virtual court proceedings could help reduce staff shortages, minimise transportation-related risks and streamline judicial proceedings, subject to the necessary amendments to the legal framework.

Special attention was also given to vulnerable categories of inmates, including elderly, fully disabled and chronically ill prisoners.

The committee considered mechanisms to facilitate the release of such prisoners based on recommendations made by medical boards.

A proposal was also discussed to establish a special judicial procedure to expedite cases involving mothers who are imprisoned with children below the age of five.

The committee noted the need to prevent children from being exposed to long-term psychological and developmental harm resulting from prolonged stays in prison environments.

On infrastructure, the committee stressed the need to renovate deteriorating prison buildings and complete construction of the new Millewa-Horana prison complex.

It also discussed making better use of existing prison capacity by transferring inmates from severely overcrowded facilities to prisons with available space.
