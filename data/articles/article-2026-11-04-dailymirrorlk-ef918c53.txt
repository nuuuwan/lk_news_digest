Kandy named world’s calmest city getaway, Colombo outranks Phuket and Bali

Kandy tops Tiny Away’s 972-city index with score of 0.1, greenest of all cities studied

Kandy has emerged as the world’s calmest city getaway for holidaymakers looking to escape crowds and bright lights, topping a 972-city index of environmental intensity. Colombo placed 64th, finishing ahead of regional favourites Phuket and Bali, according to research by cabin stays company Tiny Away.

The Overstimulation Index scores cities on nine environmental factors, namely population density, night-time brightness, PM2.5 air pollution, green space, urban heat, built-up land, humidity, summer heat and winter cold.

The first four carry the heaviest weighting, while the three climate measures account for just 9 percent of the total weighting. Each score is a percentile rank against the full sample, placing Kandy’s 0.1 at the calmest extreme of the scale and Colombo’s 6.6 within the calmest 7 percent of cities studied.

Kandy, a UNESCO World Heritage city and a fixture on the island’s Cultural Triangle circuit, recorded the highest vegetation cover of all 972 cities at around 73 percent. Its night-time brightness was the eighth lowest in the study and less than a fifth of Colombo’s, while built-up cover in the areas where residents live stood at just 11.5 percent, roughly a third of Tokyo’s.

The hill capital’s core was also 1.5°C cooler at night than the rural ring around it between June and August, the reverse of the urban heat island effect that left Colombo’s core 0.8°C warmer and Tokyo’s 2.6°C warmer than their surroundings. Tiny Away pointed to Kandy’s vegetation, low built-up cover and dim night lights as the likely factors, though it cautioned that the satellite figure was best read as “relative rather than precise”.

Kandy’s lead was built on greenery and dark skies rather than air quality. Its average PM2.5 reading of 19.1 micrograms per cubic metre placed it around the middle of the table, broadly in line with Colombo’s 19.8, which leaves both cities with room to improve.

Both cities were assessed on the Global Human Settlement Layer’s wider urban footprint rather than on municipal limits.

That footprint spans 273 square kilometres and around 696,000 people for Kandy, and 1,206 square kilometres and some 4.7 million people for Colombo, with Kandy’s footprint taking in a good deal of the greenery surrounding the city.

Colombo’s population density of 7,680 people per square kilometre was more than twice Kandy’s, yet the commercial capital’s night skies were still darker than those of around 94 percent of cities in the sample. The index measures environmental load alone and does not factor in noise, traffic or crowding.

Among the region’s best-known escapes, Colombo’s 6.6 left it calmer than Mataram on Lombok (10.4), Phuket (15.2), Yogyakarta (15.8) and Bali’s capital Denpasar (18.3). Meanwhile, Bangkok was the calmest of 11 major Asian city hubs at 55.0, ahead of Tokyo (64.7) and Singapore (68.3).

The rankings could hand the island’s tourism promoters a timely pitch, as both cities have lost ground in visitor itineraries. As per the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority’s latest survey of departing tourists, the Temple of the Tooth in Kandy featured in 51.3 percent of itineraries in 2024/25, down from 64.9 percent in 2018/19, while Colombo slipped to 46.2 percent from 68.3 percent as Galle Fort climbed to the top.

Tiny Away noted that holidaymakers were increasingly swapping packed itineraries for slower mornings, crowded streets for open landscapes and constant notifications for time spent reconnecting with nature, a shift that plays to the strengths of both cities.

“People are increasingly choosing holidays based on how they want to feel, not just where they want to go. While many travellers love the energy of bustling city destinations, we’re seeing growing demand for places that allow people to slow down, disconnect from constant stimulation and genuinely recharge,” Tiny Away co-founder Jeff Yeo observed.

The study also showed how much a short trip out of a major hub can change the experience, with Ha Long scoring around a third of Hanoi’s score. Sri Lanka goes a step further, pairing a comparatively calm gateway city with the world’s calmest hill capital a few hours inland, a combination that lends itself naturally to slow-travel and wellness marketing.

Tiny Away offers nature-based tiny house stays designed around disconnecting from everyday pressures and reconnecting with the outdoors. The company operates across Australia and in Asian markets such as Malaysia and Japan. (NF)
