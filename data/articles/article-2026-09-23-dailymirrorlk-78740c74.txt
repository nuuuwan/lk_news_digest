5,741-km elephant fence gets major resource boost

Colombo, Sep. 23 (Daily Mirror) - The Government has launched a major programme to strengthen the resources available to wildlife officials responsible for maintaining Sri Lanka’s 5,741-kilometre electric fence network, as part of a long-term strategy to address the country’s human-elephant conflict.

The initiative, spearheaded by the Ministry of Environment, is based on the policy that effective management of the conflict requires frontline wildlife officials to be provided with adequate human and physical resources.

The human-elephant conflict has developed over several decades and has been linked to factors including unplanned development and habitat fragmentation. The Government said the latest programme seeks to move away from temporary measures towards a scientific, data-driven and long-term approach.

The allocation of resources has been made following assessments of the extent of electric fencing across the country, the intensity of human and elephant casualties and the terrain conditions in different areas.

Under the programme, modern motorcycles have been provided to wildlife regulators to strengthen regular supervision and maintenance of electric fences. The objective is to enable one regulator to oversee an average of around 40 kilometres of fencing.

A total of 36 motorcycles have been allocated to Polonnaruwa, 22 to the Eastern region, 17 to Anuradhapura, 13 to Trincomalee, 12 to the Southern region and 12 to the North-Western region.

The vehicles are expected to enable officers to reach fence locations quickly, identify sections requiring repairs and conduct regular inspections of areas vulnerable to fence failures.

The operational fleet of the wildlife sector is also being restructured, with 16 double cabs, 84 single cabs, nine field tractors, one hand tractor and three specialised deep-forest vehicles being deployed to Range Forest Offices and Beat Offices.

These vehicles will support elephant drives, night operations and field activities in rugged terrain while improving the ability of wildlife officers to respond to conflict situations.

Dedicated cabs have also been provided to regional veterinary units in Pandulagama, Lunugamvehera, Nikaweratiya and Giritale, as well as the Elephant Transit Home.

The vehicles are intended to strengthen the emergency response capacity of veterinary teams and enable them to reach injured wild elephants more quickly for treatment.

The Government said the new resources would allow wildlife officers to reach conflict locations promptly, carry out daily inspections of electric fences, protect agricultural lands and property and respond to situations threatening both human and elephant lives.

The expenditure for the initiative is being funded through the Wildlife Preservation Fund, without placing an additional burden on the Treasury.

The programme is part of the Government’s broader objective of strengthening wildlife conservation through practical and scientific measures while moving national policy towards sustainable human-elephant coexistence.
