October 7:  Lies and the licence for the genocide of Palestinians

Gaza where humanity, together with international law, remains buried under the rubble

History did not begin on October 7, 2023. That day, and the horror associated with it, was merely another entry in the long continuum of Palestinian suffering. There were numerous peace efforts, but each ultimately became part of a sinister plan that denied Palestinians their freedom.

What is appalling is the normalisation of the injustice Palestinians have endured under the post-World War II global order led by the United States. Instead of upholding justice, the US has backed Israel unreservedly since the creation of the Zionist state in 1948.

Equally complicit, if not more so, is Britain, which set the process in motion with the 1917 Balfour Declaration, enabling the establishment of a Jewish homeland in Palestine as though the land were Britain’s ancestral property.

October 7, 2023 marked the breaking point of Palestinian patience. Stories that captured the horror of that day paid scant attention to what Palestinians in Gaza had been enduring for years. Israel regularly inflicted death and destruction on Palestinians. The UN described 2022 as the deadliest year for Palestinians in nearly two decades.

October 7 was Hamas’s way of asking, “How long can an oppressed people continue to endure violence and humiliation?” What Hamas did on that day has been condemned as terrorism. But what followed was genocide.

Shocking new details are now confirming what independent analysts had long argued. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Mossad knew of the October 7 attack in advance. According to recent Israeli and US media reports, Egypt’s intelligence chief personally visited Tel Aviv to warn his counterpart about the impending attack by Hamas on an Israeli border town.

In statecraft devoid of morality, attacks are sometimes allowed to happen so that larger objectives can be achieved. From Iraq’s invasion of Kuwait in 1990 and the September 11, 2001 attacks on the United States, to Sri Lanka’s Easter Sunday attacks, intelligence warnings were allegedly not acted upon.

Moreover, Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, the President of the United Arab Emirates, regarded as Israel’s closest Gulf Arab ally, reportedly phoned Netanyahu personally and warned him about a major operation Hamas was preparing. No action was taken to prevent it.

Facing a knife-edge general election on October 27, Netanyahu flew to Abu Dhabi on September 27 to meet the UAE president and urge him to deny the report. The secret meeting at Abu Dhabi Airport took place three days before the Flydubai incident, in which an Omani co-pilot allegedly attacked the Indian pilot in what has been described as a botched terror attack. The incident has raised more questions than it has answered.

So, Netanyahu deliberately chose not to prevent the October 7 attack. With this claim gaining traction, blaming Hamas for the attack tells only part of the story. No sooner had the Hamas attack occurred than reports emerged that babies had been beheaded and burned alive in ovens, women had been raped, and civilians had been murdered. Then US President Joe Biden seized on these stories and claimed he had seen photographs of beheaded babies. Days later, he said he had not seen such pictures, but by then the lies had given Israel the licence to unleash its genocide.

It has now been claimed that no babies were burned or beheaded and that allegations of women being raped lack supporting evidence. It has also been alleged that most of the 1,200 Israeli deaths occurred not during the Hamas attack but during Israel’s counterattack. Many reportedly became victims of the Israeli military doctrine known as the Hannibal Directive. According to this doctrine, Israeli forces may kill Israeli captives to prevent terrorists using them as leverage in hostage situations. The Western media amplified horror stories without verifying them. Such ugly journalism enabled the demonization of Hamas, and by extension Palestinians in the Gaza Strip. Meanwhile, Western leaders kept saying that Israel had the right to defend itself. They would not say that Palestinians have the right to resist occupation. Instead, they applied the label of terrorism to Palestinian resistance. What could be more morally reprehensible in politics than labelling a resistance movement as terrorism?

An estimated 75,000 Palestinians, including 20,000 children, were killed in this genocide. Netanyahu referred to them as Amalekites, drawing parallels with a biblical-era people mentioned in 1 Samuel 15:3. The verse depicts God as ordering the Israelites to “attack the Amalekites and totally destroy all that belongs to them. Do not spare them; put to death men and women, children and infants, cattle and sheep, camels and donkeys.”

Bradford University researchers have estimated that the total amount of explosives dropped on the Gaza Strip exceeds 70,000 tonnes, roughly equivalent to six Hiroshima-sized atomic bombs. Hardly a building remains standing on its foundations.

Hospitals protected by the Geneva Conventions were razed to the ground. Schools and universities were pulverised by 2,000-pound bombs supplied by the United States. Historic mosques and churches were reduced to rubble. The flow of food aid was restricted. Children who came to collect food were shot dead. The United Nations Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA), which attended to the basic humanitarian needs of Palestinian civilians, was declared a banned organisation.

As humanity, together with international law, remained buried under a mountain of rubble, the International Criminal Court, the United Nations Human Rights Council, and its rapporteurs were subjected to sanctions and pressure by the United States, thereby facilitating the genocide.

The live-streamed genocide carried out in Gaza by Netanyahu and Biden, dubbed “Genocide Joe” by his critics, had other accomplices. Germany and Canada supplied weapons. Britain provided weapons and shared intelligence. Shamelessly, we still call them civilised and roll out the red carpet for genocide enablers.

Arab and Islamic nations did nothing, militarily speaking, to stop the genocide. The only military responses in support of the Palestinians came from Yemen’s Ansarallah movement and Lebanon’s Hezbollah. While South Africa and a handful of nations filed a case at the International Court of Justice accusing Israel of genocide, most Muslim countries considered it unwise to engage Israel militarily. For them, it was business as usual. To the Palestinians, they offered only empty words, reinforcing the popular saying that Muslims are good only at fighting other Muslims, as the war in Yemen amply demonstrates.

Also complicit were countries that once championed the Palestinian freedom cause as frontline members of the Non-Aligned Movement. India supplied shiploads of weapons to Israel, prompting Amnesty International and other human rights organisations to accuse it of supporting the genocide in Gaza. Sri Lanka’s complicity lies in its export of cheap labour and allowing war-weary Israeli soldiers to find rest and recreation in exclusive enclaves in Arugam Bay, Weligama, and other tourist destinations, while occasionally issuing perfunctory statements supporting the two-state solution, words that were better left unsaid than said.

However, what slowed down the genocide was not Trump’s intervention, his vision of turning Gaza into a Riviera, or his later 20-point plan envisaging a Board of Peace, disarmament, and a self-funded international stabilization force. Rather, it was growing international outrage, especially student protests in the United States and other Western countries. The voice of the five-year-old Palestinian girl Hind Rajab spoke louder than the empty words of Islamic nations and the so-called Non-Aligned countries.
