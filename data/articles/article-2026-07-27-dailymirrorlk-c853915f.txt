Findings of COPE, COPA, COPF – are they media shows?

Two amendments to the Parliamentary Standing Orders (SO) were passed on April 7 allowing the Committee on Public Accounts (COPA) and the Committee on Public Enterprises (COPE), to directly report to the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption (CIABOC) or the Inspector General of Police (IGP) when they find evidence of fraud, corruption or bribery during their deliberations, for taking necessary action regarding such matters.

Accordingly, a new paragraph was inserted into SO 119(4) related to COPA, stating: “If the Committee on Public Accounts, in examining the reports presented to the Committee by the Auditor-General, comes to the conclusion that serious financial frauds, acts of bribery or corruption have occurred, the Committee may refer such matters directly to the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption or to the Inspector-General of Police.”

A separate paragraph is also added to SO 120 (4) related to COPE, stating the same in respect of findings during its sessions.

Accordingly, Parliament on July 21 approved two motions to refer reports submitted to it by the COPA and the COPE, containing findings from investigations into various institutions, to the Attorney General and the CIABOC, respectively.

Report of COPA inquiry conducted on October 24 last year on the scheme implemented from 1 May 2022 to 15 September 2023 for issuing permits to Sri Lankans employed overseas to import fully electric vehicles using foreign remittances is thus to be referred to the Attorney General.

A separate motion was also approved by the House to refer to CIABOC the reports compiled by the COPE on the activities of National Gem and Jewellery Authority, the Sri Jayewardenepura General Hospital Board, the Land Reform Commission, Sabaragamuwa University of Sri Lanka, the Civil Aviation Authority of Sri Lanka, Lanka Sathosa Limited, the Sri Lanka Bureau of Foreign Employment, and the National Savings Bank.

We salute the Parliament for amending the Standing Orders that enabled the House to allow its key oversight committees to refer their findings on shady deals to the AG and the CIABOC and for adopting these subsequent two motions under those amendments.

The Daily Mirror has been pressing the authorities over the years to find a mechanism to take follow up action on corruption in public institutions that have been revealed during the deliberations of the COPE, COPA and Committee on Public Finance (COPF), pointing out that the sessions of these committees have become mere media shows due to absence of such follow up actions.

Several Chairmen and members of these committees had also made suggestions during the past few years to develop mechanisms to refer the negative findings of these committees that had sometimes exposed corruption involving billions of rupees, to the relevant law enforcement authorities.  Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB) MP Eran Wickramaratne on October 25, 2021, questioned in Parliament the purpose of the COPE if it cannot take punitive action against frauds and corruption in State-owned entities that were being exposed during the deliberations of that committee.

Then, Prof. Charitha Herath as the Chairman of the COPE in 2022 suggested during proceedings of his committee to take up the follow-up issue in Parliament and create a mechanism for action against the culprits. In October 2023, COPF Chairman Dr. Harsha De Silva told Parliament that having a representative from the Attorney General’s Department present during meetings of the PAC and COPE would facilitate the utilisation of evidence uncovered during such meetings in future court proceedings.

Both Prof. Herath and Dr. De Silva as Chairmen of their respective committees had requested the then Speaker to amend the standing orders of the Parliament to have a representative of the Attorney General at the sessions of their committees, so that culprits of frauds in public institutions could be taken to task.

Yet, nothing happened, corruption continued unbated and revelations of frauds during these committee meetings too,  continued to be media shows. No government in the past was interested in formulating a mechanism to bring the culprits of corruption to the book. We continue with our pressure on the government to strengthen the mechanisms against corruption, frauds and waste of public funds.
