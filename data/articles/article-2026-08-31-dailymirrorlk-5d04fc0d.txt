Sri Lanka crosses 1.5mn tourist arrival milestone, eyes 2.5mn and 2027 breakthrough

Cumulative arrivals cross 1.51mn by late August; SLTDA chief confident of topping 2.5mn on winter surge

Strategic pivot sets 2027 as breakthrough year, prioritising revenue yields and sustainable, experience-based tourism over volume

Infrastructure push and niche product development underway with a special focus on Northern and Eastern provinces ahead of April global campaign

Sri Lanka’s cumulative tourist arrivals crossed the 1.5 million mark this year, reaching 1,517,000 by the evening of August 27, newly appointed Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) Chairman Suranjith Wevita said, even as arrivals remain around 2 percent below last year’s levels amid fallout from the ongoing conflict in West Asia.

Speaking to Mirror Business, Wevita said he expects the country to comfortably surpass the 2.5 million arrivals mark by the end of the year. The final four months, coinciding with the start of the peak winter season, are expected to drive the bulk of the growth needed to hit that target. Reaching 2.5 million would mean adding close to one million arrivals over that period. While the monthly average recorded so far this year is approximately 190,000, arrivals typically move closer to the 300,000 mark during the peak months of November and December.

Wevita noted that while broader European inbound travel has faced headwinds from the escalating West Asia conflict, the downturn was particularly visible in the Netherlands during July and August—the traditional peak summer holiday period for Dutch travellers. Heightened apprehension over flights transiting Middle Eastern airspace, alongside flight rerouting and suspensions by major European carriers such as KLM and Air France since January, has driven up travel times and costs across key corridors connecting Europe to Sri Lanka.

Balancing that setback, Wevita pointed to encouraging signs on connectivity. Boosting winter traffic, Russia’s S7 Airlines recently announced plans to launch direct flights connecting Novosibirsk in Siberia with Colombo. Additionally, Australian low-cost carrier Jetstar began direct Melbourne-Colombo flights in August, adding more than 100,000 low-fare seats a year to the route, while British Airways is set to resume its London Gatwick-Colombo service on October 23 after a decade-long absence. He also cited a French carrier and several other airlines starting or resuming Sri Lanka flights, without giving further details.

Looking beyond this year, Wevita described 2027 as a potential breakthrough year for Sri Lankan tourism, driven by a strategic pivot from merely chasing arrival numbers to actively maximizing revenue yields. To realize this shift, he noted the SLTDA will concentrate heavily on developing essential infrastructure and laying the structural groundwork required to support premium, experience-based, and sustainably branded offerings. He said this push builds on an interim marketing campaign already under way, ahead of a full-scale global promotional rollout scheduled to launch next April.

A central plank of that revenue-driven strategy, Wevita said, is broadening tourism income beyond the traditional southern coastal belt and Colombo, with the Northern and Eastern provinces a particular focus under his leadership. He said feasibility studies for a US$ 100 million World Bank-backed project have now been completed, alongside similar groundwork by other multilateral agencies, paving the way for Sri Lanka to roll out new experience-based tourism products targeting niche market segments as early as next year.

The push aligns with World Bank’s wider engagement in the two provinces, including its REVIVE programme covering Jaffna, Pasikuda, Trincomalee and Arugam Bay under the government’s Tourism Strategic Plan 2026-2030, and a separate US$ 100 million loan facility the Cabinet approved in August for broader regional development.

Wevita expressed confidence that these initiatives, taken together, would help Sri Lanka exceed US$ 8 billion in tourism earnings by 2030 — a target consistent with the government’s National Tourism Vision outlined in the 2026 Budget, which also envisages four million annual arrivals by the same year.

Wevita’s comments mark one of his first major public remarks on arrivals since assuming the SLTDA chairmanship on August 18, succeeding Buddhika Hewawasam, who continues to lead the Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau (SLTPB) under a restructured leadership arrangement announced by the Tourism Ministry. Sri Lanka recorded a record 2,362,521 tourist arrivals in 2025, generating around US$ 3.2 billion in revenue; the full-year revenue target for 2026 has since been revised to approximately US$ 4.2 billion.
