Clash between Minister and Deputy Minister reaches boiling point over SriLankan Airlines

Colomob, Aug. 5 (Daily Mirror) - A major administrative crisis has erupted within the Government, as the clash between Ports and Civil Aviation Minister Anura Karunathilake and Deputy Minister Janitha Kodithuwakku reaches boiling point over the controversial push to appoint an interim Chief Executive Officer at loss-making SriLankan Airlines, according several sources within Sri Lankan Airlines.

According to them, the friction peaked on Monday when the Minister directly intervened in a formal inquiry convened by his Deputy to reinstate a suspended airline employee. They noted that the Minister effectively undermined the proceedings by delaying the inquiry from the morning until the evening before shutting down the session prematurely. Although the aviation portfolio falls under the purview of the Deputy Minister, it was emphasized that ministerial interference continues to stall efforts to resolve serious administrative disputes at the national carrier.

The escalating power struggle comes against the backdrop of a prolonged and heavily criticized leadership vacuum at the airline. During a recent parliamentary Sectoral Oversight Committee hearing, Chairman MP S. M. Marikkar directed sharp criticism at the carrier's 16-month failure to appoint a CEO. Pointing to the inability to secure a candidate despite calling two interview rounds that yielded hundreds of applications, it was highlighted as a tragic situation that the airline could not find a suitable external or internal candidate for over a year and four months.

Seeking to address the vacuum, the SriLankan Airlines Chairman recently announced plans to appoint an interim CEO from within the airline's own ranks. Following the shortlisting of two internal executives three weeks ago—the Head of IT and the Head of Commercial—Minister Karunathilake has handpicked the Head of IT to assume the chief executive role without consulting his Deputy, several sources familiar with the matter told the Daily Mirror.

However, the move to elevate the Head of IT was abruptly stalled after a widespread social media campaign surfaced, exposing severe allegations of corrupt activities within the airline's IT department.

A formal grievance letter warned of a "serious escalation of mismanagement in the IT division under the stewardship of Mr. Chamara Perera". The document further alleged that Perera "has become a serious threat to the future of the Airlines Business and serious damage to the professional outlook of the organisation and its culture".

While the public backlash prompted the Minister to temporarily hold back the official appointment for the time being, he remains determined to push ahead with the elevation as interim CEO.

Adding to the tension, the JVP-affiliated union remains deeply frustrated after being entirely excluded from Monday's inquiry, with union representatives forced to wait by the door outside the proceedings.

According to several sources. The union is now planning to take action against the alleged corrupt activities of the IT head, ahead of his potential appointment, further intensifying the operational and political friction surrounding SriLankan Airlines.
