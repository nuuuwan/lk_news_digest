Sri Lanka secures 7.5% weight in JP Morgan’s new frontier local currency bond index

SL’s allocation sits half a percentage point below the ceiling on any single country

Many of the markets with the largest weightings, including Sri Lanka, have staged strong rallies in recent years following crises

Economists expect the index to help deepen local currency debt markets

Sri Lanka has been assigned a 7.5 percent weighting in J.P. Morgan’s new frontier market local currency government bond index, placing it among the eight largest components of a benchmark set to track close to US$ 330 billion of debt across 26 countries, according to details of the index released this week.

The Wall Street bank will launch the GBI-EM Edge by the end of September, a J.P. Morgan note to investors seen by Reuters showed. The benchmark arrives almost two decades after the bank’s hard-currency NEXGEM frontier index and spans markets ranging from Albania to Zambia.

Sri Lanka’s allocation sits half a percentage point below the ceiling on any single country. Vietnam, Egypt, Morocco, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Kazakhstan each carry the full 8 percent weighting, with Nigeria close behind Sri Lanka at 7.4 percent, as per J.P. Morgan’s index research dated September 14. Taken together, the seven markets at or near the cap make up 55.5 percent of the index, a share that climbs to 62.9 percent once Nigeria is added.

African countries will account for almost 45 percent of the index, while Frontier Asia, led by Vietnam, Kazakhstan, Pakistan and Bangladesh, will make up nearly a third, Reuters reported. Many of the markets with the largest weightings, including Sri Lanka, have staged strong rallies in recent years after crises. The index offers a markedly higher return profile than the bank’s established emerging market gauge. Its nominal yield of almost 10.4 percent is roughly 440 basis points above the mainstream EM local currency index, while back-testing shows returns would have been 1.2 percentage points higher on an annualised basis over the past nine years.

Inclusion matters because international investors tend to gravitate towards bonds that feature in such benchmarks. While FTSE Russell has operated a comparable index since 2021, J.P. Morgan’s indices carry greater prominence among emerging market fund managers, who also use them to measure their own performance. Analysts estimate tradeable local currency emerging market debt has trebled over the past decade to around US$ 1 trillion, meaning the new index would cover roughly a third of that pool.

Economists also expect the index to help deepen local currency debt markets, an objective long promoted by the World Bank and the IMF to reduce the risk of debt crises triggered when currency collapses leave governments unable to service dollar debt. Angola, which features in the index, cited this as one reason for opening its US$ 18.6 billion domestic bond market more widely last week.

J.P. Morgan, in its note, likened many of these markets to core emerging markets in the early 2000s, pointing to high nominal carry, improving market infrastructure and periodic volatility. According to the World Bank, frontier economies are home to a fifth of the global population yet account for just 3.1 percent of global capital flows and less than 5 percent of global GDP. However, their populations are projected to grow by 800 million over the next 25 years, more than the rest of the world combined.

Thomas Christiansen, Head of EM Fixed Income at fund manager UBP in London, sees the launch as a reflection of the growth in frontier market investing. “I don’t think this index would have been possible 10 years ago,” he told Reuters.
