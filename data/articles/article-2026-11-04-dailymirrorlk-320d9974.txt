TikTok removes over 430,000 videos in Sri Lanka in 2Q

TikTok removed more than 430,000 videos in Sri Lanka for violating its community guidelines in the second quarter of 2026, while reporting that nearly 13,000 videos were subsequently restored.

The platform’s latest Community Guidelines Enforcement Report, covering April to June, recorded 430,357 video removals in Sri Lanka. TikTok said it proactively identified 99.4 percent of those videos before the users reported them.

Of the proactively removed videos, 93.9 percent were taken down within 24 hours of posting, according to the company.

However, TikTok also reported that 12,960 videos were restored in Sri Lanka.

The company supplied no comparison with Sri Lankan removals in the preceding quarter, limiting an assessment of whether enforcement activity increased or declined. It also did not provide a country-level breakdown of violations.

Globally, TikTok removed 188.3 million videos during the quarter, equivalent to approximately 0.5 percent of all content uploaded to the platform, according to the report.

Automated detection technologies accounted for 183.3 million of those removals or about 97.4 percent of the total, underscoring the scale of the platform’s reliance on automated moderation. About 6.3 million videos were reinstated globally following further review.

Enforcement also extended to comments and accounts. TikTok removed 278.4 million comments worldwide, up approximately 17.2 percent, from 237.5 million in the first quarter. The increase reflects a higher number of comment removals, although the figures alone do not establish whether the prevalence of rule-breaking content increased.

TikTok also removed 146.8 million fake accounts globally during the quarter, alongside a further 26.8 million accounts suspected of belonging to users under the age of 13. The release did not disclose the corresponding figures for Sri Lanka.

Among the global video removals, sensitive or mature themes accounted for 19.5 percent, while body exposure and sexualised behaviour represented 13.8 percent. Violations of safety and civility standards accounted for 13.7 percent and dangerous activities and challenges for 13.5 percent.

A further 1.8 percent of removed videos were flagged under rules covering edited media and artificial intelligence-generated content.
