October exports jump 11.6% YoY

Merchandise exports rise 9.7% to US $ 1.26bn despite declines in apparel and tea

Transport and logistics account for nearly 70% of October’s rise in services earnings

India overtakes UK as Sri Lanka’s second-largest single export market

Sri Lanka’s exports climbed 11.6 percent to US $ 1.62 billion in October, with the gains in the rubber products, spices and electronics making up for the weaker apparel and tea earnings, while a surge in transport and logistics lifted the services exports.

The October gains took the earnings for the first 10 months to US $ 15.28 billion, up 6 percent from a year earlier, according to the Export Development Board (EDB).

“Moving forward, our priority must be to strengthen export competitiveness, diversify products and markets, enhance value addition and accelerate innovation and technology adoption to create greater opportunities for Sri Lankan businesses,” EDB Chairman and Chief Executive Mangala Wijesinghe said.

He said implementation of the National Export Development Plan for 2026-2030 would be central to reaching the country’s target of US $ 36 billion in annual export earnings by 2030.

The merchandise exports rose 9.7 percent to US $ 1.26 billion in October. The rubber and rubber-based products gained 24.8 percent to US $ 100 million on higher earnings from tyres and gloves, while the electrical and electronic components rose 47.8 percent to US $ 57.3 million, helped by the stronger exports of insulated wires and cables.

The exports of spices, essential oils and oleoresins climbed 76.9 percent to US $ 67.9 million. The pepper earnings nearly quadrupled to US $ 21 million, with India accounting for US $ 18.3 million, while the cinnamon exports rose 35.1 percent to US $ 35.1 million, supported by the higher shipments to Mexico.

Apparel and textiles, the largest merchandise export category, fell 3.8 percent to US $ 412.8 million, with the earnings down in the US, European Union and UK.

The tea exports fell 6.2 percent to US $ 118.7 million, as the earnings from the Middle East dropped 26.4 percent, led by a 63 percent fall in shipments to Iraq. The coconut-based exports declined 9.7 percent to US $ 111.9 million, on the lower earnings from coconut oil and liquid coconut milk. The services exports rose 18.9 percent to an estimated US $ 360.2 million.

The transport and logistics earnings increased 24.7 percent to US $ 197.9 million, accounting for nearly 70 percent of the month’s year-on-year increase in the services earnings.

The ICT and business process management exports rose 10.7 percent to US $ 148.3 million.

Over the first 10 months, the merchandise exports grew 5.7 percent to US $ 12.01 billion and the services earnings rose 7.2 percent to an estimated US $ 3.27 billion.

The electrical and electronic components led the growth among the major goods categories, rising 59.6 percent to US $ 566.1 million, while spices and concentrates grew 35.4 percent to US $ 498.4 million. The apparel and textile earnings fell 5.5 percent to US $ 4.20 billion and tea declined 6.8 percent to US $ 1.20 billion.

India overtook the UK as Sri Lanka’s second-largest single export market, with the shipments rising 14.2 percent to US $ 989.7 million in the 10 months through October. The exports to the UK fell 8.7 percent to US $ 723.4 million.

The US remained the largest single market at US $ 2.52 billion, up 0.8 percent, while the exports to the EU bloc slipped 0.9 percent to US $ 2.59 billion.
