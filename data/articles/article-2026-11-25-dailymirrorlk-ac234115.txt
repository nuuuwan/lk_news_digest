Making Sri Lanka Water Positive by 2030

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - More than two thousand years ago, Sri Lanka built one of the world’s most sophisticated water civilisations. Its kings and communities created vast networks of reservoirs, canals and village tank cascades that caught the monsoon, recharged the ground and fed the rice fields of the dry zone. Each tank fed the next, so water was used, returned and reused across an entire landscape.

King Parakramabahu the Great captured this spirit in a resolve that is still quoted today: no drop of rain should reach the sea without first serving the people. Long before the phrase existed, Sri Lanka was practising what the world now calls water stewardship.

That legacy is more than history. It is proof that a nation can live in balance with its water, and a foundation on which Sri Lanka can build a water-positive future. Today’s challenge

The same island now faces a very different water story. A changing climate brings longer droughts in the dry zone and more intense floods elsewhere, often in the same year. Many ancient tanks have silted up or fallen out of use. Growing towns release untreated wastewater into rivers, lagoons and the ground. Water quality concerns in parts of the dry zone, and the rising cost of supplying safe water, add urgency.

Sri Lanka does not lack rain or skill. What it needs is a way to bring its heritage, modern science and new sources of finance together, around one clear national goal. What “Water Positive” means

To be Water Positive is to return more water to nature and to people than is taken. For a nation, it rests on three tests:

Quantity: more water recharged, reused and restored than is consumed, basin by basin.

Quality: water returned clean enough to sustain people, farms and ecosystems.

The proposed mission, “Sri Lanka Water Positive by 2030”, would make Sri Lanka one of the first nations to commit to this goal on the world stage, measured not by promises but by verified results. Five pillars to a Water Positive Sri Lanka

A national mission of this scale needs a clear structure. I propose five pillars, each led by the Government of Sri Lanka around its own priorities, with partners in support.

Revive the cascades. Restore village tanks and cascade systems so they once again capture the monsoon, recharge groundwater and sustain farming communities. This is heritage put back to work.

Recharge the aquifers. Use managed aquifer recharge, rainwater harvesting and watershed restoration to rebuild groundwater in the most stressed districts.

Reuse every drop of wastewater. Treat sewage close to where it is produced, using decentralised, nature-based systems, and return clean water to irrigation, recharge and urban greening. Treated wastewater is a reliable source that does not depend on the rain.

Measure, verify and record. Track every project against a common standard, with metering at source, satellite checks and independent verification, so that progress is real, comparable and trusted.

Finance what works. Bring impact funds, ESG funds, corporate water stewardship, development banks, climate funds and blended finance behind verified outcomes, so that money flows to the projects that deliver.

The last two pillars make the first three bankable. They are where the ecosystem we have built at AquaKraft, and the Water Parliament, can make the greatest contribution. A complete ecosystem for water positivity

Over the years, my colleagues and I have built an ecosystem that offers Sri Lanka a single chain, from the project on the ground to the verified result recorded for the world.

AquaKraft: delivering the projects. Our Geo Soil Filter (GSF) treats sewage through engineered soil and natural processes, producing water fit for irrigation, groundwater recharge and non-potable use. It is decentralised, low-energy and modular, so it can serve a village, a town or a temple complex without a large sewer network. It is already proven in the field in India: in village plants under corporate water programmes, in nature-based sanitation, in zero liquid discharge for industries, and in water sustainability frameworks for municipalities.

AqVerium: proving and valuing the results. AqVerium is a digital water bank and exchange. It meters water outcomes at source, cross-checks them with satellite data, scores each project with a Water Sustainability Score, and records the results on a permissioned blockchain ledger, with independent assurance. Verified benefits can be certified as AquaKredits, with one AquaKredit equal to one cubic metre of verified water benefit. AquaKredits are issued only with national authorisation, priced locally, and confer no rights over a nation’s water. They help direct corporate and blended finance to the projects that deliver.

The Water Parliament: setting the common language. Through its Unified Water Protocol and the Global Water Registry, the Water Parliament gives Sri Lanka’s progress a common standard and a trusted international record. Aquas360 and the 5 i's: one framework, one method

A mission needs a framework that every ministry, district and partner can follow. At AquaKraft we have built one: Aquas360 defines what must be delivered, and the 5 i's define how it is delivered, from the first survey to the verified result.

Aquas360: the full water cycle in one model. Each letter is a block of work, and together they cover every drop, from source to reuse:

Access: rainwater harvesting, groundwater and aquifer recharge, and the revival of tanks and ponds;

Use & Reuse: circularity, water-use monitoring and efficiency, with treated wastewater returned to productive use;

Audit & Accounting: metering, water audits and a Water Balance Sheet for every site;

Sustainability: data reporting, efficiency tracking and water risk management over the long term.

The 5 i's: from problem to proven impact. Every project, in every district, moves through the same five steps:

i1 Incidence: know the problem before funding the fix, through baseline surveys, science-based targets, GIS mapping and early metering, producing a baseline Water Balance Sheet;

i2 Innovation: design the solution, the programme and the financing together, using green, low-energy technology aligned with national priorities and SDG 6;

i3 Invitation: bring government, the private sector, civil society and communities to one table, agreeing a Water Compact on who pays, who builds and who measures;

i4 Intervention: build and run the solution on the ground, operated locally, starting with a showcase cluster and then scaling district by district;

i5 Impact: measure results against the baseline, have them independently assured, and certify verified water as AquaKredits.

Aquas360 gives Sri Lanka a complete picture of its water; the 5 i's give it a repeatable way to act on that picture. Together with AqVerium for finance and the Water Parliament for standards, they turn the five pillars into a programme that can be delivered, financed and proven. Financing the mission: AqVerium as a water sustainability financing platform

Public budgets alone cannot carry a national mission of this scale, and they should not have to. Across the world, impact funds, ESG funds and companies with water stewardship commitments are looking for credible water projects to back. What has held them back is not willingness but trust: they need to know that every rupee they invest delivers real, measurable water.

This is why we see AqVerium as Sri Lanka’s platform for water sustainability financing. It aggregates non-public capital and channels it to verified projects, complementing, never replacing, the Government’s own investment:

ESG funds and institutional investors looking for credible, disclosure-ready water assets;

Private sector contributions, from corporate social responsibility budgets to water stewardship commitments; and

Development and climate finance, blended with private capital to lower risk and raise scale.

What makes this capital flow is accurate and assured measurement of impact. Every project financed through AqVerium is baselined before it begins, metered at source and cross-checked with satellite data as it runs, aligned with science-based parameters, hydrogeological analysis, and independently verified before any result is recognised, with AqVerium’s Assurance Partner.

For Sri Lanka, this creates a simple promise to every funder: you will see exactly what your money achieved, in litres, in a specific basin, assured by an independent party. For the Government, it means more projects delivered for every rupee of public money, with national priorities and national authorisation always in the lead. For Sri Lanka, this means a partner that brings not a single technology, but an integrated system, and my lifelong commitment to water sustainability. The Water Parliament: a home for every conscious stakeholder

No nation can become water positive alone, and no single company, bank or university can make it happen. Water positivity is a shared ambition, and it needs a shared platform.

The Water Parliament was created for exactly this. I conceived it, and it was launched in Washington, D.C. in February 2025 as a non-profit, multi-stakeholder global water think tank hosted at the Centre for Social Value Creation (CSVC), Robert H. Smith School of Business, University of Maryland. Its Secretariat is being hosted by Khalifa University in Abu Dhabi. I am honoured that its Governing Council brings together eminent voices, including Erik Solheim, former Norwegian Minister for Environment and Climate Change and former Executive Director of UNEP; Prof. Graciela Chichilnisky, architect of the Kyoto Protocol carbon market; Alejandro Sturniolo, Co-Founder of Aqua Positive; Nima Farshchi, Executive Director, CSVC; Dr. Amgad Elmahdi, Head of the Middle East Region, Green Climate Fund; Dr. Shadi W. Hasan, UNESCO Chair on Global Water Resilience, Innovation and Sustainable Solutions and Director, CMAT, Khalifa University; Harold Chase – Director of Government Affairs, NSF and C. Sridhar, Vice Chairman, AquaKraft Group Ventures, and Co-Founder, AqVerium.

The Water Parliament brings together everyone who aspires to be water positive:

Companies pursuing water stewardship commitments across their operations and supply chains;

Civil society and communities who live with water challenges every day.

It holds no treaty or regulatory mandate, and acts in any country only with the consent of its national authority. Its role is to aggregate, align and amplify: to give these stakeholders one common language, a trusted record of progress, and a forum for dialogue.

The Water Parliament, in association with the Centre for Social Value Creation and Khalifa University, is leading an open consultation on its Unified Water Protocol, inviting views from all stakeholders.

For Sri Lanka, joining as one of the Water Parliament’s early member countries would mean a seat at the centre of this movement, and a global stage on which to announce its water-positive ambition. I would be delighted to welcome Sri Lanka in that role.

We propose that the journey to 2030 follow five clear steps, each building on the last:

Announcement. A national announcement of the Water Positive Sri Lanka mission in December 2026, setting the goal, the common standard and the institutions that will lead it.

Pilots funded by the private sector. In 2027, the first pilots in priority districts, funded by the private sector, so that the approach is proven on the ground with a measured baseline before public funds are committed at scale.

Scale. From 2028, proven approaches scale across tank cascades, aquifers and towns by attracting impact and ESG funds, together with the Sustainability, Branding and CSR budgets of corporates, channelled through AqVerium to verified projects.

Measure verified impact with AquaKredits. By 2029, every result is metered, independently verified and certified as AquaKredits, one AquaKredit for each cubic metre of verified water benefit, and recorded in the Global Water Registry.

Achieve water positivity by 2030. In 2030, Sri Lanka declares itself Water Positive, with every claim independently verified and publicly recorded. A nation that can lead again

Sri Lanka once showed the world how to honour every drop of water. Today, it has the chance to do so again, combining the wisdom of its ancient cascades with modern science, digital verification and a global coalition of partners.

Our ecosystem brings the tools, and AqVerium brings the trusted finance. I bring my commitment, and the Water Parliament brings the people together. What remains is the most important ingredient of all: a nation’s resolve.

Water Positive by 2030 is not only a goal for Sri Lanka. It could become an example for the world.
