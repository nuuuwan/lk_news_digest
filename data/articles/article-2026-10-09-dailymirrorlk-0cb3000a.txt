Government moves to declare Diyagama forest a reserve

Colombo, Oct. 9 (Daily Mirror) - The Government is taking steps to declare the Diyagama forest area in Homagama, Colombo District, a forest reserve to protect its biodiversity and ecological importance, Deputy Environment Minister Anton Jayakody said in Parliament today (9).

Addressing Parliament, he said the area, which was once abandoned land, had naturally regenerated over the past two decades into an ecologically sensitive habitat with rainforest characteristics.

He said several wildlife species displaced by rapid urbanisation across the Colombo District had migrated to the area, making it an important refuge for wildlife.

Citing field research conducted by a team of academics from the University of Colombo, including Professors S.W. Ranwala, Ileperuma and Abeykoon, the Deputy Minister said nearly 120 species of flora and fauna had been identified in the area.

These include species endemic to Sri Lanka and rare wildlife such as the red slender loris.

The presence of pregnant animals close to giving birth also highlights the importance of the habitat for wildlife, he said.

Located near the Mahinda Rajapaksa International Stadium and the Japan–Sri Lanka Friendship Baseball Ground, the forest plays an important role in maintaining the local environment and absorbing carbon dioxide, he added.

The area also serves as a vital catchment for the Bolgoda water basin and contributes to Sri Lanka's environmental conservation efforts and its commitment to achieving net-zero emissions by 2050 under the Paris Agreement.

Jayakody said that following approval by the Colombo District Coordinating Committee, with the participation of the Divisional Secretary, government officials and environmentalists, steps would be taken to submit a Cabinet paper to formally declare the area a forest reserve.
