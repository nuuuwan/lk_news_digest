Catholic Bishops call for impartial probe into Chemmani mass graves

Colombo, Sep. 10 (Daily Mirror) - The Catholic Bishops’ Conference of Sri Lanka has called for an impartial investigation into the mass graves discovered in the Jaffna Peninsula, stressing that the long-running excavations at Chemmani must be completed to establish the truth and ensure accountability.

In a statement issued following its plenary session held from August 18 to 20, the Conference said the issue of mass graves and alleged crimes associated with Chemmani required serious attention.

The bishops said there were also other unresolved incidents involving terrorism and mass killings during the nearly three-decade civil war, as well as acts of terrorism that occurred before the conflict.

The Conference also welcomed several recent government measures aimed at promoting equality and justice.

It expressed appreciation for the Government's decision regarding the proposed wind farms in Mannar, noting that 14 wind farms had already been established and that, as promised, there would be no further expansion of such plants.

The bishops also thanked the Government for increasing the salaries of tea estate workers, saying the Conference had raised the issue with President Anura Kumara Dissanayake in August last year.

Describing the salary increase as a long-standing need, the bishops said the move would benefit the estate community while also contributing to the country's economy.

Meanwhile, the Conference raised concerns over extensive environmental damage allegedly caused by sand mining activities on Mannar Island.

It said people in the area had been disturbed by environmentally harmful activities and that it had also received information about land excavation around Mannar for fuel and gas exploration.

While acknowledging that such projects could contribute to economic development, the bishops stressed that proper environmental assessments should be conducted transparently and that the public should be adequately informed.

They called for meaningful public consultations before development projects are undertaken, particularly to ensure that the livelihoods and water resources of the fishing community are not affected.

The Catholic Bishops’ Conference also expressed concern over the implementation of the court decision concerning environmental damage caused by the MV X-Press Pearl disaster and the compensation awarded in connection with it.

It further called for attention to environmental threats arising from the ongoing reclamation of the Muthurajawela wetland, urging authorities to take necessary measures to protect the wetland from potentially harmful development projects.

The statement was signed by members of the Catholic Bishops’ Conference, including its President Bishop Harold Anthony Perera, Archbishop of Colombo Malcolm Cardinal Ranjith, Bishop of Mannar Anthony Pillai Gnana Pragasam, Bishop of Jaffna Anton Ranjith and other bishops.
