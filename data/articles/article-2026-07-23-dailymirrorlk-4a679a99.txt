Dengue vaccine not the solution to current outbreak: Expert

Colombo, July 23 (Daily Mirror) - While saying that the dengue vaccine is not a solution to the current dengue epidemic, Consultant Physician Dr. Ananda Wijewickrama urged the public to seek immediate medical attention if they develop symptoms in order to reduce fatalities.

Speaking to Daily Mirror, Dr. Wijewickrama said the increasing number of mosquito breeding sites continues to drive the spread of the disease, making vector control the most important measure in curbing the outbreak.

He noted that several factors have contributed to the rise in dengue-related deaths, including patients delaying hospital treatment after the onset of symptoms and the strain placed on the healthcare system due to the large number of dengue patients requiring medical attention.

Dr. Wijewickrama urged the public not to ignore warning signs of dengue and to seek immediate hospital care, stressing that timely medical intervention significantly reduces the risk of severe complications and death.

Responding to a query on the dengue vaccine, Dr. Wijewickrama said the vaccine is not the answer to the current epidemic, as it cannot provide an immediate solution to the ongoing surge in cases.

He emphasised that eliminating mosquito breeding sites, preventing mosquito bites and seeking early treatment remain the most effective ways to control the outbreak and minimise fatalities.

Meanwhile, the number of dengue cases reported in Sri Lanka so far this year has increased to 78,125, according to the latest data released by the National Dengue Control Unit (NDCU).

The NDCU said 1,097 new dengue cases were reported yesterday (21), while 162 Medical Officer of Health (MOH) divisions across the country continue to be identified as high-risk areas for dengue transmission.

Furthermore, a total of 8,590 cases were reported in May, followed by 21,535 cases in June.

During the first 21 days of July alone, 22,749 dengue cases have already been recorded.

The NDCU also confirmed that 56 dengue-related deaths have been reported so far this year, with the case fatality rate standing at 0.07%.
