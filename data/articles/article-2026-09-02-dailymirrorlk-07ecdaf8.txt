Yala solar-powered water system remains operational: Wildlife Department

Colombo, Sep. 2 (Daily Mirror) - The Wildlife Conservation Department has denied reports that the solar-powered water supply system at Yala National Park has been deactivated, saying the park’s main water pipeline system remains operational and is continuously supplying water to wildlife.

Additional Director General (Operations) of the Department of Wildlife Conservation, Manjula Amaratne, said the media reports claiming that the solar panel water pipe system at Yala was inactive and that wildlife was not receiving adequate water were false.

He said the Department has been implementing a long-term programme to provide water to wildlife during dry periods by filling water reservoirs in several national parks, including Yala, Kumana, Wilpattu, Udawalawe, Bundala and Kaudulla.

Under the programme, efforts are being made to ensure a continuous supply of water to reservoirs during drought conditions. The programme is currently being successfully implemented at Yala National Park, with water being continuously supplied to its water reservoirs, he said.

Amaratne said the main water pipeline system at Yala National Park is currently functioning and supplying water to both the Palatupana headquarters and wildlife within the park.

He clarified that maintenance work is currently being carried out on a pump in a sub-water pipeline system that operates in parallel with the main system.

He therefore urged the public not to be misled by reports claiming that the Yala water supply system had been deactivated or that wildlife was being deprived of the required water.
