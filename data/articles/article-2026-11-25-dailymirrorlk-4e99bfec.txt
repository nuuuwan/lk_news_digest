Sri Lanka’s children under threat: NCPA

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka has recorded more than 9,600 complaints involving child abuse and other child-related issues in just nine months, exposing the disturbing scale of threats faced by children across the country.

The National Child Protection Authority (NCPA) received 9,613 complaints between January 1 and September 30 this year, including 51 teenage pregnancies, 169 cases of child rape and 114 cases of grave sexual abuse.

The alarming figures also include 364 complaints of sexual harassment and 196 cases of cyberbullying involving threats using nude photographs, raising serious concerns about the safety of children both at home and online.

Meanwhile, nine child marriages were reported during the period, while 1,889 complaints of cruelty against children and 2,387 complaints involving children in need of care and protection were recorded.

Chairman of the Medical and Civil Rights Doctors' Trade Union Alliance, Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa, said the latest NCPA statistics revealed an alarming situation that required urgent national intervention.

Calling for a comprehensive national child protection policy, he stressed that children under 18 needed stronger institutional and legal protection.

He said Colombo, Gampaha, Kurunegala, Ratnapura, Anuradhapura and Galle were among the districts reporting comparatively high numbers of child abuse incidents.

Dr. Sanjeewa warned that the actual number of incidents could be several times higher than the reported figures, as many cases remained hidden due to fear, social stigma and other barriers preventing victims from seeking help.

He also pointed out that the NCPA had received 9,613 complaints by the end of September this year, highlighting the urgent need for stronger measures to protect children.

The figures show that child abuse is not limited to sexual offences. There were also 153 complaints of child labour and 1,592 complaints relating to compulsory education.

The NCPA further recorded 146 complaints involving the kidnapping of children from the lawful guardianship of parents or guardians, 40 human trafficking complaints and 103 complaints concerning obscene publications.

The statistics under the miscellaneous category paint an equally worrying picture. They include 719 requests for counselling and psychosocial support, 99 drug abuse complaints, 46 domestic violence complaints and 19 cases involving attempted suicide.

The situation has also remained concerning over the past two years.

According to Dr. Sanjeewa, 53 teenage pregnancies were reported in 2024, along with 500 cases of sexual assault, three cases of child abortion, 42 cases of child rape and 321 cases of child sexual abuse.

In 2025, the NCPA recorded 46 teenage pregnancies, 545 cases of sexual assault and 98 cases of child sexual abuse, among other incidents.

Dr. Sanjeewa said urgent measures were needed to raise public awareness, expand school-based education programmes and improve sexual literacy among children and parents.

He also called for a stronger legal framework and rehabilitation programmes that would protect the privacy and dignity of victims while ensuring that affected children received the necessary support.

September recorded the highest number of complaints this year, with 1,554 cases, followed by July with 1,375 and June with 1,239.

District-wise figures show that Colombo recorded the highest number of complaints at 991, followed by Gampaha with 821 and Kurunegala with 623.

However, the NCPA statistics contain a discrepancy. While the overall complaints table records 9,613 complaints, the district-wise table gives a total of 7,867. The main child abuse and related offences category alone records 7,622 complaints.

The NCPA has said that a single complaint may involve more than one offence, meaning offence counts could differ from the number of complaints recorded district-wise.
