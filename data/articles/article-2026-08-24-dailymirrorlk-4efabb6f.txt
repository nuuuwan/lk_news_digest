Lankan drug lord questioned by FBI over Iran links before deportation

Colombo, Aug. 24 (Daily Mirror) - While Sri Lankan authorities continue to investigate notorious drug trafficker Shiran Basik’s alleged drug empire, illicit assets and international links, the Daily Mirror learns that Basik was also questioned in Dubai by FBI agents over possible connections to Iran before he was eventually handed over to Sri Lankan authorities.

Basik, who was arrested by Dubai Police on May 26, 2026, reportedly came under the attention of US authorities after photographs and videos of Iranian drones and missiles were allegedly found on his mobile phone. Given his alleged links to international drug trafficking networks and the potential security implications, US agencies questioned him to determine whether he had any political or security-related connections to Iran.

However, the inquiries were subsequently dropped after US authorities reportedly determined that Basik’s connections to Iran were linked to drug trafficking networks rather than political or security activities.

As a result, the US agencies did not pursue further action against him, paving the way for his eventual deportation to Sri Lanka.

Dubai authorities later deported Basik to Sri Lanka on August 14 under heavy security. Later the Central Crimes Investigation Bureau (CCIB) has obtained a 90-day detention order to further interrogate Basik.

Sri Lankan authorities are now investigating his alleged drug trafficking operations, financial networks and assets believed to have been acquired through illicit activities. The investigations are being conducted with the involvement of the Central Bank of Sri Lanka, with authorities examining his bank accounts and other financial transactions.

During questioning, Basik reportedly provided information about additional properties allegedly linked to him. Police said he disclosed that he owns two houses and two mobile phone shops in Boralesgamuwa.

He has also reportedly revealed information about five hotels allegedly belonging to him along Marine Drive in Colombo. A court has ordered a comprehensive examination of his bank accounts as investigations into his alleged illicit wealth continue.

Meanwhile, investigators probing Basik's assets have also uncovered information about a tuition teacher who allegedly maintained close links with him and assisted in laundering drug money through investments in hotel projects.

According to police, the teacher fled to Thailand several months ago, around the time Basik was initially detained. Authorities are now considering steps to secure an international warrant in connection with the investigation.

Police said the teacher's wife has also recently left the country. Although no arrest warrant is currently in force against her, investigators believe the family has left Sri Lanka because of the teacher's alleged business and financial links with Basik.

Meanwhile, two other alleged organized crime figures arrested in Dubai, Bus Lalith, alias “Lalith Kannangara”, and underworld figure Kavishka Madushan Abeywickrama, alias “Wanathe Chathu” — are expected to be brought back to Sri Lanka this week.

A team headed by Senior Superintendent of Police Kamal Ariyawansa, Director of the Central Crime Investigation Bureau, together with officers from the State Intelligence Service and the CID, have travelled to Dubai on the instructions of the Inspector General of Police to facilitate their return.

Bus Lalith was arrested in Dubai in November 2025 at an apartment complex following information obtained after one of his close associates was arrested and questioned in Sri Lanka.

Wanathe Chathu, who is alleged to have been involved in drug distribution and organized criminal activities, was arrested in Ras Al Khaimah, Dubai. He had reportedly operated in the Borella and Wanathamulla areas and maintained connections with drug traffickers including Kalu Thushara and Anju.

Chathu had previously been arrested by the Colombo Crime Division in connection with drug-related offences and possession of Kerala cannabis, but reportedly fled overseas after being released on bail. According to intelligence information, tips provided by rival underworld groups contributed to his subsequent arrest in Dubai.

Meanwhile, police said that Red Notices have been issued through Interpol to apprehend and bring back 93 wanted suspects hiding overseas in connection with crimes committed in Sri Lanka.

They said that at least 40 suspects had been repatriated to Sri Lanka through Interpol Red Notices during 2024, 2025, and the period elapsed thus far this year.
