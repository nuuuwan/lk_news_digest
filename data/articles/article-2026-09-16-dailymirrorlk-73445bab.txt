Rabies bill could derail dog vaccination drive, raise human rabies risk: PHIs

Colombo, Sep. 16 (Daily Mirror) - The proposed Rabies Eradication and Dog Registration Bill could potentially disrupt Sri Lanka’s dog vaccination programme and increase the risk of human rabies, the Sri Lanka Public Health Inspectors Association (SLPHIA) has warned.

In a letter to President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, the association urged the Government to review the Bill, saying several of its provisions could create practical difficulties in maintaining the country’s existing community-based rabies control programme.

Sri Lanka’s Ministry of Health has identified dog vaccination as a key component of its rabies prevention strategy. The country’s National Strategic Plan targets dog vaccination coverage of more than 75 per cent and zero dog-mediated human rabies deaths by 2026.

The association said Sri Lanka’s dog rabies vaccination programme had a long history, dating back to 1953, and was currently implemented through the Ministry of Health’s Public Health Veterinary Service, District Rabies Control Units and Medical Officer of Health (MOH) areas.

Public Health Inspectors (PHIs), it said, play a key role in taking vaccination services to communities, including vaccination of domestic dogs, stray dogs and dogs found around schools, temples, public places and government and private institutions.

The association also said special vaccination programmes were conducted in areas where dog bites or suspected rabies cases were reported, based on surveillance carried out at the location.

According to the PHIs, the proposed legislation could create a major obstacle to this system.

The association specifically raised concerns over Section 4 of the Bill, which it said would require dog vaccination to be carried out by a veterinarian or under a veterinarian’s supervision.

It claimed that there are currently no veterinarians attached to MOH-level health services to conduct the existing vaccination programme, while only a limited number of the country’s 341 local authorities have veterinarians attached to them.

The association therefore warned that the existing vaccination activities could be forced to stop once the proposed law comes into effect unless an alternative mechanism is established.

The PHIs said this was particularly concerning given the link between sustained dog vaccination and the long-term reduction in human rabies deaths in Sri Lanka.

According to the Ministry of Health, 20 laboratory-confirmed human rabies deaths were reported in 2024, while dogs remained the main reservoir and primary source responsible for most human rabies deaths that year.

The World Health Organization has also identified dog vaccination and post-exposure prophylaxis as key public health interventions behind Sri Lanka’s substantial reduction in human rabies deaths, from 377 deaths in the mid-1970s to 31 in 2021.

The association further referred to a change made in 2018, when responsibility for dog vaccination was transferred to veterinarians of the Department of Animal Production and Health and District Rabies Control Units under the Health Ministry were discontinued.

It claimed that only about six per cent of dogs were vaccinated under that arrangement in 2018 and that the programme was subsequently returned to the Health Ministry in 2019.

The association said District Rabies Control Units were then reactivated and vaccination activities resumed, with the Health Ministry working towards reducing human rabies deaths and ultimately eliminating dog-mediated human rabies.

The PHIs also raised concerns over the proposed Bill’s enforcement mechanism.

It said the draft identifies Medical Officers of Health and police officers as authorised officers but does not include Public Health Inspectors, despite PHIs being directly involved in community-level health activities and frequently receiving complaints about dog bites, suspected rabies cases and problems associated with dog ownership.

The association argued that excluding PHIs could create difficulties in enforcing the proposed law at grassroots level and responding to complaints from the public.

It also questioned the practicality of requiring Medical Officers of Health to undertake all field-level activities, claiming that powers vested in an MOH under the proposed legislation could not be delegated to a PHI or another officer.

The proposed legislation is intended to consolidate Sri Lanka’s existing Rabies Ordinance and Dog Registration Ordinance, make rabies vaccination of dogs mandatory, strengthen dog population control and clarify the responsibilities of institutions involved in rabies control and dog registration. Cabinet approved its publication in the Gazette before submission to Parliament.

The association said it supported efforts to eradicate rabies but urged the Government to amend the proposed legislation to ensure that the law is practical at grassroots level and does not disrupt the existing vaccination system.

It requested President Dissanayake to review the Bill and introduce the necessary amendments before it is implemented.
