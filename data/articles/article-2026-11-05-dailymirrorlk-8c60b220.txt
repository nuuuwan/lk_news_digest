UNP-SJB coalition efforts – going nowhere?

For nearly two years, supporters of both the United National Party (UNP) and the Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB) have been eagerly anticipating the formation of an alliance between the two parties.

In this context,  remarks made over the weekend by UNP Leader and former President Ranil Wickremesinghe regarding alliance-building would have undoubtedly left the supporters of both parties stunned and disappointed.

He stated that a United National Party (UNP) and Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB) alliance does not spell victory and emphasised that to win the next election, they must win over the 8 million young generation voters known as Gen Z and Millennials. (Generations are commonly categorised based on their birth years. Accordingly, those born between 1981 and 1996 are called the Millennial generation, while those born between 1997 and 2012 are referred to as Gen Z.)  Had he stopped there, the UNP and SJB supporters waiting for a coalition would not have been confused. It is a fact that simply uniting the two parties is insufficient and that winning over the youth is vital. Electoral victory would be a cakewalk if a party or an alliance gets at least 7 million out of 17.5 million registered votes. There is no deep political philosophy in it.

However, in his comments that followed, there was absolutely no mention of an alliance. Instead, he continued stressing that the UNP must win over this young generation. He noted that the UNP, with its 65-year history, must forge a unique new political identity capable of attracting the youth. He also claimed that the party has already initiated political work with  Gen Z and Millennial generations to restructure the UNP toward this objective. This leaves the impression that he has given up the idea of an alliance at all.

Almost all print media outlets highlighted this point of his speech on Monday. They seemed to have viewed this as a death blow to coalition talks. Some media houses headlined the story stating that a UNP-SJB alliance cannot guarantee electoral success, while others framed it as meaning an alliance ‘alone’ merely cannot guarantee victory.

It is possible that the UNP dispatched this speech to all the media outlets under a uniform headline. If so, the question arises as to why the party wanted to emphasise this point. Furthermore, looking at Wickremesinghe’s continuous focus on building up his own party rather than forming a coalition, it becomes clear that the UNP has either abandoned joining forces with the SJB or is no longer seriously bothered about it.

If, according to Ranil Wickremesinghe, a UNP-SJB combination cannot win in the current landscape, the question arises as to who would win if an election were held today? It certainly cannot be the Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna (SLPP), as that Party was far behind all other major parties at the last Presidential and general elections. Besides, it is currently struggling, entangled in legal battles.

Therefore, Wickremesinghe is implicitly suggesting that public support still remains with the National People’s Power (NPP). Also, by stating that victory is impossible without winning over the younger generation, he is subtly conceding that this demographic also firmly supports the NPP. This observation inadvertently boosts the morale of the ruling party.

In the Presidential Election held in September 2024, Anura Kumara Dissanayake who won the race secured 5,634,915 votes, Sajith Premadasa obtained 4,363,035 votes, and Ranil Wickremesinghe received 2,299,767 votes. It is evident that if Sajith and Ranil had contested under a common symbol, one of them would have been elected President. This is exactly why the idea of the two parties functioning together arose among their respective supporters right after the Presidential election.

Yet, the parties failed to unite even for the General Election held two months later in November 2024. In that election, the votes secured by the NPP under Anura Kumara Dissanayake’s leadership surged to 6,863,186. Consequently, by contesting independently, the NPP achieved the prestigious feat of winning a two-thirds majority without any alliance. Even if the UNP and SJB had formed a coalition to contest the General Election, the psychological impact of the Presidential Election results might still have carried the NPP to victory.

However, following the crushing defeat suffered by both parties at the general election, the idea of building an alliance faded from the minds of their supporters for some time.

The hopes of a UNP-SJB coalition re-emerged when campaign work commenced for the local government election held in May last year. However, it was not pushed forward due to the stubbornness of the party leaders. The truth is that while the second-tier leaders and supporters of both parties were enthusiastic about such an alliance, the top leaders were not keen. This was obvious from how the two leaders behaved whenever alliance efforts cropped up over the last two years.

Many arrangements were made to hold talks between the two factions, but both leaders showed very little interest on those occasions. At times, both parties even appointed committees to conduct negotiations, but we have no recollection of these committees proceeding past a single round of talks.

The primary obstacle here is the question of who will lead if the parties merge. Setting aside ego issues, the leaders of both sides have strong grounds to claim the right to this joint leadership. Ranil Wickremesinghe has been in politics for over five decades. He has served as the country’s Prime Minister five times—in 1994, 2001, 2015, 2018, and 2022. He also served as the President from 2022 to 2024. Therefore, it is not entirely unreasonable for Ranil to dislike operating under Sajith Premadasa, who functioned under his leadership in the UNP for nearly 20 years.

On the flip side, Sajith Premadasa currently commands public support that is many times greater than what Ranil possesses. Given this reality, it is equally justifiable for Sajith to believe that he should be the leader if an alliance is formed. At the same time, Sajith is well aware that agreeing to work under Ranil once again could potentially signal the end of his political career. It is for this very reason that Sajith hesitates to join hands with other opposition parties as well.

On October 27 last year, opposition parties held a rally in Nugegoda under the slogan “Maha Jana Handa” (The People’s Voice), which the SJB did not attend. On March 11 this year, opposition factions met to formulate a common programme against the government, and the SJB stayed away from that as well. Since other opposition parties lack the public backing that the SJB enjoys, its leaders—particularly Sajith Premadasa—likely suspect that those parties are merely trying to piggyback on the SJB’s popular support.

Therefore, even if Ranil Wickremesinghe desires it, the SJB does not seem to align with him. Even if they do unite, the leadership dispute will certainly remain unresolved. In such a scenario, the UNP is entirely justified in choosing to chart its political journey alone.
