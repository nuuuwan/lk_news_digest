Deputy Minister warns legal action if Shiranthi Rajapaksa fails to return

Colombo, Sep. 30 (Daily Mirror) - Deputy Minister Eranga Gunasekera said the government is committed to upholding the rule of law and ensuring that allegations of political crimes are investigated through the country’s legal system.

He said the government is working to strengthen the independence of Sri Lanka’s legal and investigative institutions, including the police and the judiciary.

Gunasekera said individuals found responsible for political crimes, murders or actions that contributed to the country’s economic collapse would face legal consequences, regardless of whether they had left the country.

He added that the number and nature of legal cases would depend on the offences allegedly committed and the findings of investigations.

Commenting on former First Lady Shiranthi Rajapaksa, Gunasekera said a court had ordered her to provide an official statement. He also referred to reports on social media that she had travelled overseas for medical treatment.

Gunasekera said Rajapaksa would be required to return to Sri Lanka and comply with the legal process, warning that the relevant authorities could take further action if she failed to do so.

He also referred to allegations in an ongoing case involving a CT scan machine. According to Gunasekera, the case concerns funds allegedly obtained from China and a bank and purportedly presented as a birthday gift for Rajapaksa’s son.

He said the allegations and related matters would be examined through the judicial process, adding that the facts would emerge through court proceedings.
