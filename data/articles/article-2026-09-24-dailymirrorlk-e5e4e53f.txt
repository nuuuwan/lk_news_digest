The burden of road traffic accidents in Sri Lanka

Records indicate that people between 15-45 years of age are more vulnerable to road accidents

Road accidents have been identified as the biggest manmade disaster in post-war Sri Lanka. According to statistics recorded by the Sri Lanka Medical Association (SLMA), over 2000 individuals have succumbed to road accidents in 2025 alone. Sri Lanka Police statistics indicate that over 1400 people have died as a result of road accidents during the first six months of this year. This shows that despite repeated awareness and new laws coming into place, the issue perhaps lies elsewhere.

Sri Lanka’s socioeconomic context following the economic crisis in 2022 demands an individual to at least juggle between two jobs to earn a decent living. For an individual living by him or herself, depending on one salary is insufficient given the responsibilities and other expenses that need to be covered. Therefore men, especially, opt to do voluntary jobs such as taking hires on ride-hailing apps or going on food deliveries once office hours are over. But, by the time they start their temporary jobs, they are already too exhausted. According to statistics issued by Sri Lanka Police Traffic Management and Road Safety Division over 740 motorcycle-related deaths have been recorded during the first six months of 2026.

This applies to three-wheeler drivers and bus drivers as well. The Ella-Wellawaya bus accident in September 2025 claimed the lives of 16 people while injuring 18 others. In June this year, a head-on private bus collision in Lunugamwehera injured 15 people. On September 19, a road accident claimed the life of the Dambulla National School principal.

Apart from loss of lives, the economic burden to the country is another point of concern. A World Bank Report titled Delivering Road Safety in Sri Lanka estimates that the annual financial loss as a result of road accidents is around USD 3 billion. In other words,  road accidents in Sri Lanka costs an estimated 3-5% of the Gross Domestic Product.

Records indicate that people between 15-45 years of age are more vulnerable to road accidents. This in other words is the working population. Sometimes it could be the sole breadwinner of a family. Sometimes they may die or they may be disabled their entire life adding more responsibilities to their spouses and children. If the injured individual has no guardian or caretaker, then it becomes another issue altogether.

In terms of the healthcare strain around 50% of patients are admitted to Intensive Care Units (ICUs). It is a known fact that sometimes there is a waiting list to obtain a bed at hospital ICUs due to the spike in admissions for various other illnesses as well. In terms of treatment costs, treating complex trauma injuries and fractures require specialised surgical plates or sutures. Medical experts opine that this costs up to ten times more than managing chronic medical conditions like diabetes or hypertension.

Another observation by medical experts and those studying road accidents is the lack of provision of helmets for children. In most instances, children who travel in motorcycles do not wear helmets and in case of an unfortunate incident, the child’s life is at risk. Therefore the need for a behavioural and attitudinal shift has been observed.

People become aware of road discipline when a major accident occurs. But after about a week, they go back to breaking traffic rules and have no regard for lane discipline. This is partly due to the lack of a proper monitoring mechanism by the authorities. In other countries, such as member states of the European Union including France and Spain suspend or revoke licenses for severe infractions, drunk driving, and accidents causing injury or death. In UAE, reckless driving, excessive speeding, or accidents result in severe black-point accumulations leading to automatic license suspension and vehicle impoundment.

One split second is all it takes to change the fate of an individual travelling on the road. Perhaps it is high time to upgrade the laws, road infrastructure, think about pedestrian safety and also have repeated campaigns to remind people about why road safety is a serious matter to consider.
