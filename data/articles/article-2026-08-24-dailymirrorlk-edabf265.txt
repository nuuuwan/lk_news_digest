“Cashback at Till” System in  Supermarkets

I am writing to suggest the introduction of the “Cashback at Till” system  to Sri Lanka which is widely used in UK supermarkets.

In the UK, when customers pay for groceries by debit card, they can simply ask the cashier: _”Can I have cashback?”_ The requested cash amount is then added to the bill and the total is taken by card. The customer gets both goods and cash in one transaction, with no extra fee.   Max limit allowed should be Rs 4000 or 5000 only. Why do we need this in Sri Lanka? 1.Convenience for the public. Many people still need cash for buses, tuk-tuks, small shops, and temples. But finding an ATM is difficult and ATM machines often run out of cash or charge fees Rs 5 per transaction.

2.Safety: People, especially senior elders, do not have to carry large amounts of cash or visit ATMs late at night. They can withdraw cash safely while shopping.  Easy for working couples on way home.

3.  Reduces ATM dependency: Banks spend millions maintaining ATMs. This system will reduce pressure on ATMs and long queues including robberies.

4.  Boosts digital payments: More people will use debit cards instead of cash, which helps the government’s move towards a cashless economy.

5.  No cost to customer: In the UK this service is free. Supermarkets benefit because they get less excess cash to manage, count and bank next day. Major supermarket chains in Sri Lanka like Keells, Cargills, and Arpico Glowmark already have card machines. The technology is already here. We only need approval from the Central Bank and banks to enable it. It is a small change but will make a big difference to daily lives of all of us and put us on par with a global system.
