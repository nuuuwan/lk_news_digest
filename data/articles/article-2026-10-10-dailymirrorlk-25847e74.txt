Indian investor urges Sri Lanka to court India with dedicated campaign, restore Kolkata flights

Beryll Group’s Agarwal calls India “lowest hanging fruit” for island’s tourism

Wants Rumassala added to Ramayana trail and focus shifted to spend per tourist

Group pressing ahead with 220-key Taj Weligama; land acquired for Mirissa property

Sri Lanka could lift the tourist arrivals well beyond the current two to 2.5 million a year by running a dedicated marketing campaign in India and restoring the lost air links such as the Colombo-Kolkata service, while placing the spend per tourist ahead of the headcount, the Indian investor behind Taj Weligama and Otherland Galle said.

Beryll Group Managing Partner Vishal Agarwal told Mirror Business on the sidelines of a media preview of Otherland Galle that the authorities ought to promote the destination far more aggressively overseas, starting with its nearest and largest source market.

“If the Sri Lankan government runs a campaign in India, it will really transform. Very soon it can grow from two to 2.5 million to three, four, five million, because India has a huge population,” he said.

“It’s the lowest hanging fruit because it’s so close to Sri Lanka.”

India is already the island’s largest source market by a wide margin. It accounted for 51,931 of the 158,557 tourists welcomed in September or nearly a third, while its 431,162 arrivals up to September 27 made up 25.7 percent of the 1,678,745 recorded for the year, as per the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority data.

However, Agarwal maintained that the market remains under-tapped, as Sri Lanka has gone more than a decade without a global destination marketing campaign. He also urged the government to pursue higher spending per visitor instead of chasing numbers alone.

“It’s such a beautiful country you have over here. We just need to sell it,” he remarked.

He singled out Rumassala, the hill overlooking the Galle harbour that the Ramayana links to Hanuman, as an overlooked draw.

“I am surprised nobody even in the government mentions Rumassala. For Indians, it’s a big thing. It should be included in the Ramayana trail and promoted,” Agarwal stressed.

On air connectivity, the Kolkata-based investor was less than satisfied, noting that the travellers from eastern India must now route through Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai or Bengaluru. SriLankan Airlines flew to Kolkata three times a week from 2017 until March 2020 and he called on the national carrier to bring back at least that frequency.

“They have already operated on that sector, so they must be aware what kind of market they have,” he pointed out.

Agarwal had no complaint about the visa regime, observing that the tourist visas for the Indians are free, although the travellers now have to apply for an electronic travel authorisation.

Asked how the island could attract the high-end Indian travellers, he replied that the product has to keep pace with the promotion.

“The F&B, the hotels, the experiences, everything has to grow together,” he noted.

Meanwhile, Beryll, which first came to Sri Lanka in 2018 as an interior contractor for ITC Ratnadipa, is concentrating its own investments down south. Its 220-key Taj Weligama, which includes 80 luxury apartments, was announced by Indian Hotels Company Limited earlier this year and Agarwal expects the development to take four to five years, with the final investment yet to be determined.

The group has also acquired land in Mirissa for a 30 to 40 key property, where construction is yet to begin. Both projects, like Otherland Galle, are being funded by a consortium of Indian and Sri Lankan partners.

“After the opening up of the highway, we felt that this is the area we should be focusing on,” he added, indicating that the group would look at other parts of the island once it has a firm footing down south.
