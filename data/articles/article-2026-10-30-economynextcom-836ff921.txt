Sri Lanka Bairaha Farms Sept quarter profit down 50-pct to Rs.214mn

ECONOMYNEXT — Profit in Sri Lanka’s Bairaha Farms PLC fell 49.7 percent to Rs. 213.7 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to Rs. 424.6 million in the same quarter last year amid a sharp rise in income tax, higher finance costs, and losses at its joint venture operations, its interim filing to the Colombo Stock Exchange showed.

The group recorded basic earnings of Rs. 2.49 per share for the second quarter (Q2), down from Rs. 4.86 per share a year ago.

Revenue for the quarter rose 4.2 percent to Rs. 4.41 billion from Rs. 4.23 billion in the corresponding period of 2025, supported by sustained market demand.

Cost of sales increased 4.4 percent to Rs. 3.44 billion from Rs. 3.29 billion, leaving gross profit 3.5 percent higher at Rs. 975.3 million compared to Rs. 942.6 million a year earlier.

Distribution costs rose 22.6 percent during the quarter to Rs. 280.6 million from Rs. 228.9 million, while administrative expenses declined 9.6 percent to Rs. 231.2 million from Rs. 255.8 million.

Finance costs jumped 72.3 percent to Rs. 58.5 million from Rs. 33.9 million a year ago, driven by higher working capital requirements and expanded lease and financing facilities.

The group’s share of results from joint venture companies swung into a loss of Rs. 75.3 million for the quarter, compared to a profit of Rs. 32.9 million recorded in the same quarter last year.

Managing Director Yakooth Naleem attributed the joint venture losses to severe raw material cost pressures and policy constraints on animal feed inputs.

“This was mainly due to increased prices of feed raw material and increased special commodity levy on corn from Rs 25/kg to Rs 50/Kg,” Naleem said in the review.

“As only 20% of approved quantity of corn was permitted to be imported during the period, approval was instead granted for the use of local rice as an alternative, which resulted in higher input costs. As feed represents a significant share of the cost of poultry production, we trust that a stable and predictable raw material import policy framework will support the competitiveness of the feed industry and the wider poultry sector.”

Group profit before tax (including joint venture results) contracted 27.2 percent to Rs. 341.7 million from Rs. 469.3 million a year earlier.

Income tax expense surged 186.0 percent in the quarter to Rs. 128.1 million from Rs. 44.8 million.

The company explained that during the previous financial year, tax liabilities were cushioned by the utilization of carried-forward tax losses from prior years. With those past tax losses fully absorbed, the entirety of the group’s current earnings was subject to standard income tax.

“The company continues to focus on enhancing operational efficiency and maintaining product quality, while proactively managing supply chain challenges,” Naleem said.

Total assets of the group reached Rs. 12.50 billion as of September 30, 2026, compared to Rs. 10.11 billion a year earlier.

Total financing and lease liabilities stood at Rs. 2.86 billion, comprising Rs. 1.88 billion in current borrowings and Rs. 983.8 million in non-current facilities. Net cash flow from operating activities for the six months stood at an inflow of Rs. 699.2 million.

Bairaha Farms shares closed at 86.90 rupees at the end of the September quarter, and last traded at 81.70 rupees on October 29. (Colombo/Oct30/2026)
