Grand Hyatt heads to bidders; plans Colombo-first entry and four or five more destinations

Cultural Triangle, Bentota, Kandy, Trincomalee and Yala in focus, with Unbound Collection tipped as best fit

Govt.’s sale of 458-room Grand Hyatt Colombo at RFP stage with five pre-qualified bidders

Completing the tower to cost about Rs.36bn, 1.8 times the State’s outlay so far

US hotel group Hyatt, which has yet to open a hotel in Sri Lanka 14 years after signing its first management agreement for a Colombo property, plans to enter through the gateway city before expanding into at least four or five destinations across the island, according to a top company official.

Hyatt Director Growth India & Southwest Asia Prakash V spoke on the “Developing Paradise: The Next Wave of Hospitality Growth” panel at THINC Sri Lanka & Maldives 2026, hosted by Hotelivate-Savills at Hilton Colombo last week. He conceded that the group has no presence in the country yet, but insisted its interest is serious.

“As of now, we have none. But our intention is very serious to this destination. We have identified a few opportunities which we’re hoping to bring into life,” he told delegates. “We will start with the gateway city and move on to at least four or five destinations, and you will hear that soon.”

Beyond Colombo, he singled out the Cultural Triangle of Anuradhapura, Sigiriya and Dambulla as the most interesting proposition, followed by Bentota, Kandy, Trincomalee and Yala. Describing the recovery as real, he saw an opportunity to turn Colombo into a lifestyle destination with the help of Colombo Port City. He added that international brands could bring lesser-known destinations to life and make them visible to the world.

He also pointed to the shared Ramayana heritage with India as an opportunity. However, he cautioned that an itinerary of significant sites with sustainable demand must be identified before any infrastructure is built.

He was asked to pick the one brand from Hyatt’s portfolio best suited to Sri Lanka. He chose the Unbound Collection by Hyatt, describing it as a brand for unique destinations that offers “a window to any destination”. Launched in 2016, the Unbound Collection is Hyatt’s portfolio of upper-upscale and luxury hotels that each retain a distinct character. The moderator remarked that it was conveniently the brand already in Hyatt’s pipeline, which Prakash neither confirmed nor denied.

Hyatt operates 55 hotels across nine brands in India and Southwest Asia and aims to double its India presence to 100 hotels by 2030. Sri Lanka, however, remains absent from its operating portfolio.

Although Prakash did not name a property, Hyatt’s longest-standing link to the gateway city is the unfinished 47-storey tower in Colombo 3 that the government is now trying to sell. Hyatt signed a management agreement with state-owned Sinolanka Hotels and Spa in October 2012 for what was billed as Hyatt Regency Colombo, the brand’s first hotel in Sri Lanka. The project was later taken to Grand Hyatt specifications, but construction stalled with only the superstructure complete.

The tower houses 458 hotel rooms and 100 serviced apartments on 2.32 acres of oceanfront land. Sinolanka’s parent, Canwill Holdings, was established with Rs.18.5 billion in equity from Sri Lanka Insurance Corporation, Litro Gas and the Employees’ Provident Fund, and the project has absorbed over Rs.20 billion of state funds. Minister Nalinda Jayatissa told reporters in May 2025 that a further US$ 120 million, or around Rs.36 billion, would be needed to complete it. That is roughly 1.8 times the State’s outlay so far and works out to about US$ 215,000 per key, including the serviced apartments.

The sale has made more headway under the present administration than in earlier attempts. The Cabinet resolved in May 2025 to resume the suspended divestiture and retained Deloitte India as transaction advisor. The Finance Ministry then issued a Request for Expressions of Interest on December 24, 2025, to divest the Government’s entire stake in Canwill Holdings, and eight parties responded by the extended March 16 deadline.

On August 12, the Ministry named five of them as pre-qualified bidders cleared for the Request for Proposals stage. They are India’s Chalet Hotels and Juniper Hotels, a consortium of Phoenix Ventures and Bluestone Capital, Ceylon Steel Corporation, and a consortium of EML Consultants PLC and Italy’s Kimetal S.r.L. No timeline for the submission of proposals has been made public since.

Juniper Hotels is the largest owner of Hyatt-affiliated hotels in India by number of keys. At the time of its 2024 listing it operated 1,836 keys across seven properties, including the Grand Hyatt Mumbai Hotel and Residences. It is co-owned by the Saraf Group and Two Seas Holdings, a Hyatt affiliate.

Prospective bidders have raised several questions over the asset. At a pre-bid meeting in January, investors queried the validity of the agreements with Hyatt and whether tax holidays under Strategic Development Project status were still available. They also asked about the condition of the building’s lifts, escalators, electrical and plumbing systems.

The sale also covers Helanco Hotels & Spa, which holds 9.42 acres of beachfront leasehold land in Hambantota. Helanco signed with Hyatt in March 2014 to run a Hyatt Regency resort there, but its board abandoned the project in 2015 and the agreement has since lapsed. Meanwhile, Canwill remains the only state enterprise divestiture the Government has taken forward. The sale of Hotel Developers (Lanka), which owns the Hilton Colombo, has been put on hold pending new policy direction, according to its annual report.

Prakash also advised first-time hoteliers to commission a proper feasibility study rather than get emotional about building a hotel for its own sake. “Coming with a rendering is not a feasibility study,” he warned, adding that every project must pass all reality checks on financial returns. Pitching Hyatt to the owners in the room, he argued that trust is the deciding factor in choosing an operator. He noted that Hyatt runs 56 hotels in Southwest Asia, predominantly in India, for just 14 owners, an average of four hotels each. “Choose an operator where your heart says yes, this is the brand that I’m going to trust,” he urged.
