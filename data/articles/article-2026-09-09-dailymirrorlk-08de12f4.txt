NYK’s new-gen RoRo vessel Azalea Leader makes maiden call at HIP

HIPG COO Tommy Yang exchanges a memento with the ship’s captain Hertug Eugen to mark the maiden call

NYK’s Azalea Leader, a new-generation liquefied natural gas (LNG) dual-fuel pure car and truck carrier, made its maiden call at Hambantota International Port (HIP) recently, marking another significant development in the port’s growing role as a regional automotive transhipment gateway.

Built in 2026 and sailing under the Liberian flag, the nearly 200-metre Azalea Leader arrived at HIP from Singapore, carrying 4,810 vehicles for transhipment and another 47 units for local discharge.

The vessel has a maximum carrying capacity of 7,000 units and is among the latest generation of vehicle carriers entering service globally.

NYK operates regular services connecting Singapore and HIP, with Azalea Leader assigned to the route for this voyage.

Its call comes as the vehicle volumes routed through HIP to markets in the Middle East have grown significantly, reinforcing the port’s strategic position connecting the automotive supply chains across Asia and the Middle East.

The vessel also represents the shipping industry’s transition towards more efficient and lower-emission tonnage.

Azalea Leader is powered by LNG dual-fuel technology and incorporates exhaust gas recirculation technology on its main engine to reduce nitrogen oxide emissions.

“The maiden call of Azalea Leader is significant because we are increasingly seeing shipping lines deploy their newest and most sophisticated vessels through HIP,” said Hambantota Internationl Port Group (HIPG) CEO Wilson Qu.

“It demonstrates the pivotal role HIP is developing in the regional roll-on/roll-off (RoRo) sector and showcases the potential of the port as automotive volumes moving through HIP continue to grow.”

Qu added that handling vessels of this generation also demonstrates the operational capabilities HIP has developed.

“The customers need the assurance that a sophisticated vessel will be handled efficiently, safely and without unnecessary idle time. The confidence to bring modern tonnage such as Azalea Leader to HIP reflects the capability of our people, systems and infrastructure in meeting those expectations.”

HIP has been steadily strengthening its position as a regional RoRo transhipment hub, leveraging its strategic location along the major east-west shipping routes and its connectivity to the automotive markets in the Middle East and beyond.

The increasing volume of vehicles routed through the port has contributed to the growth of HIP’s overall vehicle-handling business.

The call of Azalea Leader further demonstrates the port’s ability to accommodate the new generation of large, technologically advanced vehicle carriers now being introduced into global fleets.

“As international shipping moves towards larger, more efficient and environmentally advanced vessels, ports must evolve alongside them,” Qu said.

“The ability to handle these vessels to international standards strengthens HIP’s visibility within global automotive logistics networks and supports our long-term strategy of establishing HIP as a key gateway for regional vehicle transhipment.”
