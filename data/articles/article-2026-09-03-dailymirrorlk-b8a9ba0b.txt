Investors are welcome, but do not exploit this land, its law or its resources

In years to come, Sri Lankans are likely to experience  Chinese culture right at their doorstep

Take a walk down the busy streets of Colpetty for instance and certain new establishments would surely grab your attention. From fancy-looking supermarkets to bubble tea shops, hotpot noodle restaurants and more, there’s just one thing that all of them have in common; the Chinese origin.

What is common in each one of these establishments is the fact they are frequented by Sri Lankans. This is evident if one visits the bubble tea shops on a given day. One might wonder whether Sri Lankans actually live on bubble tea! Many of these shops, with their bright lights, attractive spaces, cheap pricing and the range of products available have mainly attracted the youth and the Gen Z demographic. These establishments are also frequented by the local crowd working in corporate offices as they find it as a way to de-stress during hectic work hours.

Sri Lanka and China have maintained strong bilateral relations which dates back to the arrival of Fa Hien, the Chinese monk who arrived in Sri Lanka in 410 AD. Historical records suggest that the Ming dynasty ruler Zheng He arrived in the country on multiple occasions during the 15th century, leaving behind the Galle Trilingual Inscription. Sri Lanka became the first non-communist country as well as the first country in South Asia to recognise the People’s Republic of China in 1950. Subsequently in 1952, Sri Lanka inked the historic Rubber-Rice Pact with China and the relationship between the two countries were strengthened in years to come.

In subsequent years, China came forward to fund major infrastructure projects such as the Colombo Port City, the Hambantota Port, Norochcholai Power Station, Lotus Tower and Mattala Rajapaksa International Airport. China has supported Sri Lanka during domestic economic shortfalls, providing essential aid, medical supplies and other assistance. Today, China is one of Sri Lanka’s largest bilateral creditors.

According to an article published in our sister paper The Sunday Times, there are an estimated 1500 to 3000 independent, registered Chinese entrepreneurs running small and medium enterprises in Sri Lanka. Apart from the aforementioned businesses, some other enterprises range from hair salons to boutique hotels, grocery shops and gem trading businesses.

Media reports indicate that Sri Lanka witnessed this gradual influx of independent Chinese entrepreneurs from around 2010 with their arrivals tied to major state-backed infrastructure projects. Analysts opine that following the economic crisis, independent private capital shifted from mega scale investments to everyday retail and services.

In recent years, transnational cybercrime syndicates operating in South Asian countries have targeted Sri Lanka as a potential hub for criminal operations. Law enforcement reports indicate that arrests of foreign nationals linked to transnational cyber-scam networks doubled in 2026 compared to 2025. Major multi-raid operations occurred in waves, including the detention of 135 Chinese nationals in March, 152 individuals (mostly Chinese) in April, and subsequent arrests of dozens more in mid-2026 following high-profile incidents tied to online scam hubs operating in Colombo, Chilaw, and Anuradhapura. In July, a Chinese national was abducted from Port City and subsequently murdered. His body was recovered from Eheliyagoda.

On Monday (August 31), the Sri Lanka cabinet approved a proposal for Sri Lanka and China to enter into a bilateral Memorandum of Understanding on immigration regulatory cooperation. This  MoU will provide for the exchange of official information, intelligence and closer cooperation against organised crime linked to human trafficking and migrant smuggling.

According to the Cabinet decision, the MoU is also aimed at strengthening cooperation on visas, immigration and emigration, including facilitating travel for officials and citizens engaged in business, investment, tourism, scientific and technical activities.

In years to come, Sri Lankans are likely to experience  Chinese culture right at their doorstep.

Sri Lanka has always encouraged investors, irrespective of the scale of investment to enter into the market, but one should also keep in mind that this land, its law or its resources shouldn’t be exploited at any cost.
