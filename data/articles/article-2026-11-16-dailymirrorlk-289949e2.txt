Political violence and the shaming of the yellow robe

On Friday, President Anura Kumara Dissanayake (AKD) presented his third budget since assuming the post of  Minister of Finance. While he was presenting the country’s budget for the financial year 2027, another drama was unfolding in Anuradhapura.

A group of Buddhist monks led by Kassapa Thera of the ‘Release Gnanasara Thera’ fame had organised yet another satyagraha at the premises of the Ruwanwelisaya (one of Buddhism’s most sacred sites in the country) demanding the government drop its planned development of the sacred city of Anuradhapura.

The satyagrahees claim without any proof that the development programme is part of an Indian government plan  to construct other religious places of worship within the expanded sacred area, place restrictions on religious activities and build a proposed fence around the Ruwanweliseya itself.

They threatened a fast unto death campaign at the site unless the government dropped its proposed plan.

The Anuradhapura Magistrate, at the request of the police, ordered the satyagrahees to vacate the site. The satyagrahees rejected the Magistrate’s order. As this country has seen on earlier occasions, the police failed to implement the Magistrates order when dealing with members of the Buddhist clergy.

Balangoda Kassapa Thera said he would not comply with any court order. He claimed that no court order could be served on him and that only a Sangha Adhikarana had the authority to issue orders concerning him.  On Wednesday morning another group opposed to the satyagraha, led by an ex-monk arrived at the Ruwanwelisaya claiming the satyagraha was politically motivated and demanding the satyagrahees leave the sacred site. Soon physical violence broke out between the two groups.

As if the spectacle of physical violence between two groups of monks was not bad enough, the rival groups of monks resorted to language more akin to Billingsgate than men of the cloth. So much so that visual media broadcasters were forced to blur out sections of the audio in their podcasts.   This is not the first time sections of the Buddhist clergy have taken the law into their hands. The National Christian Evangelical Alliance of Sri Lanka (NCEASL) has documented more than 1,100 incidents of violence, discrimination, and intimidation against Christian churches and workers in Sri Lanka since the end of the civil war in May 2009.

Verite Research in its publication ‘Inaction and Impunity: Incidents of religious violence targeting Christians and Muslims 2015 – 2019’ points out: “…The state played a significant role in perpetrating and facilitating religious violence targeting Christians and Muslims during the period from January 2015 to August 2019.”

The report adds: “The level of state involvement remained the same from the previous five-year period (2010-2014). State officials were identified as  key perpetrators in over 40 percent of all incidents of violence.”

Additionally, the study records an overwhelmingly negative police response to incidents of violence perpetrated by non-state actors such as the Buddhist clergy.

In turn,  this has led sections within the Buddhist clergy to believe they are above the law. Balangoda Kassapa Thera’s statement as reported in the media (13 November 2026) that “he would not comply with any court order”… and that “no court order could be served on him and only a Sangha Adhikarana had the authority to issue orders concerning him…” comes out of this mentality. This is a dangerous trend and it needs to be nipped in the bud. Whether Kassapa Thera is merely a tool in the hands of disgruntled politicians will be realised later. But both the government and the heads of the Buddhist Nikkayas need to ensure that all persons in this country realise none is superior to the other.

Our Constitution gives foremost place to Buddhist beliefs –NOT to bigoted personalities clothed in saffron robes.

The heads of the three Nikayas used their positions to avert a confrontation between the state and a handful of bhikkus demanding the release of Gnanasara Thera in October this year.  They need to be aware of this situation and expose opportunists who attempt to use the saffron robes to achieve narrow political objectives. If not, this time around we may open a Pandora’s Box of problems based on ethnicity and religion.
