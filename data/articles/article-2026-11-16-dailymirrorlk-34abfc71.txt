First Capital Holdings reports first half results amid challenging market conditions

First Capital Holdings PLC, a subsidiary of JXG (Janashakthi Group) recorded a Loss after Tax of Rs. 1.27 billion for the first half of the financial year ended on 30 September 2026, compared to a Profit after Tax of Rs. 3.43 billion from the corresponding period of the previous year. For the second quarter of FY 2026/27, the Group’s Loss after Tax was Rs. 0.54 billion.

The Group’s performance reflects a challenging market environment, shaped by changes in monetary policy and pressure on fixed-income yields.

Operational conditions during the period were heavily influenced by a 100-basis-point increase in the monetary policy rate of May 2026, driven by inflationary pressures linked to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

Yields on government securities experienced volatility and came under pressure towards end of the first quarter and the second quarter, impacting performance across fixed-income, debt and equity trading portfolios.

However, market cycles are not unfamiliar territory for First Capital. The Group has successfully navigated similar periods of market volatility in the past, demonstrating its experience in operating through different phases of the financial cycle. During FY 2021/22, the Group recorded a Loss after Tax of Rs. 652 million amid challenging market conditions. As market conditions evolved, it returned to profitability, recording a Profit after Tax of Rs. 2.65 billion in FY 2022/23, followed by Rs. 10.16 billion in FY 2023/24, Rs. 5.02 billion in FY 2024/25 and Rs. 2.10 billion in FY 2025/26. This track record reflects First Capital’s depth of market experience and its ability to navigate shifting conditions while maintaining its focus on long-term business performance.

As a result, the Group’s core divisions experienced varied effects from the challenging trading environment. The Primary Dealer division recorded a Loss after Tax of Rs. 540 million for the first half of FY 2026/27, compared to a Profit after Tax of Rs. 1.57 billion in the same period last year. The division’s results were heavily affected by a trading loss of Rs. 1.69 billion on government securities, contrasting with a trading gain of Rs. 1.81 billion in H1 2025/26. Net Interest Income moderated to Rs. 687 million from Rs. 988 million in the prior year.

Concurrently, the Corporate Dealing Securities division posted a Loss after Tax of Rs. 834 million, compared to a Profit after Tax of Rs. 1.78 billion in H1 2025/26, following difficult trading conditions in the debt and equity markets.

In contrast, the Corporate Finance Advisory division recorded growth, reporting a Profit after Tax of Rs. 69 million, up from Rs. 25 million achieved in the corresponding period of the previous financial year.

The Wealth Management division reported a marginal Loss after Tax of Rs. 3 million against a Profit after Tax of Rs. 48 million in H1 2025/26, with the division’s Assets under Management maintaining at Rs. 92.4 billion as of 30 September 2026.

The Stock Brokering division maintained profitability, although Profit after Tax declined to Rs. 12 million for the six-month period, compared to Rs. 116 million from the corresponding period last year.

Rajendra Theagarajah, Chairman, First Capital Holdings PLC stated, “Higher interest rates and shifts in secondary-market yields created challenging conditions for debt and equity trading during the first half of the year, impacting the performance of the Primary Dealer and Corporate Dealing Securities divisions. In response, we have reaffirmed our focus on risk discipline, balance sheet strength, and operational efficiency, while remaining responsive to the evolving market environment.

We have also continued to strengthen the composition of our Boards and our broader governance and oversight frameworks as part of our ongoing commitment to organisational development and long-term institutional effectiveness.”

Dilshan Wirasekara, Managing Director / CEO, First Capital Holdings PLC noted, “Our Loss after Tax of Rs. 1.27 billion for the six months ended on 30 September 2026 was primarily attributed to the impact of higher interest rates and pressure on government securities yields. While consistent policy direction is important to supporting investor confidence and long-term market development, our focus remains on the areas within our control. We continue to strengthen our customer reach and enhance access to investment opportunities through digital services while remaining responsive to evolving investor needs and market conditions.”
