Drug regulation has collapsed, patients’ lives at risk: Specialist

Colombo, Aug. 3 (Daily Mirror) - The Medical and Civil Rights Doctors' Union, has alleged that Sri Lanka's drug regulatory system has "collapsed," warning that patients' lives are being placed at serious risk due to delays in approving medicines and persistent shortages of essential drugs.

Addressing the media, Chairman and Specialist Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa questioned the nearly two-year delay in approving the Qdenga dengue vaccine, claiming that the prolonged approval process should be investigated by the National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA) to determine whether negligence had occurred.

He further claimed that more than 40 essential medicines, including the cancer drug Oxaliplatin and Filgrastim, used to increase white blood cell counts, have remained in short supply for an extended period, leaving cancer patients and those suffering from other serious illnesses facing a severe crisis.

Dr. Sanjeewa also alleged that while internationally recognised medicines such as Mounjaro, used to treat diabetes and obesity, have yet to receive official approval in Sri Lanka, they are readily available through the black market.

"While authorised agents are legally waiting for NMRA approval, black market operators are illegally importing and selling these drugs without maintaining the required cold chain.

Who will be responsible if someone dies after using them?" he questioned.He further alleged that strict price controls have driven globally recognised pharmaceutical brands out of the Sri Lankan market, resulting in an influx of lower-cost medicines of questionable quality.

Raising further concerns, Dr. Sanjeewa claimed that price controls have also affected laboratory services, alleging that some private hospitals have been forced to use lower-quality testing reagents, leading to inaccurate laboratory reports and potentially fatal misdiagnoses.

Calling for urgent reforms, the Doctors' Union proposed introducing an online drug approval tracking system, fast-tracking the approval of internationally recognised medicines, establishing WHO-standard testing for imported drugs, strengthening action against the black market, and adopting a more flexible pricing formula based on global market conditions.

The allegations have not yet been responded to by the Health Ministry or the National Medicines Regulatory Authority.
