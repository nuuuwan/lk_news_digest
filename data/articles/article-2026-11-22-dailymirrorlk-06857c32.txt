Oil spill from Colombo Port tanker pollutes Muthuwela coastline

Colombo, Nov. 22 (Daily Mirror) - An oil spill from a tanker at the Colombo Port Complex has polluted the urban drainage canal and more than three kilometres of the Muthuwela coastline, prompting an ongoing clean-up operation.

The Marine Environment Protection Authority (MEPA) said the spill originated from JCT No. 108 oil tanker, which has a storage capacity of around 1,000 tonnes and was reportedly full at the time of the incident.

The leak, believed to have occurred through a damaged or faulty drain valve, involved Marine Gas Oil (MGO) stored in the tanker.

The spill was first reported on the morning of November 21, with MEPA receiving information about the incident at around 9.30 a.m.

MEPA officers immediately visited the site, assessed the situation and coordinated with relevant authorities to provide technical guidance on containment and clean-up measures.

The Sri Lanka Ports Authority (SLPA) managed to stop the leak by around 10.30 a.m. on the same day. However, some of the spilled oil had already entered the municipal drainage canal and flowed into the sea along the Muthuwela coastline.

The affected area includes more than three kilometres of coastline, including the B-Gate anchorage.

Following the incident, the SLPA and Sri Lanka Coast Guard launched containment and clean-up operations under the technical guidance and coordination of MEPA.

Oil containment booms have been deployed to prevent the spread of the oil, while teams have begun collecting and removing the spilled oil.

MEPA said containment, collection and clean-up operations were continuing as of November 22, with the authority closely monitoring the situation.

It said efforts were being coordinated with relevant agencies to minimise environmental damage, remove the spilled oil and restore the affected coastal environment.
