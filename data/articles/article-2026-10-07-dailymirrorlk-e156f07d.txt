Publication of certain information on media considered an act of terrorism

Colombo, Oct. 7 (Daily Mirror) - Under the Protection of State from Terrorism Bill which was presented to Parliament yesterday, publication of certain information in the Media which encourages the public or a section of the public to commit an act of terrorism, tantamount to an act of terrorism

“Any person who, with the knowledge of directly or indirectly inducing the public or section of the public, to commit, to attempt, abet, conspire to commit, or prepare to commit an offence of terrorism, publishes or causes to be published any statement, or speaks any word or words or make any sign or visible representation commits an act under the Bill,” section 9 of the Bill has stated.

At the same time ,section 11 of the Bill says fair and accurate reporting, commentary or analysis by journalists or media organizations, academic , scientific, educational research or discussion , work of civil society organizations , including human rights documentation, advocacy, or public interest compunction, artistic , literary, cultural or religious expression including satire , parody or creative work , expression of an opinion, belief, criticism or dissent, including legitimate political discourse or dissemination of information on matters of public interest will not be considered as acts of terrorism as per the legislation.

However, causing a death of a person, grievous hurt, hostage taking, causing serious damage to any place of public use, public property and private/public transportation system and infrastructure facility and causing serious risk to the health of the people are considered as offences under the Bill. Also causing damages to cyber environment domains, hacking and destruction of tangible cultural property are also considered as acts of terrorism. Furthermore, failure to provide information is also considered as an act of terrorism.

Another salient feature of the Protection of the State from Terrorism Bill is the powers given to police officers under the Criminal Procedure Code and is extended to any member of the armed forces or a coastal guard officer. The said members of the forces are given the power to stop and search any vehicle on the road, vessel or an aircraft.
