India-Sri Lanka talks to seek permanent solution to fisheries dispute in January

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - High-level bilateral talks between Sri Lanka and India will be held in New Delhi on January 22, 2027, to find a lasting solution to the long-standing fisheries dispute, Fisheries, Aquatic and Ocean Resources Minister Ramalingam Chandrasekar said.

The Minister said the government was fully committed to resolving the issue through practical and sustainable measures, adding that discussions were already underway at diplomatic and official levels.

A Sri Lankan delegation headed by the Secretary to the Ministry of Fisheries, Aquatic and Ocean Resources is scheduled to visit India on January 22 to hold discussions with senior officials of India's Ministry of Fisheries and other relevant authorities.

The meeting, to be held under the India-Sri Lanka Joint Working Group (JWG) on Fisheries, will focus on key issues affecting the fisheries sectors of the two countries.

The main concerns to be discussed include the entry of Tamil Nadu fishermen into Sri Lankan territorial waters, the use of banned bottom trawling methods and the resulting damage to marine ecosystems and the livelihoods of local fishermen. Both sides are expected to explore practical measures to bring a permanent end to these issues.

The Sri Lankan delegation will comprise representatives from the Ministry of Fisheries, Aquatic and Ocean Resources, the Department of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources, the Sri Lanka Navy, the Sri Lanka Police, the Sri Lanka Coast Guard, the National Aquatic Resources Research and Development Agency (NARA), the Attorney General's Department and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Minister Chandrasekar said the participation of all relevant state institutions would enable the government to comprehensively examine the dispute and identify the most effective solutions.

The upcoming meeting follows the previous session of the India-Sri Lanka Joint Working Group on Fisheries held in Sri Lanka in late 2024. The meeting was attended by a high-level Indian delegation led by the Secretary of India's Ministry of Fisheries, along with representatives of the Indian Navy.

The Minister expressed confidence that the Sri Lankan Government would receive the support of Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, External Affairs Minister Dr. S. Jaishankar and key stakeholders in the Tamil Nadu Government in reaching a lasting settlement.

He reiterated that the government was committed to protecting the livelihoods of Sri Lankan fishermen, safeguarding the country's marine resources and maintaining friendly bilateral relations through constructive engagement with India.
