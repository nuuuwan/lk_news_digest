Earthquake of magnitude 7 strikes China’s Tibet

Oct 22 (Reuters) - An earthquake of magnitude 7 ​struck China's southwestern region of Tibet ‌at around 10:33 p.m. (1433 GMT) on Thursday, the China Earthquake Networks Center (CENC) said.

It ​was not immediately known if there ​were any casualties or damage.

The epicentre ⁠was in a high-altitude county ​in Nagqu, northern Tibet, at a depth ​of 10 km (6.21 miles), and the average elevation within 5 km of the epicentre is ​about 4,864 metres, CENC data ​showed.

The quake, the strongest to hit China since ‌January ⁠2024, was clearly felt in the local area, CENC said. The epicentre was around 400 km from the Tibetan ​capital of ​Lhasa.

In ⁠2010, a magnitude 7.1 tremor struck Yushu, around 280 km ​away in neighbouring Qinghai province, ​killing ⁠at least 2,698 people and leaving nearly 300 people missing.
