One-off Rolls-Royce gain cushions SriLankan’s Rs.20bn five-month loss

SriLankan’s revenue climbed 35% to Rs.154.6bn between April and August, but a 78% surge in jet fuel costs tipped the airline into an operating loss of Rs.5.8bn

The President told parliament that the national carrier’s accumulated losses since 2008 have reached Rs.528bn

SriLankan Airlines would have posted a net loss more than twice as large in the first five months of its financial year but for a Rs.11.4 billion one-off reversal of Rolls-Royce withheld discounts and suspended credits, as a jet fuel shock and currency losses swamped a 35 percent rise in revenue, Finance Ministry data showed.

The national carrier’s revenue rose to Rs.154.6 billion in the April to August period from Rs.114.6 billion a year earlier. However, expenditure grew faster, climbing 35.2 percent to Rs.160.4 billion as aircraft fuel costs surged 78.2 percent, leaving the airline with an operating loss of Rs.5.8 billion.

Below the operating line, financing costs of Rs.7.1 billion and foreign exchange losses of Rs.11.9 billion, together more than three times the operating loss, pushed the group’s net loss to Rs.20.3 billion, or about Rs.4 billion a month, before the Rolls-Royce adjustment pared it to Rs.8.9 billion.

The airline’s loss before tax had already tripled to Rs.23.2 billion in the 2025/26 financial year from Rs.7.6 billion a year earlier, as debt servicing and currency costs wiped out earnings before interest and tax of Rs.26.4 billion. The ministry noted that Middle East tensions had disrupted flight operations, restricted airspace and forced costly route diversions this year, weighing on a carrier heavily exposed to Middle Eastern routes.

Presenting the 2027 budget, President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, who is also the Minister of Finance, Planning and Economic Development, revealed that SriLankan’s accumulated losses since the government took back control of the airline in 2008 had reached Rs.528 billion by 2025. That is an average of about Rs.29 billion a year and more than double the Rs.260 billion supplementary estimate for Cyclone Ditwah reconstruction in 2027.

“SriLankan Airlines has become a burden on the General Treasury,” he told Parliament.

Meanwhile, the government has extended US$ 30 million, or about Rs.10 billion, in short-term assistance to meet the airline’s immediate operational and liquidity needs. It is also restructuring the airline’s US$ 175 million sovereign-guaranteed bond that matured in 2024 and pursuing a settlement arrangement, initiated in 2025, for its legacy debt. The Treasury also guarantees Rs.3.8 billion of SriLankan’s obligations to the International Air Transport Association (IATA).

In June, the Cabinet appointed a committee chaired by Presidential Senior Adviser on Digital Economy Dr. Hans Wijayasuriya, with the International Finance Corporation (IFC) as transaction adviser, to conduct a strategic review and draw up a restructuring framework to cut the airline’s reliance on state support. The Finance Ministry warned that sustained restructuring, prudent cash-flow management and greater operational efficiency would be essential to contain fiscal risks to the government. (NF)
