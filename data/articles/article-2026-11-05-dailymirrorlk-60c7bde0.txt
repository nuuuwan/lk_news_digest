ECT delay - a textbook case of stranded capital

Your excellent analysis on “Why Colombo’s East Container Terminal (ECT) Still Isn’t Working” exposes how poor design, fragmented procurement and weak governance turned a strategic national asset into stranded capital.

The facts are damning. The first phase of ECT was completed in mid-2015, yet after 11 years, it still does not operate as intended. The President himself revealed at the Economic Summit that it handles only 500,000 TEUs against a built capacity of 3.2 million TEUs. In other words, we use only 15% of what we built with public money.

The reason is almost comic if it were not tragic. Four tenders for straddle carriers failed. We are now on a fifth tender for 15 machines, and even after award,  delivery takes 10-12 months. The promised date of  December 14, 2026 is therefore only for partial operation, with full operation unlikely before July 2027. Meanwhile,  the contractor claims Rs. 4,227 million for 548 days of delay caused by our own decisions -- money the SLPA, and ultimately the taxpayer, must pay.

The President’s proposal to retain state ownership but give management to private shipping lines for efficiency is pragmatic. It is regrettable that port unions immediately oppose any private participation, even as the port bleeds revenue. National interest must prevail over vested interests.

If we cannot operate a terminal on the world’s busiest shipping lane, how will we achieve export-led growth?
