Galle becomes launchpad for India’s Otherland as first lifestyle hotel opens

66-key property opens October 12, crowned by Galle’s largest rooftop club

Otherland Galle, a 66-key lifestyle hotel crowned by what its promoters describe as the largest rooftop club in the city, will open on October 12 as the first venture of Bengaluru-based Otherland Hotels, which is betting on music, food and surf culture to draw experience-seeking holidaymakers down south.

Set on Matara Road, about 400 metres from Dewata Beach and 2.8 kilometres from Galle Fort, the property blurs the line between hotel, ‘poshtel’ and surf club, with 24 ocean-view rooms, 25 Terra rooms overlooking the mangroves and the railway line, 12 suites and five dorms fitted with four and six bunk beds.

Otherland Hotels Founder and Chief Executive Officer Rishi Sreedharan, who earlier co-founded the Indian co-living platform Hyphen, explained that the brand functions as an asset-light management and operations company, with the hotel owned by India’s Beryll Group.

Beryll, a Kolkata-based interiors and construction contractor for hotels, hospitals and airports, first came to the island in 2018 to work on ITC Ratnadipa in Colombo. Beryll Group Managing Partner Vishal Agarwal recalled that the group purchased the Galle building in 2023, when it was largely being run as a hostel for seafarers, and spent about one and a half years refurbishing it, with the investment made by a consortium of Indian and Sri Lankan partners. He declined to disclose the amount.

“After the opening up of the highway, we felt that this is the area we should be focusing on,” Agarwal told journalists during a media preview, adding that Colombo already has plenty of options.

The choice of Sri Lanka for the brand’s first hotel, ahead of its home market, came about through that connection. Otherland Galle Co-founder and Director Emanuel Siddhartha, who was born and raised in Galle, where his family owns the Closenberg Hotel, recounted that Beryll’s owners invited Sreedharan and Otherland Hotels Co-founder Samarth Gowda to see the property, after which the three decided to build the brand together.

“Sri Lanka and India have such close proximity, and culturally there are so many connections. We decided Sri Lanka is the perfect place to start a brand like this,” Siddhartha said, pointing out that work on the project began three years ago, when the island was still emerging from the economic crisis.

“As a Sri Lankan, I can say that my Indian partners have been extremely passionate about Sri Lanka and they have always believed in this country,” he added.

The hotel’s showpiece is Creatures, a 150-cover rooftop venue that spends the day as a restaurant, cocktail bar and pool club, before the lights drop and it turns into a nightclub that runs until 3 a.m. on a Void Acoustics sound system shipped from the United Kingdom. The week on the roof moves from soulful afro and R&B on Wednesdays and live Sri Lankan folk funk and fusion jazz to house and techno on Fridays and Saturdays, winding down with an all-vinyl brunch on Sundays.

The bar at Creatures is headed by Aneesha Thapa, who carries the designation ‘Creature in Chief’, and pours a list developed with Indian bartender Arijit Bose. It opens with seven signatures and seven classics, built around arrack, gin and local ingredients such as kithul and sardine brine, with new drinks to be added every month.

“We’ve kept the menu very easy, very playful, very refreshing, so that people can just enjoy their sunsets,” Thapa noted.

The sound of the hotel is the work of Siddhartha, a DJ who made his name with the vinyl culture collective Tropical Wax. He curates the artists who play on the roof and shaped the listening corner at the ground floor Bar-kery, a 40-cover lobby bar, bakery and gelateria where guests are invited to drop the needle themselves.

“Artists that come from abroad and play in Sri Lanka get to experience Sri Lankan culture and interact with Sri Lankan artists. We believe it is through these cultural exchanges that magic really happens,” he observed.

Beyond the building, the hotel runs The Other Side, a menu of surf lessons, Rumassala hikes, Koggala mangrove picnics, curry masterclasses and arrack tastings led by surfers, cooks and guides from the neighbourhood. “What we like to do the most is nudge people outside of our rooms. It’s not just about the rooms,” Sreedharan remarked.

The kitchens and day-to-day running of the hotel fall under Gowda, the Chief Operating Officer and a civil engineer by training. “Otherland Galle should feel like yours from the moment you walk in,” he said.

Although the hotel carries a four-star classification, Sreedharan asserted that its product and pricing sit well above that tier. Published rates start from US$ 160 a night for a Terra room in the current low season and rise to US$ 485 for an Ocean Suite over the festive period, on a bed and breakfast basis before taxes and service charge.

Looking ahead, Sreedharan indicated that Galle is the first of several properties the brand intends to open, with Yala and the tea country among the locations being explored, although the priority is to build a firm footing down south before moving further afield.

However, he stressed that Otherland is not working towards a set number of rooms or revenue. “If you’re entering a market, you should do it the right way, you should do it sustainably, you should understand your place in that market. We should be one of the most thoughtful hospitality brands wherever we are,” he emphasised.

On the guests it is courting, Sreedharan pointed out that the hotel is open to travellers across age groups who are looking for an experience instead of merely a place to stay, and that it is now listed on online travel agencies while working with destination management companies and travel agents. Siddhartha added that the team had first pictured a younger crowd, but now expects families as well. “Age is not a defining thing. It’s more people who are interested in lifestyle experiences, in music, art and culture,” he explained.

At the property itself, a second phase a year from now will add a gym, a wellness centre and banquet facilities, while an Otherland Foundation is being set up to fund community programmes such as coral reef restoration through guest donations.

Meanwhile, the hotel employs about 100 staff, or roughly 1.5 for every key, of whom over 95 percent are Sri Lankan. The interiors are by Studio Camarada and the property is run by General Manager Jazib Husain, a 20-year industry veteran, with Director of Culinary Rajeev Kumar heading the kitchens.
