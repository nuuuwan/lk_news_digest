Foreign employment industry cannot be allowed to drift into further crisis: Azzam

Colombo, Sep. 6 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka’s foreign employment industry cannot be allowed to descend further into institutional disorder and internal conflict, President of the United Vision Platform M.Z.M. Azzam said, calling for an urgent resolution to the ongoing crisis surrounding the Association of Licensed Foreign Employment Agencies (ALFEA).

Azzam said ALFEA was not merely a voluntary association of recruitment agencies, but an institution established within the statutory framework created by the Sri Lanka Bureau of Foreign Employment Act No. 21 of 1985.

He said Part VIII of the Act provides for the constitution of ALFEA through a Gazette notification and requires every licensed foreign employment agency to become a member of the association.

According to Azzam, the Sri Lanka Bureau of Foreign Employment (SLBFE) serves as the regulator, while ALFEA provides collective representation for licensed foreign employment agencies, making both institutions important components of the country’s foreign employment framework.

He said the prolonged dispute involving ALFEA, including ongoing court proceedings and competing interests, had created uncertainty for around 1,000 licensed agencies and those dependent on the industry.

“I fully respect the judicial process, and no individual or institution should attempt to interfere with matters properly before a court,” Azzam said.

However, he argued that the wider institutional paralysis surrounding the issue could not be allowed to continue indefinitely.

He called on all parties to resolve their differences through lawful, democratic and transparent means and urged the authorities to ensure that the industry has a functioning representative body with legitimate leadership and accountability.

Azzam also criticised what he described as a lack of direct engagement by the relevant Ministry with licensed foreign employment agencies.

He claimed that the Ministry had not convened a meeting bringing together all licence holders and urged it to use whatever lawful administrative and policy powers were available to help restore stability to the sector while respecting the independence of the judiciary.

“If there are legal limitations preventing intervention, those limitations should be clearly explained to the industry. If administrative action is possible, it should be taken,” he said.

He warned that prolonged inaction could further weaken governance within the sector.

Azzam further expressed concern over the increasing use of social media platforms, including TikTok and other online channels, by individuals within the industry to make accusations and engage in public disputes.

He claimed that several groups were presenting themselves as associations despite not being formally registered associations, saying this had contributed to confusion over legitimate representation.

He warned that personal attacks and public confrontations among licence holders could damage the reputation of the entire foreign employment recruitment sector.

“The public already views our industry with considerable suspicion because of years of complaints, controversies, fraudulent operators and negative publicity,” he said.

Azzam said industry stakeholders should instead work collectively to rebuild public confidence and strengthen professional standards.

Azzam said he had previously criticised ALFEA leadership on several issues and had raised concerns about weaknesses within the foreign employment industry through the United Vision Platform.

However, he stressed that criticism of individuals should not be allowed to undermine the institution itself.

“ALFEA is bigger than any President, Secretary, committee, group or individual licence holder,” he said, adding that institutions must remain stronger than personalities.

Azzam also said he was preparing to leave the foreign employment recruitment industry in the near future, making what he described as a final appeal to stakeholders to resolve the ALFEA crisis.

He urged the Ministry, SLBFE, ALFEA stakeholders and licensed agencies to work within the existing legal framework to find a sustainable solution.

“Stop the personal battles. Stop humiliating one another publicly. Stop turning an entire national industry into a social-media battlefield,” he said.

Azzam stressed that thousands of Sri Lankan migrant workers and their families depended on the foreign employment sector and warned that failure to resolve the dispute would ultimately harm the industry and the country.

“Sri Lanka needs a strong SLBFE, a properly functioning ALFEA and responsible licensed foreign employment agencies. All three must be capable of working together,” he said.
