SEC highlights CSE as most attractively valued equity market in Asia despite YTD decline

Sri Lanka currently stands as the most attractively valued equity market in the Asian region, trading at a steep discount compared to its peers despite recording a year-to-date decline, according to the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC).

A regional valuation analysis released by the capital market regulator shows the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) trading at a price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of 11.3x, positioned well below other major regional bourses. The lower valuation multiple comes alongside a 6 percent year-to-date (YTD) contraction recorded in 2026. The SEC noted that this valuation discount could present significant upside potential for investors as corporate earnings and broader market confidence improve alongside ongoing economic recovery efforts.

However, the regulator cautioned that a low P/E multiple is only a single metric of market attractiveness. It urged market participants to take a holistic approach, weighing essential indicators such as listed company earnings growth, underlying macroeconomic conditions, market liquidity, and investor sentiment when evaluating the market’s forward trajectory.

The regulator’s regional comparison reveals varied market performance across Asia. High-growth markets such as Japan, Thailand, and Singapore have advanced by roughly 30 percent, 27 percent, and 23 percent YTD, while carrying steeper valuations with P/E ratios of 18.5x, 16x, and 19x, respectively. Malaysia posted modest gains of 4 percent with a P/E multiple of 13.5x, while Hong Kong remained relatively flat with a 1 percent dip at a 14x multiple.

Meanwhile, larger regional markets facing steeper sell-offs this year continue to command higher price tags than Sri Lanka. South Korea and India experienced sharper contractions of approximately 15 percent and 11 percent YTD, yet maintain elevated valuation multiples of 17.8x and 20.3x.
