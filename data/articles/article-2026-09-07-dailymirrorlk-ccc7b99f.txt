Sri Lanka storm into semis as sports world erupts

1. Harshitha Holds Nerve as Sri Lanka Storm into Asia Cup Semis

Sri Lanka booked their place in the Women’s Asia Cup semi-finals after a thrilling four-wicket victory over Bangladesh in Dubai, with Harshitha Samarawickrama producing a match-winning unbeaten knock.

Chasing a modest target of 115, Sri Lanka suffered early setbacks as fast bowler Marufa Akter removed captain Chamari Athapaththu and Imesha Dulani to put Bangladesh on top.

The pressure continued to build as wickets fell regularly, leaving Sri Lanka in trouble during the middle overs.

Harshitha, however, stood firm under pressure and anchored the chase with a composed unbeaten 48. She found valuable support from Nilakshika Silva as the pair steadied the innings before Harshitha shifted gears in the closing stages.

With Sri Lanka needing 33 runs from the final four overs, Harshitha struck crucial boundaries and a six in the 19th over to guide her side home with four balls to spare.

Earlier, Bangladesh posted 114 after disciplined bowling from Sri Lanka’s spinners. Chamudi Praboda starred with the ball, claiming impressive figures of 3-21, while Sobhana Mostary top-scored for Bangladesh with 34.

Sri Lanka finished the group stage unbeaten and advanced to the semi-finals, while Bangladesh remain in contention and can still qualify with a victory over the United Arab Emirates.

Aryna Sabalenka overcame a tricky challenge from Taylor Townsend to beat the American 6-4, 6-3 and reach the US Open quarter-finals for a sixth consecutive year.

The two-time defending champion recovered from 3-1 down in the second set, winning five consecutive games to seal victory.

The match featured 10 breaks of serve as Townsend pushed Sabalenka throughout, but the Belarusian eventually found another gear to close out the contest.

Sabalenka extended her winning streak at Flushing Meadows to 18 matches and will next face Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova for a place in the semi-finals.

Kimi Antonelli produced one of Formula 1’s most remarkable victories, charging from 19th on the grid to win the Italian Grand Prix at Monza.

Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff said he had sensed the young Italian could produce something special even before the race.

Antonelli’s recovery began with a strong opening phase before Charles Leclerc’s crash brought out the red flag.

He restarted in 12th, climbed to eighth a lap later and then passed Lando Norris to move into seventh, quickly joining the fight at the front.

Wolff said Antonelli had effectively “walked over water” through the traffic.

George Russell, who started on the front row, admitted he knew Antonelli would be a threat once he saw his team-mate surge through the field.

“When a driver and a car are one, then there’s nothing that can stop them,” Wolff said after Antonelli completed a victory that will go down in F1 folklore.

FIFA President Gianni Infantino will stand for re-election in 2027 despite growing criticism of his leadership and the threat of legal proceedings.

FIFA confirmed that the 56-year-old has not changed his plans to seek another term.

Infantino avoided questions about his future at the Under-20 Women’s World Cup in Poland but later said there would be a “right place and a right time” to address the issue.

His support has increasingly weakened among football confederations and national associations.

Critics and rights groups have challenged his bid for a fourth term, while UEFA is considering criminal proceedings in Switzerland over FIFA’s now-abandoned plan to transfer commercial rights to a new subsidiary.

Infantino has also faced criticism over his close ties with US President Donald Trump and plans to maximise FIFA’s World Cup revenues.

British racing driver Ollie Millroy said he is “only alive” thanks to rival Loek Hartog, who pulled him from his burning Ferrari during a China GT Championship race in Shanghai.

Hartog abandoned his own car, used a fire extinguisher to tackle the flames and dragged Millroy from the wreckage after the crash.

Millroy suffered five broken ribs, a broken collarbone, a broken hand, a broken finger and a bruised lung.

Millroy’s team, AAI Motorsport, criticised the safety response and said it would no longer compete in China GT races.

Organisers said they would investigate the accident and review shortcomings in competition organisation, emergency response, rescue and medical services.
