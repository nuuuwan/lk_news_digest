Govt. says CESS cuts aim to boost manufacturing, export competitiveness

Cuts on 800 import lines to proceed from 1 Apr. 2027 as gazetted, says Finance Ministry

Aims to remove trade barriers and put import tariff structure on a rational basis

Phase-out began with the 2023 Budget; 1,523 consumption goods lines to follow in 2029

The Finance Ministry said yesterday that the CESS cuts on 800 import tariff lines under the 2027 Budget are meant to promote manufacturing, remove barriers to international trade and make Sri Lankan exports more competitive. It confirmed that the reductions under Budget Proposal No. 36.11 will take effect from 1 April, 2027, as published in Extraordinary Gazette No. 2479/38 on 10 March, 2026.

The phase-out began under 2023 Budget Proposal No. 34.6, which aimed to promote manufacturing industries and help domestic industries and enterprises, including agriculture, to grow. The 2025 Budget extended it with the aim of “presenting the import tariff structure on a rational basis, increasing export competitiveness and enhancing transparency in the tariff mechanism,” the Ministry said. CESS on 1,523 consumption goods lines is due to be removed in 2029.

The Daily FT reported on 14 November that CESS will go entirely on 499 lines from 1 April, 2027, and that all the listed lines will be cess-free by 1 April, 2029 (See https://www.ft.lk/front-page/CESS-cut-on-795-import-lines-from-April-2027/44-800095).
