Coral reef survey conducted in Kayankerni

Colombo, Aug. 6 (Daily Mirror) - The Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC) has completed a comprehensive coral reef survey within the Kayankerni Marine Sanctuary and the adjacent Pasikuda coastal area to evaluate the current status of coral reef ecosystems and strengthen future conservation and management initiatives.

The survey, carried out by the DWC's Marine Unit, assessed the health of coral reefs inside the protected sanctuary as well as in nearby unprotected waters. It also identified natural and human-induced threats affecting the reefs while collecting scientific data to support their long-term conservation.

The survey further explored opportunities to promote sustainable tourism in the area while ensuring the protection of these ecologically significant marine ecosystems.

Under Sri Lanka's Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance (FFPO), corals are listed as protected species under the Seventh Schedule. Coral reefs are formed by reef-building coral polyps living in a symbiotic relationship with microscopic algae known as zooxanthellae, enabling them to create complex reef structures that support some of the world's richest marine ecosystems.

The DWC said that coral reefs play a crucial role in maintaining marine biodiversity by providing habitat, shelter, breeding and feeding grounds for numerous marine species. They also protect coastlines from wave action and coastal erosion, support fisheries, and contribute significantly to tourism and the livelihoods of coastal communities.

According to the department, the findings of the survey will provide valuable baseline data for the conservation, restoration and sustainable management of coral reef ecosystems in the Kayankerni Marine Sanctuary and the Pasikuda area. The information will also help guide future conservation measures and management decisions in collaboration with relevant stakeholders.
