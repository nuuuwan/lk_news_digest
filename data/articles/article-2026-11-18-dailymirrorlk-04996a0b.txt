Gold slides to over one-year low on firm dollar, US rate outlook

Reuters - Gold prices fell more than 1% on Wednesday to their lowest level ‌in over a year, as a firmer dollar and expectations of higher US interest rates weighed, while investors awaited minutes from the Federal Reserve's meeting for clues on the policy outlook.

Spot ​gold was down 1.6% at $3,787.65 per ounce, as of 0428 GMT, after hitting ​its lowest level since September 2025 earlier in the session.

US gold ⁠futures for December delivery dropped 1.9% to $3,789.40.

"The bearish movement in gold prices have ​been primarily driven by sentiment and technicality, with hawkish reaffirmation from Fed officials and ​a push up in the US Treasury yields in turn leading to a firmer US dollar," said Kelvin Wong, senior market analyst at OANDA.

The dollar held steady near a 19-month high, making greenback-priced ​bullion more expensive for buyers overseas.

Fed Bank of New York President John Williams said ​on Tuesday the US central bank will likely need to raise rates again to lean against ‌inflationary ⁠pressures, but he pushed back against the idea the Fed needs to shift to a more aggressive path of action.

Markets see a 95.7% chance of a 25-basis-point US rate hike in December, while the probability of a larger 50-basis-point move is priced ​at 4.3%, according to ​CME's FedWatch tool.

Higher ⁠rates increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion, reducing its appeal to investors.

"Gold is again being treated as a liquidity ​asset, with positions cut to cover volatility and free up ​capital elsewhere ⁠in the market," Tim Waterer, chief market analyst at KCM Trade, said in a note.

Investors now await the minutes of the Fed's October 27-28 Federal Open Market Committee meeting, ⁠due ​at 1900 GMT.

Among other metals, spot silver slipped ​3.1% to $45.94 per ounce, platinum dropped 1.5% to $1,408.30 and palladium slid 2% to $951.42.
