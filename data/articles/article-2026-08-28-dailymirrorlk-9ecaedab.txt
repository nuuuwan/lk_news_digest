Two years almost gone; State bungalows await productive use

Colombo, Aug. 28 (Daily Mirror) - Despite the lapse of nearly two years, the government is still in the process of converting around 30 state bungalows formerly occupied by Cabinet ministers into productive-use properties, the Daily Mirror learns.

The National People’s Power (NPP), in the run-up to the Presidential Election held on September 21, 2024, pledged not to use the colonial-era bungalows as official residences for its ministers.

The Ministry of Public Administration, Provincial Councils and Local Government took charge of these properties, along with residences vacated by former presidents, under the newly introduced Presidents’ Entitlements (Repeal) Act.

The Ministry has since handed the properties over to the Presidential Secretariat, which is overseeing efforts to identify productive uses for them.

Public Administration Minister Chandana Abeyratne told the Daily Mirror that one of the bungalows would be converted into a courthouse.

“The Presidential Secretariat has taken charge of these houses. There have been requests from foreign missions to use some of them, while there are also proposals for commercial activities. The Presidential Secretariat is carrying out the necessary work in this regard,” he said.
