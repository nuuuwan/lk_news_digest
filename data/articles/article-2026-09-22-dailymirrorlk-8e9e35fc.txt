Referendum cannot be called as a result of protests: Nalinda Jayatissa

Colombo, Sep. 22 (Daily Mirror) - A referendum cannot be called as a result of protests or public demonstrations against the proposed 22nd Amendment to the Constitution, Cabinet Spokesman Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa said today.

He said any party had the right to oppose the Supreme Court’s determination and organise protests, but the decision on whether a referendum was constitutionally required rested with the Supreme Court.

“The right to hold protests and public demonstrations remains. There is no issue with that. However, a referendum cannot be called as a result of protests or demonstrations,” he said, addressing the Cabinet decision media briefing.

The Supreme Court has determined that the provisions of the 22nd Amendment Bill, or any part thereof, do not require approval by the people at a referendum under Article 83 of the Constitution. The Court has, however, directed specific amendments to the Bill.

Minister Jayatissa said the Government had followed all procedures required under the Constitution in proceeding with the proposed amendment.

He said the Supreme Court had provided parties with sufficient opportunity to submit petitions and present their arguments on the Bill, adding that the Bar Association of Sri Lanka had also made submissions before the Court.

According to the Minister, the question of whether a referendum was required for a constitutional amendment was ultimately determined by the Supreme Court in accordance with the Constitution.

The Speaker informed Parliament today of the Supreme Court’s determination, which stated that the 22nd Amendment did not require approval at a referendum. The Court also directed amendments to Clause 2 of the Bill, including a change concerning the age of 67 and an exception to the proposed six-year term limit for a person holding office as Chief Justice when the Act comes into operation.

Parliament will hold a two-day debate on the 22nd Amendment Bill on September 24 and 25, with the vote expected to be held after the debate on September 25, Minister Jayatissa said. Parliament has scheduled the Second Reading debate on the Bill and the Judicature (Amendment) Bill for September 24 and 25.

Minister Jayatissa said those who disagreed with the Supreme Court’s determination would remain free to express their views in Parliament or organise demonstrations, while the Government would proceed according to the constitutional procedure.
