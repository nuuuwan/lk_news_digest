No fuel shortage, private supply cuts and panic buying trigger queues: CPC Chairman

Colombo, October 7 (Daily Mirror) - The recent fuel queues across the country were caused by private petroleum companies reducing their daily fuel release and panic buying triggered by false reports on social media, while there is no shortage of fuel in the country, Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) Chairman D. J. A. S. De S. Rajakaruna said yesterday.

Addressing the media, Rajakaruna said private petroleum companies had reduced their daily fuel supplies, citing losses incurred by them, forcing the CPC to increase its own daily fuel releases to bridge the resulting supply gap.

He said the CPC had increased its daily diesel issuance from 3,074 metric tons to 3,900 metric tons to meet the shortfall created by reduced supplies from private companies.

“There is an issue with the daily release of fuel stocks by the other private petroleum companies. Their daily fuel release capacity has been reduced. This was seen with diesel stocks since last October, while the limitation on petrol stocks was observed later,” he said.

According to Rajakaruna, the Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) has reduced its daily diesel issuance by 45%, from around 965 metric tons in January to 535 metric tons at present.

He said Sinopec had reduced its daily diesel issuance by 66%, from 587 metric tons in January to 201 metric tons, while RM Parks had reduced its daily diesel issuance by 48%, from 450 metric tons to 236 metric tons.

The CPC Chairman said private companies had also reduced their daily petrol releases.

“Sinopec has reduced its petrol issuance by 30%. It is now issuing 331 metric tons of petrol, compared with 471 metric tons in February. RM Parks has also reduced its petrol issuance by 30%, from 471 metric tons to 313 metric tons,” he said.

He said the CPC had been compelled to fill the supply gap created by the reductions in private-sector fuel releases.

Rajakaruna also attributed the queues to panic buying following the circulation of what he described as false statements and reports on social media.

“Due to the queues, certain false statements and stories have circulated on social media, and people have gathered at filling stations as a result,” he said.

He urged motorists not to panic and requested them to obtain their fuel requirements from the nearest CPC filling station, assuring that the CPC has sufficient capacity to meet the existing supply gap.

Although private petroleum companies have been unable to maintain their previous supply levels due to losses, the CPC has the capacity to compensate for the shortfall and ensure uninterrupted fuel supplies, he said.

Meanwhile, Rajakaruna assured motorists that there would be no fuel price revision during the next three months, provided global market conditions remain unchanged.

The Sri Lanka Podujana Progressive Employees' Union-Petroleum (SLPPEU-P) has urged the Government to reconsider or abolish private petroleum companies, alleging that they slow fuel sales when prices are reduced while selling normally when prices increase.

Chief Secretary Bandula Saman Kumara claimed that private companies already have fuel stocks stored at Kolonnawa and Sapugaskanda and should release them at revised prices. He alleged that when private companies reduce sales, the CPC has to spend additional foreign exchange to cover the supply gap.
