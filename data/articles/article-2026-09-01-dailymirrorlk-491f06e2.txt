Construction activity gathers pace in July despite material shortages

Sri Lanka’s construction sector expanded at a faster pace in July, supported by a steady flow of projects, although the shortages of key inputs and elevated material prices continued to constrain the industry.

The Purchasing Managers’ Index for Construction rose to 61.4 in July, from 60.0 in June, remaining firmly above the 50-point threshold that separates expansion from contraction, the Central Bank said yesterday.

The surveyed companies reported a sustained flow of construction work during the month, despite the input constraints, particularly a shortage of bitumen.

New orders increased at a faster rate, with the sub-index rising to 57.1, from 54.3 in June, reflecting a steady inflow of projects.

Employment also strengthened marginally, with the index increasing to 61.8, from 61.4. However, the construction firms continued to report shortages of skilled labour, which could limit the sector’s ability to meet the rising demand.

The quantity of purchases recorded the strongest improvement among the surveyed indicators, climbing to 64.7 in July, from 60.0 a month earlier, as the companies increased material procurement in response to the higher construction activity.

However, the survey respondents continued to flag the shortages of construction materials and elevated input prices. Supplier delivery times remained lengthy, although the relevant index eased to 60.0, from 62.9 in June.

The construction companies remained optimistic about activity over the next three months, supported by the expectations of new project awards. Nevertheless, concerns persisted over securing a steady supply of construction inputs.

The Central Bank’s survey covers about 35 of the highest-graded local construction companies registered with the Construction Industry Development Authority.
