Oil shock weighs on CSE as ASPI falls for third straight session

The Colombo Stock Exchange closed lower for a third consecutive session on 10 September 2026, as escalating tensions in the Middle East and the sharp rise in global oil prices weighed on investor sentiment. Brent crude moved above USD 100 per barrel amid renewed U.S.-Iran hostilities and heightened concerns over disruptions to regional energy supplies, adding to risk aversion across global markets.

The ASPI fell 108.26 points, or 0.50%, to 21,357.74, while the S&P SL20 declined 29.94 points, or 0.50%, to 6,003.92. The ASPI slipped below 21,400 and briefly approached 21,350 before a modest late-session recovery.

The deterioration in global risk sentiment appeared to spill over into the CSE, with higher oil prices particularly negative for Sri Lanka given the potential impact on import costs, inflation expectations and the external position. Global equities also faced pressure as the oil surge revived inflation concerns.

Market turnover stood at LKR 1.45 billion, with 35.08 million shares traded. The Banks sector led activity with LKR 600.63 million in turnover, largely driven by Sampath Bank, which recorded LKR 456.65 million.

Crossings accounted for around 38% of total turnover, led by a LKR 427.50 million block in Sampath Bank involving 3.00 million shares at LKR 142.50. While these large transactions supported headline turnover, underlying market sentiment outside the major crossings remained firmly negative.

Market breadth deteriorated further, with only 57 gainers against 151 decliners, resulting in an advance-decline ratio of 0.38. Foreign investors provided limited support by turning marginal net buyers with an inflow of LKR 16.13 million.

Melstacorp was the largest negative contributor to the ASPI, followed by Lion Brewery, Dialog Axiata, Carson Cumberbatch and Cargills. Aitken Spence provided the strongest positive contribution.

The combination of escalating geopolitical risk, rapidly rising oil prices and heavily negative market breadth placed the CSE under clear pressure during the session. With turnover partly supported by large crossings, the broader market remained weak, suggesting sentiment could stay cautious as investors monitor developments in the Middle East and the direction of global energy prices.
