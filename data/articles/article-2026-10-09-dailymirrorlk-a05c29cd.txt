‘Emission Eye’ unit to crack down on smoke-emitting vehicles

Colombo, Oct. 9 (Daily Mirror) - The Vehicle Emission Testing Programme has established a special unit, named the ‘Emission Eye Unit’, to identify vehicles emitting excessive smoke on public roads and take legal action against their owners.

Operating under the Department of Motor Traffic (DMT), the unit monitors vehicles on the move and photographs those emitting excessive visible smoke to initiate enforcement action.

The initiative aims to strengthen roadside enforcement, following the integration of the Vehicle Emission Testing Trust Fund into the DMT.

Officers deployed at strategic locations in major cities will use photographs as evidence to issue maintenance orders to vehicle owners on the same day.

Owners who receive such notices must repair their vehicles and present them to the Vehicle Emission Testing Centre in Werahera by the specified deadline.

Vehicles whose owners fail to comply will be blacklisted in the DMT database, potentially creating difficulties when obtaining revenue licences or transferring ownership.

The initiative comes amid concerns over the health impact of air pollution. Data presented by the Ministry of Health around two months ago indicated that approximately 7,000 people in Sri Lanka die prematurely each year due to air pollution, with vehicle emissions identified as a major source.

The Vehicle Emission Testing Programme is implementing several measures to control vehicular emissions and improve air quality.
