Court grants interim order to prevent removal of two Lincoln Navigator vehicles

Colombo, Aug. 10 (Daily Mirror) - The Court of Appeal has issued an interim order preventing a Sri Lankan company operating under a licence issued by the Board of Investment (BOI) from removing, exporting, shipping, transporting, selling, transferring, alienating, encumbering or otherwise parting with possession, custody or control of two 2024 Lincoln Navigator L Black Label vehicles.

Accordingly, the Court of Appeal has permitted an application to be heard on an urgent basis seeking constitutional injunctive relief in exceptional circumstances in a dispute between an overseas company and a Sri Lankan company.

A two-member bench comprising Justice R. Gurusinghe and Justice A. Premashankar, on Friday (7), issued an interim order against the BOI-licensed company, which is engaged in converting left-hand-drive high-end vehicles into right-hand-drive vehicles.

The application was filed by Atlantic Auto Performance Ltd., a Canadian-based company engaged in exporting American and other high-end vehicles to markets worldwide.

According to the petition, Atlantic Auto had regularly shipped vehicles to Sri Lanka to be converted by the Sri Lankan BOI company before being exported to countries where right-hand-drive vehicles are used.

The dispute before the Court relates to two 2024 Lincoln Navigator L Black Label vehicles with an approximate combined value of USD 260,000.

Atlantic Auto claimed that the vehicles had been shipped to Sri Lanka after the respondent company had paid an advance, with the balance due to be paid before the vehicles were released from the Colombo Port.

However, the petitioner alleged that the two vehicles had been cleared from the port and converted without its consent and without payment of the outstanding balance, which it characterised as fraudulent conduct.

The petitioner told the Court that it was compelled to invoke the jurisdiction of the Court of Appeal because the vehicles were in the possession of a company operating under a BOI licence.

It was further submitted that, under the BOI legal framework, courts of first instance are restricted from granting injunctive relief against a BOI company without first hearing the other party. Atlantic Auto argued that any delay could result in the vehicles being shipped out of Sri Lanka to another country.

The petitioner further informed Court that its Chief Executive Officer and a director had travelled to Sri Lanka in connection with the matter and that there was an imminent threat that the vehicles could be removed from the country.

Having considered the petition, supporting evidence and submissions made by counsel, the Court of Appeal issued notice on the respondents and granted an interim order effective until August 19, 2026.

Counsel Nishan Sydney Premathiratne with Sasheen Aresecularatne, Shenali Dias and Sidath Gajanayaka on the instructions of Chanuka Ekanayake appeared for the Petitioner.
