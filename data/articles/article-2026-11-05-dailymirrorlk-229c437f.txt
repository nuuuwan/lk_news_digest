How did Govt pay Rs.101 m to private channel while suing Basil for similar offence ? MP

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The payment of Rs. 101 million by the government to a private TV channel to fund the Asian Broadcasting Union (ABU) conference in Colombo came under scrutiny in Parliament yesterday, as an Opposition MP questioned how it happened when former Minister Basil Rajapaksa was sued for a similar offence.

SJB MP Mujibur Rahman yesterday raised concerns that the government had released Rs. 101 million to a private TV channel to fund the Asian Broadcasting Union (ABU) conference in Colombo questioning how the Treasury had released funds for the purpose.

“We have information that the government has released Rs 101 million to spend for the ABU conference which was held in Colombo. The Minister in charge of Mass Media said state funds have not been used for the conference. If so, why did President Anura Kumara Dissanayake seek cabinet approval to release funds? One wonders as to why the Minister has lied at the cabinet briefing,” the MP said.

The MP said in a similar incident, Former Minister Basil Rajapaksa had allegedly misused Rs. 7.8 million funds of the Tourism Promotion Bureau in 2014 to print T-shirts in the guise of tourism promotion and he had been sued over the incident.

"if everyone is equal before the law, the theory applies to all," Rahman said.

Chief Government Whip Nalinda Jayatissa, responding to the allegation, said the former minister had not used the funds for tourism promotion but had instead allocated them to print T-shirts for an election campaign.

He said the Rs. 101 million referred to by the MP was being spent on tourism promotion by the Tourism Promotion Bureau.

“Tourism Promotion Bureau has the right to spend funds for the promotion of tourism. It has spent funds for a kite festival, Colombo Marathon and a Rugby tournament to promote tourism through sports. But, Basil Rajapaksa had used funds for an election campaign,” he said.

MP Rahman insisted that Minister Jayatissa had misled the public through his remarks at the Cabinet briefing.
