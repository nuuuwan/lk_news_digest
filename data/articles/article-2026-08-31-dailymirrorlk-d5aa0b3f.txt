Illicit drug shortage sparks surge in rehabilitation demand

Colombo, Aug. 31 (Daily Mirror) - A shortage in the availability of illicit drugs has raised concerns among authorities as more drug users seek treatment and rehabilitation, while experts warn that substance abuse continues to contribute to a significant number of deaths in Sri Lanka each day.

National Dangerous Drugs Control Board (NDDCB) Director of Treatment and Rehabilitation U.G. Shantha Gamage said authorities were observing signs of a shortage of illicit drugs in society, with the reduced availability accompanied by an increase in the number of users seeking rehabilitation.

He made the remarks at a media briefing on the progress of the Government’s “Ratama Ekata” (The Country United) national drug eradication programme.

Gamage said the growing demand for treatment had placed pressure on existing rehabilitation facilities, which were already insufficient to accommodate the increasing number of people seeking voluntary treatment.

In April, the Cabinet approved the establishment of six short-term treatment and rehabilitation centres after authorities identified a shortage of residential facilities to meet the demand.

The Government has also continued efforts to expand rehabilitation services as part of its national anti-drug programme.

Meanwhile, Dr. Manoj Fernando, Dean of the Faculty of Applied Sciences at Rajarata University, warned that drug and alcohol use was contributing to the deaths of between 80 and 100 people every day in Sri Lanka.
