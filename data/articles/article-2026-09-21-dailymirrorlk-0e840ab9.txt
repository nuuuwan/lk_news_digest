Police to introduce on-site drug testing for drivers within three months - IGP

Colombo, Sep. 21 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Police is to introduce specialised on-site drug testing kits within the next two to three months to crack down on the growing trend of motorists driving under the influence of narcotics, Inspector General of Police (IGP) Priyantha Weerasuriya said.

He said Police have observed changes in the factors contributing to fatal road accidents, with driving under the influence of drugs becoming an increasing concern alongside drunk driving.

"While drunk driving was previously the primary focus of law enforcement, driving under the influence of narcotics is now the most frequently observed cause of severe accidents For example, regarding the road accident that occurred last week in Mattakkuliya, we conducted that test, and upon checking, it was found that the individual had used narcotics," he said.

A recent pilot operation targeting buses in and around Colombo found that more than 5 percent bus drivers had been operating vehicles under the influence of narcotics, the IGP said.

IGP Weerasuriya said that unlike alcohol use, which can often be detected by police officers at the scene, suspected narcotics use requires medical testing. Police have therefore made drug testing mandatory for drivers involved in serious accidents.

"While a police officer can immediately detect alcohol consumption on the spot, to confirm driving under the influence of narcotics, we must present the individual to a medical doctor and conduct a test. For that, as an alternative for the police in the future, we are taking steps to purchase the necessary testing kits. Regarding that, I believe we will adopt that methodology in another two to three months," the IGP said.

He added that driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs, reckless driving, speeding and violations of traffic laws remain among the major factors contributing to fatal road accidents in Sri Lanka.

IGP Weerasuriya said that the daily death toll from traffic accidents now surpasses the average number of daily fatalities recorded during the height of the civil war. "During the war, even at its most severe stages, an average of around four people died per day from both sides combined. However, today, between seven and ten people are dying every single day due to road accidents," he said.

The IGP said that Sri Lanka continues to record more than 2,500 fatal road accidents annually, with 2,549 fatal accidents reported in 2025.

According to the IGP, 2025 also saw 7,830 accidents involving serious injuries, more than 11,151 causing minor injuries and nearly 5,900 accidents resulting in property damage.

He said 1,849 fatal accidents have been recorded so far in 2026. Compared with the same period last year, the number of fatal accidents has increased by eight, while the number of deaths has risen by 16.

Pedestrians remain the most vulnerable group, with many being killed while standing by the roadside or using roads for various purposes, the IGP said.

Motorcyclists are the second most vulnerable group. He said that an increasing number of motorcyclists are dying after crashing into roadside obstacles rather than in collisions with other vehicles.

Pillion riders and cyclists have also recorded a significant number of deaths, with 199 and 195 fatalities respectively during the period under review.

The IGP said three-wheelers are also frequently involved in accidents, with 3,515 accidents involving three-wheelers recorded. He said that motorcycles and three-wheelers account for a large proportion of vehicles on Sri Lankan roads.

Meanwhile, authorities have identified 712 accident-prone locations across the country following a nationwide survey conducted in response to the rising number of serious road accidents.
