Sri Lanka tops global youth optimism survey

Colombo, Nov. 19 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lankan students have emerged as the most optimistic young people in the world, according to a new global survey that assessed Gen Z's confidence in their personal futures, the economy, the environment, politics and society.

Sri Lanka ranked first among 41 countries included in the study, scoring 4.32 out of five for overall optimism, despite the economic turbulence experienced in recent years.

The survey, conducted by Oxford Scholastica Academy, a summer school provider, gathered responses from 1,433 students across 100 countries to examine how young people view the future and the challenges facing their generation.

Sri Lankan students recorded the highest environmental optimism in the study, scoring 4.62, while personal optimism stood at 4.75. They also expressed strong confidence in the future of society, scoring 4.25.

Kenya ranked second with an overall optimism score of 4.31, with students expressing particularly strong confidence in the future of society, scoring 4.43, tied for the highest in the study alongside Cyprus. Kenya also recorded the highest political optimism, at 3.96.

Nepal ranked third, with an overall score of 4.30, and was the only country to record a perfect score of 5.00 for personal optimism. It also recorded the highest economic optimism, at 4.80, indicating strong confidence among students in their individual prospects despite the country's lower-income status.

Kazakhstan ranked fourth, scoring highly for economic optimism at 4.56 and recording the third-highest political optimism in the study, at 3.76.

Nigeria and Indonesia also featured among the most optimistic countries, scoring 4.08 and 4.07, respectively. Nigerian students recorded environmental optimism of 3.92, while Indonesian students expressed economic confidence, scoring 4.41.

Among Western countries, Canada ranked highest at sixth with an overall score of 4.08, ahead of the United States, which scored 3.99. Germany recorded 3.73, the UK 3.61 and France 3.22.

Oxford Scholastica Academy Co-Director Lavinia Abell said young people faced numerous reasons for uncertainty and pessimism, including the rise of artificial intelligence, wars and the increasing cost of living, particularly as they prepare to enter the workforce.

She said the study highlighted how young people in different countries viewed these uncertainties, pointing to differences in attitudes towards the future across nationalities.

Abell also noted that Italy's political optimism was particularly low compared with other European countries, while France and Poland, which also ranked near the bottom, showed slightly different patterns.

The findings suggest that despite economic and global challenges, young people in Sri Lanka remain particularly hopeful about their personal prospects and the future of the country.
