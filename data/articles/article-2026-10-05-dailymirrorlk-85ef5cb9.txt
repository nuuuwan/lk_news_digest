Finance companies’ borrowings double past Rs.1trn to fund lending spree

Borrowings up 124% to Rs.1,057.3bn, now fund a third of sector assets

Sri Lanka’s licensed finance companies more than doubled their borrowings to over Rs.1 trillion in the 12 months to end-June 2026, turning to wholesale funding as deposit mobilisation failed to keep pace with an almost 48 percent expansion in a loan book dominated by vehicle financing and gold-backed lending, the latest Central Bank data showed.

The sector’s borrowings climbed 124 percent to Rs.1,057.3 billion from Rs.472.1 billion a year earlier, an increase of Rs.585.2 billion that was more than twice the Rs.267.2 billion added in deposits, which grew 22.9 percent to Rs.1,433.1 billion.

Reflecting this shift in the funding mix, borrowings now finance 32.9 percent of the sector’s assets, when compared to 20.7 percent in the same period last year, while the share of deposits slipped to 44.6 percent from 51.1 percent and the credit-to-deposit ratio rose to 185.8 percent from 157.6 percent.

Term loans and securitisations, which more than doubled to Rs.853.4 billion from Rs.365 billion, accounted for the bulk of the increase, with dues to financial institutions rising 134 percent to Rs.960.7 billion, equivalent to nearly 91 percent of total borrowings.

Foreign currency borrowings expanded over three and a half times to Rs.89.8 billion from Rs.25.2 billion.

The funds were channelled almost entirely into credit, as net loans and advances grew 47.8 percent, or by Rs.833.6 billion, to Rs.2,578.7 billion, about twice the pace of the 23.6 percent credit growth recorded by the banking sector, lifting the sector’s asset base by 41 percent to Rs.3,214.5 billion.

Vehicle loans, finance leasing and hire purchase made up 63.8 percent of the loan book, while gold loans and pawning accounted for a further 21.4 percent, leaving 85.2 percent of the portfolio concentrated in two collateral-backed products and exposed to movements in vehicle and gold prices.

The credit expansion gathered pace after the removal of restrictions on vehicle imports last year, with rising gold prices and lower market interest rates lending further support.

The Central Bank capped the loan-to-value ratio on gold-backed facilities at 70 percent and tightened the limits on vehicle financing by 10 percentage points with effect from May 25, and raised the Overnight Policy Rate by 100 basis points to 8.75 percent in the same week.

Lending momentum eased in the June quarter, with net loans growing by Rs.130 billion, down from Rs.220.8 billion in the March quarter.

However, asset quality indicators point to emerging pressure, as stage 2 loans, where credit risk has increased significantly, nearly doubled to Rs.409.7 billion from Rs.213.3 billion a year ago and rose by Rs.92.5 billion in the June quarter alone, taking their share of gross loans to 15.4 percent from 11.6 percent.

Stage 3 loans, although 10.5 percent lower than a year ago at Rs.135.8 billion, increased by Rs.23.6 billion from March, pushing the gross stage 3 ratio up to 5.1 percent from 4.4 percent in the previous quarter. Stage 2 and stage 3 loans together accounted for about 85 percent of the Rs.136 billion increase in gross loans during the quarter.

The provision cover on stage 3 loans slipped to 41 percent from 46.1 percent a year earlier, well below the 61.3 percent maintained by the banking sector.

Meanwhile, the heavier reliance on borrowings has pushed up the cost of funds, with interest expenses rising 62.3 percent to Rs.61.7 billion in the June quarter, outpacing the 41.5 percent growth in interest income and narrowing the net interest margin to 10.4 percent from 11.5 percent.

With the quarterly impairment charge rising to Rs.4.96 billion from Rs.1.3 billion, profit after tax grew 16.4 percent to Rs.21 billion, a marked slowdown from the 59.3 percent growth recorded in the corresponding period last year.

The sector’s gearing has also risen, as equity grew only 13.4 percent to Rs.578.2 billion, lifting the borrowings-to-equity ratio to 1.83 times from 0.93 times. The total capital adequacy ratio fell to 19.3 percent from 22 percent on the back of a 37.6 percent increase in risk weighted assets.

Although liquidity remained above the minimum regulatory requirement, the surplus over the required amount narrowed to Rs.100.5 billion from Rs.114.6 billion, with liquid assets declining to 8.5 percent of total assets from 10.8 percent.
