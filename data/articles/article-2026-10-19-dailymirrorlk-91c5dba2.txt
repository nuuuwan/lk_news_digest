Sri Lanka faces jobs test as educated youth bulge enters workforce ahead of shrinking generations

A better-educated bulge of 15 to 19-year-olds will enter Sri Lanka’s labour force over the next three to four years, even as births have slumped by a third in the wake of repeated financial crises, according to JB Securities CEO Murtaza Jafferjee.

That leaves Sri Lanka needing to create jobs for the incoming cohort, while preparing for a workforce that will thin out as far smaller generations follow. The UN Population Fund (UNFPA) projects the working-age population will begin shrinking from 2030.

Delivering the economic keynote at THINC Sri Lanka & Maldives 2026 in Colombo, Jafferjee traced nearly two decades of falling births.

“In 2008, we had 370,000 births in the country. By 2019 that has dropped to 319,000. Last year it was 240,000. So one third less children,” he noted.

Official data paint an even starker picture, with live births falling to 214,570 in 2025 from 319,010 in 2019. That is a 33 percent drop in six years, leaving last year’s tally 42 percent below his 2008 figure. “Repeated financial crises have created a huge demographic issue,” Jafferjee observed. The population aged four and below stands at only 1.2 million, he pointed out, against a “huge bulge” among those aged 15 to 19.

He framed that bulge as an opportunity, given gains in educational attainment, digital literacy and examination pass rates.

“This population is fairly better educated. So there is a huge amount of human capital that is going to be available,” he told the gathering.

However, absorbing that intake will test a labour market already straining at its youngest end. Unemployment among those aged 15 to 24 stood at 17.8 percent in the second quarter of 2026, more than four times the national rate of 4 percent, as per official data. Joblessness among those aged 25 to 29 climbed to 12.1 percent from 9.3 percent a year earlier, a 30 percent increase.

Unemployment among those educated to GCE Advanced Level and above also rose, to 6.7 percent from 5.9 percent. An IMF analysis put unemployment among Sri Lankans aged 15 to 24 with post-secondary education at about 43 percent in 2025, the highest among South Asian peers, though the Labour Ministry has questioned the methodology.

Artificial intelligence is also beginning to erode the entry-level roles that absorb young workers. At the same conference, Sterling Holiday Resorts Managing Director and CEO Vikram Lalvani revealed that night and income audits across the company’s 85 hotels are now run on AI. Any exceptions are cleared by a shared-service team of just two people.

Meanwhile, the generations behind the bulge are shrinking fast. The 2024 Census showed the share of under-15s falling to 20.7 percent from 25.2 percent in 2012. Over the same period, the share aged 65 and above rose to 12.6 percent from 7.9 percent, and it is projected to reach 22.1 percent by 2037.

Having engineered a recovery few expected, Sri Lanka must now tackle “the harder things”, Jafferjee argued, pointing to structural reforms that open up trade, drive costs down and lift productivity.
