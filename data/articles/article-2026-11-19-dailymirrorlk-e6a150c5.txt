Who is not afraid of the US?

The NPP finds itself unable to follow, as a government, the policies it promoted as a political party, especially regarding IMF, PTA, Palestine, imperialism and socialism

The government has decided to purchase four drones from Israel at a cost of Rs. 4.2 billion through a government-to-government arrangement without calling for tenders on the global market.

Strongly condemning this decision, Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB) Member of Parliament and Leader of the Sri Lanka Muslim Congress (SLMC), Rauff Hakeem, sharply criticised the foreign policy of the National People’s Power (NPP) government last Monday.

Speaking during the budget debate, Hakeem stated that these drones are being purchased to spy on Iranian ships around Sr Lanka and questioned whether Sri Lanka is going to use its Navy and Air Force to conduct espionage on behalf of the United States.

He also raised the question, “Who knows how much commission was received from the Israeli government for this drone purchase?”

Also, he strongly criticised several decisions taken by the government in the past regarding foreign affairs. Although the Leader of the House and Minister of Transport, Bimal Rathnayake, attempted to respond, it was apparent that he struggled to justify the incidents pointed out by Hakeem regarding the government’s foreign policy.

While Hakeem’s arguments appear reasonable overall, doubts arise as to whether some of his points are valid and whether others are justified. In particular, the suspicion he raised that the government or ministers might have taken a commission for the Israeli drones is unjustified, as there is no credible evidence to support it.

So far, the government has put an end to corruption at the political level. The corruption allegations leveled against the government by opposition parties to date are in no way justifiable.

During the budget debate, Hakeem stated that these drones are being purchased to spy on Iranian ships around Sri Lanka

Opposition parties have been talking about the release of 323 containers from the Colombo Port in January last year without physical inspection. However, the Department of Customs announced that these containers were released based on a decision made in June 2024—when Ranil Wickremesinghe was President. According to that decision, containers of certain companies have been released without physical inspection on several prior occasions also, during times of severe port congestion, based on past trust, the department said.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake during his budget speech also stated that containers were released without physical inspection 13 times under the above decision, and those who kept their mouths shut during the previous 12 occasions are now talking about the 13th occasion, as if they have discovered something new. Nobody has contested this point so far.

Similarly, the import of substandard coal that took place last year was not the first time in history. Since Sri Lanka began importing coal in 2009, substandard coal has been imported on multiple occasions. An investigation regarding this was also conducted by the Auditor General’s Department in 2016. Since 2020, fines have also been imposed on the coal suppliers for sending substandard coal to Sri Lanka.

Meanwhile, Hakeem served as a minister in governments where massive corruption—which is currently being exposed—took place. How many of those corruption cases he questioned remains a big question mark. Therefore, attempting to instill suspicion in the minds of the people without evidence by claiming the government received commissions from Israel is inappropriate.

Nevertheless, his questions regarding the government’s foreign policy are important. Hakeem’s argument that the NPP makes decisions in foreign affairs out of fear of the United States, even if not always true, is largely a fact. However, the question also arises here as to whether this fear is entirely unjustified.

Hakeem stated that the government is responsible for the lives of more than a hundred naval personnel killed when an Iranian ship was attacked by the US navy in the sea near Sri Lanka. However, this is a complex issue, because if the United States had wanted to, it could have carried out that attack right inside a Sri Lankan port.

In the context of the atrocities taking place in Palestinian territories, including Gaza, if the main thrust of Hakeem’s statement regarding the Israeli drones is that Israel must be isolated globally, that argument cannot be rejected.

However, the contention that Sri Lanka is buying drones from Israel just to spy for the United States does not seem plausible. If Sri Lanka wanted to do that, it could have purchased the drones directly from the United States itself. The question then arises: if they had done so, would Hakeem have questioned it?

Hakeem also spoke about Venezuela. On January 3rd, U.S. forces carried out an attack inside Venezuelan capital, Caracas, killing more than 80 people, and abducted the country’s President, Nicolás Maduro, and his wife, Cilia Flores. This is an act that violates a nation’s sovereignty. The NPP government merely expressed its concern regarding this. However, the ruling party, the NPP, strongly condemned it. Hakeem mockingly remarked that the government acted that way out of fear of the United States.

However, not only Sri Lanka, but many powerful nations such as India, Japan, China, Malaysia and Indonesia also refrained from condemning the United States in this regard and merely expressed their concern. True, the policies of the leaders of most of those non-condemning nations and the policies of the NPP are not the same. The NPP is, more or less, the Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna (JVP) which is an anti-imperialist party.

In that sense, it is true that Sri Lanka’s actions must be watched closely. Yet, the core issue here is whether Sri Lanka is in a position to antagonise the United States—especially a President like Donald Trump, who does not respect international law and whose mind cannot be read.

The Palestine issue is another complex problem facing the government. Even though the NPP had been stating that Israel is engaging in genocide, the NPP government does not use the word “genocide.” Pointing this out, Hakeem criticised the government reminding that Minister Bimal Rathnayake is the President of the Sri Lanka Committee for Solidarity with Palestine.

Nevertheless, Sri Lanka government, in September 2011, supported the UN General Assembly vote to grant Libya’s UN seat to the National Transitional Council (NTC), a Libyan opposition coalition,  after the forcible ouster of Muammer Gaddafi. Mr. Hakeem was the justice minister of that government. These are compulsions.

In October 2024, the U.S. and Israeli intelligence agencies issued reports claiming there was a threat to the safety of Israeli tourists in Arugam Bay in the East. Several individuals were arrested in connection with that under the Prevention of Terrorism Act (PTA). From the very beginning, the JVP had rejected this law as a draconian, repressive act. Yet, the government used that very same law against those suspects.

Ironically, U.S. officials, who had been urging for the repeal of that law, also found no fault in its use at that time. Later, when a youth pasted a sticker at a hotel in Colombo condemning Israel’s atrocities in Gaza without making any threats whatsoever, he too was arrested under this same repressive law.

The NPP finds itself unable to follow, as a government, the policies it promoted as a political party, especially regarding IMF, PTA, Palestine, imperialism and socialism. Although other parties exploit the situation by portraying it as dishonesty, it, in fact is the result of a convoluted context.
