President stresses need to fast-track district development projects

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - President Anura Kumara Dissanayake has stressed the need to expedite district-level development projects while ensuring the efficient and effective utilisation of public funds.

The President instructed ministries and government institutions to prepare plans for implementing their respective development projects from the beginning of each financial year and conduct monthly reviews to assess their progress.

He made these remarks at a recent meeting of the Ministerial Consultative Committee on Finance, Planning and Economic Development, held at Parliament under his chairmanship.

The President explained that funds remaining unutilised at the end of a financial year could be carried forward to the following year only if certain conditions had been met, including the opening of Letters of Credit (LCs) for the relevant projects.

Otherwise, the allocated funds would have to be returned to the General Treasury, and fresh allocations would need to be obtained through the following year's Budget.

Against this backdrop, he stressed the importance of closely monitoring district-level project implementation based on the project report books submitted to District Development Committees.

The meeting also discussed the provision of fertiliser subsidies to farmers and the management of paddy stocks during the Maha cultivation season. Particular attention was drawn to the need to dispose of or utilise the remaining paddy stocks in storage facilities before the next Maha harvest is received.

Several options were considered for converting the existing paddy stocks into rice for distribution through welfare programmes or utilising them for other purposes, including animal feed and other products.

The President further pointed out that tax concessions alone were insufficient to attract investment, stressing that delays in allocating land to investors also needed to be addressed. He noted that the existing process of obtaining land takes approximately 18 months, posing a significant obstacle to investment.

In this regard, the Committee discussed identifying State-owned lands and bringing them under the purview of the Board of Investment or a specialised land bank. It was proposed that a mechanism be established to expedite land allocation based on the scale of investment, employment opportunities and the potential contribution to the national economy.

Other matters discussed at the meeting included the regulation of cryptocurrency, the digitalisation of the insurance and transactions sectors, and VAT-related issues faced by cooperative societies.
