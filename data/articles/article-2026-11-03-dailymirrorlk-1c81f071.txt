Digital payment histories pitched as new collateral for SMEs

Sri Lanka’s digital payment ecosystem exceeds 2.2mn transactions daily, with around Rs.82bn average value

Digital transaction-generated data called “liquid gold” that generative AI can turn into personalised, trusted services

SL targets to increase digital economy’s share of GDP from 3-4% to over 12% by 2030

The banks were urged to look beyond today’s creditworthy clientele and lend to small merchants on the strength of their digital payment records, as the policymakers addressed the CEOs Night that opened the inaugural Sri Lanka FinTech Festival 2026 in Colombo yesterday.

Organised by the FinTech Forum Sri Lanka in collaboration with the Digital Economy Ministry, the invitation-only evening at Shangri-La Colombo drew the bank and fintech chief executives, regulators and government officials, with former DBS Bank Chief Innovation Officer Neal Cross delivering the keynote address.

Digital Economy Deputy Minister Eranga Weeraratne framed the challenge around a Kurunegala shopkeeper accepting her first digital payment, arguing that the real test was whether she would offer the option to her next customer.

“We can encourage her first transaction but we have to earn the second,” he told the gathering.

Weeraratne noted that, over time, the shopkeeper’s digital payment record could unlock formal credit for her business.

“For an SME with limited physical assets to attract lending, her financial history can be the new collateral,” he remarked, while urging the banks to partner with fintech firms and help build the customers who could qualify for credit tomorrow rather than only those who qualify today.

The government, he outlined, would back the drive through promotion programmes such as the gamified Lucky Draw and possibly by subsidising the transaction costs, along with a Fund of Funds and regulatory frameworks for qualified venture capital funds and virtual assets.

Meanwhile, Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) Senior Deputy Governor Dr. Chandranath Amarasekara revealed that the country’s digital payment ecosystem now handles more than 2.2 million transactions a day with an average daily value of around Rs.82 billion, which works out to roughly Rs.37,000 per transaction.

Dr. Amarasekara pointed to LankaQR, JustPay, CEFTS and GovPay as the rails on which future innovation would travel, adding that the CBSL was using regulatory sandboxes to help promising ideas reach the market.

He also observed that high-frequency payment data was giving the central banks richer insights into spending, credit conditions and the transmission of monetary policy.

“Public trust takes years to build and moments to lose,” he cautioned.

Describing the banks and fintechs as complementary rather than competing, Dr. Amarasekara asserted, “A financial system cannot be innovative if it is unstable. Neither can it be stable if it fails to innovate.”

However, Chief Advisor to President on Digital Economy and GovTech Sri Lanka Chairman Dr. Hans Wijayasuriya argued that innovation must solve the real problems rather than merely produce clever services if the digital economy’s share of GDP is to rise from 3-4 percent to over 12 percent by 2030, which would require that share to grow three to fourfold.

He urged the industry to close gaps in inclusion, trust and language and to connect the top of the economic pyramid with the bottom, so that the largest businesses transact with the smallest.

He also described the data generated by every digital transaction as “liquid gold” that generative AI could turn into personalised and trusted services.

FinTech Forum Sri Lanka Chairman and LankaPay Chief Executive Officer Channa de Silva explained that the forum rests on three pillars, namely connecting the ecosystem, enabling responsible innovation and creating opportunities for investment and international expansion. He added that the forum wants the Sri Lankan fintech companies to export their innovations and compete confidently on the global stage.

“The future of banking and finance is not something we should wait for. It is something we must collectively create,” he stressed.
