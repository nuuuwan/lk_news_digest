Fitch upgrades six Sri Lankan insurers after sovereign rating lift

State-owned SLIC Life and SLIC General rise to AA-(lka), the highest ratings among the eight entities reviewed

Fitch says the stronger sovereign credit profile has reduced insurers’ investment and liquidity risks

Continental Insurance stays at A(lka) following a Rs. 654 million loss in 2025

Fitch Ratings raised the national financial strength ratings of six Sri Lankan insurers by one notch yesterday, saying the sovereign’s stronger credit profile had reduced investment and liquidity risks for a sector heavily exposed to government securities and domestic financial institutions.

State-owned Sri Lanka Insurance Corporation Life and Sri Lanka Insurance Corporation General were upgraded to AA-(lka) from A+(lka). HNB Life, HNB General Insurance and People’s Insurance moved to A+(lka) from A(lka), while Co-operative Insurance rose to BB+(lka) from BB(lka). Fitch affirmed Continental Insurance Lanka at A(lka) and the Construction Guarantee Fund at BB(lka). All eight outlooks are stable.

The rating actions followed Fitch’s October recalibration of its Sri Lankan national rating scale after it raised the sovereign’s long-term local-currency rating to ‘B-’ from ‘CCC+’ on September 22. The national ratings rank financial strength relative to other issuers in Sri Lanka.

“We believe the sovereign’s credit profile improvement has reduced insurers’ investment and liquidity risks, which are closely linked to those of the sovereign and domestic banks because insurers’ investment portfolios are dominated by local-currency fixed-income government securities, corporate debt and deposits with financial institutions,” Fitch said.

SLIC Life, Sri Lanka’s third-largest life insurer with a 14 percent share of gross written premiums, had a risk-based capital ratio of 478 percent at end-June. SLIC General, the country’s largest non-life insurer with a 20 percent market share in 2025, had a ratio of 265 percent. Fitch expects SLIC General’s underwriting performance to improve gradually through tighter claims management and pricing discipline after Cyclone Ditwah-related claims weighed on its 2025 results.

Fitch cited strong group profitability in upgrading HNB Life and its wholly owned general insurance subsidiary. HNB Life’s gross written premiums grew by an average of 30 percent over 2023–2025, ahead of the industry’s 19 percent. HNB General’s risk-based capital ratio fell to 198 percent at end-June from 219 percent in 2024. Fitch expects HNB Life to provide capital support if needed.

People’s Insurance’s annualised return on equity rose to 12 percent in the first half of 2026 from 6 percent in 2025, helped by tighter underwriting and better investment returns. Co-operative Insurance’s annualised return on equity reached 6 percent in the first half, against a three-year average of 0.3 percent. Fitch also improved its assessment of Co-operative’s corporate governance following measures to address earlier weaknesses.

Continental swung to a Rs. 654 million net loss in 2025 from a Rs. 436 million profit a year earlier after higher medical claims pushed its combined ratio to 119 percent from 100 percent. The ratio eased to 113 percent in the first half of 2026, and Fitch said continued losses could weaken its capital position. Janashakthi PLC’s proposed initial acquisition of an 81 percent stake from Melstacorp PLC remains subject to final regulatory approval.

The state-owned Construction Guarantee Fund recorded a recovery in core guarantee income in the first half as foreign-funded infrastructure projects lifted demand for bonds and guarantees. Fitch said investment income remained the main contributor to profitability and earnings were sensitive to the pace of project implementation.
