Oil prices rise as Middle East, Ukraine-Russia conflict fuel supply worries

October 31- Oil prices rose with the market on edge about possible military escalation in the Middle East after the U.S. midterm elections, while an attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, Houthi claims of more attacks on Saudi refineries and a Ukrainian strike on a Russian refinery fed supply concerns.

According to foreign media, December Brent crude went off the board at $117.64 a barrel, up 2.4%, for a monthly gain of 14%. The most-active January Brent contract rose 2.4%, to $113.43 a barrel. December West Texas Intermediate crude rose 2.1%, to $104.12 a barrel, for a 15% monthly gain.

As tensions continue to mount, global oil supply security is increasingly threatened, and risk appetite is further expanded in keeping the energy complex up-trending ahead of next week's mid-term elections," trading advisory firm Ritterbusch & Associates said in a note.

Oil prices have been supported by Middle East supply disruptions and tight refined-product markets, according to Saxo Bank's Ole Hansen. Energy has been among the strongest-performing commodity sectors in October, with crude and diesel gains contrasting with declines in precious metals and grains.

Prices have risen despite a recovery in Persian Gulf oil exports, which have at times returned to or exceeded prewar levels. Exports averaged 15.7 million barrels a day in the week through Oct. 26, but shipping remains more dangerous and expensive, with higher insurance and freight costs adding to the cost of moving crude, The Wall Street Journal reported.

A tanker transiting the Strait of Hormuz was attacked by a small boat with two explosive devices, the U.K. Maritime Trade Operations said Friday.

The incident followed claims made Thursday by Yemen's Houthi militants that they had attacked several sites in Saudi Arabia, including the Ras Tanura and Rabigh refineries and a Saudi Aramco production facility in Abqaiq. Saudi Arabia didn't respond a request for comment.
