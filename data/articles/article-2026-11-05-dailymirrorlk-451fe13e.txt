Food earnings lift Harischandra Mills’ September-quarter profit

Harischandra Mills PLC reported a 23 percent rise in group net profit for the September quarter, supported by higher food earnings and a return to profit in fuel and lubricants, although the rising costs squeezed the gross margins and the losses widened at its soap business.

Profit after tax increased to Rs.59.8 million in the three months ended September 30, 2026, from Rs.48.4 million a year earlier. The earnings per share rose to Rs.31.14, from Rs.25.24.

Revenue grew 25 percent to Rs.2.08 billion but the cost of sales increased faster, rising 27 percent to Rs.1.73 billion. Consequently, the gross profit rose a more modest 14 percent to Rs.342.2 million, with the gross margin narrowing to 16.5 percent, from 18.1 percent.

The food business remained the main earnings contributor. The quarterly revenue increased 14 percent to Rs.1.39 billion, while the segment operating profit rose 31 percent to Rs.66.5 million, from Rs.50.9 million.

The fuel and lubricant revenue more than doubled to Rs.500.2 million, from Rs.247.7 million, accounting for about 62 percent of the increase in the group quarterly revenue. The segment recorded an operating profit of Rs.2.1 million, reversing a loss of Rs.95,000 a year earlier.

However, the soap business continued to weigh on the earnings. Revenue fell 8 percent to Rs.188.4 million, while its operating loss widened to Rs.9.7 million, from Rs.4.1 million.

At group level, the selling and distribution expenses increased 28 percent to Rs.144.3 million, outpacing the revenue growth. The administrative expenses declined 2 percent to Rs.139 million, providing some relief.

The operating profit, including other income, increased 21 percent to Rs.86.6 million. Other income contributed Rs.27.7 million, compared with Rs.25 million a year earlier.

The improvement was partly offset by the weaker finance income, which fell 40 percent to Rs.7.2 million. Although the finance expenses dropped 80 percent to Rs.516,000, the net finance income declined 30 percent to Rs.6.7 million.

Profit before tax rose 15 percent to Rs.93.4 million. A smaller 2 percent increase in the tax charge to Rs.33.6 million helped the net profit grow faster than the pre-tax earnings.

For the six months ended September 30, the group net profit increased 77 percent to Rs.151.6 million, while revenue rose 28 percent to Rs.4 billion. The earnings per share advanced to Rs.78.99, from Rs.44.65.

The half-year food segment operating profit rose to Rs.149.8 million, from Rs.86.8 million. Fuel and lubricants contributed Rs.4.3 million, against a loss of Rs.380,000, while the soap segment’s loss widened to Rs.12.4 million, from Rs.3.7 million.

The net cash generated from the operating activities increased to Rs.206.9 million, from Rs.49 million. The spending on the property, plant and equipment totalled Rs.222.6 million, up from Rs.160.3 million.

The bank overdrafts declined to Rs.100.7 million at end-September, from Rs.262.9 million at end-March. The fixed deposits also fell to Rs.315.7 million, from Rs.634 million.
