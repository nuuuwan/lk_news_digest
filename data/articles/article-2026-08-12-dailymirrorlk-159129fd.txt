Dairy sector pivots to tech and climate -smart farming following cyclone Ditwah

In the wake of the devastating Cyclone Ditwah, Sri Lanka’s dairy industry is undergoing a structural transformation, accelerating its adoption of climate-smart agriculture and advanced technology to ensure future operational resilience.

The natural disaster served as a stark catalyst for domestic dairy producers to rethink their operational models, exposing severe vulnerabilities within the country’s agricultural supply chain.

According to the Lanka Milk Foods (CWE) PLC 2025/26 Annual Report, the catastrophic flooding inflicted over US$ 3.4 billion in damages and economic losses across the island.

Reflecting on the challenging operating environment, Group Chief Executive Officer Sasanka Perera noted, “Cyclone Ditwah disrupted agriculture and infrastructure, while the depreciation of the Sri Lankan Rupee increased the cost of imported packaging materials, fuel and utilities, contributing to inflationary pressures”.

For Lanka Milk Foods, the corporate entity behind prominent household brands such as Lakspray, Ambewela, Daily, MyJuicee, and the newly launched Suddhi fresh milk, the shift toward modernisation is about securing the long-term viability of the national milk supply.

Recognizing the increasing frequency of extreme weather events, the group is actively prioritising investment in medium-scale production units to modernise infrastructure and improve yield efficiency. This includes the rollout of precision dairy management technologies. Computerized herd-management systems are now utilized to monitor individual animal activity, milk yield, body condition, mastitis indicators, and heat cycles. The report emphasizes that “automation enables greater precision in the management of individual animals while reducing the need for intrusive manual intervention,” allowing for timely farm-management decisions.

While the group’s overall profit surged to Rs. 2,621 million, the economic and human fallout from the disaster highlighted the urgent need to insulate operations from climate shocks and supply chain bottlenecks. The company did not record direct significant physical asset write-offs, but the localized impact was undeniable. Emergency assistance structures were deployed to address the crisis, including targeted financial assistance for employees affected by the Ditwah weather disturbances and an expansion of distress loan facilities for eligible Ambewela Products (Pvt) Limited staff.

In addition to weather-related operational strains, the group faced separate subsidiary-level financial pressures. Ambewela Livestock Company Limited and Pattipola Livestock Company Limited posted net losses of Rs. 204 million and Rs. 67 million respectively, driven heavily by GDP deflator-linked lease escalations inflating non-operating costs.

To combat these intersecting climatic and financial vulnerabilities, Lanka Milk Foods is implementing heat-tolerant herd management and water-efficient infrastructure to mitigate the impacts of increasingly unpredictable weather patterns on milk yields.

Furthermore, there is a coordinated push toward promoting localised fodder cultivation and advanced silage production to decrease dependency on volatile, high-cost imported animal nutrition. By cultivating 20 to 22 percent of the herd’s feed requirement within the farms themselves, the company is establishing a crucial buffer against external supply disruptions while supporting greater feed efficiency and lowering its environmental impact.

“Our continued investment in domestic dairy production contributes directly to Sri Lanka’s economic resilience by strengthening local milk collection and processing capacity, reducing reliance on dairy imports,” Perera explained.

Lanka Milk Foods backed this strategic pivot with substantial capital allocation, deploying Rs. 1,194 million in capital expenditure during the financial year to enhance its physical and technological capabilities. This infrastructure development is deliberately designed to support safe and efficient interaction between animals, employees, and operating systems. Advanced chilling and storage units have also been comprehensively upgraded to protect the cold chain, resulting in the successful elimination of spoilage at the upgraded units. As the sector navigates the post-cyclone recovery phase, the integration of technology and climate-smart farming practices stands as a critical defense mechanism. Turning operational data into actionable insights and prioritizing resource efficiency ensures the industry is taking decisive steps to protect its agricultural assets. As Chairman C.R. Jansz stated, “By pushing boundaries and setting higher benchmarks, our horizons remain infinite as we shape a resilient future for Sri Lanka’s dairy sector”. (NF)
