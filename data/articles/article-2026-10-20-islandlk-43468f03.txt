External stock market volatility drags bourse into drabness

The ÇSE continued yesterday on its dull, volatile track due to external market uncertainty along with crude oil price increases compounded by internal negative factors, market analysts said.

The All Share Price Index went down by 71.67 points, while the S and P SL20 declined by 13.98 points.

Turnover stood at Rs 795 million with four crossings. Those crossings were; Sampath Bank 1 million shares crossed to the tune of Rs 138 million; its shares traded at Rs 138, Melstacorp 323,000 shares crossed to the tune of Rs 58 million; its shares sold at Rs 178, CAL3 Fund 500 million shares crossed for Rs 54 million; its shares traded at Rs 10.70 and Access Engineering 500,000 shares crossed for Rs 45 million; its shares sold at Rs 75.

In the retail market, companies that mainly contributed to the turnover were; Dialog Axiata Rs 37 million (868,,000 shares traded), HNB (Non-Voting) Ra 32 million (102000 shares traded) LOLC Rs 24 million (51000 shares traded), NTB Rs 24 million (82000 shares traded), Prime Lands Residencies Rs 19 million (562,000 shares traded), Sampath Bank Rs17 million and Cargills Rs 16 million (23000 shares traded). During the day 26 million shares volumes changed hands in 10954 transactions.

It is said said that banking sector counters, especially Sampath Bank and HNB, performed well. Further telecom sector counters, especially Dialog, also performed well. The Real estate sector also performed well, especially Prime Lands.

Meanwhile, Access Engineering said shareholders have agreed not to proceed with the South Asia Commercial and Logistics Hub project and will enter into a settlement agreement for its orderly termination. Under the planned settlement arrangements, the company anticipates recovering approximately Rs 1.453 billion from its total investment of roughly Rs 1.762 billion.

Yesterday the rupee was quoted at Rs 331.75/90 to the US dollar in the spot market, weaker from Rs 331.60/70 the previous day, while bond yields were quoted higher, dealers said.
