50 deep-sea vessels planned to supply local canned fish industry

Colombo, Nov. 17 (Daily Mirror) - The Sri Lanka Consumer Cooperative Societies Federation Limited (COOPFED) and the Richwin Group have jointly launched 28 new “CoopFish” canned fish products under a public-private partnership aimed at strengthening the domestic fisheries industry and expanding the cooperative sector’s role in the market.

The products were officially launched on November 15, at a ceremony held at Hotel Royal Ramesses in Seeduwa, under the patronage of Minister of Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Cooperative Development Wasantha Samarasinghe and Deputy Minister Upali Samarasinghe.

Addressing the event, Minister Wasantha Samarasinghe said the Government was rebuilding the cooperative movement as a key pillar of the national economy. Plans were underway to establish 10,000 cooperative retail outlets and 25 modern “Mega Coop City” complexes across the country by 2029, enabling consumers to purchase quality goods at competitive prices, he said.

He also said import restrictions had been imposed to protect the local canned fish industry, while the Government aimed to save approximately US$20 million in foreign exchange spent annually on importing raw materials for canned fish production.

As part of efforts to develop the sector, concessionary loans of up to Rs. 500 million per vessel at an interest rate of 5% had been introduced to facilitate the purchase of deep-sea fishing vessels. The Government had also allocated Rs. 15 billion to develop the fisheries industry into an export-oriented sector, he said.

COOPFED Chairman Dr. Shantha Jayarathne said the CoopFish products would be distributed at fair prices across the country through a network of nearly 4,000 outlets operated by 305 cooperative societies, serving a membership base of around 8.2 million.

He said the federation also intended to expand the products into international markets. During the ceremony, COOPFED employees presented Dr. Jayarathne with a token of appreciation, crediting him with turning the federation into a profitable institution within four months after it had faced the prospect of liquidation following 15 consecutive years of losses.

Richwin Group Director Vikum Fernando said the company's factories in Madurankuliya and Galle had a combined production capacity of 350,000 cans of fish per day and operated in compliance with international standards, including FSSC and ISO.

The joint venture was expected to achieve annual sales of Rs. 25 billion within the next two years, he said.

He added that a project involving 50 deep-sea fish collection vessels was planned to enable the canned fish industry to rely entirely on locally sourced fish within three years, while generating around 6,000 employment opportunities.
