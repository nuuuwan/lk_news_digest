Colombo inflation rises to 8.3%

Food inflation edges up to 8.8%; non-food inflation reaches 8.0%

Non-food items account for nearly two-thirds of annual price increase

Colombo’s annual inflation accelerated to 8.3 percent in October, from 8.0 percent in September, with higher transport, housing and food costs adding to the household expenditure, according to the Census and Statistics Department.

The increase followed two consecutive months at 8.0 percent and took inflation to its highest level so far in 2026. Annual inflation stood at 2.3 percent in January, fell to 1.6 percent in February and has risen each month since March, apart from September’s pause.

The non-food items accounted for nearly two-thirds of October’s annual price increase, underscoring the pressure from expenditure beyond the food basket. Transport alone contributed 2.56 percentage points to headline inflation, while housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels added 1.22 percentage points.

Food inflation edged up to 8.8 percent, from 8.7 percent in September, while non-food inflation increased to 8.0 percent, from 7.7 percent.

The Colombo Consumer Price Index (CCPI), compiled on a base of 2021=100, rose to 209.9 in October, from 209.1 in September. The Census and Statistics Department reported a monthly increase of 0.35 percent, equivalent to an additional Rs.666.63 in the expenditure value of the representative market basket.

The monthly increase was driven overwhelmingly by the non-food items, which added 0.33 percentage points to the overall movement. Food contributed just 0.02 percentage points, as the declines in several staples and protein sources largely offset the increases in the fresh produce.

Restaurants and hotels made the largest contribution to the monthly rise, at 0.17 percentage points, followed by transport, at 0.13 percentage points.

Within transport, the higher petrol costs contributed 0.09 percentage points, while the vehicle servicing charges added 0.03 percentage points and diesel a further 0.01 percentage points. The lower airfares partially offset those increases.

Smaller upward contributions came from health, household furnishings and maintenance, recreation and culture, miscellaneous goods and services and alcoholic beverages and tobacco.

The housing and utilities category provided some relief during the month, making a negative contribution of 0.01 percentage points, with the lower LP gas prices among the factors reducing expenditure. The education prices remained unchanged, while clothing, footwear and communication recorded slight increases.

Limes contributed 0.14 percentage points to the monthly increase in the overall index, followed by vegetables at 0.11 percentage points and fresh fruits at 0.06 percentage points. Bread, coconuts and red onions also exerted the upward pressure.

However, the falling sea fish prices reduced the index by 0.25 percentage points. The lower prices for green chillies and eggs each subtracted 0.06 percentage points, while chicken contributed a further decline of 0.03 percentage points. The prices of prawns, potatoes and coconut oil also fell.

Those offsets kept the net monthly food contribution small, even as the food prices remained substantially higher than a year earlier.

On an annual basis, food contributed 2.87 percentage points to headline inflation. Sea fish was the largest individual upward contributor within the food basket, adding 0.74 percentage points, followed by vegetables at 0.52 percentage points, fresh fruits at 0.43 percentage points and dried fish at 0.37 percentage points.

Big onions and milk powder added 0.26 and 0.23 percentage points, respectively. Chicken, dried chillies and green chillies also contributed to the annual increase.

The lower coconut and rice prices cushioned the rise, subtracting 0.31 and 0.22 percentage points, respectively. Limes, despite becoming more expensive during October, remained cheaper than a year earlier and reduced annual inflation by 0.22 percentage points.

The non-food items contributed 5.42 percentage points to annual inflation, with the fuel-related expenditure prominent among the drivers.

Petrol and other fuel within the transport category accounted for 1.42 percentage points of the annual increase in the overall index. The bus fares contributed 0.27 percentage points, motor cars 0.20 percentage points, diesel 0.19 percentage points and vehicle servicing 0.17 percentage points. School transport added another 0.13 percentage points.

Within housing and utilities, the higher rents contributed 0.52 percentage points, followed by LP gas at 0.28 percentage points, electricity at 0.19 percentage points and maintenance materials at 0.16 percentage points.

Restaurants and hotels added 0.87 percentage points to annual inflation, while education contributed 0.31 percentage points, reflecting the increases in international school fees, higher education course fees, tuition and vocational training charges.

Miscellaneous goods and services contributed 0.21 percentage points, household furnishings and maintenance 0.15 percentage points and health 0.10 percentage points. Recreation and culture, along with alcoholic beverages, tobacco and narcotics, recorded negative annual contributions.

Meanwhile, inflation measured by the 12-month moving average increased to 5.0 percent in October, from 4.5 percent in September. The corresponding food measure rose to 4.1 percent, from 3.7 percent, while the non-food average inflation increased to 5.4 percent, from 4.8 percent.

The CCPI tracks the prices of goods and services purchased by the urban households in the Colombo district. Its basket comprises 426 items, with expenditure weights derived from the 2019 Household Income and Expenditure Survey.
