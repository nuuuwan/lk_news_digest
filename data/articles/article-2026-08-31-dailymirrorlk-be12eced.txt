Sri Lanka faces heightened Maha season risks from El Niño, high costs

Sri Lanka is facing an elevated risk of disruption to food production during the upcoming Maha cultivation season due to the combined effects of strengthening El Niño conditions, rising input costs and the possibility of heavy rainfall, according to a new United Nations report.

The Asia-Pacific regional assessment has placed Sri Lanka among countries of **“elevated concern”**, warning that above-average rainfall is possible from October with the onset of the Second Inter-Monsoon.

While increased rainfall could help replenish reservoirs and improve water availability for agriculture, the report cautioned that excessive rain during the Maha planting period could trigger flooding and waterlogging, while also disrupting and delaying cultivation activities.

The report further warned that elevated fuel and fertilizer costs could compound the impact on agricultural production and food security.

Diesel and petrol prices in Sri Lanka remain around **44% and 41% higher**, respectively, than their levels before the Middle East crisis. The country also remains vulnerable to fluctuations in imported fertilizer costs and international wheat prices.

The assessment was jointly prepared by the **Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), World Food Programme (WFP), UN Women, World Meteorological Organization (WMO)** and other UN humanitarian agencies.
