President pledges tax relief in 2027 Budget

Colombo, Sep. 21 (Daily Mirror) - Acknowledging the tax burden faced by the public, President Anura Kumara Dissanayake yesterday said the Government was expecting to remove certain taxes through the 2027 Budget to provide relief to people affected by rising costs.

Addressing a public rally in Akuressa, the President said the Government had focused on strengthening the economy, livelihoods, food security and opportunities for economic advancement, while stressing the need to translate these efforts into tangible benefits for the people.

He said the Government had planned programmes to channel funds collected by the Treasury to communities at the grassroots level. According to the President, Rs. 2 billion has been allocated for capital expenditure in next year's Budget.

The Government would also take measures to support vulnerable sections of society, he said, emphasizing the need to ensure that relief reaches those who need it most.

“Even in developed countries, certain groups of people live on relief. Funds in the Treasury must be channeled to the village level as development and as relief as well,” he said.

Referring to rising international fuel prices, President Dissanayake said fuel prices in the global market had increased and that, based on the new prices, domestic fuel prices would have to be revised from October 1.

However, he said the Government had decided to provide concessions to minimixe the impact on consumers.

“We allocated Rs. 600 billion to provide fuel concessions in April, May, June and July. The Government must give a concession if fuel prices are increased unbearably,” he said.

The President said the Government was also giving priority to the development of education, health and transport.

“I have instructed the authorities to provide updates on modern health methods. I will allocate funds. We will commence education reforms for Grade Six next year. The electric train project will commence next year,” he said.

He added that the Government was placing particular emphasis on digitalization, with plans to construct 1,000 communication towers within three years. Funds would be allocated for 750 towers next year, he said.

President Dissanayake also highlighted the Government's islandwide public rallies and consultations covering various sectors, including women and youth, saying they were intended to strengthen engagement between the Government and the people.

“The rulers must be responsible for the people. If the rulers are not directed on the correct path, people have a right to protest. We need to ensure full democracy. We cannot move forward by suppressing the media or contracting democracy,” he said.

The President further claimed that public approval of the Government had increased compared with the level of support it had received when it first assumed office.
