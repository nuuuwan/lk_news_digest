Sri Lanka’s assistance to 40 Iranian sailors raises U.S. sanctions concerns

Colombo, November 3 (Daily Mirror)- In response to a question regarding Sri Lanka’s action under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) to repatriate 40 Iranian crew members, the U.S. said facilitating crew replenishment to sanctioned vessels, among others, also risks exposure to U.S. sanctions.

A State Department spokesman told the Daily Mirror in an email response that providing fuel, provisions, maintenance services, or facilitating crew replenishment to sanctioned vessels risks exposure to U.S. sanctions.

“The United States will continue to disrupt, expose, and dismantle Iranian military and proliferation activities, the procurement schemes, malicious cyber operations, and illicit oil trade that sustain the regime's malign behaviour. We call on the international community to join us in holding these actors to account,” the spokesman said.

Sri Lanka's foreign ministry responded recently to a distress call from the Iranian vessels stranded in its Exclusive Economic Zone but outside its territorial waters for medical treatment of 40 sailors. The operation was planned by the Iranian Embassy in Colombo. There are around 20 Iranian vessels sanctioned by the U.S.

Sri Lanka maintains that it adheres to its neutral foreign policy. The sailors who were taken ashore left the country by plane to Iran.
