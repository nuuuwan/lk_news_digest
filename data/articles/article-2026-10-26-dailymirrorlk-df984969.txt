Sri Lanka walks diplomatic tight rope as 21 Iranian oil tankers remain stranded

Colombo, Oct 26 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka is attempting to balance its diplomatic relations with the United States while remaining prepared to provide humanitarian assistance to Iran, with around 21 Iranian oil tankers reportedly stranded just outside the country’s territorial waters.

Sources said the vessels, which are reportedly empty, remain near Sri Lanka’s maritime borders. However, the Government has refrained from providing any commercial supplies or logistical support, particularly after the US threatened secondary sanctions against those providing assistance to US-sanctioned Iranian vessels.

On Saturday, two Iranian vessels made distress calls to the Sri Lanka Navy through the Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre (MRCC), seeking urgent medical assistance for 40 sailors who had reportedly been stranded at sea for months.

Bound by its international humanitarian obligations, Sri Lanka responded to the distress calls, rescued all 40 sailors and provided them with medical treatment before repatriating them to Iran yesterday morning.

“Yes, it is correct. We rescued 40 sailors, provided medical treatment and repatriated them on Sunday morning,” Foreign Minister Vijitha Herath told the Daily Mirror.

However, the Government has remained cautious about providing commercial supplies or logistical assistance to the US-sanctioned Iranian vessels, mindful of Washington’s warning on secondary sanctions. Colombo is seeking to avoid unnecessary diplomatic complications while maintaining relations with all countries, including Iran.

According to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the sailors were aboard merchant vessels located approximately 24 nautical miles off Galle when the distress calls were received over their medical conditions.

The evacuation was arranged and facilitated by the Iranian Embassy in Colombo, while Sri Lankan authorities coordinated the transfer of the crew members to shore through the Port of Galle.

The Iranian vessels have reportedly been unable to return to their home ports amid a US blockade, while sanctions have disrupted Iranian maritime operations in the region.

Defence officials said reconnaissance aircraft and naval patrol boats had been deployed to monitor the vessels.

The latest humanitarian operation follows an earlier maritime incident in March, when a US submarine sank the Iranian frigate IRIS Dena in international waters near Sri Lanka, killing 104 sailors.

The Sri Lanka Navy rescued 32 Iranian sailors following that incident, while a second Iranian naval vessel subsequently sought safe harbour in Trincomalee.

Despite the broader geopolitical tensions, Sri Lanka has maintained that its assistance to Iranian seafarers is based on humanitarian considerations, while exercising caution over any action that could expose the country to sanctions or strain its diplomatic relations with Washington.
