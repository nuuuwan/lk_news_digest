T-bill auction yields rise; yield curve declines from belly to long end

The secondary market yield curve experienced a mixed sentiment yesterday, broadly similar to the previous session, amidst the moderate trading volumes and activity.

The yield curve underwent a downward adjustment from the belly to the long end compared with the previous day.

Amongst the traded maturities, the 01.08.2030 and 15.10.2030 maturities traded within the range of 11.10 percent to 10.95 percent. Meanwhile, the 01.02.2031 maturity traded between 11.15 percent and 11.00 percent, while the 15.12.2032 maturity traded in the range of 11.40 percent to 11.35 percent.

Further along the curve, both the 01.06.2033 and 01.11.2033 maturities traded between 11.70 percent and 11.50 percent. The 15.10.2034 maturity also recorded trades within the range of 11.85 percent to 11.80 percent.

Yesterday, the PDMO concluded its weekly T-bill auction, raising Rs.60.0 billion, in line with the total amount offered. For the three-month T-bill, Rs.20.0 billion was initially offered, while Rs.30.1 billion was accepted. The weighted average yield rate (WAYR) increased by 2bps to 9.20 percent.

For the six-month T-bill, the PDMO accepted Rs.19.5 billion against an offered amount of Rs.25.0 billion, with the WAYR rising by 1bps to 9.37 percent. For the 12-month T-bill, Rs.10.4 billion was accepted, which was below the offered amount, while the WAYR increased by 5bps to 9.93 percent.

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee appreciated against the US dollar, standing at Rs.329.89/US dollar, compared to Rs.330.81/US dollar seen earlier. Market liquidity in the banking system expanded to Rs.363.87 billion, compared to Rs.294.09 billion recorded previously.
