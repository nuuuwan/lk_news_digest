Protection of State from Terrorism Bill will suppress media freedom: Joint Opposition

Colombo, October 28 (Daily Mirror) - The Protection of State from Terrorism Bill, which has come into the limelight, will suppress media freedom in Sri Lanka, the Joint Opposition (JO) said yesterday.

“The Government is looking to enact the Protection of State from Terrorism Bill as soon as possible. This legislation will end media freedom in this country. Accordingly, it will mark the end of media institutions, editors and journalists. It will also affect the lives of the entire population,” JO Convener Prof. G.L. Peiris told a media briefing.

“The job of a journalist is to report information. Reporting cannot be considered an act of influencing someone to do something under the Bill. However, the legislation provides for a media report to be considered an act of terrorism.

“It was media personnel who revealed the disappearance of 323 containers and the purchase of inferior coal. Journalists investigated these matters and reported their findings. They usually obtain information from their sources. The Protection of State from Terrorism Bill contains provisions that could compel media personnel to reveal their sources. They will be unable to obtain information from their sources if they are forced to disclose their identities. In that respect, the Bill could bring reporting to a halt,” he said.

Prof. Peiris added that the Bill contained provisions to ban media briefings, rallies, seminars and similar events.

“The media also cover seminars, press conferences and rallies. The legislation provides for the Government to ban all these events,” he said.

“There are serious issues concerning the Bill. The definition of terrorism in the proposed legislation is questionable and overly broad. There is no other legislation in the world in which the definition of terrorism is so broad-based.

“Archaeological sites, public health and the power grid are included as areas vulnerable to terrorism under the Bill. These areas could be covered subject to a specific threshold of harm being caused. However, no such threshold has been specified in the legislation. This puts everyone at risk,” he added.

“Influencing the Government to do something or refrain from doing something could be considered an act of terrorism under this legislation. A person who commits an offence of this nature could face a prison sentence of 15 years or a fine of not less than Rs. 15 million.

“If a speech delivered at a rally could be considered an act of terrorism, even a person distributing leaflets inviting people to attend that rally could be penalised if the speech were deemed an act of terrorism.

“The Bill also contains provisions allowing the Government to ban events such as rallies and protests and subsequently report the matter to the courts in accordance with its provisions.

“Under the proposed law, the Defence Secretary would be empowered to prohibit a person from entering any location. Accordingly, the Defence Secretary could make decisions according to his whims and fancies.

“The IGP or a DIG could seek the Defence Secretary’s approval to arrest anyone suspected of engaging in terrorism. Such a person could be detained for two months without trial,” he said.
