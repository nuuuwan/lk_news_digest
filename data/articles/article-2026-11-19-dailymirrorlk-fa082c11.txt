FIU collects Rs.3.3mn in fines from 2 firms over screening lapses

Sri Lanka’s Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) collected Rs.3.3 million in fines from Commercial Credit & Finance PLC and New Natasha (Pvt.) Ltd, in the April-June quarter of 2026, after the examinations found lapses in how both companies screened the customers against the lists of designated individuals and entities.

Commercial Credit paid Rs.3 million after an FIU examination found it had established and maintained a business relationship with an individual designated under United Nations Regulations No. 1 of 2012.

The FIU said the finance company failed to verify the customer effectively at the outset, screen its existing customer base when the lists changed and promptly update its systems with two lists published in the government gazette.

New Natasha paid Rs.300,000 after a Central Bank examination found it had failed to screen the customers against the designated lists when establishing business relationships. The examiners found no instances of the company maintaining relationships with the designated individuals or entities.

The examination also found that New Natasha had failed to obtain the mandatory customer information, including the purpose of the transaction, source of funds and whether the customer was a politically exposed person, for transactions above Rs.200,000 or the equivalent in foreign currency.

The company also failed to obtain and retain copies of the identification documents for such transactions and to carry out a comprehensive assessment of its money laundering and terrorist financing risks.

The FIU imposed the penalties under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act.

Commercial Credit paid its fine on April 21 and New Natasha paid on June 3. The proceeds were credited to the Consolidated Fund.
