Local businesses urged to adapt as AI transforms online consumer behaviour

Artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly changing how Sri Lankans search for information, discover brands and make purchasing decisions, signalling a fundamental shift that businesses can no longer afford to ignore, according to the Digital Outlook Sri Lanka 2026 report.

The study found AI has moved beyond being a niche technology to becoming part of everyday online behaviour, with more users relying on AI-powered tools to obtain information and complete daily tasks. The report noted that 81 percent of internet users now incorporate AI into their information-seeking habits, with a growing number bypassing traditional search engines altogether.

Researchers said the change marks a new phase in Sri Lanka’s digital economy, where businesses will increasingly need to optimise content and customer engagement not only for conventional search engines, but also for AI-driven platforms.

The report also highlighted changing consumer expectations in online shopping. While confidence in e-commerce continues to improve, Sri Lankan consumers remain cautious, preferring cash on delivery over digital payments and placing significant weight on customer reviews before purchasing from unfamiliar brands.

The findings suggest companies investing in digital commerce will need to strengthen customer trust through reviews, reputation management and transparent online engagement rather than relying solely on paid advertising.

Based on a survey of 2,948 internet users across all 25 districts, the report found smartphones remain the dominant digital device, owned by 92 percent of internet users, reinforcing mobile-first behaviour across the country. Laptop ownership stood at 57 percent, while smartwatches continued to gain traction among consumers.

The report, jointly produced by the Asia Pacific Institute of Digital Marketing and the University of Kelaniya’s Department of Marketing Management, said Sri Lanka has entered a period of more mature digital adoption, with consumers increasingly integrating digital platforms into shopping, communication and everyday decision-making rather than simply using them for entertainment.
