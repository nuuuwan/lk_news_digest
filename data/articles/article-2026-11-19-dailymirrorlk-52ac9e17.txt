Court told of threat to wildlife from 13,207 state-issued firearms

Colombo, Nov. 19 (Daily Mirror) - The Court of Appeal was told that the iconic tusker Kawanthissa had died after being shot at with a firearm, highlighting the dangers posed to wildlife by the issuing of 13,207 firearms to members of the public, including farmers.

Appearing for the Wildlife and Nature Protection Society of Sri Lanka (WNPS), President’s Counsel Sanjeeva Jayawardena made these submissions when a writ petition challenging the issuing of 13,207 firearms to farmers to protect their crops from wild animals was taken up before a two-judge Bench of the Court of Appeal, comprising its President, Justice Rohantha Abeysuriya and Justice Priyantha Fernando.

Jayawardena told court that several elephants had died after being shot at, including Bhathiya and Kawanthissa, while the lives of many others remained at risk. He cautioned that any further increase in the number of firearms issued to the public would aggravate the threat to wildlife.

The President’s Counsel informed court that firearms had previously been issued to farmers for crop protection, subject to a limit of five acres of cultivated land.

However, he maintained that a previous government decision to reduce the minimum extent of cultivated land required for eligibility from five acres to two acres could result in an increase in the number of firearms issued.

He further submitted that the number of firearms issued, which stood at 305 in 2002, had increased to 490 by 2026.

President of the Court of Appeal, Justice Rohantha Abeysuriya, expressed concern over the issue, stating in open court that elephants must be protected as a national asset.

"Tourists do not come to this country to look at buildings. The Court of Appeal is deeply concerned about wildlife and the environment," Justice Abeysuriya observed.

The petitioner is seeking the urgent intervention of the court to address the alarming increase in the killing of wild elephants in Sri Lanka.

The petition also raises concerns that a substantial number of the 13,207 firearms issued by the State remain unlicensed and unaccounted for, posing a serious threat to the country's endangered elephant population.

The petitioner is further seeking an order directing the relevant authorities to investigate into wildlife-related offences promptly and prosecute those responsible in accordance with the law.

The petitioner was represented by President’s Counsel Sanjeeva Jayawardena, together with Attorneys-at-Law Prashanthi Mahindraratne, Dilumi De Alwis and Lakmini Warusevitane.
