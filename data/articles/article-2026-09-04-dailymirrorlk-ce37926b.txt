Police do not send SMS or WhatsApp links for fine payments: Traffic DIG

Colombo, Sep. 4 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Police have warned the public about a fraudulent WhatsApp and SMS scam circulating under the guise of the Colombo Traffic Police, demanding online payments for alleged traffic violations.

Deputy Inspector General (DIG) of Traffic and Road Safety W.P.J. Senadheera said the fraudulent messages inform recipients that they have committed a traffic offence and demand payment of a fine using a fake case number.

The messages reportedly threaten recipients with additional daily charges if the fine is not paid within two days and warn of legal action if the payment remains outstanding for 30 days.

According to DIG Senadheera, the messages contain links that direct recipients to fake payment portals designed to obtain sensitive personal information and bank card details.

He said several people had fallen victim to the scam, with some discovering that their bank accounts had been emptied after providing their financial details.

DIG Senadheera urged anyone who has been victimized to immediately lodge a complaint with the Officer-in-Charge (OIC) of their nearest police station. Such complaints will subsequently be referred to the relevant special investigation units.

Clarifying the official procedure, he said that the Sri Lanka Traffic Police does not send SMS or WhatsApp messages containing links for the payment of traffic fines and does not operate a separate website for such payments.

He said the only legitimate method for making online payments for traffic fines is through the official GovPay system.

Under the official procedure, motorists who are issued a spot fine are provided with a spot fine number by the traffic officer. The fine can then be paid through a banking or fintech application using GovPay.

Once the payment is completed, the relevant police officer receives an instant confirmation on their device, enabling the driver's licence to be returned immediately.

Police have urged the public to remain vigilant, ignore suspicious traffic fine messages, avoid clicking on unknown links and never share confidential banking or card details through such platforms.
