Melbourne-Colombo flights already running near-full, beating expectations: Jetstar’s Zeglin

Melbourne-Colombo flights already over 90% full each way, even outside peak season: Zeglin

Jetstar taps Intrepid Travel tie-up to unlock Australia’s leisure potential beyond its VFR-heavy visitor base

Jetstar Airways’ inaugural Melbourne–Colombo flight touched down at Bandaranaike International Airport last week to a ceremony marking the airline’s entry into the Sri Lankan market.

Speaking to Daily Mirror Business on the sidelines of the event, Jetstar Airways Executive Manager Commercial Scott Zeglin said demand on the new route has already outpaced the airline’s own forecasts, even outside peak season.

“We were 92 percent full coming in, and 91 percent on the way out today. What we have noticed is that the flights have a good baseline load, but then they book really late too. With this inaugural flight, we were surprised how full it got, we weren’t expecting it to be this full, but it has been very, very strong,” Zeglin said.

The service, operated with a reconfigured widebody Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner, runs three times weekly year-round and introduces more than 100,000 low-fare seats annually on the corridor, establishing Sri Lanka’s first low-cost direct link to Australia, though SriLankan Airlines already operates the route on a full-service basis. Jetstar’s pitch is squarely on price, with promotional launch fares starting from Rs. 67,770.

According to the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA), Australia is the island’s sixth-largest source market, generating 67,138 arrivals in the first seven months of 2026, up 11.1 percent year-on-year. The market’s underlying profile, however, is unusual: visiting friends and relatives (VFR) accounts for 34.21 percent of Australian arrivals against 57.72 percent travelling purely for leisure. While Victoria’s sizeable Sri Lankan expatriate community underpins a dependable passenger base, Zeglin said Jetstar’s low-fare model is designed to convert the broader Australian holidaymaker segment that has historically defaulted to Southeast Asian destinations.

Of the inaugural flight, Zeglin said about 85 percent of passengers were flying point-to-point between Melbourne and Colombo, with the remaining 15 percent connecting beyond Melbourne—roughly split between domestic Australia and New Zealand.

On whether Jetstar would add frequencies for the December–February peak season, Zeglin was measured, noting the thrice-weekly service must first prove its business case amid global cost volatility.

“Since we started planning this, we’ve had fuel go through the roof in terms of costs with the Middle East pieces,” he said. “We definitely need to make sure we can cover those increased cost pressures on the route, but so far, loads are good, revenue is good, and we’re very pleased with the performance.” He added that peak-season demand “is excellent, and we’re really looking forward to that.”

To build sustained leisure demand, Jetstar has partnered with tour operator Intrepid Travel, which brought organised tour groups on the inaugural service and ranks Sri Lanka as its fifth most popular destination for Australian travellers. The airline also hosted a delegation of Australian content creators and New Zealand journalists on the flight.

“That was really targeting that Australian and New Zealand tourist who can go to Bali, can go to Thailand, can go anywhere in the Asia-Pacific,” Zeglin said. “A lot of travellers on full-service carriers are visiting friends and relatives or looking at higher price tiers. Our low-fare model allows Aussie leisure travellers looking for budget-friendly holidays to consider Sri Lanka. Someone once tried to describe Sri Lanka as ‘the Bali of 30 years ago’—and I said that doesn’t do Sri Lanka justice at all! Sri Lanka is way better than that.”

Qantas Group has previously used Jetstar’s low-cost network to test emerging leisure routes ahead of a possible mainline entry, and a senior Qantas Group Asset Management executive, Rohan Garnett, was part of the delegation that travelled with Zeglin.

The bilateral significance of the connection was echoed by Australian High Commissioner to Sri Lanka Matthew Duckworth, who drew parallels to the historic World War II “Double Sunrise” flights operated between Perth and Koggala Lake by Qantas and the Royal Australian Air Force, noting that as both nations approach the 80th anniversary of formal diplomatic ties next year, direct air links serve as an important bridge for trade, education and two-way tourism.

MJF Leisure Chair and Tourism Alliance Co-Chair Malik J. Fernando wrote in a social media post that direct services mark a structural shift for the industry.

‘‘Beyond the headlines, this is about reducing our historic overreliance on Middle East transit hubs, diversifying our source markets, and attracting the kind of direct-flight traveller who spends more once they’re here,” Fernando wrote. “Securing these routes is only half the job—it now falls to our industry to support them with sustained, targeted marketing so airlines have the confidence to stay, and ideally go year-round.”

On that front, the Sri Lanka Tourism Promotion Bureau (SLTPB)’s Rs. 1.5 billion interim marketing campaign—which this paper reported kicked off in August and runs through April 2027—has already turned to Australia as a lead market, with roadshows rolling out there before extending to New Zealand, Germany and the UK. The interim push, which combines digital marketing, public relations and market-specific roadshows, precedes a larger Rs. 5 billion global branding campaign due to roll out from around March, becoming fully operational by April 2027.

Zeglin remarked  that Jetstar is open to joint promotional activities with Sri Lankan tourism authorities to build on the route’s early momentum. SLTPB already runs a similar tie-up with Emirates and several other airlines under a memorandum of understanding first signed in 2022 and renewed through 2027, covering joint destination marketing and familiarisation trips for travel trade and media.

A senior Jetstar and Qantas delegation—including Zeglin and Garnett, alongside Hayleys Advantis Deputy Managing Director Shano Sabar, whose company serves as Jetstar’s General Sales Agent (GSA) in Sri Lanka—also met Minister of Ports and Civil Aviation Anura Karunatilaka, Ministry Secretary W.W.S. Mangala, and Civil Aviation Authority of Sri Lanka (CAASL) Director General and CEO Capt. Daminda Rambukwella, to discuss passenger facilitation, terminal handling capacity and potential frequency growth.

Capt. Rambukwella also proposed that Jetstar evaluate future operations to Mattala Rajapaksa International Airport (MRIA) to leverage the country’s regional airport network. (NF)
