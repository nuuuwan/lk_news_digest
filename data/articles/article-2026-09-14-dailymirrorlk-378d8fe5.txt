In a recent interview with Daily Mirror, MP Dilith Jayaweera said that Sarvajana Balaya’s vision is to govern rather than act as a political power broker for major political parties. Drawing on his background as an entrepreneur and brand strategist, Jayaweera acknowledged that he isn’t happy about his political campaign. He underscored that fact that lasting support cannot be built on media presence alone though it could attract attention for a short period of time. Instead, he believes that earning public trust through hands-on experience as a long-term challenge; one he believes he will achieve by cultivating strong, youth-led second-tier leadership. The Parliamentarian stated that as a new political party he is happy with the party’s progress. He further said that NPP is governing the country in the most archaic manner and added that bringing 22nd Amendment would be disastrous and would result repercussions which would affect the country’s democracy.

QSarvajana Balaya was formed as an alternative to the existing system. But don’t you think there is a clash of ideologies? You contested forming a coalition with few parties including the communist party. While you promote concepts like entrepreneurial state, CP’s political ideology is on socialism. Isn’t this impractical?

My entry into politics itself is different from the other politicians who took to politics. I decided to get into active politics due to the fact that I saw a huge gap. Traditionally Sri Lankan politics grew through these traditional political parties. People enter politics through them. When I first entered politics, the first challenge for someone like me, who has not been in politics, was how to make myself relevant in the sphere of politics.

I had a few choices. First to go on my own without any support from any traditional political party. Second was to make a noticeable entry with the support of some existing political parties and politicians. That way I will be seen as serious contender than an ad hoc overnight political entrant.

To answer your question- when I had to work with, for example the Communist Party and whether it clashes with the concept of entrepreneurship and entrepreneurial state-in today’s context socialism does not clash with these two.

Traditionally it was seen more a class struggle and the difference between the capital holders and workers. Today if you look at even the socialist countries, they have fully and decisively embraced the entrepreneurial state concept. For example Vietnam and China are supposed to be socialist states. They are promoting entrepreneurship as the nation’s main slogan. What it really says it that these nations believe wealth creation is the core of anything that they do. What all these successful nations have done is wealth creation using the entrepreneurial mind which I believe should be applied to the state itself. Then only it can trickle down to the people of a nation.

This is a quite a complex arrangement, both with the people, political parties, leaders and the rest of the stakeholders. You’ve got to work with the state. In such as arrangement creating effective brands is a challenge. We as a team take it as a challenge

QYou are known as a strategist who created brands and personalities. But do you think you have been successful in your own political campaign so far?

No a lot more needs to be done. As they say, just because I have been an effective strategist for other political campaigns does not necessarily guarantee the same for myself. So that’s a challenge. We need to work on our strategies in crafting, communicating and building the brand ‘Sarvajana Balaya’. We need to work on strategies. We take challenges. But brands and personalities or political parties as brands is an evolving process. So it does not mean that you give your best product on the first day itself.

Because this is a quite a complex arrangement, both with the people, political parties, leaders and the rest of the stakeholders. You’ve got to work with the state. In such as arrangement creating effective brands is a challenge. We as a team take it as a challenge. But we are confident that we can build a brand especially which is relevant to youth, GenZs, Gen Alpha and the millennials. But to answer your question am I happy so far? I really work harder on it, every day, 365 days while on the go.

I know we have not unleashed our full potential, and we have not opened the doors of our armoury yet. A relationship with people is not something where you can create a campaign, which can go on traditional media or new media and create a brand. You need to win the people alongside and it is a task by itself. So just because you can place television advertisements or you can do a TIkTok on these brands, probably you can do it in the short run, but they don’t sort of live in the hearts of the people. So it’s evolving.

QOne common criticism levelled at the opposition- mainly the SJB and UNP- is that the leadership did not allow or give opportunities to build a second-tier leadership. Both the parties seem to be person centric. Even Sarvajana Balaya, we do not see much of the faces except you. Would you agree? Have you taken measures to build a second tier?

Our whole plan is to build a second tier. We saw this as the biggest gap in Sri Lankan politics. But it is unfortunate that we haven’t yet been successful enough in communicating the message that we are building our second tier.

We have taken very non-traditional steps to bring these new faces to the forefront. We have a programme called hundred new faces. So we are exposing this, especially the youth as our next leadership.

Why you are asking this question is because I have more visibility because I give leadership. The truth is that the media likes to take voice cuts or appearances of mine than the others in the party. If you take a look at district leaders and chief organizers in the districts, we have many professionals.

Which is why so far, we have been successful as a new party which is more than two years old. As you can see, we don’t have to transport people to our meetings. Instead, we work with our ground level network. There we promote entrepreneurship, creativity. We got youth and it’s working organically.

QIf the NPP adopts economic policies, similar to what you advocate, would you support them or consider forming an alliance with them?

Anyone who accepts the basics of what we talk about on the concept of wealth creation for the nation we support. Whether we can form an alliance is a complexed situation to which I have to answer.

A political force like NPP, whether they are open enough just for the sake of it. They can say that this is an entrepreneurial state or something that. Whether they believe in it, whether they have it in their DNA, in terms of implementation, whether they have the courage, and determination, those things matter.

But my biggest problem with the NPP is its, central ideology steming from the JVP. And the JVP isn’t a party which believed in modern Economy. They still follow traditional beliefs. We need more than that. So, my submission and my proposition is that, when a political party does not have a progressive core philosophy, it is difficult to adapt to suit the modern world and the global competition. And realise the global economic challenges. So today, what is evident in their governance is that they are not susceptible, and ready. They are instead going in the most archaic manner that you can look at the world today.

QIn your opinion, why is the government campaigning strongly for 22nd Amendment irrespective of the strong opposition from the BASL?

There’s a belief that government should have control over the judiciary. Traditionally, every government has tried to control the Judiciary. No one can say they didn’t. But all of them have failed, wherever they have tried this trick. Starting with JR Jayewardene to Mahinda Rajapakse. This government also believes that by having their favourite people in those positions they can have control. I believe, it is to their detriment, that they continue to believe so, and they are in fact taking a disastrous step of changing the constitution; just to keep one person in that seat. The country’s foremost law is being changed, just to keep one person in that seat. Such a thing is not welcome by anyone who believes in democracy. And the repercussions are going to be terrible. If you look at the rest of the world, in a civilised world, this is considered to be araw and uncivilised attempt.

Today if you look at even the socialist countries, they have fully and decisively embraced the entrepreneurial state concept. For example Vietnam and China are supposed to be socialist states. They are promoting entrepreneurship as the nation’s main slogan

QAt present, Sri Lanka relies on funding such as IMF to stabilise the economy. At the same time there have been calls from the west to dismantle laws which penalise sexual minorities. Recently EU had a concern over discrimination of LGBTQ community. In the National Strategic Plan which was published in 2024 by Sarvajana Balaya there is no reference regarding gender. Any particular reason for that? What is your party’s view on quota system for women’s representation?

The document that we published, is not a traditional manifesto. It is a strategic plan. Here we have summarised our strategy if the country is to be taken to the next level. Our attempt was to establish the thought that the country needs a strategy. This is a document where we discuss strategies.

In other parties, they published manifestos, where they don’t talk about a strategy. They talk about handouts. I would say that those are simply to cater to politics. And these are promises which are not fulfilled. Our document is the exact opposite of it. We talk about a central strategy. And the implementation, of gender equality, it is only obvious that when the country’s females from more than 50% of the population, they have to be given more than equal opportunity to participate in governance. It is understood and it doesn’t need a special line with bold letters. There is a dedicated chapter for women. In that, we have discussed why they should be included; not from the traditional Western concept of gender equality slogan. If they don’t have the equal opportunity and if they don’t engage in the activities of the nation on equal terms, the country can’t move forward. How will we create wealth if you are going to keep these 50% of the population as housewives? How will the economy move? How will the wealth be created? It is obvious that we believe females should be given more than equal opportunity. We should be creatively making them participated.

QWhat is Sarvajana Balaya’s long term objective? Is it to govern Sri Lanka long-term or to become a decisive power broker capable of determining which major party forms a government?

We need to govern the country, and we don’t believe in becoming a power broker, because it will not solve the problems that the country has been facing for a long time. As Sarvajana Balaya we believe that we should build a national political narrative. But that must be done while protecting our civilizational values. We must build an entrepreneurial nation. In the short run, we want to influence every possible political party, the bureaucracy. This is the way forward. Finally, what we want is to implement this. To implement this, you must have power. But if you become a power broker, we will not be able to implement this strategy. Instead, we will become a party to a programme, which again is going to be very traditional and having a conventional model. Sarvajana Balaya is a progressive party that doesn’t want to be party of a failed project.

QIn 10 years’ time, where would you position yourself and Sarvajana Balaya?

If all goes well, in 2029, we will have power. We must test our trajectory whether it has been convincing enough. So if people are convinced, we will then have another seven years. According to your question where we would be in ten years, we have expectations to implement our plans and show how competitive Sri Lanka will be in our region and also work towards people’s happiness. We shall see.
