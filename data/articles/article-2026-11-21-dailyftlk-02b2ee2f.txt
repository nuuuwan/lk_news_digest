Justice for All urges President to withdraw anti-terror, organised crime Bills

Says both Bills contain ‘draconian’ clauses that could sustain abuses seen under PTA

Welcomes President’s offer to consult rights community, seeks early meeting

Justice for All has urged President Anura Kumara Dissanayake to withdraw the Protection of the State from Terrorism Bill (PSTB) and the Prevention of Organised Crimes Bill (POC), both now before Parliament. It argues that the two Bills threaten human rights and the rule of law.

In a letter dated 19 November and signed by 29 people, the organisation welcomed the President’s recent announcement that he would consult the human rights community and other stakeholders on the two Bills. It said it was writing in that spirit.

“It is in this spirit that we urge you to withdraw the two Bills as they both contain clauses that are detrimental to human rights and the rule of law in Sri Lanka,” the letter said.

Justice for All said it had demanded the repeal of the Prevention of Terrorism Act (PTA) for years. “We note that both Bills contain draconian clauses that can sustain the abuses witnessed under the PTA and have no place in a democracy,” it said.

The organisation welcomed the President’s offer to consult on law reform, said it looked forward to an open and transparent dialogue, and requested an early meeting with him to raise its concerns.

The letter was written following a decision at a meeting held on 16 November. Justice for All had announced the meeting to the President in a letter dated 9 November, inviting him to the discussion at ICES in Colombo 8, or to nominate a representative if he could not attend. It said it had also invited other members of the Government and others involved in its 2022-2023 campaign.

In that earlier letter, Justice for All said it had organised several initiatives demanding the repeal of the PTA, including a signature campaign in all 25 districts in 2022-2023. It recalled that the President and senior members of his party had supported that campaign.

“We acknowledge the support and solidarity extended by you and senior members of your party during this period, including attending events held across the country,” the organisation said.

It said engaging the human rights community, people affected under the PTA and other stakeholders was vital to repealing the Act and discussing the two Bills. “We believe such constructive engagement is critical in Sri Lanka’s law reform, with implications for human rights, the economy and the rule of law,” it said.
