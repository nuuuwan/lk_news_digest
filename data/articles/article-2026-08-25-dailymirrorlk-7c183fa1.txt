Tug-of-war delays cancer treatment equipment for five years

Colombo, Aug. 25 (Daily Mirror) - The procurement of advanced cancer radiation treatment machines for Sri Lanka’s public health system has been delayed for nearly five years, putting the lives of thousands of cancer patients at risk, Trade Union Alliance for Medical and Civil Rights Chairman Specialist Dr Chamal Sanjeewa said.

He alleged that a prolonged “tug-of-war” between health officials and certain specialists had stalled the procurement of linear accelerators, which are to be purchased through funding involving the Asian Development Bank and the General Treasury.

He said the procurement process, which was expected to be completed during the tenure of former Health Minister Keheliya Rambukwella, was halted following revelations before the Parliamentary Committee on Public Enterprises (COPE) over the proposed purchase of used and inferior equipment instead of the latest cancer treatment technology.

Although successive Health Ministers had subsequently taken steps to revive and expedite the project, the procurement process had again stalled, he alleged.

Dr Sanjeewa said Sri Lanka continues to rely heavily on cobalt-based radiation treatment, a technology that has largely been phased out in many countries in favour of more advanced systems.

He claimed that cobalt treatment could cause significant side effects and that some specialists were increasingly reluctant to use the method where more advanced alternatives were available.

According to him, linear accelerator technology can provide more precise radiation treatment, reduce damage to surrounding healthy tissue and improve the quality of life of cancer patients.

He said the Government had already approved the procurement of new equipment for several hospitals providing cancer treatment, but the process remained incomplete due to disagreements among officials and specialists.

As a result, cancer patients who cannot access the required treatment through the State health system are forced to seek treatment from private institutions, often at costs running into hundreds of thousands of rupees, he claimed.

Dr Sanjeewa also called for an investigation into whether private-sector interests or a “mafia” were influencing the prolonged delay in procuring advanced radiation treatment equipment for the free health service.

He said that the Government should urgently resolve the dispute and complete the procurement process, warning that further delays could have serious consequences for cancer patients who depend on the State health system.
