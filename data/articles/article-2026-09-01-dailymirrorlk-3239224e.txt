Inflation overshoots CB band despite easing fuel prices

Sri Lanka’s Colombo inflation accelerated to 8.0 percent in August, moving above the Central Bank’s upper tolerance margin, as the food prices rose sharply and the transport costs remained the largest source of the annual price pressure.

Headline inflation, measured by the Colombo Consumer Price Index (CCPI), increased from 7.3 percent in July, recording its sixth consecutive monthly acceleration since February, the data released by the Census and Statistics Department showed.

The August reading exceeded the upper margin of the Central Bank’s 5 percent inflation target, which allows a deviation of two percentage points on either side.

However, compliance with the target is assessed using the quarterly average inflation, while a formal breach occurs only when inflation remains outside the specified range for two consecutive quarters.

Food inflation climbed to 8.5 percent in August, from 6.3 percent in July, overtaking non-food inflation, which eased marginally to 7.7 percent, from 7.8 percent.Central Bank Governor Dr. Nandalal Weerasinghe said in a mid-August interview with Bloomberg’s The Asia Trade that inflation was expected to decline towards the 5 percent target later this year and into early next year, provided the oil prices remained around the Central Bank’s baseline assumption of US $ 80 a barrel.

The outlook could face pressure if food inflation remains elevated or the global oil prices rise above that assumption. While the lower domestic fuel prices helped contain the monthly inflation in August, transport remained the single largest contributor to the annual increase.

Despite the acceleration in the headline rate, the price growth during August was relatively contained. The CCPI rose 0.28 percent month-on-month, compared with an increase of 0.24 percent in July.

The food prices accounted for 0.20 percentage points of the monthly increase, driven by the higher prices for milk powder, dried fish, fresh fruit, ice cream, green chillies, eggs, big onions, coconuts and dried chillies.

Milk powder made the largest positive food contribution during the month, adding 0.09 percentage points to the index. The higher prices for the dried fish, fresh fruit, ice cream and green chillies each contributed 0.05 percentage points.

These increases were partly offset by the lower prices for the sea fish, vegetables, limes, rice and red onions. The sea fish and vegetables reduced the monthly index by 0.14 and 0.10 percentage points, respectively.

The non-food items contributed 0.07 percentage points to the monthly increase. The higher costs for the household equipment and maintenance, restaurants and hotels, clothing, healthcare and communication were partly offset by the lower housing and transport costs.

The reductions in the petrol, diesel and liquefied petroleum gas prices helped moderate the monthly increase. The average petrol price declined to Rs.414 a litre in August, from Rs.419 in July, while diesel fell to Rs.382, from Rs.388.25. The average price of a 12.5-kilogramme gas cylinder dropped to Rs.4,590, from Rs.4,726.25.

On a year-on-year basis, however, the non-food items remained the dominant source of inflation, contributing 5.20 percentage points to the overall rate. Transport alone contributed 2.45 percentage points, mainly reflecting the higher fuel prices compared with August 2025.

Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels contributed another 1.14 percentage points, while restaurants and hotels added 0.72 percentage points.

Food accounted for 2.77 percentage points of annual inflation, with the sea fish making the largest individual contribution of 0.97 percentage points.

Several essential food items recorded steep annual increases. The green chillies averaged Rs.1,414.12 a kilogramme, up 178 percent from a year earlier, while the big onion prices rose 67 percent and dried chillies increased 60 percent. The egg prices climbed 35 percent to an average Rs.44.71 each.

The CCPI rose to 208.8 index points in August, from 208.2 in July and 193.3 a year earlier. The 12-month moving average inflation increased to 3.9 percent, from 3.3 percent, signalling a continued build-up in broader price pressures.
