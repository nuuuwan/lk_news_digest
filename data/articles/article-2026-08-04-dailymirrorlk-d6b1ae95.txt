Dengue wards remain overcrowded despite decline in daily cases

Colombo, August 4 (Daily Mirror) - Hospitals across Sri Lanka continue to face congestion in dengue treatment wards despite a noticeable decline in the number of daily infections, hospital sources told the Daily Mirror.

According to official data, the number of daily dengue cases, which had peaked at around 1,000, has now dropped to approximately 500.

However, sources said the reduction has not been sufficient to ease pressure on hospitals, with dengue wards in many state hospitals still operating beyond capacity.

Commenting on the situation, Government Medical Officers’ Association (GMOA) Secretary Dr Prabath Sugathadasa told Daily Mirror that the severe congestion experienced during the peak of the outbreak has eased slightly, but overcrowding remains a major concern.

“Although the number of new dengue cases has declined, the wards designated for dengue patients are still overflowing. Hospitals continue to experience congestion, particularly in units treating dengue patients,” he said.

Dr Sugathadasa urged the public not to become complacent due to the recent decline in reported cases, as the risk of infection remains high and preventive measures must continue.

Dengue cases in Sri Lanka stand at 86,385 with 62 confirmed deaths.
