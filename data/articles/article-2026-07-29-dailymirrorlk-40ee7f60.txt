JKH operating earnings rise  26% to Rs.16.35bn

Premier blue-chip John Keells Holdings PLC (JKH) reported stronger operating earnings for the June quarter, powered by its ports, bunkering, supermarkets and property businesses; however, the rupee depreciation and higher taxes absorbed much of the improvement before it reached the shareholders.

Group revenue for the first quarter of 2026/27 rose 24 percent year-on-year to Rs.141.65 billion, while the earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) increased 26 percent to Rs.16.35 billion.

Profit after tax grew by a more modest 8 percent to Rs.773 million. The profit attributable to the equity holders was just Rs.62 million, although this marked a turnaround from the Rs.804 million loss reported a year earlier.

The sharp divergence between the operating earnings and bottom-line profit was largely caused by a Rs.1.89 billion net exchange loss, up from Rs.388 million in the corresponding quarter. The loss arose mainly from the translation impact of the weaker rupee on the foreign currency-denominated long-term debt of City of Dreams Sri Lanka.

The rupee fell to Rs.337 against the US dollar at end-June, from Rs.315 at end-March, creating an immediate translation impact on JKH’s net foreign currency exposure of about US $ 100 million.

Group profit before tax rose 31 percent to Rs.4.08 billion, despite the currency loss. Excluding the net exchange losses, pre-tax profit climbed to Rs.5.97 billion, from Rs.3.49 billion a year earlier, offering a clearer view of the improvement in the underlying earnings. The profit attributable to the shareholders, excluding the exchange losses, reached Rs.1.95 billion, reversing the adjusted loss of Rs.418 million recorded in the previous year.

The higher tax expenses, including an increased withholding tax on the dividends received, further constrained the conversion of the operating gains into net profit. Transportation was the standout contributor, with the EBITDA more than tripling to Rs.5.01 billion, from Rs.1.55 billion. Growth came mainly from Lanka Marine Services and the Colombo West International Terminal, alongside an improvement at the South Asia Gateway Terminals. The bunkering volumes rose 38 percent, while the margins benefited from the demand and energy-price volatility linked to the supply-chain disruptions in the Middle East.

The Colombo West International Terminal handled 460,840 twenty-foot equivalent units during the quarter and effectively reached full utilisation of its first-phase capacity based on its recent volume run rate. Completion and operationalisation of the full terminal remain scheduled for December.

Retail continued to generate the largest divisional EBITDA at Rs.6.16 billion; however, this was 16 percent below the previous year, as the lower profitability from the vehicle business outweighed growth at the Keells supermarkets.

The supermarket EBITDA increased 16 percent to Rs.2.83 billion, supported by a 13 percent rise in the same-store sales. The average basket value increased 7 percent and customer footfall grew 6 percent, although the higher electricity and distribution costs moderated the earnings improvement. The group operated 150 Keells outlets at end-June, having opened three stores during the quarter. By contrast, the combined EBITDA from John Keells CG Auto and John Keells Office Automation declined 32 percent to Rs.3.34 billion. More than 2,400 vehicles were handed over, compared with over 2,300 a year earlier but the sales mix shifted towards the lower-priced models. The result shows that the vehicle volumes remained strong but became less profitable as the market moved beyond the initial release of the pent-up demand following the reopening of vehicle imports. The consumer foods EBITDA rose 21 percent to Rs.1.52 billion. The beverage volumes grew 27 percent, improving the absorption of fixed costs, while the confectionery volumes increased 13 percent. The property EBITDA surged 358 percent to Rs.680 million, although from a relatively low base, following the profit recognition from the apartment sales at VIMAN, TRI-ZEN and Cinnamon Life.

The financial services EBITDA increased 9 percent to Rs.2.02 billion, supported by Nations Trust Bank’s loan growth and the integration of HSBC Sri Lanka’s retail banking business from May. Union Assurance also recorded a double-digit growth in gross written premiums.

Leisure remained the principal drag on the group’s broader recovery. The segment recorded a negative EBITDA of Rs.53 million, compared with a negative Rs.170 million a year earlier. Excluding City of Dreams Sri Lanka, however, leisure swung to a Rs.442 million EBITDA loss, from a profit of Rs.830 million.

The travel disruption and weaker sentiment arising from the Middle East conflict affected both Sri Lankan and the Maldivian properties. The tourist arrivals during the quarter fell 9 percent in Sri Lanka and 11 percent in the Maldives, with the latter also facing higher fuel and energy costs.

City of Dreams Sri Lanka moved to a positive EBITDA of Rs.389 million, from a Rs.1.00 billion loss. The comparison, however, is not like-for-like, as the previous-year period included only the Cinnamon Life hotel while the latest quarter covered the hotel, mall, Nuwa hotel and casino-related operations.

At pre-tax level, City of Dreams remained heavily loss-making. It reported a loss of Rs.5.85 billion, including a Rs.3.72 billion exchange loss on its US dollar-denominated loan as well as Rs.2.52 billion in depreciation, amortisation and interest expenses.

Chairperson Krishan Balendra said the project’s net profit continued to be affected by the depreciation and interest charge, while the operating performance across the integrated resort was expected to improve progressively.

The forward bookings for JKH’s leisure portfolio have strengthened from August onwards, according to Balendra but he cautioned that the outlook remained dependent on the regional geopolitical developments and their effect on the travel sentiment and airline connectivity.
