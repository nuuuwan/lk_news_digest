Target consultation draws only about 100 responses

Only about 100 responses were received during the month-long public consultation that fed into the review of Sri Lanka’s inflation target, despite what was described as tremendous public interest in the subject, Central Bank Senior Deputy Governor Dr. Chandranath Amarasekara disclosed.

That works out to roughly one response for every 220,000 people in a country of about 22 million.

The online questionnaire was open to anyone through the Central Bank website for a month and was promoted on social media. Dr. Amarasekara defended the turnout, noting that similar consultations in larger economies have at times drawn fewer than 100 responses.

“We thought the responses would be one-sided. It was not so,” he remarked, adding that the exercise produced no compelling evidence in favour of changing the existing parameters.

The 5 percent target emerged as the single most preferred option. Respondents who favoured a lower target of 2 to 4 percent cited the protection of purchasing power and savings, whereas those seeking a higher target pointed to greater flexibility in accommodating supply shocks.

Most respondents agreed with the accountability margin of 2 percentage points and preferred headline inflation over core inflation, considering it a better reflection of the cost of living. However, opinion was evenly divided on whether the target should be based on the Colombo Consumer Price Index or the National Consumer Price Index.
