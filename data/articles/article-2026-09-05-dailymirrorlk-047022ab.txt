Fems with govt. continues to support ‘Thilinapath’

Nearly 1mn schoolgirls across 6,108 schools benefit from government-backed initiative, with Fems supporting programme since inception

For many schoolgirls across Sri Lanka, managing their periods with dignity remains an uphill task, not simply because the sanitary products are hard to afford but because of the layers of silence, stigma and gaps in information that surround menstruation.

Limited awareness of menstrual health, discomfort discussing periods openly at home or in school and a lack of proper facilities to dispose of sanitary waste all compound the challenge, often pushing girls to miss school or manage their periods unsafely. Fems, a leading feminine hygiene brand under Hemas Consumer Brands, has been working alongside the government stakeholders to chip away at these barriers, not just through a single intervention but through a combination of access, education and infrastructure, including ‘Thilinapath’, a government-led initiative launched by the Education Ministry in June 2024, to provide free sanitary napkins to schoolgirls in Grade Six and above.

The programme now reaches close to one million students across 6,108 government and government-assisted special schools islandwide, with Fems on board as a key brand partner since its inception, giving schoolgirls the freedom to redeem their entitlement for the product and variant of their choice on their school premises. “Thilinapath is a government supported Initiative to strengthen access for sanitary napkins to school girls and being part of it from the outset has allowed us to reach schoolgirls with a full year’s supply of pads at no cost to them, while giving them the independence to choose their preferred brand and variant,” said Hemas Consumer Brands General Manager Marketing Sachini Gamage. However, access to products is only one part of the picture. Fems has also worked to close gaps in menstrual health knowledge through the ‘Api Katha Karamu’ (Let’s Talk) Menstrual Health and Hygiene Education Programme, a school-level initiative that brings qualified medical professionals into classrooms to speak openly with schoolgirls about menstrual health, hygiene practices and period management.
