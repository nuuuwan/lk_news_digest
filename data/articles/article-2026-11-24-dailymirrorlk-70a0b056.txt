ADB’s US$ 200mn package to ease credit barriers for small businesses in SL

US$ 100mn for investment loans, including US$ 40mn for green projects

Reforms to expand credit guarantees, improve credit reporting and curb predatory lending

At least one in five firms receiving climate-related support to be owned or led by women

Sri Lanka’s small businesses could find it easier to borrow for expansion under a US$ 200 million financing package approved by the Asian Development Bank (ADB), which will fund new investment loans and back reforms to widen access to credit guarantees.

Half the package will fund investment loans for micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) through participating financial institutions, with US$ 40 million earmarked for green lending. The remaining US$ 100 million is a policy-based loan to improve how lenders assess borrowers and strengthen oversight of the microfinance sector. MSMEs contribute more than 52 percent of Sri Lanka’s gross domestic product and account for 45 percent of employment, according to the ADB. Limited access to finance and low adoption of digital tools hold back the sector, while climate-related disasters leave businesses vulnerable to disruptions.

“This program will help the Government of Sri Lanka build a stronger, more inclusive financial ecosystem that enables MSMEs to grow, innovate, and withstand economic shocks and disasters related to the changing climate,” ADB Country Director for Sri Lanka Shannon Cowlin said. The programme will support legal reforms to establish the Microfinance and Credit Regulatory Authority, strengthen oversight and curb predatory lending. It will also strengthen credit reporting so lenders have better information to assess borrowers and expand guarantees that help lenders share the risk of lending to smaller firms.

Other measures include strengthening digital financial infrastructure and consumer protection, formalising institutional procedures and improving financial literacy and access to financial information for small businesses.

The green credit lines will finance environmentally sustainable investments. At least 20 percent of firms supported to invest in climate mitigation and adaptation will be owned or led by women.

The programme will also help growth-stage small and medium-sized companies raise equity.
