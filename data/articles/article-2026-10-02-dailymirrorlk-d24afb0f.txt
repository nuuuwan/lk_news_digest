Govt urges public to cut fuel use amid energy transition

Colombo, Oct. 2 (Daily Mirror) - The Government has urged the public to reduce fuel consumption and adopt more efficient energy-use practices as Sri Lanka moves towards cleaner sources of energy.

Minister of Ports and Civil Aviation and Minister of Energy Anura Karunathilaka said controlling fuel prices alone was not sufficient to strengthen the country's energy security, stressing the need to also reduce fuel consumption.

Speaking at the commencement of construction of new petroleum infrastructure at Muthurajawela yesterday, Karunathilaka said Sri Lanka was moving towards an energy transition, with electric vehicles and solar power expected to play a key role.

“As a country, we must focus not only on controlling fuel prices but also on controlling fuel consumption. The public also has a major responsibility in this regard,” he said.

He said changing patterns of energy consumption was particularly important to achieving greater energy security.

The Minister urged the public to avoid periods of high energy demand, make greater use of electricity and shift towards fuel-efficient vehicles, particularly electric vehicles.

He said the Government expected to add 1,200 megawatts of solar power capacity to the national grid by 2029 as part of efforts to expand renewable energy.

The remarks came as the Government commenced construction of a new Jet A-1 aviation fuel pipeline and storage complex and three new petroleum storage tanks at Muthurajawela.

The Jet A-1 project includes five new storage tanks with a combined capacity of 92,000 cubic metres and a 21-kilometre underground pipeline connecting the Muthurajawela complex with Bandaranaike International Airport in Katunayake.

Meanwhile, the Ceylon Petroleum Storage Terminal Limited is constructing three new storage tanks at Muthurajawela, adding a further 40,000 cubic metres to the country's petroleum storage capacity.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, who attended the event, said the Government's objective was to maintain a strong state presence in the energy market while providing an efficient service to the public.
