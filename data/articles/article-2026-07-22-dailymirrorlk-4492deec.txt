Govt. dismisses false claims over EPF, ETF reforms

Labour Minister and Deputy Finance Minister Dr. Anil Jayantha yesterday dismissed social media claims that the government planned to merge the Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF) and Employees’ Trust Fund (ETF) or transfer their administration to the private sector as “false and misleading”.

There has been growing speculation over the future of the country’s two largest employee retirement savings schemes following the submission of a Cabinet paper on their administration.

Jayantha said the Cabinet paper was intended solely to improve the administrative efficiency of the funds and that no decision had been taken to merge the EPF and ETF or alter their ownership and governance.

“The EPF and ETF, built through the hard-earned savings of employees, are secure. The government has not taken any decision to merge these funds or hand over their administration to the private sector,” he said.

According to the minister, the proposal seeks to appoint a committee of officials to recommend ways to streamline administration, improve the integration of data systems, strengthen investment management and enhance benefits available to members.

At present, the EPF’s administrative functions are handled by the Department of Labour while the fund itself is managed by the Central Bank of Sri Lanka. The ETF operates under a separate institutional framework.

Jayantha said maintaining multiple administrative structures had created operational inefficiencies, with workers often facing delays in accessing their savings due to discrepancies such as minor differences in names across databases.

He added that billions of rupees remained in unidentified or unresolved accounts because of poor integration between existing data systems, highlighting the need for a unified digital platform to improve service delivery.

The minister said the committee’s role would be limited to making recommendations on improving administration, technology integration, secure investment practices and member services. It has not been given any mandate to recommend merging the funds or transferring their management to private entities.

Once completed, the committee’s report will be released for public consultation and discussed with employees and the National Labour Consultative Council before any policy decisions are taken, he said.

Jayantha stressed the government’s objective was to strengthen governance and maximise benefits for contributors while protecting workers’ retirement savings, urging the public not to be misled by unverified information circulating online.
