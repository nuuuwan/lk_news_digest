Govt. eyes economic transformation as IMF reviews progress

President  Dissanayake in discussion with a delegation led by IMF Mission Chief Evan Papageorgiou at the Presidential Secretariat yesterday

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake yesterday said the government would seek to convert Sri Lanka’s hard-won economic stability into stronger growth and improved living standards, as the International Monetary Fund (IMF) assessed the country’s progress under its US$ 3 billion bailout programme.

Meeting an IMF delegation led by Mission Chief Evan Papageorgiou at the Presidential Secretariat, the President said recent indicators showed that the economy had moved towards stability, providing a platform for a broader transformation.

“Our objective is to use this stability as a strong foundation to take the country towards a transformative era,” the President was quoted as saying.

He stressed that the gains from the recovery must translate into tangible improvements in household living standards, as higher energy costs and other pressures continue to weigh on the public.

The IMF is in the island nation to conduct the seventh review of Sri Lanka’s Extended Fund Facility (EFF) and its 2026 Article IV Consultation. The mission, which began on September 10, is scheduled to conclude on September 23.

Papageorgiou acknowledged Sri Lanka’s economic progress, including its fiscal discipline, stronger government revenue collection and efforts to attract foreign investment, according to the President’s Media Division.

He also underscored the need to preserve the stability achieved so far as the country moves into 2027 and beyond.

The President said the IMF-supported programme had provided an important foundation for restoring stability following Sri Lanka’s worst economic crisis in decades. He added that the improved economic position had strengthened the country’s ability to absorb the fallout from Cyclone Ditwah and the conflict in the Middle East.

He pointed to the economy’s 4.2 percent year-on-year growth in the second quarter of 2026 as evidence that the recovery remained on track.

However, the latest growth data also showed an uneven rebound, with stronger performances in some productive sectors yet to translate fully into broad improvements in household welfare.

The President reiterated that safeguarding economic stability remained a central priority, citing the government’s tax revenue performance over the past two years.

The IMF delegation also reviewed progress in implementing the country’s social welfare benefit programme, a key commitment under the EFF, while the impact of higher energy costs linked to the Middle East conflict was also discussed.

Central Bank Governor Dr. P. Nandalal Weerasinghe, Finance Ministry Secretary Dr. Harshana Suriyapperuma and Senior Economic Advisor to the President Duminda Hulangamuwa were among the Sri Lankan officials who participated in the discussions.
