Life in place of death? New plan heads to Cabinet

Colombo, Aug. 24 (Daily Mirror) - Despite renewed attention to capital punishment at a high-level anti-narcotics meeting chaired by President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, the government is now preparing to submit a Cabinet paper proposing a formal framework that would include commuting death sentences to life imprisonment, reducing life sentences to 20 years and allowing home leave for eligible prisoners.

A formal framework for granting presidential pardons to prisoners serving death or life sentences is expected to be submitted to the Cabinet shortly, Minister of Justice and National Integration Harshana Nanayakkara said.

The Minister made the remarks during an inspection of the Colombo Remand Prison, New Magazine Prison and Welikada Prison.

He said a committee headed by retired Supreme Court Judge S. Thurairaja completed a 10-month study on establishing a structured procedure for presidential pardons and submitted its report.

According to Nanayakkara, the committee recommended establishing a formal presidential pardon procedure, commuting death sentences to life imprisonment, reducing life sentences to 20 years and introducing home leave for eligible prisoners.

The Minister said the recommendations would be submitted to the Cabinet of Ministers without delay.

Earlier this month, Prison authorities said 806 inmates are currently on death row, including 58 convicted in drug-related cases. Of those convicted in drug-related cases, 52 are male and six are female. One foreign national is also among those on death row.

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake recently highlighted capital punishment during a high-level anti-narcotics meeting, where officials discussed the possibility of imposing the death penalty for major drug offences. However, the government later clarified that no final policy decision or official agreement had been reached to end the country’s long-standing moratorium on executions.

Meanwhile, an integrated mechanism involving key state institutions has been established to strengthen emergency response and improve inmate welfare at the Colombo Remand Prison, New Magazine Prison and Welikada Prison.

The mechanism is aimed at improving coordination among government agencies and ensuring that necessary assistance can be obtained promptly during emergencies within prison premises.

During the inspection of the prisons, the Justice Minister also met male and female remand prisoners affected by delays in Government Analyst reports and assured them that measures had been taken to expedite the release of those reports.

Senior officials from the Department of Prisons, Sri Lanka Police, armed forces, Colombo District Secretariat, health authorities, fire services, the National Water Supply and Drainage Board and other relevant institutions participated in the discussions and inspections.

Meanwhile, Sri Lanka’s prison population has declined gradually this month, falling to 38,464 inmates as of August 23, compared with 39,516 recorded on August 1.

Despite the reduction of more than 1,000 inmates, official figures continue to show a significant disparity between convicted prisoners and those awaiting trial or sentencing. Unconvicted inmates account for more than 72 per cent of the total prison population.

According to the latest figures, 36,449 inmates are male and 2,015 are female.

Of the total prison population, 10,362 are convicted prisoners, comprising 10,101 males and 261 females. Meanwhile, 28,102 inmates remain unconvicted, including 26,348 males and 1,754 females.

The figures also show that 103 convicted prisoners (102 males and one female) were released, while 16 unconvicted prisoners (including 12 males and four females) were released on bail.
