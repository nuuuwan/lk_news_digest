Hakgala to get plant conservation centre, laboratory and new eco-tourism zone

Colombo, Aug. 31 (Daily Mirror) - Plans are underway to establish a plant conservation centre, basic research laboratory and a new eco-tourism zone at Hakgala Botanical Garden, as part of a programme aimed at promoting tourism while protecting the area’s unique montane biodiversity.

A special field observation visit was conducted at the Hakgala Botanical Garden on Thursday (28) under the patronage of Deputy Environment Minister Anton Jayakody to assess the proposed developments.

The visit and subsequent discussions were attended by Environment Ministry Secretary K. R. Uduwawala, the Director General of the Department of National Botanic Gardens and other officials.

Preliminary discussions focused on establishing a plant conservation centre to protect rare and unique species endemic to the Hakgala area, taking into consideration its distinctive geographical characteristics.

Officials also discussed plans to establish a basic laboratory for research on the area’s flora and fauna, together with the preparation of necessary plans and facilities.

A new eco-tourism zone featuring walking trails and footpaths is also proposed to attract both local and foreign visitors while minimising the impact on the natural environment.

The programme further includes the construction of greenhouses to protect identified rare and endemic plant species and the creation of facilities for the public and researchers to study the area’s plant and animal life.

Officials are also considering developing Hakgala as a “zero-emission” zone, enabling visitors to experience the natural environment without activities that could harm the ecosystem.

Deputy Minister Jayakody highlighted that the main objective was to promote sustainable tourism while conserving montane biodiversity for future generations.

He instructed officials to expedite the preparation and implementation of the formal proposal.
