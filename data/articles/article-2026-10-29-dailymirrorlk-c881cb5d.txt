Private movie rooms poses threat to legitimate cinema halls

Colombo, Oct. 29 (Daily Mirror) - The Film Exhibitors Association has raised concerns over the growing number of private “movie rooms” operating in cities across the country, warning that they pose a threat to legitimate cinema halls and raise concerns over regulatory compliance and public morality.

Association President Anuradha B. Rekawa said these establishments rent out rooms by the hour to couples and groups under the pretext of watching films, allegedly operating without proper regulatory oversight.

"What happens in these places is that for a certain amount of money, for an hour or two, a room is given for a couple or two, or other groups, to spend two hours inside a room. The situation is that nobody regulates the activities taking place inside these given rooms, which poses a huge threat to us. If these continue to crop up more and more in every town in the future, it will disrupt our cinema halls from starting to screen movies," he said.

He warned that the continued expansion of such facilities could adversely affect conventional cinemas, which operate under established regulations and contribute taxes to the State.

The Association pointed out that legitimate cinema halls pay taxes amounting to 35 per cent of each ticket issued, while the private movie rooms allegedly operate outside the tax system and do not make payments to the National Film Corporation.

The Association also raised concerns over the lack of supervision at these venues, particularly the potential access of minors to age-restricted content.

Against this backdrop, representatives of the Film Exhibitors Association recently met with officials and the Chairman of the National Film Corporation, urging them to take immediate action against unauthorised movie rooms.

According to the Association, the Chairman has agreed to deploy investigation teams to examine the issue and take appropriate regulatory measures.

Rekawa also stressed that cinema owners remain committed to providing quality entertainment despite seasonal fluctuations in business, adding that measures were needed to protect and sustain legitimate cinema halls across the country.
