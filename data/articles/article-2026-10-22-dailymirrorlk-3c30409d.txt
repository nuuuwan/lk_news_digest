Premium advances Sri Lanka’s pharmaceutical manufacturing ambitions

Premium International Injectable Managing Director and CEO Thushira Raddella in discussions with Health Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa and key stakeholders regarding the progress of the pharmaceutical project and future sector investments

Premium International Injectable (Private) Limited is advancing the development of its pharmaceutical manufacturing facility in Sri Lanka, marking a significant step in the company’s commitment to strengthening local manufacturing capabilities and establishing a foundation for future growth in the country’s pharmaceutical sector.

The state-of-the-art facility, which is being developed to the European standards within a Board of Investment zone, is intended to support the manufacture of biotechnology-based insulin and generic small volume parenteral (SVP) products.

The plant is also geared to receive the EU GMP certification, which is issued by the National Competent Authorities within the European Union member states.

The project reflects the company’s ambition to establish a long-term manufacturing presence in Sri Lanka while exploring opportunities to expand its production capabilities in response to the market requirements.

The visit by Health Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa provided an opportunity to review the facility’s development and highlight the company’s plans for pharmaceutical manufacturing in Sri Lanka. It also provided an opportunity to discuss the role of private-sector investment in developing local manufacturing capabilities and strengthening the country’s pharmaceutical industry.

Premium International Injectable Managing Director and CEO Thushira Raddella said, “Our investment in this facility reflects our long-term commitment to Sri Lanka and our confidence in the opportunities available within the country’s pharmaceutical manufacturing sector. We are focused on progressing the project towards completion and establishing a strong foundation for future growth. Beyond the current facility, we see opportunities to explore additional product strengths, doses and formulations in response to the market requirements, while building strategic relationships that can support the next phase of our development.”

The facility’s planned focus on biotechnology-based insulin and generic SVP products represents an opportunity to develop specialised pharmaceutical manufacturing capabilities locally.

Insulin manufacturing involves specific biotechnology and production requirements, while the SVP products encompass injectable medicines administered in relatively small volumes. Both areas require appropriate manufacturing infrastructure, robust quality systems and compliance with applicable regulatory requirements.
