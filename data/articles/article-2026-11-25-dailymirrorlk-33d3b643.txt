US prepares higher tariffs on China, EU and 14 other nations

Wall Street Journal - The US is preparing to raise tariffs on dozens of countries, including China, for government subsidies and industrial support that Washington argues undercut American manufacturers, according to a report Tuesday by The Wall Street Journal.

People familiar with the plans told the newspaper that US Trade Representative (USTR) Jamieson Greer is expected to unveil the findings of an investigation into industrial subsidies in China, the EU and 14 other nations as soon as this week.

Greer has maintained that state subsidies fuel excess industrial capacity, enabling foreign firms to flood global markets with cheaper goods. The upcoming announcement will likely raise duties on dozens of economies, including Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, India and the EU's 27 member states, while excluding products such as steel, aluminum and automobiles already covered under separate national-security tariffs, said sources.

Although final rates remain unannounced, officials intend to mirror the tariff levels US President Donald Trump set in 2025. Under prior trade agreements, the EU, Japan and South Korea would likely face tariffs of 15% or lower. Vietnam and India could incur higher duties under their respective deals, the sources noted.

China, where Trump visited last week, is expected to receive a steeper tariff than many other targeted economies due to what officials describe as extensive, market-distorting state subsidies.

The new duties would also stack on top of separate 10% to 12.5% forced-labor tariffs unveiled in July for 60 economies.

The USTR declined to comment to the newspaper, and the White House did not respond to requests for comment.

Under Trump, Washington has increasingly used tariffs as a core economic and diplomatic leverage tool to extract trade concessions, counter perceived unfair state subsidies and press foreign governments on broader policy goals.
