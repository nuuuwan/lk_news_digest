US Midterm Polls: From historic firsts to meaningful governance

The 2026 US midterm polls held on November 3 produced a series of historic firsts across the country. Women and candidates in minorities broke longstanding barriers in races for governor and Congress flipping seats in Republican strongholds.

Keisha Lance Bottoms became the first Black woman governor in US history defeating Republican businessman Rick Jackson in Georgia. Former US Interior Secretary Deb Haaland became the first Native American woman to secure a seat. In 2021, she made history as the first Native American woman to serve as a US cabinet secretary. In another historic first, Abdul El-Sayed won the Michigan State race becoming the first Muslim senator while Adam Hamawy became New Jersey’s first Muslim member of Congress. Gina Hinojosa became the first Latina governor in Texas,  defeating Republican incumbent Greg Abbott.

During the 2024 Parliamentary Elections, Ambika Samuel became the first Tamil politician representing the Malaiyaha community to be elected to Parliament. National People’s Power candidate Sugath Wasantha De Silva was elected as the country’s first visually impaired Member of Parliament.

It would be interesting to have members of indigenous communities for instance being represented in Parliament. The 54th New Zealand Parliament included a record 33 Māori MPs. Ahead of their upcoming election on November 7, the spotlight is now focused on seven Māori electorates.

In India,  out of 543 elected seats in Lokh Sabha, 47 seats are strictly reserved for candidates belonging to Scheduled Tribes under Article 330 of the Indian Constitution.

A diverse political landscape ensures that citizens across all communities — regardless of ethnicity, religion, caste, gender, and socioeconomic background — have a seat at the table and a voice in governance. When political systems is truly inclusive, the resulting legislation and policies reflect the genuine needs and lived realities of our whole society.

Embracing Sri Lanka’s pluralistic identity fosters cross-cultural understanding, encouraging communities that rarely interact to,  listen to, learn from, and respect one another’s perspectives. Inclusivity also leads to better decision-making.

The 2024 Parliamentary Election saw a record high of 22 female MPs elected to Parliament. But even though it is a record high, this number remains well below the global average. As of August 1, the global average of women in parliaments is 27.4% across all chambers. Sri Lanka legally mandates a 25% quota for women in local government authorities under the Local Authorities Elections (Amendment) Act No. 16 of 2017. This increased female representation in local councils from about 2% to nearly 23%.

But some of the more difficult questions and challenges arise once these representatives are elected to Parliament. Women face structural challenges when working alongside their male counterparts. Women candidates complain that they are not given a space to speak during meetings.

Many women candidates have been elected by communities living in respective wards. These communities expect their elected representatives to voice concerns on behalf of them. Apart from not being given enough time to speak, female representatives sometimes do not have the opportunity to get budget proposals approved for different projects, and they eventually become unpopular among voters. Unlike in a Parliamentary election, a local council election is more homegrown and it concerns people in respective wards. Therefore,  people are of the view that small scale community projects such as building a community hall or constructing a drainage system, a maternity clinic could be easily approved and fulfilled.

Despite painstaking efforts to convince voters to vote for various candidates, once elected, these candidates may not have the opportunity to walk their talk. Certain first-timers in the current Parliament also faced severe backlash due to their inexperience and lapses in communication.

While inclusivity and diversity in politics matter, one other important aspect is to ensure is that these newcomers and first-timers really walk the talk. Because ultimately, one major quality of becoming a good politician is accountability and that includes being accountable for promises that are being delivered.
