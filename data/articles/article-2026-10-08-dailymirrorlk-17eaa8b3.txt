Breast cancer claims three lives a day, 17 new patients are diagnosed daily

Colombo, Oct. 8 (Daily Mirror) - Breast cancer claims the lives of around three people in Sri Lanka every day, while more than 6,000 new cases are reported annually, according to the National Cancer Control Programme.

Dr. Senaka Talagala, Director of the National Cancer Control Programme, said breast cancer remains the most common cancer among women in Sri Lanka, with more than 6,000 cases detected each year and over 1,000 deaths recorded annually.

He was speaking at a programme on cancer prevention organised by the Colombo Municipal Council.

“Breast cancer is the most common cancer in Sri Lanka, particularly among women. At present, we see more than 6,000 cases being reported annually, while more than 1,000 patients die from the disease,” Dr. Thalagalla said.

“Put simply, around 17 new patients are diagnosed every day, while three people die from the disease,” he said.

Dr. Talagala stressed the importance of early detection, receiving appropriate treatment, regular follow-up and proper care of patients.

However, he said many patients seek medical attention only after the disease has progressed and symptoms have become clearly visible.

“Unfortunately, we see many patients coming to hospitals very late, even after the symptoms have become clearly evident. At that stage, it becomes difficult to achieve good outcomes through treatment,” he added.
