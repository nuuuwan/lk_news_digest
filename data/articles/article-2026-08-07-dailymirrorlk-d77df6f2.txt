Over 271,000 helmet-related cases filed so far this year

Colombo, Aug. 7 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka Police have filed 271,890 cases relating to safety helmet violations during the first seven months of 2026, highlighting continued non-compliance among motorcycle riders.

Director of the Road Safety, Traffic and Control Division of the Sri Lanka Police, Senior Superintendent of Police Manoj Ranagala, said the cases were recorded between January 1 and July 31 this year. In 2025, police had filed 349,823 such cases.

Speaking at a special media briefing held today (7) at the Ministry of Transport Auditorium in Colombo, SSP Ranagala urged motorcyclists to wear helmets primarily to protect their lives rather than simply to avoid police action or legal penalties.

He said 749 people had died in motorcycle accidents between January 1 and July 31 this year, including 13 persons under the age of 18.

According to Ranagala, Sri Lanka recorded 1,626 road accidents during the first seven months of 2026, compared with 1,585 during the same period in 2025.

Chairman of the National Road Safety Council Manjula Kularatne said motorcycle riders must wear standardized and SLS-certified helmets to ensure adequate protection.

He said more than 2,500 people die in road accidents in Sri Lanka each year, with more than 1,000 deaths reportedly linked to motorcycle accidents. Fatal head injuries are among the major causes of death in such accidents.

Kularatne said the Ministry of Transport, Highways and Urban Development has launched a two-year Road Safety Action Plan consisting of 96 measures to reduce road accidents and improve road safety. Thirty of the measures have already been implemented.

He added that the Sri Lanka Standards Institution, Sri Lanka Police and the Consumer Affairs Authority are conducting a joint programme to ensure that helmets available to riders meet required safety standards, with raids being carried out to enforce the relevant regulations.

Consumer Affairs Authority Chairman Hemantha Samarakoon and Director Asela Bandara also participated in the briefing.
