Court acquits accused in 1999 election-period bombing

Colombo, Oct. 2 (Daily Mirror) - The Anuradhapura High Court acquitted the accused, who faced several charges in connection with the bombing of the residence of Ananda Weragoda, an Anuradhapura Food and Medicine Inspector, during the 1999 Presidential election period, citing contradictions in the prosecution evidence.

The case concerned an attack on Ananda Weragoda’s residence, in which property damage estimated at more than Rs. 3 million was caused. At the time, Weragoda was also serving as an officer involved in promoting the United National Party (UNP) membership in the Anuradhapura District.

The accused included the Mayor of Anuradhapura, a former Chairman of the North Central Provincial Council, the Deputy Chairman of the Rajanganaya Pradeshiya Sabha and two others.

A significant issue that arose during the trial was the evidence relating to the identification of the alleged perpetrators.

According to the evidence presented before Court, the complainant maintained that he had identified the accused persons in connection with the incident. However, another prosecution witness who was said to have been an eyewitness gave materially different evidence regarding identification.

The eyewitness stated that he was unable to identify the persons who had carried out the attack, as the alleged perpetrators had covered their faces with black cloths.

The differing accounts given by the prosecution witnesses created a significant contradiction in the evidence concerning the identification of the accused. While one witness claimed that the accused had been identified, another eyewitness testified that identification was not possible because the assailants' faces had been concealed.

The High Court, having considered the evidence placed before it and the charges against the accused, acquitted them of the several charges relating to the incident.

The case therefore ended with the accused being acquitted after the prosecution evidence, particularly the evidence concerning identification, was subjected to scrutiny before the High Court.

Senior Counsel Darshana Kuruppu, together with Counsel Pramuditha Senevirathne and Manoj Gunawardana  appeared for the accused.
