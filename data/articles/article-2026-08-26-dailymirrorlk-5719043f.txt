Union urges railways to withdraw legal action against SLRSMU Chief Secretary

Colombo, Aug. 26 (Daily Mirror) - The Joint Development Officers’ Union has urged the General Manager of Sri Lanka Railways to immediately withdraw the decision to initiate legal action against the Chief Secretary of the Sri Lanka Railways Station Masters’ Union (SLRSMU) for publicly highlighting shortcomings in the administration of the Railway Department.

Meanwhile, the SLRSMU has called for an urgent investigation into the recent breakdowns of locomotive engines, alleging that the current Railway General Manager should also be among those scrutinised over the incidents.

SLRSMU General Secretary Kasun Chamara, speaking to the media, said a proper investigation would establish responsibility for the repeated locomotive failures. He alleged that the Railway General Manager was responsible for the current situation.

Addressing the media, the Joint Development Officers’ Union Secretary said a letter had reportedly been issued seeking legal action against the SLRSMU Chief Secretary nearly one year after the General Manager assumed office.

He stressed that trade unions have the right to express their views on matters concerning their services and issues faced by their members.

“This is something the General Manager, Ministers, Secretaries, Deputy Ministers and all senior management officials should understand,” he said.

The union secretary said the General Manager had held several discussions with trade unions regarding their grievances, but claimed that the issues raised by the unions had not been properly resolved.

He alleged that the move to initiate legal action against a trade union official could be seen as an attempt to suppress trade union activities.

“Although these issues have been discussed at various levels, the real problems have not been resolved,” he said.

He warned that all trade unions could resort to trade union action if the General Manager failed to withdraw the letter seeking legal action against the SLRSMU Chief Secretary.

The unions urged the Railway Department administration to resolve the matter through discussion and address the operational issues affecting the railway service, rather than taking action that could further escalate tensions between railway management and trade unions.
