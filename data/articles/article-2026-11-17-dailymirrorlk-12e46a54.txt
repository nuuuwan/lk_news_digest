Public Roads and Private ‘No Parking’ Boards

I am writing about a growing practice on many residential roads in Colombo and the suburbs.  Almost every house now puts a “No Parking” board in front of its gate, on the pavement.

Ballant violation is seen in houses next to Stafford International Schòol just opposite Kurunduwatta.Police Station.

I understand the concern. No one should park a vehicle  blocking a householder from taking his own vehicle out. But we must ask a simple question: Who owns the road?

The road and the pavement are public property. They do not belong to the householder. A private board put up by a householder is not a Police notice. It is not a Municipal Council notice. It has no legal force. There is also a clear difference between parking and waiting. If a driver leaves his vehicle and walks away, that is parking.

If the driver is seated at the wheel, ready to move at once, that is waiting. He is not parking. He can be asked to move, but he cannot be told that a private board makes waiting an offence.

What is wrong is obstruction. No driver, whether waiting or parked, should block a gate so that the householder cannot enter or leave. That is the real offence. I would suggest that the Police and the Municipal Councils make the rule clear: 1. Only an official sign placed by the authorities can create a No Parking zone. 2. A private board can be a request, not an order.

3. No vehicle should block access to a gate, whether the driver is present or not.
