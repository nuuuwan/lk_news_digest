Supreme Court determines Clause 17 of Anti-Corruption (Amendment) Bill requires referendum

Colombo, Sep. 22 (Daily Mirror) - Speaker Jagath Wickramarathne informed Parliament today that the Supreme Court has determined that Clause 17 of the “Anti-Corruption (Amendment)” Bill is inconsistent with Article 4(c) of the Constitution and is required to be passed with the Special Majority required under Article 84(2) and approved by the People at a referendum in terms of Article 83 of the Constitution.

The Speaker also announced the Court’s determinations on several other provisions of the Bill.

Clauses 3, 4, 6(4), and 6(5) are not inconsistent with the Constitution and may therefore be passed by a Simple Majority in Parliament.

However, Clause 6(7) was determined to be inconsistent with Articles 12(1) and 14(1)(a) of the Constitution. The provision may therefore be validly passed only with the Special Majority stipulated under Article 84(2).

The Supreme Court also determined that Clause 7 is inconsistent with Article 12(1). As a result, the proposed repeal of Section 81(e) should be withdrawn.

If Clause 7 is retained in its present form, it may be validly passed only with the Special Majority prescribed under Article 84(2), read together with Article 82(5) of the Constitution.

Meanwhile, Clause 11(1), which proposes the introduction of Section 88(1)(f), was determined not to be inconsistent with Articles 12(1) or 14A(1) of the Constitution and may be passed with a Simple Majority.

The Court also determined that Clause 11(2), as proposed to be amended at the Committee Stage, is not inconsistent with Articles 10, 12(1), 14(1)(a), or 14A of the Constitution and may likewise be passed with a Simple Majority in Parliament.
