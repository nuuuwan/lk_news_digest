Trade deficit widens 69% to US$ 6.5bn

Import expenditure increased 25.8% YoY to US$ 14.64bn during January-July

Fuel remained a major source of import pressure, with expenditure surging 68% YoY

The merchandise trade deficit widened by nearly 69 percent to US$ 6.51 billion in the first seven months of 2026, as a sharp increase in imports outpaced export growth, Central Bank data showed.

The deficit stood at US$ 3.85 billion in the corresponding period of 2025.

Import expenditure increased 25.8 percent year-on-year to US$ 14.64 billion during January-July, driven partly by higher spending on fuel and motor vehicles. In contrast, export earnings rose only 4.4 percent to US$ 8.14 billion.

The deterioration was more pronounced in July, when the monthly trade deficit nearly doubled to US$ 1.02 billion from US$ 580 million a year earlier.

Imports increased 19.6 percent year-on-year to US$ 2.25 billion during the month, while exports fell 5.3 percent to US$ 1.23 billion.

Fuel remained a major source of import pressure, with expenditure surging 68 percent year-on-year to US$ 453 million in July, largely due to higher crude oil costs.

The seven-month fuel import bill rose 59.9 percent to US$ 3.62 billion, reflecting the impact of developments in the Middle East on global energy prices. Motor vehicle imports, which resumed following the easing of import restrictions, cost the country US$ 241 million in July. Cumulative expenditure on personal and commercial vehicle imports reached US$ 1.50 billion during the first seven months. The Central Bank said Sri Lanka’s terms of trade also deteriorated in July and during the seven-month period, as import prices increased faster than export prices.

The widening merchandise deficit contributed to a US$ 142 million current-account deficit in July, marking the fourth consecutive month in which the external current account remained in the red.

As a result, the country recorded a cumulative current-account deficit of US$ 387 million during January-July, reversing a US$ 1.67 billion surplus in the same period last year.

Stronger workers’ remittances provided some relief, rising 21.4 percent to US$ 5.38 billion during the seven-month period. Remittances increased 11.5 percent year-on-year to US$ 778 million in July.

However, the services-account surplus contracted 22.4 percent to US$ 1.84 billion during January-July, partly due to weaker tourism earnings and higher spending on overseas travel and transport services.

Tourism earnings fell 11.5 percent to US$ 1.80 billion, while overseas travel expenditure surged 72.7 percent to US$ 737.5 million.

Gross official reserves, including the swap facility with the People’s Bank of China, stood at US$ 6.6 billion at end-July, supported by foreign exchange purchases by the Central Bank.
