Vehicle registrations jump18.3% in September

Sri Lanka’s vehicle registrations rose 18.3 percent in September, with two-wheelers and three-wheelers driving nearly four-fifths of the monthly increase as smaller vehicles continued to dominate the market.

Total registrations reached 54,214 units, up from 45,811 in August, according to vehicle registration analysis by J B Securities.

Most major categories recorded increases, although overall volumes remained below levels seen earlier in 2026.

Two-wheeler registrations increased to 38,747 units from 33,498, while three-wheelers rose 37.3 percent to 5,375 units from 3,914. Together, the two categories accounted for just over 81 percent of September registrations. Motor car registrations rose 30.9 percent to 3,414 units from 2,609, led by a recovery in pre-owned vehicles. Pre-owned car registrations increased to 2,729 units from 1,978, while brand-new cars edged up to 685 units from 631.

The figures showed the continued dominance of smaller cars. Vehicles classified in the report as having engine capacities below 1,000 cc accounted for 96.5 percent of brand-new car registrations and 98.1 percent of pre-owned registrations.

Suzuki led the pre-owned car segment with 946 units, including 770 WagonR. Toyota followed with 683 units, led by the Yaris and Roomy, while Daihatsu recorded 542 units and Nissan 405.

BYD led brand-new car registrations with 338 units, including 316 ATTO 1s. BAW recorded 67 E7s, while Perodua registered 48 vehicles.

SUV and crossover registrations increased 22.2 percent to 3,941 units from 3,226. Toyota led with 1,357 units, including 1,032 Raizes, followed by Honda with 753 units, predominantly Vezels. Suzuki recorded 394 units, including 385 Fronx models.

Electric vehicle registrations rose 19 percent to 6,048 units from 5,084, although electric two-wheelers accounted for 5,030 units, or about 83 percent of the total. Electric motor cars contributed 495 registrations, with BYD accounting for 338.

Hybrid registrations increased to 2,168 units from 1,942, with SUVs and crossovers accounting for 2,117 units.

In the two-wheeler market, TVS remained ahead of Bajaj for a second consecutive month, recording 8,684 registrations against Bajaj’s 8,035. Honda followed with 7,673 units. Bajaj continued to lead brand-new motorcycle registrations, while Honda led the scooter segment.

Financing accounted for 54.4 percent of two-wheeler registrations, compared with 47 percent for motor cars and 43.9 percent for SUVs and crossovers.

Commercial vehicle registrations also largely improved. Pickups rose to 361 units from 317, mini-trucks to 246 from 190 and medium trucks to 386 from 336. Heavy trucks and buses moved against the broader trend, declining to 218 and 235 units, respectively.

Large-tractor registrations more than doubled to 273 units from 136, with financing accounting for 78.8 percent of the category.

Locally assembled vehicle registrations increased to approximately 4,999 units from around 4,324, dominated by two-wheelers.
