Government to strengthen electric fences to tackle human-elephant conflict

Colombo, Oct. 28 (Daily Mirror) - The government will take steps to strengthen electric fences, address land issues affecting farmers and improve wildlife protection facilities to tackle the human-elephant conflict, Minister of Public Administration, Provincial Councils and Local Government Prof. Chandana Abayarathna said.

He was speaking at the Thenuwara Divisional Council meeting held on October 26 under his patronage. The Minister is also the Chairman of the Puttalam District Coordinating Committee.

Village representatives told the meeting that residents living near wildlife reserves were severely affected by the human-elephant conflict, which had threatened farming activities and public safety.

They said elephants frequently entered villages due to the lack of an adequate forest corridor connecting two reserves situated about 2.5 kilometres apart. The failure to properly clear government-owned and forest conservation lands within the village had also contributed to the problem, they said.

Farmers were finding it increasingly difficult to protect their paddy fields and other crops, while elephants entering areas near schools and temples had created serious safety concerns.

The villagers called for the strengthening of the 3.5-kilometre electric fence extending from the Weerakkodicholle reserve, the construction of a new electric fence from the Sellakanda reserve and the introduction of a proper patrol service using existing guard huts.

Responding to these concerns, Prof. Abayarathna said steps would be taken to expedite the necessary measures. He said 5,000 personnel who had served in the Civil Security Force had been attached to the Department of Wildlife Conservation to strengthen field operations.

Transport facilities, including cab trucks and motorcycles, had also been provided to improve the efficiency of wildlife officers, he said.

The Minister further said President Anura Kumara Dissanayake had emphasised the importance of constructing durable and effective electric fences, with priority given to their long-term benefits rather than merely their cost.

Attention was also being given to restoring tanks associated with forest areas, including the construction of the Mahasen Tank, to ensure water availability for wildlife and reduce elephant incursions into villages in search of water.

On land-related concerns, the Minister said a suitable programme would be formulated in consultation with the Divisional Secretary and Grama Niladhari after strengthening the electric fence. The programme would examine the possibility of allocating suitable land bordering the village to newly married young families.

However, he stressed that land allocation should be carried out while safeguarding wildlife habitats and supporting the government's objective of increasing the country's forest cover to 30 per cent.

The meeting also discussed measures to strengthen the rural economy by helping farmers adopt modern agricultural practices and develop income-generating activities on limited land.

Prof. Abayarathna said farmers could improve their livelihoods through the cultivation of bitter gourd, mushrooms, passion fruit and big onions, as well as poultry farming, goat rearing, dairy production, floriculture and dressmaking.

He emphasised that the government's objective was to strengthen rural communities through productive agriculture, modern technology and self-employment opportunities rather than continued dependence on subsidies.

The Minister said practical solutions would be pursued by balancing environmental conservation with improvements in the living standards of rural communities.

Karuwalagaswewa Pradeshiya Sabha Chairman Neil Navarathna, provincial council members, divisional council representatives and residents of Thenuwara attended the meeting.
