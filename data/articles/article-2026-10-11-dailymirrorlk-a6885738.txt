Rs. 20 million fine, imprisonment for hazardous waste offences: PM

Colombo, Oct. 11 (Daily Mirror) - The National Environmental Amendment Act has been passed in Parliament, strengthening the law to impose fines of up to Rs. 20 million and imprisonment on those who dispose of hazardous waste in the environment, Prime Minister Harini Amarasuriya said.

The Prime Minister made these remarks while attending the 36th anniversary celebrations of the National Namal Uyana and the distribution of school supplies to more than 1,500 schoolchildren at the site on October 10.

The school supplies were provided with assistance from South Korea. The event began with the Prime Minister planting a tree to mark the anniversary, followed by the distribution of school supplies and a tribute to local and foreign individuals, including journalists, who have contributed to protecting Namal Uyana.

A memorandum of understanding was also signed between Namal Uyana and South Korea.

Addressing the gathering, the Prime Minister appreciated the efforts of Ven. Wanawasi Rahula Thera and those who have worked to preserve Namal Uyana, known for its rose-coloured quartz mountain range and forest ecosystem.

She said initiatives that support children's education should be valued, stressing that a country's most important investment was not in buildings but in the education and character development of its children.

The Prime Minister said the ongoing education reforms aimed to develop children who could think independently, appreciate the environment and grow into well-rounded individuals with strong human values, rather than focus solely on examinations.

She also emphasised that children had a responsibility to protect natural environments such as Namal Uyana, which serve as living classrooms.

Ven. Rahula Thera said he was neither the owner nor the guardian of Namal Uyana, but only its custodian. He also said he had worked independently of politicians and welcomed President Anura Kumara Dissanayake's decision to declare Namal Uyana a national heritage site for the first time.

Health and Mass Media Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa said Namal Uyana and Ven. Rahula Thera were inseparable, noting that the monk's 36-year struggle had contributed to the site's declaration as a national heritage site under the patronage of the President in March.

He said Namal Uyana played an important role in environmental conservation, improving the livelihoods of local residents and promoting tourism.

Minister of Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Cooperative Development Wasantha Samarasinghe, Anuradhapura District National People's Power MP Thilina Samarakoon, Palagala Pradeshiya Sabha Chairman Karunaratne Bandara, Anuradhapura District Secretary Ranjith Wimalasuriya and several other officials, Korean honorary award recipients and schoolchildren attended the event.
