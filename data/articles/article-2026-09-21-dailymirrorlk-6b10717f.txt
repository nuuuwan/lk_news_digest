Govt considers allowing private firms to set fuel prices

Colombo, Sep. 21 (Daily Mirror) - The government is examining legal options to allow private fuel distributors to set their own prices within a specified upper and lower range, Energy Minister Anura Karunathilaka said.

He said the move is being considered as several private fuel distribution companies have raised concerns over heavy losses under the existing pricing structure, leading to restrictions on diesel supplies.

Three private fuel companies in Sri Lanka—Lanka IOC (LIOC), Sinopec, and RM Parks—have restricted diesel distribution by about 50% due to severe financial losses caused by surging global oil costs and the removal of the government diesel subsidy. The operators are lobbying the Ministry of Power and Energy for an immediate price revision to prevent potential station closures.

The Minister said the government will also conduct its monthly fuel price review at the end of September, based on fuel import costs.

The Energy Minister said the Middle East conflict has pushed global fuel prices higher after a period of lower prices that allowed Sri Lanka to pass some savings on to consumers.

However, he said that fuel prices are already significantly high and said discussions are underway on whether consumers can bear any further price increase.

“We review fuel prices based on import costs and expect to conduct the next review at the end of September. However, fuel prices are already significantly high, and the government is discussing whether consumers can bear any further increases.

Several private fuel distributors have also said they cannot absorb the losses under the current pricing structure, resulting in restricted diesel supplies. We are therefore examining legal options to allow these companies to set prices within a specified upper and lower range,” the Minister said.
