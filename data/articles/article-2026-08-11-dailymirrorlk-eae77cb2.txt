Two arrested with protected animal parts in Kuruwita raid

Colombo, Aug. 11 (Daily Mirror) - Two suspects have been arrested with a large quantity of highly protected animal parts during a surprise raid in the Paradise area of the Kuruwita Police Division yesterday (10), based on intelligence provided by the Sri Lanka Air Force.

Wildlife officers, together with officers of the Police Special Task Force (STF), conducted the raid and recovered several animal parts, including three antlers, four deer antlers, an ivory pendant, an ivory ring, two two-inch-long ivory sticks, an ivory crystal casket, seven elephant tooth fragments, an antler-handled sword, part of a fox skin and a piece of musk.

According to wildlife officials, investigations have revealed that the suspects were allegedly in possession of the animal parts for the purpose of selling them.

The suspects, aged 40 and 31, are residents of the Kuruwita area. They, along with the recovered items, were handed over to the Provincial Wildlife Office and are due to be produced before the Ratnapura Magistrate's Court today (11).

Authorities said the possession, transportation, exhibition and trade of parts of strictly protected and endangered animals are prohibited under the Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance.

The Department of Wildlife Conservation has urged the public to provide information on individuals involved in the illegal possession or trade of protected wildlife parts by calling the 1992 emergency hotline.

The raid was conducted jointly by officers of the Ritigala Wildlife Office, Kuruwita STF, the Kurunegala Assistant Director's Office, Bulathkohupitiya and Provincial Wildlife Offices, following the Air Force intelligence information.
