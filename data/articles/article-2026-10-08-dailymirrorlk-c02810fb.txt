Geopolitical tensions drive financial sector risk concerns

The global economic threats have moved to the forefront of Sri Lanka’s financial sector concerns, even as confidence in the country’s financial system has strengthened, according to a Central Bank survey.

The second-half 2026 Systemic Risk Survey showed that the global macroeconomic risks accounted for 26 percent of the identified risks, up sharply from 14 percent in the previous round, emerging as the largest risk category.

The Central Bank said the increase largely reflected the heightened concerns over the geopolitical tensions, potential spillovers from other countries and uncertainty surrounding the global economic outlook.

However, the respondents’ confidence in financial system stability improved over both the next year and next three years, suggesting that a stronger sentiment towards the system was accompanied by a greater caution over the external developments.

The survey also found that the respondents perceived a slightly higher probability of a high-impact negative event affecting the financial stability over the next year. Their assessment of the probability over the next three years declined slightly compared with the previous round.

The findings placed the external uncertainty at the centre of the financial sector’s risk outlook, while several domestic risk categories accounted for smaller shares of concerns.

The fiscal and sovereign-related risks declined to 12.3 percent of identified risks, from 15.4 percent in the first-half survey. The general domestic macroeconomic risks eased to 16.6 percent, from 17.6 percent.

Together, the two categories accounted for 28.9 percent, down from 33 percent in the previous round. The survey summary did not specify the factors behind the decline.

The risks related to the financial institutions also fell, accounting for 14.1 percent, compared with 19.4 percent previously. The financial market risks declined to 14 percent, from 15.7 percent.

The financial infrastructure concerns, however, gained prominence, with their share increasing to 12.5 percent, from 9.3 percent. The summary did not detail which infrastructure risks drove the increase.

The survey was conducted between July 17 and August 14, with all 158 institutions in the sample participating. The respondents included risk officers from the licensed banks, finance companies, insurers, primary dealers, investment firms, stockbrokers and financial infrastructure providers.

Conducted twice a year, the survey monitors the financial market participants’ perceptions of risks to Sri Lanka’s financial system and their confidence in its stability.
