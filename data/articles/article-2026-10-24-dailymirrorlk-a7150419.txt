Eight-hour bus ride for blockbuster puts Jaffna on PVR’s map

For some filmgoers in Jaffna, a Tamil blockbuster means an eight- to nine-hour bus journey to Colombo, a weekend away and, sometimes, a second viewing before heading home.

Those trips have caught the attention of PVR, helping steer the cinema operator’s search for its next Sri Lankan locations towards the north.

PVR Lanka Director Renaud Palliere told Mirror Business that bus operators were buying tickets to its One Galle Face cinema and selling them as part of transport packages to customers in Jaffna.

“They come here for the weekend, see the movie once, sometimes twice, and then come back,” he said.

What began as an unexpected customer pattern has become a business signal for an operator considering expansion beyond its sole Colombo property.

The journeys have also supported a small transport trade around major releases, with operators packaging the bus trip and cinema admission into a single purchase.

For PVR, the significance lies in the commitment: customers are spending both time and money to reach an experience unavailable to them closer to home.

Palliere said the pattern had prompted the company to explore Jaffna as a potential market. Tamil films lead the language categories at its Colombo box office, adding weight to the northern opportunity.

Reaching that audience locally would involve a different offering. PVR is considering smaller cinemas, with four to five screens and pricing tailored to individual markets, as part of its wider expansion strategy.

A Jaffna cinema remains a possibility rather than a confirmed project. The customers making the long journey south have nevertheless given PVR a reason to look north.
