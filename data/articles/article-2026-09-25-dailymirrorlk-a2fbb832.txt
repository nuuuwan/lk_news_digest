20 more forests to be gazetted as reserves tomorrow

Colombo, Sep. 25 (Daily Mirror) - Twenty more forests covering nearly 9,538 hectares in six districts are scheduled to be declared forest reserves tomorrow (26) under the Government’s ‘Vanaspathi’ national programme aimed at protecting Sri Lanka’s forest ecosystems for future generations.

Environment Minister Dr. Dammika Patabendi is scheduled to sign the relevant Gazette notifications during a field programme at the Sadathenna Forestry College in Nuwara Eliya.

Deputy Minister Anton Jayakody, Conservator General of Forests S. C. Palamakumbura, the Nuwara Eliya District Secretary and other government officials are expected to participate in the event.

The 20 forests to be declared reserves are located in Nuwara Eliya, Kegalle, Ampara, Badulla, Vavuniya and Anuradhapura districts.

According to the Ministry of Environment, 93 forests have so far been gazetted as reserves by the present Government in collaboration with the Department of Forest Conservation. With the addition of the 20 forests tomorrow, the total extent of forests protected under the programme is expected to reach nearly 65,000 hectares.

The ‘Vanaspathi’ national programme was launched by the Ministry of Environment on World Environment Day in 2025 to safeguard Sri Lanka’s forest resources and biodiversity for future generations.

The programme is being implemented through the Forest Conservation Department and is aimed at legally protecting environmentally sensitive forest areas as part of the Government’s commitment to increasing Sri Lanka’s total forest cover to 32% by 2032.

The Ministry said the programme had resulted in the protection of 86 forest reserves between its launch in 2025 and World Environment Day 2026, making it the largest number of forests protected in a single year in Sri Lankan history.

The protected forests include tropical moist evergreen forests, montane and submontane forests, tropical dry mixed evergreen forests, intermediate-zone semi-evergreen forests, and coastal and mangrove forests.

The Ministry said ‘Vanaspathi’ goes beyond tree planting and represents a long-term, science-based approach to ecosystem management involving the protection, restoration and sustainable management of forest systems, with the participation of local communities.
