Pakistan’s first astronaut goes to space aboard Chinese rocket

China’s Shenzhou-24 spacecraft successfully reached orbit today (November 25), carrying Pakistani Air Force pilot Muhammad Zeeshan Ali alongside two Chinese astronauts, marking a historic milestone in China-Pakistan space cooperation.

The spacecraft lifted off aboard a Long March-2F rocket at 8.18 a.m. Beijing time (12.18 a.m. GMT) from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre in northwestern China.

The three-member crew comprises Chinese astronaut Tang Hongbo, who serves as mission commander, Chinese flight engineer Wu Yi and Pakistani astronaut Ali, who is participating as a payload specialist.

The successful launch makes Ali the first Pakistani national to travel into Earth's orbit and the first foreign astronaut to participate in a crewed mission to China's Tiangong space station.

Following the launch, the spacecraft separated from its carrier rocket and entered its planned orbit. Chinese state media reported that all three crew members were in good condition.

The spacecraft is now heading towards the Tiangong space station, where Ali is expected to become the first foreign national to enter the Chinese orbital facility following docking.

During his stay, Ali will participate in scientific experiments alongside the Chinese crew, including research involving biotechnology and photosynthetic microorganisms. The mission is expected to last around a week, after which he will return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft.

The mission follows a space cooperation agreement signed between China and Pakistan in February 2025, under which Pakistani astronaut candidates were selected for training in China.
