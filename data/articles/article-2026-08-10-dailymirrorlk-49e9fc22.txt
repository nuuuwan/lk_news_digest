Sri Lanka considers death penalty for drug-related offences

Colombo, Aug. 10 (Daily Mirror) - Attention has been drawn to the possibility of imposing the death penalty for drug-related offences during a meeting of the “Ratama Ekata” (Country Together) National Operation Council held at the Presidential Secretariat on Monday (10).

The meeting was chaired by President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, with discussions focusing on the escalating drug crisis and its impact on Sri Lanka’s economy and society, according to the President’s Media Division (PMD).

Officials noted that the illicit wealth generated through drug trafficking has contributed to the growth of a criminal ecosystem posing a serious threat to the national economy, tourism industry and social values.

The council discussed the urgent need for stronger laws and measures to deter drug traffickers and other criminals. President Dissanayake stressed that addressing the drug menace, which continues to harm society and threaten future generations, requires immediate practical action and a change in public attitudes.

He also called for the active involvement of religious leaders, parents and community organizations in supporting efforts to combat drug abuse and trafficking.

Meanwhile, amendments to laws relating to drug control and bail procedures are currently being finalized and are expected to be gazetted and presented to Parliament shortly.

The meeting also reviewed progress in clearing delays caused by pending Government Analyst reports. A total of 6,414 reports were issued during July, officials said.

The President directed authorities to categorize drug-related cases according to the quantity of drugs seized, including cases involving less than five grams and less than 10 grams, in order to expedite legal proceedings.

He also instructed the Police, Prisons Department and Government Analyst’s Department to improve coordination so that suspects can be produced before courts promptly once the relevant analytical reports are available.

Steps are also being taken to expand the capacity of the Government Analyst’s Department, including obtaining assistance from the Sri Lanka Navy to clear existing backlogs.

In addition, President Dissanayake instructed officials to allocate the necessary funds for the installation of CCTV camera systems to strengthen law enforcement and monitoring efforts.
