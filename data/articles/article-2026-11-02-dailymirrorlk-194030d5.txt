Health experts warn of disease outbreaks as floodwaters recede

Colombo, Nov. 2 (Daily Mirror) - Health experts have warned of a possible rise in waterborne and other infectious diseases over the next two weeks as floodwaters recede in several areas affected by minor flooding amid continued rainy weather.

Speaking to the Daily Mirror, Senior Consultant Physician Dr Ananda Wijewickrama said diseases such as leptospirosis, hepatitis A, typhoid and diarrhoeal illnesses could spread after flooding due to contaminated water sources.

He also warned that dengue remains a significant health threat during the rainy season, as the prevailing conditions could create favourable breeding grounds for mosquitoes.

Dr Wijewickrama said leptospirosis, commonly known as rat fever, was another major concern even following minor flooding.

“Minor flooded areas can become contaminated with the urine of infected animals, leading to the spread of leptospirosis,” he said.

He added that contamination of drinking water sources during the monsoon season could also lead to an increase in diarrhoeal diseases.

Although the effects may not be immediately apparent, he warned that such illnesses could emerge over the next two weeks as floodwaters recede.

Dr Wijewickrama stressed the importance of taking preventive measures, including ensuring access to safe drinking water, maintaining proper sanitation and practising good personal hygiene.

He also advised the public to avoid contact with muddy floodwater and consume only boiled or properly treated drinking water to minimise the risk of infection.

According to the National Dengue Surveillance Unit, Sri Lanka had recorded 105,495 dengue cases as of midnight on October 27, with 81 deaths reported so far this year.

Gampaha District recorded the highest number of cases at 22,122, accounting for 20.97% of the total, followed by Colombo with 20,478 cases (19.41%) and Kandy with 8,440 cases (8.00%).

The Western Province accounted for the highest share of cases, with 54,866 (52.01%), followed by the Southern Province with 14,969 (14.19%) and the Central Province with 10,603 (10.05%).
