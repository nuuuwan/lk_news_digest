“How can money paid under an agreement be considered a bribe?”- defense questions

Colombo, Oct. 7 (Daily Mirror) - Defence counsel opposed the prosecution’s requests, with Ali Sabry PC, appearing for Namal Rajapaksa, arguing that the payments in question had been made under sponsorship agreements and questioned how such payments could be considered bribes.

Counsel Jeevantha Jayatilleke, appearing for the first suspect Amit Kattayar, submitted that his client had not ignored the investigation and was willing to provide a statement.

Former Krrish company director Janaki Siriwardena and Parliamentarian Namal Rajapaksa, who are accused in connection with the alleged Krrish deal, were further remanded till October 16 by Colombo Chief Magistrate Asanga S. Bodaragama today.

When the case was called, the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption filed a further report and informed Court of the progress of its investigations.

An investigating officer of CIABOC told Court that a statement had been recorded from an accountant of Krrish Transworks Corporation (Pvt) Ltd.

According to the officer, the accountant had stated that payments had been made to an account belonging to a person named Nimal Perera since 2012 and that the payments had been made outside the company’s normal procedure.

The officer said the statement had further revealed that the money had been credited to the account upon obtaining only the approval of the company’s Board of Directors.

The accountant had also stated that he did not know the reason for making payments to the relevant account.

The investigating officer further told Court that Nimal Perera had neither supplied any goods or services to the company nor worked as an employee of the company.

A statement had also been recorded from an executive officer of the Super Branch of Sampath Bank, the officer said.

According to the investigation, an account under the name of Ceylon Premier Sports Ltd. had been maintained at the bank. The company had been established under the leadership of Namal Rajapaksa, although he had not held any official position in the company. Nimal Perera had served as its chairman, the officer told Court.

The CIABOC officer further informed Court that several notices had been sent to the first suspect Indian national Amit Kattayar and second suspect Rajendra Prasad Gupta, requesting them to appear and provide statements in connection with the investigation.

However, the officer said the two suspects had not responded to the notices.

CIABOC therefore requested Court to issue warrants to obtain their statements, adding that it expected to take steps to obtain international Red Notices against them as a further measure.

The investigating officer further told Court that on December 15, 2012, fourth suspect Namal Rajapaksa had sent a letter to the Chief Executive Officer of Krrish Transworks (Pvt) Ltd thanking Nimal Perera for providing funds for the Carlton Super 7 Rugby Tournament.

The officer said the letter had already been produced before the High Court in a case being heard there and that the Commission intended to examine the handwriting of the fourth suspect.

The officer said Namal Rajapaksa had been produced before Court under Section 149 of the Anti-Corruption Act, together with a certificate issued by the Director of the Commission.

As further investigations were being conducted, CIABOC requested that the suspect be further remanded.

Meanwhile, Ali Sabry PC and Counsel Amila Dissanayake, appearing for Namal Rajapaksa and Janaki Siriwardena respectively, submitted written submissions to Court in support of the bail applications made on behalf of their clients on the previous date.

Appearing for Namal Rajapaksa, Ali Sabry PC submitted that the High Court case clearly established that the payments in question had been made in connection with sponsorship agreements.

“How can money given under an agreement be considered a bribe? I also raised this issue in the bail submissions made on the previous date,” he submitted.

Meanwhile, Counsel Jeevantha Jayatilleke, appearing for first suspect Amit Kattayar, requested Court not to issue a warrant against his client.

He submitted that his client had been summoned to appear before the Commission on August 17, 2026, but the summons had only been received on August 22.

He said his client had responded by sending a letter to the Commission on August 24.

“My client is unable to travel by air. He is currently in New Delhi and is ill. However, he is prepared to give a statement if an investigating officer travels to New Delhi. Officers had also travelled there in 2016 and recorded a statement from him. He is willing to cooperate with the investigation. Therefore, I respectfully request that a warrant not be issued against him at this stage,” Counsel submitted.

After considering the submissions, the Colombo Chief Magistrate ordered the prosecution to inform Court of its position regarding the bail applications on October 12.

The Magistrate fixed October 16 for the pronouncement of the Order on bail.
