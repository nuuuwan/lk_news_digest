September arrivals edge back into growth as Japan, Australia gain ground

Island welcomed 143,623 tourists in first 27 days of September, up 0.4% when compared to same period last year

Holidaymakers from Japan and Australia have already outnumbered their full September 2025 totals, while arrivals from Germany, Spain and Bangladesh trail well behind

Sri Lanka’s tourist arrivals rose 0.4 percent in the first 27 days of September when compared to the same period last year, as a strong growth from Japan and Australia put the month on course to end three consecutive months of decline, as per the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority data.

The island welcomed 143,623 tourists between September 01 and 27, against 143,085 over the same days last year.

To surpass last September’s 158,971, it needs a further 15,348 arrivals over the final three days, fewer than the 15,886 it welcomed over the same three days in 2025. That would mark its first monthly increase since May.

Japan sent 5,187 holidaymakers, already exceeding last September’s full-month total of 4,137 and rose to sixth from 10th among the source markets.

Australia likewise surpassed last September’s 9,105, with 10,067 arrivals, climbing to third from fifth, following the launch of Jetstar’s direct low-fare Melbourne-Colombo service on August 25. China moved up to second, with 10,081 arrivals.

However, Germany recorded 6,745 arrivals, against 9,344 over the whole of September last year, while Spain drew 3,163, against 4,841 and Bangladesh 3,923, against 4,953.

The Netherlands, ninth last September with 4,433 arrivals, dropped out of the top 10. The cumulative arrivals of 1,678,745 by September 27 were 30,863 or 1.8 percent, lower when compared to the same period last year.  Tourist arrivals grew 12.9 percent in January and February but have declined 7.8 percent since March, as the West Asia conflict disrupted air connectivity to the island.

The Gulf airspace closures forced the cancellation of 205 flights to and from Sri Lanka in the conflict’s first days, while the flights to Europe were rerouted to bypass the Middle Eastern airspace.

The Indian arrivals are already 14.9 percent above the first nine months of last year, at 431,162, whereas the Russian arrivals are 32.3 percent lower, at 82,733.

Meanwhile, air connectivity is set to improve ahead of the peak winter season. British Airways will resume London Gatwick-Colombo flights on October 23, ending an absence of more than 11 years, while Edelweiss will increase its seasonal Zurich-Colombo service to three weekly flights from October 26. French Bee is also set to launch Paris Orly-Colombo flights on December 19.

The Russian carriers are set to bring close to 50,000 winter seats from eight Russian cities into Mattala and Colombo between October and April, as reported earlier by Mirror Business, which could revive a market whose 39,411 decline in arrivals this year exceeds the island’s overall shortfall.

The added capacity will be critical to the 2.5 million year-end target, which requires 821,255 arrivals between September 28 and December 31. That would mean a growth of 25.8 percent when compared to the same period last year, more than double the 12.2 percent growth recorded over the same stretch in 2025, when the island welcomed 652,913 tourists, against 581,744 a year earlier.
