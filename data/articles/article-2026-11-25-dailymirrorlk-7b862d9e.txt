Key economic sectors contract; Opposition raises alarm

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - The Joint Opposition criticized the Government over the contraction in several key economic sectors, including paddy cultivation, fisheries, ornamental fish exports and tea, saying the Budget has failed to adequately address the declining performance of sectors vital for employment generation and income growth.

Speaking at the weekly press conference, its convener former minister G.L. Peiris said these sectors were needed for the country’s development and employment generation.

Now when you compare the second quarter of last year with the second quarter of this year, all the sectors that need growth have contracted. Paddy cultivation has contracted by 15 percent, fisheries by 10 percent, aquarium fisheries for export has contracted by 61% and tea by two percent, according to him.

He highlighted that unemployment was the biggest issue the country was facing.

“We have 40,000 to 50,000 unemployed graduates, and the situation is likely to worsen in the near future. Sri Lanka has a population of 1.8 million in the 15–19 age group and five million in the 10–24 age group. These young people will gradually enter the labour market, but there is insufficient employment opportunities for them,” he said.

He said 50 percent of educated youths seek to migrate because there are no jobs available in this country.

“The largest number of people leaving the country were doctors. There are 7000 vacancies in the health sector. This is going to drastically impact on the quality of health care,” he said.

Asserting that the same was true of the higher education sector, quoting the Federation of University Teachers’ Association (FUTA), he said 12,000 academics were required for universities to operate and there were 6,000 vacancies.

“Obviously you can't deliver a quality product in such a situation. The obvious answer is generation of employment. It means there has to be greater government investment. The irony of the current situation is that money that is allocated is not spent.

According to the World Bank estimate by September this year only 16.7% of the last capital budget has been spent. By the end of the year, it will be 40%. The Government is not only incapable of utilising the resources which have been allocated to the different sectors but there is also a fundamental problem with regard to priorities. The Government must prioritise the sectors which are relevant from the point of view of everyday life of people, he said.
