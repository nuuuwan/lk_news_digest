Pilots carry Air Lanka legacy into next generation

Colombo, September 18 (Daily Mirror) - The legacy of Sri Lanka’s National Carrier is being carried into a new generation of pilots, with two SriLankan Airlines flight crew members sharing their experiences at a special aviation talk organised to mark the airline’s 47th anniversary.

Captain Shavantha Pedris and Senior First Officer Nuwan Jayawickrama took part in the sixth episode of The Skyline Stories, an aviation talk series organised by SriLankan Aviation College.

The session provided a unique connection between the early years of Air Lanka, established in 1979, and the present-day SriLankan Airlines, as both pilots are sons of members of the airline’s pioneering flight crew.

Captain Shavantha Pedris is the son of Captain Ranjit Pedris, while Senior First Officer Nuwan Jayawickrama is the son of Captain Nihal Jayawickrama.

Sharing his experience, Captain Pedris said growing up with a father involved in aviation had a strong influence on his decision to pursue a career in the industry.

He said his father inspired him from a young age and encouraged him to aim higher.

“Each experience has shaped who I am and strengthened my passion for aviation. Now, I feel it is my turn to give back to the industry that has given me so much,” he said.

Senior First Officer Jayawickrama said aviation had always been part of his family life and becoming a pilot therefore felt like a natural possibility.

However, he said following in the footsteps of a successful parent also came with expectations.

“The goal is not simply to follow someone else’s journey, but to learn from it and build your own,” he said.

The discussion highlighted the experiences of the pioneering generation that helped establish Air Lanka and the challenges faced by today's aviation professionals.

The session also reflected on the role played by the National Carrier in the development of Sri Lanka’s aviation and tourism sectors over the past 47 years.

SriLankan Aviation College said the event demonstrated how professional experience and family heritage could help inspire the next generation of aviation professionals.

The college, which serves as the training arm of Sri Lanka’s National Carrier, provides students with opportunities to learn directly from experienced pilots and aviation professionals.
