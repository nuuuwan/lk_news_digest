Economic outlook takes centre stage at EY post-Budget forum

Ernst & Young (EY) Sri Lanka successfully marked another edition of its annual Post-Budget Forum last week, at Cinnamon Life at City of Dreams. The event brought together senior Government officials, policymakers and business leaders from across the country to examine the key highlights of the National Budget 2027 and the opportunities and considerations they present for Sri Lanka’s economy and businesses.

The forum commenced with a welcome address by EY Sri Lanka and Maldives Country Managing Partner Gayathri Manatunga, who welcomed the distinguished guests and set the stage for the evening’s discussions on the National Budget 2027 and its implications for Sri Lanka’s economic landscape.

The program featured insights from EY Sri Lanka and Maldives Partner and Head of Tax Sulaiman Nishtar, who delivered a comprehensive presentation on the key tax proposals and fiscal measures outlined in the National Budget 2027.

His presentation covered areas including the growth agenda and priority sectors, investment and capital formation, tax policies and tax administration, and the cost of doing business, providing participants with an overview of the potential implications for businesses and the wider economy.

The presentation was followed by a panel discussion moderated by Nishtar, bringing together Secretary to the Treasury Dr. Harshana Suriyapperuma, Senior Economic Adviser to the President and Board of Investment of Sri Lanka Chairman Duminda Hulangamuwa, Sri Lanka Ports Authority Chairman Dr. Parakrama Dissanayake, Ceylon Chamber of Commerce Chairman Krishan Balendra and Ernst & Young Sri Lanka Partner Tax Shehani Paranavitane.

The discussion explored the broader economic direction outlined in the Budget, with particular attention to fiscal priorities, economic resilience, investment opportunities and the implications of policy measures for Sri Lankan businesses. Dr. Suriyapperuma shared perspectives on the Government’s fiscal direction and the economic considerations underpinning the Budget, contributing to a broader discussion on the country’s economic priorities and the way forward.

The evening concluded with a cocktail reception, creating an opportunity for participants to extend the conversation and exchange perspectives with leaders from the public and private sectors.

A fixture on Sri Lanka’s corporate calendar for over a decade, the EY Sri Lanka Post-Budget Forum continues to advance public-private dialogue and provide businesses with timely insights into the country’s fiscal and economic direction.
