NGOs under evaluation for loopholes in terrorist financing

Colombo, Oct. 29 (Dialy Mirror) - Sri Lanka is currently under evaluation by the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) for loopholes in its legal system relating to terrorist financing.

The FATF, the global intergovernmental money laundering and terrorist financing watchdog, has dispatched a team to the country. Its representatives sought the views of Sri Lankan civil society representatives, particularly on the proposed legislation to monitor and regulate the activities of NGOs.

The Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering (APG) is visiting Sri Lanka at a time when the Government is implementing various initiatives, including legislative reforms, which it believes are required to achieve a ‘compliant’ rating under FATF standards.

The FATF recognises non-profit organisations (NPOs) for their ‘vital role’ in the global economy and in many national economies and social systems. However, in the two decades since the abuse of NPOs by terrorists and terrorist organisations was formally recognised as a concern, the sector has taken numerous measures relating to governance, transparency and accountability to mitigate the risks of terrorist financing (TF) abuse.

In rare cases, NPOs continue to be misused and exploited by terrorists through a variety of means. Terrorists and terrorist organisations may seek to exploit NPOs to raise and move funds, provide logistical support, encourage terrorist recruitment, provide a veil of legitimacy or otherwise support terrorist organisations and operations. Thus, NPOs are expected to take mitigation measures, supported by appropriate government oversight, to detect and counter such abuse, according to a report compiled by the FATF.

The Government is required to ensure that NPOs are not abused for terrorist financing.

Meanwhile, the National Secretariat for Non-Governmental Organizations has issued an amendment to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) Guidelines for NGOs in Sri Lanka.

The new annex aims to assist NGOs in identifying circumstances that may warrant closer examination for potential money laundering (ML) or terrorist financing (TF) risks and to strengthen their internal risk management and reporting practices, according to an article published on its website.

Donations and donor conditions: Unusual or substantial donations from unverifiable sources, unexplained repayment arrangements or suspicious donor conditions.

Movement of funds and use of accounts: Unusual refunds, unexplained use of multiple foreign accounts or substantial cash transactions without a credible operational explanation.

Identity, financial records and expenditure: False or unverifiable identification, concealed control of partner organisations, suspicious invoices, unexplained cash withdrawals or discrepancies in financial records.

Partners and delivery of assistance: Obstruction of reasonable monitoring, unverified project activities, unexplained intermediaries or credible complaints that assistance is not reaching its intended beneficiaries.

Indicators linked to terrorist abuse: Credible information indicating the diversion of charitable resources to terrorist organisations, misuse of NGO facilities or programmes, or fundraising conducted for terrorist purposes.

Sanctions, concealment and digital payments: Potential matches against applicable sanctions designations, attempts to circumvent restrictions, digital-asset transactions designed to obscure the source or destination of funds, or purchases inconsistent with stated project objectives.
