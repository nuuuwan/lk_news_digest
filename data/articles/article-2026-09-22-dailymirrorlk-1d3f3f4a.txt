Was negligence behind former Ampara hospital director’s death?

Colombo, Sep. 22 (Daily Mirror) - A Health Ministry investigation into the treatment of a patient who later died at the Sri Jayewardenepura General Hospital has raised serious questions over the medical and nursing procedures followed during the patient's treatment, according to the investigation report.

Medical expert Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa has called on the Health Secretary to make the findings of the investigation public, saying the report contains several matters that require the attention of the health authorities.

The patient, identified in the report as a former Director of the Ampara Hospital, Dr. Lanka Jayasinghe had been admitted to the Sri Jayewardenepura General Hospital in July 2020 and received treatment at several units, including the Emergency Treatment Unit (ETU), wards and the Intensive Care Unit (ICU).

According to the investigation report, the inquiry examined the treatment provided to the patient, subsequent complications and the decisions taken by medical and nursing staff during the patient's stay in hospital.

Particular attention had been paid to the procedure involving Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy (PEG) feeding. Statements obtained from doctors and nurses reportedly included differing details concerning the procedure, the timing of feeding and the monitoring of the patient's condition.

The report also examined changes in the patient's blood pressure, complaints of chest pain, cardiac investigations, Troponin tests and ECG examinations before the patient was subsequently transferred to the ICU.

It further considered the treatment provided after the patient developed breathing difficulties and other complications.

The investigation had also examined the coordination between doctors and nursing staff, the response to emergencies and hospital administrative procedures.

Among its observations, the report stresses the importance of correctly identifying a patient's primary medical condition, particularly in an emergency setting, while ensuring proper triage and referral procedures.

Medical statements, hospital records and other documents obtained during the investigation had been considered in reaching the observations contained in the report.

The report was signed and submitted by the investigating officer on June 21, 2023.

However, the report itself should not be interpreted as a final determination of criminal or professional liability against any individual doctor or health worker. Any responsibility would have to be determined by the relevant authorities after considering the complete investigation report, medical records and other available evidence.

Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa has now urged the Health Secretary to release the investigation findings and take appropriate action based on the conclusions reached by the inquiry.

The demand comes amid renewed concerns over accountability and transparency in the public health sector, particularly when serious complications or deaths occur during hospital treatment.
