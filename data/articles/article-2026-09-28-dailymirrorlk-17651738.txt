WindForce and Vidullanka eye Rs.1.5bn battery storage investment after successful bids

Solar power ventures owned by WindForce PLC and Vidullanka PLC have emerged as winning bidders to add 37.15 MW and 186.75 MWh of battery storage to their existing solar plants, about a quarter of a National System Operator (NSO) tender for 150 MW and 600 MWh, separate stock filings by the two companies showed.

The NSO tender, which closed on September 24, called for battery energy storage systems (BESS) to be integrated with existing ground-mounted solar plants of between 1 MW and 10 MW, on a build, own and operate (BOO) basis for a 15-year period.

WindForce disclosed that six of its solar ventures, namely Solar Universe, Solar One Ceylon, Kebitigollewa Solar Power, Sooryashakthi, Diya Janani and Vydexa, bid for 28.25 MW and 143.75 MWh. Vidullanka’s four ventures account for 18.7 MW and 93 MWh, and include Solar Universe and Sooryashakthi, which the two firms own on a 50:50 basis.

Together, the eight ventures represent just under 25 percent of the tender’s power capacity and 31 percent of its storage capacity.

Vidullanka was the only one of the two to reveal its tariff bids, which ranged from Rs.24.88 per kWh for Solar Universe to Rs.27.96 per kWh for its fully owned VidulSolar plant in Madampe, a gap of about 12 percent. The two jointly owned ventures offered the lowest rates, while the average across Vidullanka’s four bids, weighted by storage capacity, stood at Rs.26.38 per kWh.

WindForce estimated its equity investment at around Rs.944 million, while Vidullanka put its share at Rs.600 million, bringing their combined commitment to about Rs.1.54 billion. Vidullanka noted that its investment would be funded mainly through cash generated by the existing solar plants, alongside debt raised at the level of the individual ventures, and that the amount could change once the final capital structure is set.

“Retrofitting battery storage to existing operational ground-mounted solar facilities will optimize solar power evacuation, reduce energy curtailment, and provide grid stabilization benefits,” Vidullanka stated.

However, both companies cautioned that the bids do not yet amount to contract awards, as they remain subject to formal awards, regulatory approvals and the signing of final agreements.

Meanwhile, the two firms have partnered on battery storage before. In February, the Ceylon Electricity Board (CEB) issued WindForce Letters of Award for 12 standalone storage projects totalling 120 MW. One of those projects, a 10 MW facility in Vavunathivu, is being developed jointly with Vidullanka.
