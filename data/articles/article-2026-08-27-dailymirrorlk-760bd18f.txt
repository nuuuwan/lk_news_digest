SL ranks 120th among 130 countries in ILO wage study

Sri Lanka stands at 120th among 130 countries surveyed in a global minimum wage comparison published by "Visual Capitalist" with its purchasing power-adjusted monthly minimum wage estimated at $200.

The ranking, based on 2024 data from the International Labour Organization, compares minimum wages after adjusting for purchasing power parity, or PPP. The adjustment reflects differences in living costs and what workers can buy with their earnings in each country.

Switzerland stands at No. 1 with a PPP-adjusted monthly minimum wage of $3,804, although its minimum wages are determined regionally rather than through a nationwide wage floor.

Germany ranks second at $2,928, followed by the United Kingdom at $2,902 and the Netherlands at $2,876.

Australia is fifth at $2,819, followed by Belgium at $2,752, Iceland at $2,730 and New Zealand at $2,673. France and Ireland complete the top 10 at $2,465 and $2,433, respectively.

South Korea records Asia's highest figure at $2,362 and ranks 11th globally. Japan ranks 16th at $1,839.

The United States stands at 25th with a PPP-adjusted monthly minimum wage of $1,257. The calculation uses the federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour, which has remained unchanged since 2009. Many U.S. states impose higher wage floors.

Among Sri Lanka's South Asian neighbors, Pakistan ranks 68th with $570, Nepal 78th with $490, Bangladesh 89th with $379 and India 111th with $233.

Sri Lanka's $200 figure places it below all four countries and among the lowest-ranked economies in the survey.

Only 10 countries rank below Sri Lanka. They include Niger, Bhutan, Haiti, Guinea, Central African Republic, Sierra Leone, Ghana, Kyrgyzstan, Guinea-Bissau and Gambia.

The figures do not represent direct U.S. dollar wages. PPP adjustments convert wages into comparable values based on local purchasing power. They also do not account for taxes, benefits or major differences in living costs within individual countries.
