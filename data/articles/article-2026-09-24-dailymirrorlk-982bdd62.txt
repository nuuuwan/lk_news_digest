No regrets whatsoever, accused still hold onto their ideology: ASG

Delivering the judgment, High Court Judge Navaratne Marasinghe observed that a conspiracy does not require all those involved to be physically present together or to have directly communicated with one another. He explained that an agreement to commit an offence could be formed either expressly or by implication and that individuals who joined the conspiracy at different stages could nevertheless be considered parties to the same conspiracy.

Marking one of the darkest chapters of cruelty in Sri Lanka’s recent history, the Colombo High Court Trial-at-Bar comprising Judges Navaratne Marasinghe, Ramanathan Kannan and Sujeewa Nishshanka yesterday imposed sentences of rigorous imprisonment on 15 accused in the Easter Sunday attacks case.

- Accused No - 1, 9, 10, 16 and 18 - 220 years of rigorous imprisonment each.

- Accused - 2, 3, 4 and 5 - 240 years of rigorous imprisonment each.

- Accused - 6, 7, 11, 15 and 19 - 200 years of rigorous imprisonment each.

The Court held that the prosecution had failed to establish the charges against nine accused beyond reasonable doubt.

The 17th accused, Yasin Bawa Abdul Rauf, died in prison during the course of the trial.

The Court imposed separate sentences in respect of the various incidents and charges.

In the case of the first accused, Mohamed Ibrahim Mohamed Naufer alias Naufer Moulavi, two individual charges were also considered, for which he was sentenced to 20 years of rigorous imprisonment on each charge.

For the attack planned against St. Anthony’s Church, Kochchikade, the accused were sentenced to,

- 20 years in respect of the persons killed in the attack; and

The sentences relating to that incident are to run concurrently.

Similar sentences of 20 years each were imposed in respect of the deaths, injuries and conspiracy relating to the attacks on St. Sebastian’s Church, Katuwapitiya, Shangri-La Hotel, Colombo, Cinnamon Grand Hotel, Colombo, Zion Church, Batticaloa, and Tropical Inn Hotel, Dehiwala.

In respect of the second bombing targeting the Shangri-La Hotel, separate 20-year terms were also imposed in relation to the conspiracy, deaths and injuries.

The Court ordered that the relevant sentences relating to each incident be served concurrently within that incident, while the sentences relating to the separate incidents would commence after the completion of the sentence imposed for the preceding incident.

Accordingly, the first accused, Naufer Moulavi, was sentenced to a total of 220 years of rigorous imprisonment.

Similar sentencing orders were made against the other convicted accused in respect of the individual incidents and the separate charges applicable to each of them.

The Court further ordered that, in accordance with Section 4 of the Prevention of Terrorism Act, the immovable and movable property of the accused convicted under the relevant provisions would be forfeited to the State.

The order also prevents the transfer or sale of such property from the date on which the sentence takes effect.

The trial at the Colombo High Court Trial-at-Bar Case No. TAB 2972/21, which commenced on October 10, 2023, concluded on August 24 this year.

Government Analyst reports, DNA reports and medico-legal reports relating to the persons who died in the attacks were also produced as evidence by the prosecution.

Another significant feature of the trial was that 2,076 productions, including explosives, chemicals used to manufacture explosives, swords and firearms, were produced by the prosecution before the Court.

Accordingly, Trial-at-Bar President Judge Navaratne Marasinghe commenced delivering the judgment.

“An indictment consisting of nine volumes and 8,900 pages has been served on the accused. The Attorney General had instituted this case against 25 accused under the provisions of the Prevention of Terrorism Act, filing separate and common charges against them."

The present case against the accused concerns eight suicide bomb attacks carried out on April 21, 2019, by eight Muslim religious extremists led by Zahran Hashim, targeting St. Anthony’s Church in Kochchikade, Kotahena, St. Sebastian’s Church in Katuwapitiya, Zion Church in Batticaloa, the Shangri-La Hotel in Colombo, three tourist hotels and the Tropical Inn restaurant.

Subsequently, before the special three-member High Court appointed by the Chief Justice in August 2021, the Attorney General filed this case against all 25 accused on 23,270 indictments.

This case will also enter world history as a case filed with the highest number of charges against an accused or a group of accused in the world.

During the trial, the proceedings had to be postponed due to the accused not having legal representation.

Accordingly, during the trial, the accused were informed that they could represent themselves and were also given relevant instructions.

As many of the accused conducted lengthy cross-examinations of witnesses regarding their evidence during the trial, the case had to be heard until after 10 p.m.

As the accused personally faced the trial, arrangements were made to provide them with all the necessary materials, including pens and pencils.

The president of the High court trial-at-Bar stated that the Court had paid special attention to the fact that the accused had conducted extremist lectures before the attacks, conducted weapons training workshops and maintained safe locations to carry out such activities.

"The Judge further stated that persons involved in a conspiracy do not necessarily have to be standing in a line at the same place and that a person can become involved in a conspiracy directly or indirectly, at the beginning, in the middle or at the end, and that it was clear from their actions that the accused had acted in such a manner.”

Colombo High Court Judge Navaratne Marasinghe,“The indictment against the accused in this case alleges that they had committed offences punishable under the provisions of the Prevention of Terrorism Act by conspiring to carry out the attacks, aiding and abetting, propagating extremist ideas and creating disharmony among races.

The Court has paid special attention to the fact that the accused conducted extremist lectures before the attacks, conducted weapons training workshops and maintained safe locations to carry out such activities.

Persons involved in a conspiracy do not necessarily have to be standing in a line at the same place. A person can become involved in a conspiracy directly or indirectly, at the beginning, in the middle or at the end. Anyone who becomes involved in such a manner is a party to the conspiracy.

The Court also considered the statements made in defence by each accused during the trial.

The Court rejects and dismisses the defence statements of the first, second, third, fourth and fifth accused. The Court observes that those statements had been made after due consideration due to the seriousness of the charges levelled against them.

The Court also paid special attention to a video recording contained in a pen drive, which was produced before the Court by the prosecution.

It was revealed before the Court that eight persons, including Zahran, were present in the relevant video recording and that they had been clearly identified from the footage.

It was further revealed before the Court that, in the relevant video recording, they had stated matters such as that “a crusade should be carried out” and that something should be done in Sri Lanka in relation to the attack on a muslim church in New Zealand.

It was also revealed before Court that, in the relevant video recording, they had stated that they would take revenge for incidents that had happened to Muslims in Sri Lanka, that they were coming with bombs, that they would do this the following day and that they were coming smiling.

The Court revealed that the video had been recorded between around 1.30 p.m. on April 20, 2019, the day before the attacks that took place on April 21, 2019.

It was also revealed before the Court that the persons in the video contained in the relevant pen drive had made a pledge of allegiance while an ISIS flag was present.

The prpresident of the Trial-at-Bar further explained to the Court that one person was seen holding a T-56 firearm while another person was holding a sword.

It was revealed that the video had been recorded in the Mount Lavinia “Spam Tower” area.

Thereafter, Counsel Anil Maddumage, appearing for accused Nos. 2, 7, 8, 10, 15, 18 and 19, requested the Court to impose lenient sentences on the accused.

"All these accused have been in prison for approximately seven and a half years. During this period, they have also lost their freedom.

We also do not want something like what happened on April 21, 2019, to happen again. Therefore, considering these facts, I request Your honor to impose lenient sentences.”

The first accused, Mohamed Ibrahim Mohamed Naufer alias Naufer Moulavi, stated that according to the teachings of Islam, it was not appropriate to make requests from authorities. Therefore, he said that he would accept any sentencing order imposed by the Court.

Thereafter, Senior Additional Solicitor General and Haripriya Jayasundara (PC), who appeared for the prosecution together with State Counsel Sajith Bandara and Additional Solicitor General Sudarshana de Silva, made the following submissions, requested that sentencing orders be imposed on the accused in a manner that would serve as an example to anyone in any religious social environment who holds extremist views.

“My Lord, in this case, During this trial, I felt as though I wished the hands of the clock would turn faster. That is how distressing the examination of this evidence was.

Can one group of people take away the happiness of others? When Zahran was giving those lectures, there was a T-56 and a sword. Is this what religion means? Can others not follow another religion? ‘We are going to carry out a series of attacks tomorrow. We must go happily.’

People were peacefully sitting in churches and praying on Easter Sunday for the resurrection of Jesus Christ. It was a very happy day for those people.

Those who were having meals at hotels - none of these people had done anything wrong to anyone. Now, although those people are alive, they are like dead people. Those in wheelchairs have no sense of what happened. What happened to these completely disabled people?

"None of them have any regret. They are still holding on to their ideology.” She said.

She further made submissions to Court by highlighting Supreme Court judgments, including Sumandiran v. Harsha Ilupitiya, and explained the matters that should be considered by Court when imposing sentences.

She also referred to the judgment relating to the Central Bank bomb attack, in which Prabhakaran was sentenced to 200 years of imprisonment. The incident took place in 1996 and the judgment was delivered by Court in 2002.

Highlighting this case, she stated that the then counsel appearing for the Attorney General’s Department in that case was the president of the Trial-at-Bar, Judge Navaratne Marasinghe, who was also one of the judges hearing the present Easter Sunday attacks case.

She stated that, in that case, the sentences imposed for the charges were ordered to commence after the completion of the sentence imposed for the previous charge.

She requested Court to impose the sentences in the present case in the same manner.
