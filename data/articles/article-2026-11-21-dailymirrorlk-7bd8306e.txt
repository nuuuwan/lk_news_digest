Sri Lanka hits 2mn tourists for 3rd conservative year

Arrivals 1.4% behind 2025 as March-April slump erases early gains

Beating last year’s record needs about 8,740 arrivals a day to year-end

Sri Lanka crossed two million tourist arrivals for the third consecutive year on November 20, three days later than in 2025, with a sharp slump in March and April, still leaving the cumulative arrivals behind last year’s pace, despite the strong growth from India, the official data showed.

The island’s two millionth visitor of the year was part of a Russian family of three, Sergei, Nataliya and their daughter Anna, according to the Foreign Affairs, Foreign Employment and Tourism Ministry.

The milestone was reached on November 17 last year and only on December 26 in 2024, when Sri Lanka first returned to the two million mark, after a six-year gap following 2018.

Addressing Parliament, Tourism Deputy Minister Prof. Ruwan Ranasinghe described the achievement as remarkable, amid the Middle East conflict, disruptions to international flight routes, rising aviation fuel prices and global economic challenges, while also recalling the setback the industry faced after Cyclone Ditwah struck on November 28, 2025.

He stressed that crossing two million was “not merely a numerical achievement” but a reflection of the industry’s resilience and international appeal.

However, the cumulative arrivals stood at 1,986,666 as of November 18, 1.4 percent or 28,746 visitors, below the same period last year, as per the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) data.

The shortfall is rooted in March and April, when the arrivals fell 20.9 percent to 319,622, when compared to the same two months of 2025, a loss of 84,284 visitors during a period that coincided with the Middle East conflict’s disruption of flight routes. That slump more than erased the cushion of 63,677 built in January and February, when the arrivals rose 12.9 percent to 556,655. The arrivals from May to October have since tracked last year almost exactly, slipping just 0.8 percent to 986,027.

The arrivals of 1,862,304 in the first 10 months were also 1.2 percent below 1,885,045 recorded in the same period of 2018, which shows how close the island remains to where it stood eight years ago. Even last year’s record total of 2,362,521 exceeded the 2018 figure of 2,333,796 by just 1.2 percent.

Meanwhile, the composition of the island’s visitors has shifted noticeably. The Indian arrivals climbed 18 percent to 499,625 in the first 10 months when compared to the same period last year, adding 76,220 visitors and lifting India’s share of total arrivals to 26.8 percent, from 22.4 percent.

India also accounted for three in every 10 arrivals in the first 18 days of November.

China and Australia grew as well, by 10 percent to 123,740 and by 12 percent to 95,498, respectively.

Russia, home to the milestone family, moved the other way, with the arrivals down 32.8 percent to 89,741 in the 10 months, a drop of 43,899 visitors. The Russian arrivals averaged 676 a day in the first 18 days of November, compared with 832 a day across November 2025, which suggests the anticipated winter surge from the market is yet to show up in the numbers.

Germany, France and Poland declined 9.8 percent, 10.1 percent and 25.6 percent, respectively, while the United Kingdom, the second largest source market, eased 1.0 percent to 173,129.

November itself has offered mixed signals. The ministry highlighted a 4.9 percent rise in average daily arrivals since November 10, though the first 18 days of the month delivered 124,362 arrivals, virtually unchanged from 124,725 in the same period last year, as a 7.8 percent drop over the first nine days offset the later gains.

Prof. Ranasinghe told Parliament that surpassing the 2025 record by the end of this year is a key objective of the government. Getting there requires 375,855 arrivals between November 19 and December 31 or about 8,740 a day, against the 347,109 arrivals, roughly 8,070 a day, recorded over the same 43 days last year. Merely matching last year’s pace would close 2026 at about 2,333,775, almost exactly the 2018 total.

That goal is a step down from the 2.5 million arrivals that SLTDA Chairman Suranjith Wevita told Mirror Business in August he expected by year-end, a figure that would now demand close to 11,940 arrivals a day. Even the lowest of the SLTDA’s revised scenarios, at 2,377,370, would require about 9,090 a day.

Prof. Ranasinghe credited the year’s progress to new international airlines, expanded direct flight connections, entry into new markets and stronger promotion. He added that air connectivity, international promotion and new market development would be further strengthened to meet the target.

The ministry is also revising how it estimates tourism revenue, in line with the international standards, to reflect the changes in the exchange rates and tourist spending patterns since 2018. (NF)
