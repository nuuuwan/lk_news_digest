Manivannan makes global appeal at APSCO 2026

R. M. Manivannan delivering his address at the 4th Leadership Development Forum of the Asia-Pacific Space Cooperation Organisation (APSCO), held in Beijing, China

SupremeSAT Chairman calls on international space industry leaders to invest in Sri Lanka, share advanced technologies and empower the next generation of innovators

Supreme Global Holdings and SupremeSAT Chairman R. M. Manivannan delivered a powerful address at the 4th Leadership Development Forum of the Asia-Pacific Space Cooperation Organisation (APSCO) in Beijing (18-20 November 2026), calling for greater international investment, technology partnerships and knowledge-sharing opportunities for developing nations.

Addressing international space industry leaders, policymakers, scientists and technology experts, Manivannan urged advanced economies to help countries such as Sri Lanka become active participants in the global space industry rather than remain dependent on technologies developed elsewhere.

“Talent exists everywhere. Opportunity should too,” he declared, highlighting the need to create opportunities for talented young people in developing countries.

Recalling Sri Lanka’s entry into the commercial space industry, Manivannan described the historic launch of SupremeSAT-1 on 27 November 2012 from China’s Xichang Satellite Launch Centre, made possible through cooperation with Chinese industry partners.

“They did not look at how small our country was. They looked at how big our dream was,” he said, expressing gratitude to China for supporting Sri Lanka’s ambitions.

Today, SupremeSAT operates its Kandy Teleport, providing satellite and ground-station services to international customers, with ambitions to establish Sri Lanka as a regional satellite communications hub.

Moving beyond his company’s achievements, Manivannan invited international investors, space technology companies, universities and research institutions to establish meaningful partnerships with Sri Lanka through investment, technology transfer, professional training and employment creation.

“We are not asking for charity. We are asking for partnership,” he emphasised.

He also highlighted Supreme Global Holdings’ involvement in emerging sectors, including artificial intelligence data centres, renewable energy, green ammonia and satellite communications.

His vision is to create a future where Sri Lankan engineers can work with world-class technologies without leaving their homeland, while children in remote communities gain access to global educational opportunities.

Calling for stronger cooperation through APSCO, he urged international partners to use space technology to address practical challenges, including natural disasters, climate change, food security and education.

Concluding his address, Manivannan delivered a memorable appeal: “Fourteen years ago, together, we helped Sri Lanka reach space. Now, together, let us use space to help everyone reach a better future.”

His message reinforced Sri Lanka’s potential to become a meaningful participant in tomorrow’s global technology economy.
