Who protects those behind bars? Namal asks

Colombo, Nov. 2 (Daily Mirror) - SLPP MP Namal Rajapaksa has questioned the protection afforded to people in State custody, alleging that individuals are being beaten, tortured and dying while under State custody.

In a post on X, Rajapaksa said that although a person's freedom could be restricted, their fundamental rights to life, dignity and protection could not be taken away.

He stressed that the State has s duty to protect every life in its custody and that duty is not optional.

"Being in custody has made one reality impossible to ignore: behind prison walls are human beings whose lives depend entirely on the institutions entrusted with their protection.

People are being beaten, allegedly tortured and dying while in State custody. These are not isolated incidents," he said.

Referring to the Negombo Prison incident, Rajapaksa said the tragedy had claimed 28 lives, while medical findings submitted to court had revealed that 17 victims died of gunshot injuries and the remaining 11 from blunt-force trauma.

He also cited the recent deaths of 29-year-old Ishara Harshakumara in Homagama Police custody and a 41-year-old man in Kobeigane, Kurunegala, saying the incidents had raised serious questions about the treatment and safety of people in State custody.

Rajapaksa said the Human Rights Commission had expressed serious concerns and called for independent investigations. However, nearly four months after the Negombo incident, forensic reports were still pending, families were awaiting answers and accountability remained unresolved, he said.

Emphasising the importance of the rule of law, he said justice should not be applied selectively but must remain impartial and protect individual rights irrespective of political interests or the identity of those involved.

"A person's freedom may be restricted, but their right to life, dignity and protection cannot be taken away. The question is simple: Who protects those who are behind bars, and who ensures that the truth does not die with them?

"The State has a duty to protect every life in its custody. That duty is not optional," he said.
