Compliance clock ticks for EU-bound exporters

Sri Lankan exporters targeting the European Union (EU) have been urged to begin preparing immediately for a series of stringent sustainability regulations that will take effect from next year, with non-compliance carrying the risk of substantial financial penalties and possible loss of market access.

According to Peterson Solutions Sri Lanka, the new regulatory framework will significantly change how companies market products, manage supply chains and substantiate environmental and social responsibility claims.

The first major requirement will come into force on September 27, 2026, under the EU’s Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive (EmpCo), aimed at eliminating misleading environmental claims, commonly referred to as “greenwashing”.

Asia Pacific Regional Manager of Peterson Solutions Sri Lanka, Dr. Rukshan Gunatilaka, said businesses would no longer be able to make broad claims such as “green”, “eco-friendly” or “sustainably produced” without credible evidence to support them.

“The era of simply saying a product is ‘green’, ‘eco-friendly’ or ‘sustainably produced’ is coming to an end. Businesses must now be able to produce credible evidence supporting every sustainability claim they make,” he said.

He warned that companies found making unsubstantiated sustainability claims could face penalties of up to €2 million or 4% of annual turnover in the relevant EU member state, in addition to reputational damage and restrictions on market access.

Dr. Gunatilaka noted that the rules extend beyond product labels and apply to websites, advertisements, social media content, sustainability reports and other promotional material.

He said exporters must also prepare for the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR), which begins applying from August 12, 2026, and the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR), whose due diligence obligations take effect for larger companies on December 30, 2026.

The regulations are expected to affect key Sri Lankan export sectors, including tea, spices, apparel, textiles, rubber products, fisheries, plastics and tourism-related industries.

Assistant Manager – Business Communications and Marketing at Peterson Solutions Sri Lanka, Dulini Wijeratne, said businesses should view compliance as a strategic investment rather than an additional cost.

“The question regulators, investors and consumers are increasingly asking is no longer whether a company has sustainability ambitions. The question is: Can you prove it?” she said.

Peterson Solutions Sri Lanka will conduct a complimentary webinar titled “ESG Reporting Basics: Why, When & How” on July 29 to help businesses understand and prepare for the evolving regulatory requirements.
