Lakdhanavi secures $ 75 m contract to build 57 MW power plant in India

Colombo, Nov 16 (Daily Mirror) - Lakdhanavi Ltd., a member of the LTL Group, has signed a turnkey Engineering, Procurement and Construction (EPC) contract worth approximately Rs. 25 billion ($ 75 million) to build a 57 MW natural gas-based fired power plant on India’s Andaman Island.

LTL Group is the largest power sector company in Sri Lanka with interests in power generation, transmission, and also distribution. Originally set up as a joint venture between the Ceylon Electricity Board (CEB) and a European company, currently National Transmission Network System Provider Ltd., (NTNSP), a Government-owned entity under the Energy Ministry, holds 35% stake in LTL Holdings Ltd.

The contract was awarded by NTPC Vidyut Vyapar Nigam Ltd., (NVVN), a wholly owned subsidiary of National Thermal Power Corporation (NTPC) Ltd., which is owned by the Government of India, following an international competitive bidding (ICB) process in which Lakdhanavi emerged as the lowest priced bidder (L1).

The contract was signed in September at the NTPC Office Complex in Noida, New Delhi, in the presence of senior representatives of NVVN, Lakdhanavi, and LTL Holdings. The parties also discussed project implementation and future collaboration between NVVN and Lakdhanavi.

The project will be located at Hope Town, Port Blair, in Andaman Island and is expected to strengthen the island’s electricity infrastructure, enable long-term energy security through LNG infrastructure, and facilitate future economic development. The Lakdhanavi Ltd., engineering team would be responsible for the execution of the project, with Wärtsilä Corporation of Finland being the supplier of the power-generating equipment to Lakdhanavi Ltd.

The award marks Lakdhanavi’s entry into India’s power plant EPC market and further expands its regional presence. Established in Sri Lanka in the late 90s as a subsidiary of LTL Holdings, the company has decades of experience in power generation as an Independent Power Producer (IPP) and a provider of Operations and Maintenance (O&M) solutions for thermal and renewable energy projects. Its overseas power project experience includes projects in Bangladesh, Nepal, and the Maldives.

Energy Minister Anura Karunathilaka met LTL’s management and expressed the Government’s delight over this achievement, welcoming the success of a Sri Lankan company in securing a major infrastructure contract in a large and competitive market such as India. This underscored the national significance of the achievement and the value of Sri Lankan companies taking their expertise to international markets.

Beyond its commercial significance, the project is a practical reflection of longstanding economic ties between Sri Lanka and India. While bilateral economic engagement has often been viewed through the lens of Indian investment, development assistance, and infrastructure participation in Sri Lanka, Lakdhanavi’s success demonstrates that opportunities can arise in both directions and that Sri Lankan companies should pursue them with diligence.

The contract demonstrates how Sri Lankan businesses can contribute their skills and capabilities for mutual development of both countries, complementing the close cooperation of the two Governments with tangible private-sector partnerships. It offers an encouraging example of how the two neighbouring countries can deepen economic cooperation and create mutually benefitting opportunities.

Lakdhanavi Ltd., and LTL Holdings CEO Eng. Nuhuman Marikkar described the contract as a significant milestone for the company and a notable achievement for Sri Lankan engineering. He said the award reflected the strength of the company’s technical capabilities, the dedication of its team, and the confidence placed in its ability to deliver complex power infrastructure projects successfully, both in its home market and elsewhere.

He added that Lakdhanavi is looking forward to working closely with NVVN to deliver the project successfully.
