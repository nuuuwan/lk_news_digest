GMOA warns proposed dog vaccination law could disrupt rabies control

Colombo, Oct. 2 (Daily Mirror) - The Government Medical Officers’ Association (GMOA) has raised concerns over proposed legal amendments governing dog vaccinations, warning that the changes could have an adverse impact on Sri Lanka’s rabies control programme.

In a statement, the GMOA said a provision in the proposed legislation would require dogs to be vaccinated only under the direct supervision of a veterinary surgeon.

The association said such a requirement would be impractical given the limited number of government veterinary surgeons in the country and could disrupt the existing dog vaccination programme conducted through the Ministry of Health.

According to the GMOA, dog vaccinations are currently administered by specially trained vaccinators across 362 Medical Officer of Health (MOH) areas under the supervision of health authorities.

The association said Sri Lanka’s current dog vaccination coverage is around 60%, while the target is 70%.

The GMOA also referred to the transfer of the vaccination programme to the Department of Animal Production and Health in 2017. It claimed that vaccination coverage, which had been above 60%, dropped to around 6% within a year following the transfer.

The programme was subsequently returned to the health authorities, the GMOA said.

The association urged authorities to consider the practical implications of the proposed legal changes and ensure that any amendments do not undermine the country’s ongoing efforts to control rabies.
