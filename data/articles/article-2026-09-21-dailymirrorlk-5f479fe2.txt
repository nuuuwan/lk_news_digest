190,000 cancer injections allegedly imported without approval

Colombo, Sep. 21 (Daily Mirror) - Questions have been raised over the importation and release of a stock of around 190,000 G-CSF injections, reportedly worth approximately Rs. 250 million, amid claims that the drugs were imported without proper labelling and required standards.

Chairman of the Medical and Civil Rights Doctors' Trade Union Alliance, Specialist Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa, questioned who authorized the release of the consignment from Customs and called for the National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA) to disclose all relevant information.

He alleged that the G-CSF injections, which are administered to cancer patients, including children, to help restore the immune system following radiation therapy, have not been used because of concerns over their labelling and standards.

"If the stock is questionable, the Chairman of the NMRA, Specialist Dr. Ananda Wijewickrama, should reveal to the country as to who got these medicines released from Customs," Dr. Sanjeewa said.

He questioned how such a large consignment could have entered the country if the required regulatory procedures, including registration, quality certification, certificates of analysis, import control approvals and labelling and packaging requirements, had not been fulfilled.

Dr. Sanjeewa said the NMRA and the State Pharmaceuticals Corporation (SPC), which imported the medicines, should explain how the consignment was brought into the country and subsequently released from Customs. He also accused responsible officials of withholding information while cancer patients and doctors faced difficulties over the availability and administration of medicines.

According to him, doctors at several hospitals have raised concerns on social media over problems associated with the continued administration of certain medicines to cancer patients.

Dr. Sanjeewa further claimed that shortages of medicines, including cancer drugs, had contributed to price increases at private pharmacies, while some medicines available in the market were allegedly unregistered.

He also raised concerns over shortages of doctors and specialists in the public health system, claiming that delays in approving the required cadre positions and salary and tax-related issues were discouraging doctors from joining the public service.

He said the continued migration of young doctors overseas was also affecting the health sector.

Dr. Sanjeewa claimed that the health service should have at least around 5,000 specialists and 30,000 medical officers, but that delays in Treasury approval of the required cadre had created further difficulties.

The allegations could not be independently verified, and the NMRA and SPC have not been given as having responded to the claims.
