Tea industry overview: January to June - 2026

During the period January to June 2026, Sri Lanka’s tea industry recorded declines in Production and Export volumes compared to the same period in 2025, while the National Tea Sale Average Price showed a marginal increase. Total tea production dropped by 3.89 Mn/Kgs, falling from 135.77 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 131.89 Mn/Kgs in 2026. Total exports also declined by 3.71 Mn/Kgs, decreasing from 126.81 Mn/Kgs to 123.10 Mn/Kgs. Meanwhile, the National Tea Sale Average recorded a marginal increase of Rs. 3.55 per kilogram, increasing from Rs.1,158.54 in 2025 to Rs. 1,162.09 in 2026.

In terms of export destinations, Turkey emerged as the largest importer of Ceylon Tea during the first half of 2026. Turkey demonstrated a significant increase in demand, with imports nearly tripling compared to the previous year, rising from 7.91 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 19.71 Mn/Kgs in 2026. Iraq, which had been the largest importer during Q1 2026, experienced a notable decline of 5.29 Mn/Kgs but remained the second-largest destination. Russia maintained stable demand with a marginal increase of 0.40 Mn/Kgs to 11.61 Mn/Kgs. Azerbaijan demonstrated strong growth of 2.76 Mn/Kgs to reach 6.56 Mn/Kgs. However, markets such as China, Libya, and Saudi Arabia registered noticeable declines, with China dropping by 0.23 Mn/Kgs, Libya declining by 6.90 Mn/Kgs, and Saudi Arabia falling by 0.04 Mn/Kgs.

FOB prices, when measured in Sri Lankan Rupees, showed year-on-year improvement across the cumulative period, rising from Rs. 1,745.10 in 2025 to Rs. 1,810.28 in 2026, largely reflecting the currency depreciation. FOB values in USD terms remained under slight pressure throughout the period, declining from USD 5.86 in 2025 to USD 5.70 in 2026. Consequently, equivalent US Dollar export earnings for the cumulative period declined by approximately USD 41.87 Mn (5.63 percent), falling from USD 743.12 Mn in 2025 to USD 701.25 Mn in 2026.

The total tea production for the January to June 2026 period recorded a decline compared with the corresponding period in 2025, although production trends varied across the different elevations. Total tea production decreased by 3.89 Mn/Kgs, falling to 131.89 Mn/Kgs in 2026 from 135.77 Mn/Kgs in 2025.

High Grown teas recorded a marginal increase of 0.44 Mn/Kgs, reaching 30.90 Mn/Kgs during the first six months of 2026 compared with 30.46 Mn/Kgs in the corresponding period of 2025. Medium Grown teas experienced a notable decline, decreasing by 1.15 Mn/Kgs to 24.31 Mn/Kgs, compared with 25.46 Mn/Kgs recorded during the same period in 2025.

Low Grown teas, which continued to account for the largest share of national tea production at 58.14 percent, recorded the most significant volume decline. Production fell by 3.17 Mn/Kgs, decreasing to 76.68 Mn/Kgs in 2026 from 79.85 Mn/Kgs in 2025.

Although High Grown tea production recorded a slight increase during the first half of 2026, this improvement was insufficient to offset the declines in Medium and Low Grown production. The substantial reduction in Low Grown teas, which account for the largest share of Sri Lanka’s total tea output, was the primary factor contributing to the overall decline in national tea production compared with the corresponding period of 2025.

The comparison of CTC Tea Production for the January–June 2026 period against the corresponding period in 2025 reflects a strong overall improvement across all elevations, with total production increasing by 1.45 Mn/Kgs, from 11.95 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 13.41 Mn/Kgs in 2026.

High Grown CTC production recorded a moderate increase of 0.21 Mn/Kgs, rising from 2.62 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 2.83 Mn/Kgs in 2026. Medium Grown CTC teas registered the most significant improvement during the period, with production increasing by 1.13 Mn/Kgs, from 4.39 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 5.51 Mn/Kgs in 2026. This category contributed the largest share of the overall increase in CTC production.

Low Grown CTC production also recorded an increase of 0.11 Mn/Kgs, rising from 4.95 Mn/Kgs in 2025 to 5.06 Mn/Kgs in 2026, further supporting the overall upward trend.

Overall, CTC tea production increased from 11.95 Mn/Kgs in January–June 2025 to 13.41 Mn/Kgs in the corresponding period of 2026, reflecting an improvement across all elevations. Medium Grown teas were the principal driver of this growth, followed by High Grown and Low Grown teas, contributing to the overall expansion in CTC tea production during the first half of 2026.

The comparison of Sri Lanka Tea Sale Averages for the period January to June 2026 against the corresponding period in 2025 indicates a marginal improvement in the overall average price in Sri Lanka Rupee terms, while the US Dollar values declined across all elevation categories.

In the High Grown category, the average price increased in Sri Lanka Rupee terms from Rs. 1,078.13 in 2025 to Rs. 1,127.48 in 2026, reflecting a gain of approximately Rs. 49.35. However, in US Dollar terms, the average declined by USD 0.07 from USD 3.62 to USD 3.55. The Medium Grown category recorded a decline in both Rupee and US Dollar values. The average price decreased by approximately Rs. 39.49, falling from Rs.1,029.89 in 2025 to Rs. 990.40 in 2026. Similarly, the US Dollar value declined by USD 0.34 from USD 3.46 to USD 3.12.

In the Low Grown category, which continues to command the highest average price among the elevation categories, the Rupee value remained largely stable, declining marginally by Rs.3.79, from Rs. 1,228.85 in 2025 to Rs.1,225.06 in 2026. However, the US Dollar average declined by 0.28 from USD 4.13 to USD 3.85, reflecting a decrease in export value.

Overall, the National Tea Sale Average Price for January to June 2026 increased marginally in Sri Lanka Rupee terms, rising from Rs.1,158.54 in 2025 to Rs.1,162.09 in 2026, an improvement of approximately Rs.3.55. However, the average in US Dollar terms declined from USD 3.89 to USD 3.66, mainly due to the impact of currency fluctuations.

For the period January to June 2026, Sri Lanka’s tea exports declined in volume to 123.10 Mn/Kgs, compared to 126.81 Mn/Kgs during the corresponding period of 2025, reflecting a decrease of approximately 3.71 Mn/Kgs (2.9%). Despite the lower export volume, total export earnings increased to Rs.222.85 billion from Rs.221.29 billion recorded in the same period of 2025.

The average FOB price also improved to Rs.1,810.28 per kg in 2026, compared to Rs.1,745.10 per kg in 2025. However, in US dollar terms, export earnings declined to approximately USD 701.25 Mn from USD 743.12 Mn, while the average FOB price decreased to USD 5.70 per kg from USD 5.86 per kg, largely reflecting the depreciation of the US dollar value relative to the Sri Lankan Rupee.

Segment-wise, tea in bulk recorded declines in both export quantity and value in Rupee terms, although the average FOB price increased to Rs.1,528.80 per kg from Rs.1,497.12 per kg. Tea in packets, which remained the largest export category, also experienced lower export volumes and a slight decline in export value, while the average FOB price improved to Rs.1,666.07 per kg from Rs.1,623.36 per kg.

Tea in bags remained relatively stable in volume but recorded a notable increase in export value, supported by a significant rise in the average FOB price to Rs.2,909.40 per kg. Instant tea recorded strong growth in both export volume and value. However, its average FOB price eased slightly compared to the previous year. Green tea exports declined marginally in volume but registered higher export earnings, with the average FOB price increasing to Rs.4,088.96 per kg from Rs.3,808.94 per kg.

Overall, although Sri Lanka’s tea export volume declined during the first half of 2026, the increase in average FOB prices, particularly for value-added tea categories such as tea bags and green tea, helped offset the reduction in quantity and enabled the industry to achieve a modest increase in export earnings in Rupee terms. Nevertheless, export earnings in US dollar terms remained lower than the corresponding period of 2025 due to exchange rate movements.

During the period January to June 2026, Türkiye emerged as the largest importer of Sri Lankan tea, recording a significant increase to 19.71 Mn/Kgs, compared to 7.91 Mn/Kgs during the corresponding period of 2025. In contrast, Iraq, traditionally Sri Lanka’s largest export destination, experienced a substantial decline in imports to 13.42 Mn/Kgs from 18.71 Mn/Kgs. Russia maintained relatively stable demand, with imports increasing marginally to 11.61 Mn/Kgs from 11.21 Mn/Kgs.

The comparison of the monthly average exchange rates for the period January to June 2026 against the corresponding period in 2025 indicates a consistent depreciation of the Sri Lankan Rupee against the US Dollar throughout the first half of the year.

The average exchange rate increased from Rs. 296.18/USD in January 2025 to Rs.309.65/USD in January 2026, and continued to rise steadily each month, reaching Rs.334.43/USD in June 2026, compared with Rs.299.90/USD in June 2025. The exchange rate remained relatively stable during the first half of 2025, fluctuating within a narrow range of Rs. 295.91 to Rs. 299.90 per USD.

In contrast, 2026 recorded a persistent month-on-month increase, reflecting the continued weakening of the Sri Lankan Rupee.

The year-on-year difference widened progressively from Rs.13.47 in January to Rs. 34.53 by June, demonstrating acceleration in currency depreciation during the second quarter. The most significant increase occurred between May and June 2026, when the exchange rate rose from Rs.324.91 to Rs. 334.43, highlighting heightened exchange rate pressure.
