CSE opens the week lower as retail selling and geopolitical concerns weigh on market sentiment

The Colombo Stock Exchange commenced the week on a weak note, with both benchmark indices closing sharply lower as investor sentiment remained subdued amid persistent geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and cautious positioning ahead of the upcoming quarterly earnings season. Retail-driven selling pressure dominated the session, pushing the ASPI down by more than 1%, while limited participation from institutional and high-net-worth investors kept overall market activity subdued.

The absence of strong bargain hunting further weighed on market performance, as investors remained on the sidelines awaiting clearer signals from upcoming corporate earnings announcements and developments in the global geopolitical landscape. Despite the broad-based selling pressure, selective buying interest continued to emerge in a handful of fundamentally strong counters, although it was insufficient to offset the overall decline.

The ASPI fell by 233.18 points, or 1.07%, to close at 21,532.38, while the S&P SL20 Index declined by 55.27 points, or 0.91%, to settle at 6,022.19. Total market turnover stood at LKR 1.03 Bn, with 79.72 Mn shares traded, reflecting subdued trading activity compared to recent sessions.

The Banking sector emerged as the largest contributor to market turnover, generating LKR 0.28 Bn with 2.36 Mn shares traded. SAMP.N led activity within the sector, recording LKR 0.10 Bn in turnover on 0.79 Mn shares traded. Crossing transactions accounted for LKR 0.13 Bn, representing 12% of total market turnover, with the largest crossing recorded in MELS.N amounting to LKR 0.09 Bn involving 0.50 Mn shares traded.

Market breadth remained notably weak, with 163 decliners significantly outnumbering 39 gainers, highlighting widespread selling pressure across the broader market. BUKI.N emerged as the largest positive contributor to the ASPI, adding 3.17 points, followed by RICH.N, CDB.N, MERC.N, and DIMO.N. On the downside, CTHR.N exerted the largest drag on the index, reducing it by 19.08 points, while COMB.N, SAMP.N, MELS.N, and HNB.N also weighed heavily on overall market performance.

Among notable trades, SAMP.N attracted the highest investor interest of the session, generating LKR 0.10 Bn in turnover on 0.79 Mn shares traded. The counter declined by 1.08% during the day, reflecting the broader weakness in the banking sector and the cautious sentiment prevailing across the market. Overall, investors remained defensive as they continued to monitor geopolitical developments and await the upcoming earnings season for stronger catalysts that could determine the market's near-term direction.
