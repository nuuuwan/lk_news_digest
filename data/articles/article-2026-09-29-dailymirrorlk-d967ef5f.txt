President calls for single digital system for public services

Colombo, Sep. 29 (Daily Mirror) - President Anura Kumara Dissanayake has instructed officials to work towards establishing a single national digital system covering public services, while reviewing the ongoing digitalisation of local authorities and Divisional Secretariats.

The President gave the directive during a progress review meeting on the Government’s digital transformation programme held at the Presidential Secretariat today (29).

The meeting focused on the digitalisation of local authorities and Divisional Secretariats, with the aim of providing faster, more efficient and transparent public services.

A key area of discussion was the **National Framework for the Digitalisation of Local Authorities**, currently being developed by the Ministry of Public Administration, Provincial Councils and Local Government.

President Dissanayake instructed officials to identify all services that local authorities are required to provide to the public and determine which of them could be incorporated into digital systems.

He also called for an assessment of whether existing digital platforms adequately cover these services.

The President emphasised the need to move away from separate digital systems operated independently by different government institutions and instead develop a single national system covering the public service.

He instructed the Ministry of Public Administration, Provincial Councils and Local Government and the Ministry of Digital Economy to closely coordinate their efforts towards this objective.

Three officers from the Ministry of Digital Economy are also to be assigned to coordinate the further development of the national system operated under the Ministry of Public Administration, Provincial Councils and Local Government.

The officers will also identify shortcomings in existing systems and oversee necessary amendments and improvements.

The meeting reviewed several digital services already being provided through local authorities, including payment of rates, miscellaneous revenues such as charges for display boards, bookings of playgrounds and public halls, shop rentals and water bills.

Digital mechanisms for obtaining permits and licences and systems introduced to provide prompt solutions to public grievances were also discussed.

Officials also examined ways of further developing digital payment systems such as GovPay under the programme.

The digitalisation programme at Divisional Secretariat level was also reviewed, with more than 300 services currently provided by Divisional Secretariats having been identified for the process.

Progress in connecting services from the Grama Niladhari level through Divisional Secretariats to District Secretariats was also discussed.

Particular attention was given to the digitalisation process being implemented in the Polonnaruwa District, including experiences gained from activities such as paddy purchases and the provision of disaster relief.

President Dissanayake instructed officials to initially establish a fully equipped model for the Divisional Secretariat Digitalisation Programme in one selected district, incorporating all necessary facilities.

The meeting also reviewed the **GovHR** digital system, which is being developed to manage the entire administrative process relating to public officers, from their appointment until retirement.

Deputy Minister of Digital Economy Eranga Weeraratne, Secretary to the President Dr Nandika Sanath Kumanayake, Senior Adviser to the President on Digital Economy Hans Wijayasuriya and senior officials from the relevant ministries and agencies attended the meeting.
