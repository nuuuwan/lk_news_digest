Sri Lanka Embassy alerts nationals after sudden flooding in Nepal

Colombo, Oct. 11 (Daily Mirror) - No Sri Lankans have been affected by the sudden flooding in Nepal’s Budhigandaki River so far, while the Sri Lankan Embassy in Kathmandu is closely monitoring the situation and alerting the Sri Lankan community, the Foreign Ministry said.

The Embassy is in close contact with community members and has advised them to remain alert to developments and avoid travelling to areas that could be affected by the flooding.

The incident occurred in the Gorkha District of Nepal’s central region.

According to Nepal’s Flood Forecasting Division, preliminary information about the sudden flooding was received at around 6 a.m. on October 11. As there had been no rainfall in the area during the preceding 24 hours, authorities suspect that the flooding may have resulted from the sudden release of water from a glacial lake.

However, a detailed technical analysis is required to establish the actual cause, officials said.

Water levels at the Budhigandaki-Arughat flood monitoring station rose from 2.28 metres at 6.30 a.m. to 5.01 metres at 7.10 a.m., exceeding the danger level of five metres. The water level subsequently declined, but the monitoring equipment was damaged, preventing further readings.

Nepalese authorities issued approximately 200,000 SMS alerts in two phases to residents in potentially affected areas of Gorkha, Dhading, Nuwakot and Chitwan districts.

Residents along the Budhigandaki River and downstream areas have been urged to remain in safe locations, stay away from riverbanks and vulnerable settlements, and avoid travelling along roads and bridges that could be affected by flooding.

Authorities have advised the public to follow instructions issued by local administrations and security agencies as monitoring and technical assessments continue.
