Urubokka to be developed as hub for traditional Southern food culture: Handunnetti

Colombo, Nov. 19 (Daily Mirror) - New Hela Bojun outlet aims to preserve local food heritage while creating opportunities for women and rural entrepreneurs

Urubokka has the potential to become a major centre for promoting traditional Southern food culture, while creating new economic opportunities for rural communities, Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Minister Sunil Handunnetti said.

Speaking at the opening of a new Hela Bojun outlet in Urubokka, the Minister said the area had a rich agricultural and culinary heritage that could be developed into an important attraction by preserving its traditional food culture.

Recalling Urubokka's former commercial importance, Handunnetti said the town was once a major agricultural trading hub, attracting businessmen from Colombo and other areas who travelled there to purchase black pepper, fruits, vegetables and bananas.

"Urubokka was once a significant commercial hub. Businessmen from Colombo used to travel here specifically to purchase produce such as black pepper, fruits, vegetables and, most notably, bananas. It was renowned across Sri Lanka as a major banana collection point," he said.

The Minister said lorries from Colombo and Ambalangoda would arrive in the evening and remain until dawn, while farmers from surrounding villages, including Ginnaliya and areas towards Kolonna, brought their harvests to the town.

"Much like Dambulla is today, Urubokka once functioned as a vibrant trading hub. It was a bustling marketplace, with farmers bringing their produce and traders arriving from different parts of the country," he said.

However, he said that much of this commercial activity had gradually declined as successive generations moved away from agriculture, with many relocating to cities or migrating overseas.

Handunnetti said this transformation had also affected traditional village lifestyles and food culture, making it important to revive and preserve the country's regional culinary heritage.

"We must revitalise our Sri Lankan food culture. It possesses unique characteristics. The cuisine of the South is distinct, particularly in the Matara area," he said.

Pointing to the traditional preparation of halapa, he said even familiar Sri Lankan food items had different flavours and preparation methods depending on the region.

"If you try a halapa in another part of Sri Lanka, it will be quite different from the halapa we make here in Urubokka. Even the method of preparation is different. We have a specific food culture here in the Southern region, particularly in Matara," he said.

The Minister said Urubokka could once again become an important destination by promoting its traditional cuisine and protecting the authenticity of local recipes and preparation methods.

"Urubokka can certainly become a hub for promoting that culture, provided, of course, that its authentic quality is preserved," he said.

The new Hela Bojun outlet was established in the Urubokka Grama Niladhari Division under the 2026 Decentralised Budget Programme, with the Pasgoda Divisional Secretariat implementing the project.

The initiative is also expected to create income-generating opportunities for women and rural entrepreneurs while promoting nutritious, locally prepared traditional food.

The outlet is intended to support the preservation of Sri Lanka's food heritage while strengthening rural livelihoods through traditional food production and sales.
