SRR hike to cost banks about Rs.12bn a year in forgone interest

EXTRA PERCENTAGE POINT TO PARK ABOUT RS.140BN OF BANK FUNDS WITH CBSL AT ZERO INTEREST FROM 9 DEC

Raising the Statutory Reserve Ratio (SRR) to 3 percent from 2 percent will cost commercial banks roughly Rs.12 billion a year in forgone interest income, as the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) locks a larger share of their rupee deposits in non-remunerated accounts, First Capital Research (FCR) estimated.

Rupee deposits subject to the SRR stood at about Rs.13.7 trillion at end-August 2026, the research house pointed out, so the additional percentage point would park around Rs.140 billion with the Central Bank from 9 December. Valued at the Standing Deposit Facility Rate (SDFR) of 8.50 percent, those funds would have earned close to Rs.12 billion a year before tax. On the same deposit base, the total required reserves banks must hold would rise by half, from about Rs.274 billion to around Rs.411 billion.

The hike is the first change to the ratio since August 2023, when the Central Bank halved it from 4 percent to 2 percent, a move expected to release around Rs.200 billion into the money market at the time. According to First Capital Research, it is also the first increase since September 2021, when the ratio was raised from 2 percent to 4 percent.

“ You can think of this as SRR normalisation,” Central Bank Senior Deputy Governor Dr. Chandranath Amarasekara told reporters at the post-policy press briefing on Friday.

He explained that the ratio had been lowered in 2023 to support banking sector liquidity, which has since recovered on the back of continued forex purchases.

However, while the tightening caught markets off guard, First Capital Research observed that a stricter SRR framework had been on the cards since the Central Bank’s 2026 policy agenda, with Sri Lanka’s ratio low by regional standards. The research house had anticipated a hike as early as March. In the interim, the Central Bank tightened the framework without touching the ratio, phasing out in April and September 2026 the concession that allowed banks to count vault cash towards the SRR.

Meanwhile, excess liquidity, the funds left over once banks meet the SRR, has climbed to record highs above Rs.475 billion, the research house noted, meaning the Rs.140 billion to be absorbed would mop up less than a third of that surplus. It expects outstanding market liquidity to ease towards Rs.200 billion to Rs.300 billion by year-end, though it cautioned that liquidity would stay elevated for some time.

First Capital also read the move as an indirect signal that the Central Bank intends to keep buying dollars from the market, having now dealt with a by-product of those purchases. The Central Bank bought around US$ 1.7 billion this year up to October, with purchases continuing in November, helping lift gross official reserves to US$ 7.1 billion by end-October from US$ 6.9 billion at end-August.
