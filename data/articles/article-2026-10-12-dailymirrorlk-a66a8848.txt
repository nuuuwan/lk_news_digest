Akzo Nobel leaves Sri Lanka paints stake out of Nippon Paint deal

Dutch paints giant Akzo Nobel N.V. has confirmed that its controlling stake in Akzo Nobel Paints Lanka, the company behind the Dulux brand in Sri Lanka, is not part of the US$ 1.35 billion sale of its South East Asian decorative paints business to Japan’s Nippon Paint, CIC Holdings PLC said in a stock filing.

The confirmation came in a communication addressed to the CIC Holdings Chairman, received on October 8, three days after the Amsterdam-based group announced the seven-market divestment. Akzo Nobel holds 50.63 percent of the voting shares in the local company through Imperial Chemical Industries Limited.

However, the Dutch group’s own announcement made no reference to Sri Lanka, although it described the sale as the conclusion of its decorative paints portfolio review in Asia, following earlier exits from India and Pakistan.

A list of subsidiaries Akzo Nobel filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission in May puts its interest in the local company’s issued share capital at 40 percent. CIC Holdings owns 49.37 percent of the voting shares, together with all of the non-voting shares, and accounts for the paint maker as an associate.

The associate contributed Rs.605.7 million to CIC’s group profit in the year ended March 31, 2026, up 14.6 percent from Rs.528.5 million a year earlier, and made up 5.3 percent of the group’s Rs.11.42 billion profit before tax, as per its annual report.

Akzo Nobel Paints Lanka’s revenue rose 9.6 percent to Rs.9.13 billion during the year, while its profit after tax grew at a slower 4.4 percent to Rs.1.01 billion.
