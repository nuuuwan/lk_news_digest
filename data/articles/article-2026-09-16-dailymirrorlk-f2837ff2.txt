Children’s Homes & Donations:  - The Wall Between Donors and Children

The recent news of 3 children escaping from a children’s home in Monaragala shocked all of us. Sadly, we see many such incidents happening around Sri Lanka regarding children mistreated at caring centres.

My wife and I, along with many others, often take meals/dana to children’s homes and elder homes. But we have noticed one troubling pattern. We are NOT allowed to speak to the children or mingle with elders.

The supervisors and caretakers keep a clear distance between donors and children. As soon as the food is served, the children are taken away. We are not even given 5 minutes to ask, “How are you?”

Why is this? Are they afraid that if we talk to the children, they will tell us about mistreatment, lack of food, or abuse?

Most donors go with a good heart to help. If we were allowed to speak to the children for even 10 minutes, with a supervisor present:

1.  The children will feel that someone outside cares for them and help is at hand away from the Hitlers controlling them.

2.  If there is any wrongdoing, we can immediately alert the Ministry of Social Services for corrective action.

Right now, it feels like we just hand over the food, get a receipt, and leave. No one knows what happens inside.

Please allow donor groups to interact with children for at least 10 minutes under supervision and CCTV. Transparency is the only way to ensure child safety.

Without transparency, there is no protection.  To prevent another Monaragala incident, all of us including the ministry need to open our eyes.
