Israel and its little empire in turmoil: Deadlocked elections  leave Israel facing  possible civil strife

Let me explain --  else,  we may have the non-resident Israeli Ambassador based in New Delhi together with his boss, the US Ambassador in Colombo, rushing into our news office taking umbrage at our headline and accusing us of ‘fake news’. But that in fact, is the reality facing Israeli society today.

Israel’s empire consists of the Palestinian territories, which they control with great brutality, having imposed a system of apartheid on its occupants. In addition,  basics such as drinking water are controlled. Building of houses is also under the control of the occupiers. Travel is restricted, and particular roads blocked with no rhyme or reason.

Since Israel commenced its war on unarmed Palestinian civilians in October 2023,  over 75,000 Palestinians have been killed –this excludes the thousands still buried under the rubble, or missing. In  June 2026,  a UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry reported Israeli forces had killed approximately 20,000 children and injured 44,000 more.

The Palestinian city of Gaza has been reduced to rubble. According to the World Health Organisation (WHO) and the UN Office for the Co-ordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), 34 out of 36 hospitals (94%) in the Gaza Strip have been damaged. Roughly half  the health facilities are either partially functioning or completely out of service.

UNICEF reported that nearly 97% to 98% of school buildings and all universities in the Gaza Strip have been damaged or destroyed since October 2023.

Meanwhile,  in Israel itself, large sections of the population  have turned against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu because he has refused to take any personal responsibility for the Oct. 7 security lapse. Reports indicate that he had advance warning of a major  Hamas attack. This factored into his poor election showing.

Many believed that democracy in Israel was under attack, and they particularly opposed the effort by Netanyahu’s government to weaken the power and independence of the Israeli judiciary, which sparked massive street protests in 2023.

They also resented Netanyahu’s government allowing ultra-Orthodox men to avoid military service, despite the Israeli military’s growing manpower shortage. On a personal level, Netanyahu is widely perceived as being dishonest. He is also still on trial for corruption.

It is in this context that the October elections were held. For the colonised people of Palestine,  there is little difference regarding whichever Israeli party wins the election –they are both dedicated to the ethnic cleansing of Palestine and continued genocide of Palestinians.  That election itself has now come to an end,  and no party has received sufficient numbers to form a government.

Benjamin Netanyahu, the outgoing Prime Minister,  and his extreme right-wing colleagues in the out-going regime have won 53 seats in the Knesset. The opposition faction, also a coalition of parties led by Gadi Eisenkot’s ‘Yashar Party’, won a total of 48 seats. The Palestinian-Israeli Parties (two Arab-majority lists, the Joint List and Ra’am) who form around 20% or more of Israel’s population won 19 seats.

Yet, to form a government, one or the other Israeli parties need at the least 61 seats in the Knesset to form a government… and therein lies the problem for both Netanyahu and Eisenkot.

On earlier occasions most Palestinians kept away from the voting on the ground it made no difference which Israeli party won. However, Netanyahu’s genocidal pogrom in Gaza made it imperative that Israeli Palestinians voted. They needed to ensure Netanyahu did not receive the sixty-one seats needed to form a new government.

As it is, Netanyahu will remain caretaker-prime minister until the stalemate ends. His Likud party and its partners in coalition won the largest number of seats in the Knesset, but lost their majority. On their own,  they cannot form a government. Netanyahu’s main rival –  Eisenkot - does not have sufficient seats to replace him.

Eisenkot has ruled out joining with Natanyahu’s coalition as long as he is linked with either Ben Gavir’s ‘Jewish Power’ party or Bezalel Smotrich’s ‘Religious Zionism’ party. His other option is to join hands with the Palestinian-Arab parties.  There is speculation that Eisenkot may come to an understanding with the Israeli-Palestinian group.  However, Eisenkot, has set three conditions for any party joining his coalition –  accepting Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, the values of the Declaration of Independence, and  a duty of service, military or civilian.

He has since added a fourth condition -the next government’s basic guidelines, he said, will state that whoever does not accept that Hamas is a terrorist organisation that must be dismantled, cannot be a partner in government.

There appears therefore, to be little chance of a coalition government between Eisenkot’s coalition of parties and the Palestinian-Israeli parties. However, there is a possibility of Eisenkot coming to an agreement with the Palestinian-Israeli parties to support his government while the latter remain in the opposition.

Although Eisenkot is the self-proclaimed ‘Voice of Change’, he is little different from Netanyahu on continuing the latter’s policy on Gaza, the invasion of Lebanon, Syria or the war in Iran.  Again, Eisenkot’s support for continuing attacks on Gaza, his support for the war on Iran and continuing attacks on alleged Iranian/Hamas/Hizbolla camps in Syria, Lebanon, makes such a plan a nonstarter.

On the other hand,  Netanyahu who is facing corruption charges and fighting to remain in power may feel it is to his benefit to end his partnership with Ben Gavir and Smotrich (who together have 13 seats) and join hands with Eisenkot’s group which has won 48 seats in the Knesset.

The fly in the ointment,  however,  is whether Gavir will be willing to simply walk away and give up his power and position. Reports from human rights organisations and watchdogs (such as the Association for Civil Rights in Israel or DAWN) note that thousands of private Israeli citizens and settlers have been provided arms and co-opted into a newly formed National Guard which is directly under Gavir’s control.

Numbering around 250,000, these armed civilians are under Gavir’s command. Reports from human rights organisations and watchdogs such as the Association for Civil Rights and DAWN confirm that thousands of private citizens and settlers have received firearms through these programmes,  and it is these groups more than the regular army which are carrying out porgroms in the West Bank.

This situation has created an alternate source of armed power. Critics say it could undermine security in the country.  A classified CIA assessment titled “Fracture From Within” warn that these deepening political, economic, and social pressures could place Israel at risk of civil war or state collapse.
