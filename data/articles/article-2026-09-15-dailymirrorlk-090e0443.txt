Retailers urged to go regional as uneven recovery leaves households behind

Poverty still double pre-crisis levels; real wages falling even as per capita GDP

Retailers squeezed between energy, tax and import costs and shoppers, who now compare prices online and in-store

Asset-light overseas models, FDI and private equity flagged as route past a 22mn-consumer ceiling

The Sri Lankan retailers and consumer brands must look beyond the confines of the domestic market, adopt asset-light international expansion strategies and tap foreign private capital to achieve sustainable growth, as the country’s macroeconomic stabilisation is yet to translate into a broad-based recovery for the households, the keynote speakers told Sri Lanka Retail Forum 2026.

Delivering a keynote address on macroeconomic productivity and retail bottlenecks at the forum, organised by the Sri Lanka Retailers’ Association, in Colombo, under the theme ‘Retail Without Boundaries: Building the Next Growth Engine’ yesterday, Centre for Poverty Analysis Consultant Dr. Roshan Perera pointed out that while the headline economic indicators have stabilised over the past three years, a significant divide persists between stabilisation and tangible economic expansion.

She noted that while the GDP per capita has returned to pre-crisis levels, the post-crisis rebound has been uneven, leaving the consumers with diminished purchasing power.

“Poverty has come down from where we were but it’s still double what we were prior to the crisis. There are still more than one in five people who are still living below the poverty line and the real wages have actually fallen, even as the per capita GDP has increased,” Dr. Perera said.

“What this implies really is that this recovery has not been necessarily evenly shared across the households. That’s something probably you retailers will feel, that the spending power or spending capacity of the households has probably declined.”

Compounding the consumer pressures is the resurgence of inflation, which Dr. Perera warned is once again trending above the Central Bank’s target band of 5 percent. She cautioned that this uptick is not merely transitory, as the underlying price pressures have broadened across the economy.

“The worrying thing is that core inflation, which sort of strips out fuel and food prices, is also moving above this 5 percent target. That is worrying because it suggests the pickup is not just in terms of food and fuel but it is a bit more broad-based and this will obviously matter directly for your own retail input costs and also for how long the Central Bank can keep the policy rates on hold,” she warned.

Dr. Perera stressed that the retailers are caught between the escalating external overheads such as the elevated energy tariffs, taxation and import costs and constrained domestic pricing power. Addressing whether the retailers can pass these rising expenses onto the buyers, she noted that the consumer dynamics have fundamentally shifted.

“I think the retailers are now very conscious of value and I don’t think you can just pass the costs to the consumers because there’s much more opportunity to shop around. It’s not just physical stores; there are online stores. Being more value-conscious, reducing costs, making your operations more efficient would be important for the retailers if you want to keep your customers,” she said.

With Sri Lanka’s consumer base limited to just 22 million people, smaller than metropolitan Mumbai, Dr. Perera urged the retailers to tap regional integration, leverage the bilateral free trade agreements and plug into the regional value chains, pointing to India’s over 400-million-strong middle class as an accessible supply and consumer base.

Echoing these sentiments, fellow keynote speaker Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu India LLP Partner Soumya Dwibedi urged the local businesses to capitalise on the global supply chain realignments, including the ‘China Plus One’ and ‘India Plus One’ shifts, to build internationally competitive enterprises.

Drawing parallels to the emerging market success stories such as the Philippines’ Jollibee, Brazil’s Havaianas, South Korea’s beauty sector and Sri Lanka’s own Dilmah, Dwibedi asserted that the local brands do not need to dilute their native identity to succeed overseas.

“Build an international brand but you don’t need to look international,” Dwibedi said, emphasising that the authentic domestic heritage and storytelling resonate globally when paired with the local customisation and hero products.

Addressing the capital constraints within the domestic financial system, Dwibedi urged the retailers to leverage their domestic proof-of-concept to attract foreign direct investment (FDI) and private equity to scale the regional operations.

“If you’re worried about capital, you can tap outside international capital. You can tap international capital through partnerships, joint ventures or strategic M&A,” Dwibedi noted.

“It’s almost like you’re going to a country, telling that private equity: ‘I am from Sri Lanka. I have done this. I have this product proposition. I took it to the market. I want the money to expand.’ The difference is that you have a proven track record in Sri Lanka.”

Dwibedi also pointed to Sri Lanka’s reviving tourism sector as an underutilised springboard for global brand trial and digital customer acquisition. Recounting his personal habit of purchasing local herbal wellness remedies, such as the herbal tea sachets for sore throats, during visits to the island, he highlighted how the tourist footfall can initiate a broader international discovery cycle.

“What you’re doing is using tourists as the first sampling point. If they like it, they will do digital discovery when they are back. The tourism can lead to trial; trial can lead to good memory; they can share it digitally. Others discover it digitally and you create an engine loop for a global brand,” he said.

To navigate the immediate domestic headwinds while preparing for foreign expansion, Dwibedi advised the retailers to adopt the asset-light digital marketplace models abroad, invest in daily supply chain visibility and auto-replenishment and embed artificial intelligence into product discovery to protect margins and retain customer loyalty.
