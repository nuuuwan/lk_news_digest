Netherlands to return two historic Kandyan guns to Sri Lanka

Colombo, Nov. 26 (Daily Mirror) - The Government of the Netherlands has decided to return two historic 18th-century Sri Lankan firearms, known as Maha Thuwakku (rampart guns), which were taken from the Kingdom of Kandy during the Dutch colonial period.

The two weapons, dating back more than 250 years, are currently housed at the Rijksmuseum, the Netherlands’ national museum in Amsterdam.

The decision was approved by Dutch Minister of Education, Culture and Science Rianne Letschert and announced by Dutch Ambassador to Sri Lanka Wiebe de Boer.

The repatriation follows joint provenance research conducted by Rijksmuseum specialists and Sri Lankan scholars, which established that the firearms originated in Sri Lanka.

Researchers identified the weapons as traditional Sri Lankan Maha Thuwakku and found historical evidence suggesting that they were captured during the 1765 war between the Dutch East India Company (VOC) and the Kingdom of Kandy before being taken to the Netherlands.

One of the firearms also bears inscriptions associated with the King of Kandy, further supporting its historical connection to the island.

The two weapons share similarities with two historic firearms previously returned to Sri Lanka by the Netherlands in 2023.

Arrangements for the formal return of the two cultural heritage artefacts will be made in due course.
