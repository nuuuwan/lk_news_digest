Court of Appeal fixes beer production petition for October 2

Colombo, July 30 (Daily Mirror) - The Court of Appeal today ordered that a writ petition seeking an order restraining the Excise Commissioner General and other relevant authorities from approving the use of rice and sugar as primary ingredients in beer production be taken up on October 2 for the filing of objections.

The petition was called before a bench comprising Justices R. Gurusinghe and A. Prema Shankar.

Counsel appearing for several respondent liquor manufacturing companies requested the Court to grant a date for the filing of objections in relation to the petition.

Accordingly, the Court granted the respondents permission to file their objections and directed that the petition be called again on October 2.

The petition has been filed by a group of licensed liquor sellers, naming the Commissioner General of Excise and relevant officials, the Sri Lanka Standards Institution, the Attorney General and the Inspector General of Police, among others, as respondents.

The petitioners state that Section 2 of the Excise Ordinance defines “beer” as a fermented alcoholic beverage made from malt. However, they claim that information has emerged indicating that many manufacturers are currently using rice and sugar as the primary raw materials in beer production.

They contend that such practices are contrary to the provisions of the Excise Ordinance.

The petitioners further argue that, at a time when the Government is providing substantial subsidies from public funds for rice production, diverting rice for commercial industries such as beer manufacturing could have a serious adverse impact on the national economy and undermine the country’s efforts to achieve self-sufficiency in rice production.

Accordingly, the petitioners have sought an order restraining the respondents, including the Commissioner General of Excise, from granting approval for the use of rice and sugar as primary raw materials in the production of beer.
