Minneriya National Park earns over Rs. 10 mn daily from elephant tourism, court told

Colombo, Nov. 26 (Daily Mirror) - Senior Attorney-at-Law Vijitha Wickramanayake, appearing on behalf of the Sri Lankan chapter of Protect Elephant World Global Association, told court that foreign tourists visit Sri Lanka to see living elephants in their natural habitats, not merely the tusks of dead elephants.

He made the remarks while opposing the handling of the investigation into the shooting of the tusker and highlighting the importance of protecting the country's elephant population.

Meanwhile, Mahawa Magistrate Anuradhika Senaviratne rejected a request by lawyers to release the two suspects in the shooting incident on bail under any conditions and ordered that they be further remanded until December 9.

The Magistrate also directed CID officers to appear before court on that date to conduct the prosecution.

Attorney-at-Law Wickramanayake also pointed out that although the court had ordered CID officers on November 11 to appear and conduct the prosecution, they had failed to appear yesterday. He accused the CID of failing to comply with the court's directive.

Appearing for the suspects, Attorney-at-Law Udaya Rajapaksa said his clients had been in remand custody for 37 days.

He told court that the Government Analyst's reports on the firearms and ammunition seized during the investigation had been received. Although the reports confirmed that the firearms had been used to fire bullets in the past, they had not established that the bullets recovered from the abdomen of the Kavanthissa tusker matched the firearms seized from his clients.

Citing this as an exceptional circumstance, he sought the release of the suspects on bail under any conditions in terms of the Bail Act.

However, after considering submissions made by all parties, the Magistrate rejected the bail application.
