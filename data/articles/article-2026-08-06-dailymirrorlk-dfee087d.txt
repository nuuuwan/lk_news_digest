ASPI surges for a second day as turnover hits LKR 12.24 Bn

The Colombo Stock Exchange extended its positive momentum for a second consecutive session on Thursday, 6 August 2026, as broad-based buying interest lifted both benchmark indices despite another substantial foreign outflow.

The All Share Price Index gained 102.73 points, or 0.49%, to close at 21,269.67, while the S&P Sri Lanka 20 Index advanced by 26.40 points, or 0.44%, to end the session at 5,971.50.

The ASPI rose strongly during the morning and briefly approached the 21,300 level before easing from its intraday high. The S&P SL20 also remained firmly positive throughout the session, supported by gains in several large-cap counters.

Total market turnover surged to LKR 12.24 billion, with approximately 231.09 million shares changing hands. However, the exceptionally high level of activity was largely driven by sizeable crossing transactions in Commercial Credit and Finance PLC and United Motors Lanka PLC.

Crossing transactions generated approximately LKR 10.15 billion, accounting for nearly 83% of total equity turnover. Commercial Credit recorded the largest combined crossing activity, with transactions valued at LKR 9.03 billion involving approximately 89.78 million shares.

The Diversified Financials sector dominated market activity, generating LKR 10.00 billion in turnover from 157.67 million shares. Commercial Credit accounted for the majority of the sector’s activity, recording LKR 9.38 billion in turnover from approximately 93.0 million shares.

Commercial Credit also emerged as the highest individual turnover generator for the session. The counter gained 1.65% to close at LKR 107.50.

Despite the positive market performance, foreign investors remained significant net sellers, recording a net foreign outflow of LKR 7.97 billion. The heavy foreign selling was largely associated with the high-value crossing transactions executed during the session.

Market breadth remained positive, with 135 securities advancing against 78 decliners, indicating that buying interest was distributed across a broad range of counters.

Central Finance was the strongest positive contributor to the ASPI, adding 13.56 points to the index. Commercial Bank, Cargills, C T Holdings and Hayleys also supported the market’s advance.

Ceylinco Insurance was the largest negative contributor, reducing the ASPI by 7.86 points. Bukit Darah, Ceylinco Insurance non-voting, Hatton National Bank and Royal Ceramics also weighed on the index.

Overall, the session reflected strengthening domestic investor sentiment and broad-based buying interest. However, the high concentration of turnover in crossing transactions and the substantial foreign outflow suggested that underlying market participation remained selective.
