CSE, GGGI step up push for sustainable bonds

Sri Lanka’s sustainable debt market is set for a fresh push, with the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) and Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI) opening a new technical assistance programme aimed at bringing more Green, Social, Sustainable and Sustainability-Linked (GSS+) bonds to the market.

GSS+ bonds gain greater traction as an alternative source of long-term capital, with 12 issuances raising more than Rs. 92 billion since the CSE introduced the instruments in 2024.

Significantly, GSS+ instruments accounted for 40 percent of debt capital raised in 2025, underscoring their growing role in the domestic capital market.

Under the latest initiative, prospective issuers can apply for technical assistance from GGGI to support the development and eventual listing of GSS+ bonds. Applications will remain open until 26 October 2026.

Selected issuers will receive needs-based support covering the development of GSS+ bond frameworks, coordination of Second Party Opinions (SPOs), post-issuance allocation and impact reporting during the first year, as well as capacity building.

All technical assistance under the programme, including the cost of obtaining a Second Party Opinion, will be provided free of charge.

The initiative could help lower some of the technical and preparatory barriers faced by companies and institutions considering thematic debt issuance, particularly the specialised requirements associated with establishing credible frameworks and demonstrating how proceeds are allocated and their impact measured.

GGGI has already played a role in developing Sri Lanka’s sustainable finance market. Through the Global Trust Fund on Sustainable Finance Instruments, funded by the Government of Luxembourg, it previously supported three thematic bond issuances in Sri Lanka, including the country’s first blue bond.

Those issuances collectively mobilised more than US$ 110 million for climate-aligned investments, according to the CSE. Two of the institutions supported through the programme subsequently received recognition at the 2026 Environmental Finance Sustainable Debt Awards.

GGGI, together with the United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific, has also supported the Finance Ministry in designing Sri Lanka’s first Sovereign Green Bond Framework.

The CSE said the domestic market is also gaining international visibility, with three Sri Lankan institutions receiving recognition at the 2026 Environmental Finance Sustainable Debt Awards for GSS+ bond issuances.

The latest programme is expected to broaden the prospective issuer pipeline by providing institutions considering sustainable debt instruments with technical support before entering the market.
