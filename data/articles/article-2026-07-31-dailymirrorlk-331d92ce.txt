Dengue:  Why Must We Wait for Deaths Before Acting?

It is both tragic and unacceptable that only after the first dengue deaths are reported that we suddenly spring into action. Yet we know only too well that dengue returns every year with the monsoon. Why, then, do we spend the other twelve months in deep slumber, waiting for innocent lives to be lost before waking up?

This collective failure extends far beyond households. Local authorities neglect drains and garbage disposal. The Road Development Authority leaves blocked drains unattended or cleans them only to dump the debris by the roadside. The Electricity Board stores discarded lamp posts and other materials that collect stagnant water. Transport Board depots and garages are notorious mosquito breeding grounds. Hundreds of fast-food outlets carelessly discard plastic containers that become ideal breeding sites after rain.

Above all, this reflects a serious failure by the Government to coordinate the many institutions responsible for dengue prevention. Prevention cannot be a seasonal publicity exercise. It must be a year-round national effort.

Equally disappointing is the apparent disappearance of the much-publicised “Clean Sri Lanka” campaign. We vividly remember Ministers, local politicians and government supporters enthusiastically cleaning bus stands, railway stations and roadside drains before television cameras. Today, that enthusiasm appears to have vanished, while dengue continues to claim lives.

Dengue is not an unexpected disaster. It is an annual, predictable threat. The real question is why the authorities continue to react only after deaths occur, instead of preventing them in the first place. Sri Lankans deserve a permanent, coordinated and accountable dengue control programme — not another cycle of panic, publicity and neglect.
