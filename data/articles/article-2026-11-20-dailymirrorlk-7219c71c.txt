Central Bank hikes policy rate to 9% as inflation surges

Colombo, Nov. 20 (Daily Mirror) - The Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) has increased its policy interest rate by 25 basis points, raising it from 8.75% to 9%, amid mounting inflationary pressures driven by higher energy and food prices.

The decision was taken at a meeting of the Monetary Policy Board held yesterday (Nov. 19), following an assessment of domestic and global economic developments and the outlook, the Central Bank announced.

The CBSL said the prolonged conflict in the Middle East, which has continued beyond initial expectations, has kept global energy prices elevated. Together with rising food prices, this has intensified inflationary pressures in Sri Lanka.

Accordingly, year-on-year headline inflation rose to 8.3% in October 2026, while core inflation also increased due to the impact of higher energy and food costs.

Although global energy prices have eased somewhat in recent weeks, the Central Bank warned that inflationary pressures could persist in the coming period due to the second-round effects of previous price increases.

Based on the latest projections, inflation is expected to remain higher than previously anticipated and persist for a longer period before returning to the targeted level of 5% in the second half of 2027.

Meanwhile, the Central Bank noted that market interest rates had adjusted significantly following the monetary policy tightening measures introduced in May 2026.
