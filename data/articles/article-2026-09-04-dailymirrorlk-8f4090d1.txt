Dengue cases fall, but Sri Lanka not out of the woods

Colombo, Sep. 4 (Daily Mirror) - Although the number of dengue cases has declined significantly, the risk of the disease remains, with health authorities urging the public to continue taking precautions against mosquito breeding.

Community Physician Dr. Prashila Samaraweera of the National Dengue Control Unit said dengue infections were currently being reported at a relatively low level, but the situation should not lead to complacency.

Speaking at a media briefing held at the Health Promotion Bureau, she urged the public to remain vigilant and regularly inspect their homes and surrounding areas for places where dengue mosquitoes could breed.

Dr. Samaraweera said the mosquito population tends to increase in some districts before a corresponding rise in dengue cases is recorded.

She therefore stressed the importance of taking preventive measures even when the number of reported infections is relatively low.

Meanwhile, hospital sources told the Daily Mirror that dengue treatment wards at several state hospitals continue to face congestion despite the noticeable decline in daily infections.

According to official data, the number of daily dengue cases, which had peaked at around 1,000, has now dropped to approximately 500.

However, hospital sources said the decline has not yet been sufficient to ease the pressure on the health system.

Health officials warned that a reduction in reported cases does not mean the threat has disappeared, particularly as changes in mosquito populations in certain areas could be followed by an increase in infections.

They urged the public to continue eliminating stagnant water and other potential breeding sites around homes, workplaces and public places to prevent a resurgence of dengue.
