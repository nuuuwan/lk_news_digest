UN and the Govt. of Sri Lanka strengthen dialogue to build trust and support inclusive recovery

Colombo, Nov. 25 (Daily Mirror) - The United Nations and the Government of Sri Lanka concluded a two-year joint initiative aimed at fostering social cohesion and supporting an inclusive economic recovery by strengthening dialogue across public institutions, private workplaces, and local communities.

A central milestone of the ‘Social Dialogue for Peace and Crisis Prevention in Sri Lanka’ project was the adoption of a Declaration on Social Dialogue in the Public Service, which establishes South Asia’s first national framework for structured dialogue within the state sector.

Launched in response to the socio-economic pressures of Sri Lanka’s economic crisis, where higher taxes, rising living costs, and reduced purchasing power have strained households, the initiative sought to prevent disputes from escalating by bringing government officials, employers, workers, and community members to the same table.

At a closing event at Hotel Cinnamon Grand on Friday, state representatives, private sector leaders, trade unions,[1] civil society, and development partners, stakeholders reviewed the programme’s outcomes and outlined strategies to sustain the mechanisms long-term.

“Sri Lanka’s recovery must deliver better public services, fairer conditions at work and greater opportunity for communities to shape their future,” said Marc-André Franche, UN Resident Coordinator in Sri Lanka. “This initiative puts dialogue at the heart of that effort, bringing people together to resolve concerns before they deepen into divisions. The responsibility now is to turn listening into action, so that people see their concerns addressed and have reason to trust the institutions that serve them.”

Speaking at the formal conclusion of the project "Social Dialogue for Peace and Crisis Prevention in Sri Lanka[2] ”, S Alokabandara Secretary, Ministry of Public Administration, Provincial Councils and Local Government stated that "the experience throughout this initiative has confirmed a fundamental governance principle that social dialogue is not merely a labour management exercise; it is a vital instrument for human security, social cohesion, and peacebuilding"

Funded by the UN Sri Lanka SDG Fund, the project was jointly implemented by the International Labour Organisation (ILO), the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organisation (UNESCO), and the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA).

Working alongside the Ministry of Public Administration, the initiative focused on giving a voice to Sri Lanka’s roughly 1.4 million public service workers through a new three-tier escalation ladder:

Workplace Level: 87 institutional forums were created to address immediate operational issues, ranging from promotion processes to workplace safety. Sectoral Level: Seven sectoral forums were established to handle complex disputes that cannot be resolved at the institutional level. National Level: The newly launched National Public Service Dialogue Forum addresses cross-sectoral issues requiring central government intervention.

In the private and plantation sectors, project partnered with key industry and labour bodies, including the Employers’ Federation of Ceylon (EFC), the UN Global Compact Network Sri Lanka, the National Chamber of Commerce of Sri Lanka (NCCSL), and the National Labour Advisory Council (NLAC), to advance gender equality and workplace safety.

The efforts included strengthening grievance mechanisms to prevent and respond to workplace harassment and promoting women's leadership. In the plantations sector, senior managers and frontline officers were trained to adopt gender-responsive practices, while Labour Tribunal Judges were engaged to ensure alternate dispute resolution mechanisms[3] and gender-related issues[4] are factored into the adjudication of labour disputes.

With roughly two-thirds of Sri Lanka’s workforce operating within the informal economy, the project also targeted grassroots participation. The project partnered with the Centre for Poverty Analysis (CEPA), the Coalition for Inclusive Impact, and World Vision Lanka to support 33 community forums across the Gampaha, Badulla, and Batticaloa districts.

Over 180 forum members received policy and advocacy training to better articulate local priorities to district-level authorities. As a result, workers in fisheries, agriculture, and plantations now have direct channels to raise concerns with district officials, ensuring grassroots realities help inform national policymaking.

As the formal project concludes, UN agencies and government partners emphasised that maintaining active engagement across all tiers of society will be critical to preserving these trust-building gains and advancing long-term stability in Sri Lanka.
