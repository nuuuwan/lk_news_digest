UL 503 incident triggers fresh measures against bird strikes

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - In the wake of the latest bird strike which damaged the jet engine blades of SriLankan Airlines flight UL 503 bound to London, airport authorities, along with the relevant stakeholders, have stepped up action to ensure aviation safety against possible wildlife incidents.

The pilots were compelled to abort the take-off after a bird identified as a Sea Eagle struck the engine of the plane. A top aviation source said that the engine could be repaired at a cost, as a few blades had been damaged. An insurance claim could be made for the repair, the source said.

The source said that the Ministry of Civil Aviation had appointed a committee some time ago to take measures for wildlife hazard management at airports, and that it would pay extra attention to the matter.

The committee comprises representatives of the Department of Wildlife Conservation, the Civil Aviation Authority, Airport and Aviation Services, and the Ministry. According to the source, recommending steps such as culling birds or shooting animals such as dogs in the event of runway incursions is not feasible in Sri Lanka due to pressure from animal rights activists and cultural considerations.

Bird strikes are among the most common accidents in the aviation sector. The Bandaranaike International Airport is the main airport in Sri Lanka. Besides, airports in Mattala, Batticaloa and Ratmalana have also been declared international airports.

Asked for comments, Minister of Ports, Civil Aviation and Energy Aruna Karunatilake said that the committee was coordinating with the relevant stakeholders to avert hazards posed by all kinds of wild animals. He said that attention had also been paid to avoiding any danger posed by wild elephants to the Mattala airport.

The Mattala airport has been properly cordoned off to control peacocks. That threat is now under control, he said.

“We want to ensure safety from any kind of animal, be it birds or beasts,” he said.
