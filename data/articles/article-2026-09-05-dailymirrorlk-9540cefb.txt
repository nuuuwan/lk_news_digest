People’s Bank’s landmark CSR initiative to reduce elephant-train collisions

Secretary to President Dr. Nandika Sanath Kumanayake and People’s Bank  Chairman Chandana Guniyangoda exchange agreement

People’s Bank CEO and GM Clive Fonseka addresses gathering and People’s Bank director board members

Demonstrating its commitment to environmental sustainability and wildlife conservation, People’s Bank has launched a pioneering corporate social responsibility (CSR) initiative aimed at minimising elephant-train collisions in Sri Lanka.

The project, fully funded by People’s Bank, is implemented in collaboration with Clean Sri Lanka, Sri Lanka Railways, the Wildlife Conservation Department and Forest Conservation Department and brings together advanced technology and conservation expertise to address one of the country’s most critical wildlife conservation challenges.

This initiative is also aligned with the government’s Clean Sri Lanka Programme, supporting its broader vision of creating a sustainable and environmentally responsible nation. Initiating a three-year project to make this vision a reality, People’s Bank, Sri Lanka Railways, the Wildlife Conservation Department and Forest Conservation Department officially signed a memorandum of understanding on September 1, 2026.

Secretary to President Dr. Nandika Sanath Kumanayake, People’s Bank Chairman Chandana Guniyangoda, Chief Executive Officer and General Manager Clive Fonseka, members of the board of directors of People’s Bank and other officials participated in the event.

The Elephant-Train Collision Prevention Project seeks to significantly reduce elephant fatalities caused by train collisions through the deployment of an advanced early warning system at identified high-risk railway locations. The innovative system combines ground vibration sensors, acoustic sensors, FMCW radar, thermal and beam sensors, supported by solar power and long-range wireless communication (LoRa), to detect and confirm elephant movements on or near railway tracks.

Once an elephant is detected within a defined danger zone, the system provides timely warnings through trackside warning lights, in-cab alerts for train drivers and notifications to the railway control system, giving railway personnel valuable additional time to respond

The project will be implemented through a collaborative effort among the key stakeholders, each undertaking distinct responsibilities to ensure its success and long-term sustainability.

As the principal financier, People’s Bank will fund the procurement and installation of advanced technological infrastructure and essential equipment required for the project.

Sri Lanka Railways will support the construction of the necessary infrastructure, implement speed control measures, multi-aspect signalling systems and train locomotive drivers to adhere to enhanced safety protocols in identified high-risk areas.

The Wildlife Conservation Department will focus on habitat management initiatives, including the creation of artificial water sources and increasing food availability within forest habitats to reduce elephant movement near railway tracks. A Centralised Monitoring Station will oversee the operation of the system through regular performance monitoring, fault detection, maintenance coordination and reporting.

In addition, Sri Lanka Railways, the Wildlife Conservation Department and Forest Conservation Department will conduct awareness and educational programmes targeting railway personnel, local communities and the public to enhance understanding of elephant conservation and support for the proposed solution.

A total of 39 critical locations spanning approximately 89 kilometres of railway track have been identified as collision hotspots requiring the installation of the early warning system. During the first phase of the project, People’s Bank will fund the installation of 13 systems at priority locations identified by the Wildlife Conservation Department.

As the next step of the initiative, a pilot project will be implemented along a 14-kilometre railway stretch between Habarana and Hataraskotuwa, one of the most vulnerable areas for elephant-train collisions in the country.

Guniyangoda stated, “As a state-owned bank, People’s Bank is proud to support this vital national initiative. By bringing together technology, collaboration and conservation, we aim to contribute towards protecting Sri Lanka’s elephant population while enhancing railway safety and promoting sustainable development.”

Fonseka added, “The elephant population is an invaluable asset of our nation and preserving it is a cause that brings the entire nation together. As the pride of the nation, People’s Bank considers this initiative a national responsibility that reflects People’s Bank’s commitment to environmental sustainability and responsible corporate citizenship. Through our partnership with the Wildlife Conservation Department, Forest Conservation Department and Sri Lanka Railways, we are supporting an innovative solution that will help reduce elephant fatalities and promote safer railway operations.”
