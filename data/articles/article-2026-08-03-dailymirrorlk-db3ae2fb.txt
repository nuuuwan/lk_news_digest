Prison riots not isolated from systemic issues

This trauma might turn the prisoners riotous when the facilities in prisons are trimmed due to congestion

The National People’s Power (NPP) government is under pressure after the second major prison riot within a month took place on Saturday (August 1), causing extensive damage to the prison buildings and facilities within the premises.

However, unlike the Negombo prison riot that raged on July 5 and 6, leaving over 20 inmates and 8 prison officers, the human cost was minimal in the Mahara incident,  where only one inmate had lost his life.

The acute congestion in prisons that has created an unbearable situation in them is always blamed for such riots,  while the law delay is mainly blamed in turn for the congestion. Underscoring the severe delays plaguing the justice system, while speaking at a recent event Supreme Court Justice Yasantha Kodagoda said that completing a single cycle of a criminal case in Sri Lanka takes an average of 10 years and 2 months. As an example, he stated that if a burglary is reported today, the first phase of the case would only conclude in August 2036.

He highlighted that there are currently 1.1 million cases pending across the country’s courts. These are handled by just 333 courts, including 80 magistrate courts, two child magistrate courts, 64 district courts, 94 combined district magistrate courts, 53 High Courts, five commercial courts, 23 civil appellate High Courts, seven judges at the Court of Appeal, and five divisions of the Supreme Court. “If distributed evenly, each court would be handling over 3,300 cases,” he said.

These are not mere numbers. They point the severe trauma the prisoners, both remand and convicted, undergo. The trauma and the frustration are manyfold when the people who are remanded for a long time, especially under the draconian Prevention of Terrorism Act, for offences that they have never committed.

Vasanthi Ragupathy Sharma, a mother of three,  was remanded in July 27, 2000 (date of arrest likely to be earlier) as a suspect for the 1999 Town Hall bomb case and indicted in 2002. After 15 years, the High Court  determined she was not guilty. Another prisoner, Kandappu Rajasekar from Karaveddy in Jaffna who was also held for 12 years under the PTA,  was finally released after being acquitted of all charges on February 16, 2022.  Another inmate, a suspected LTTE activist from Akkaraipattu who was incarcerated for over 12 years, was similarly released from prison on October 11, 2021.

During a speech in Parliament on June 23, 2021,  the then Youth and Sports Minister Namal Rajapaksa said that some of those who have been detained under the PTA have been in remand prisons longer than his age (35 years then). However, it was his party, Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna (SLPP) that ruled the country for the succeeding three years, but they did not take action to resolve the issue.

The repercussions of the law’s delay are very serious. Former Justice Minister Mohamed Ali Sabri told Parliament on December 2, 2020 that 4,620 cases were being heard in Sri Lankan courts for more than 20 years while 3,418 were being heard for more than 15 years and 8,947 cases for more than 10 years.

Not all accused of these cases are languishing in prisons, but a court case against a person is a Sword of Damocles hanging over his head every day, especially if he is an accused.

The amendment brought in to the Poisons, Opium and Dangerous Drugs Ordinance in 2022 aggravated the situation as it allowed the Court of Appeal to hold exclusive jurisdiction to release a suspect on bail when the  quantity of the dangerous drug trafficked, imported, exported, or possessed is 10 grams or more. And determining the purity of drugs takes a long time due to shortage of staff at the Government Analyst Department.

This trauma might turn the prisoners riotous when the facilities in prisons are trimmed due to congestion. Sometimes, Outside influences, as Justice Minister Harshana Nanayakkara suspects, might be another stimulus for the prison riots. Therefore, the prison riots are not isolated incidents. They are the results of more serious issues in the administration of justice.
