Tourism earnings cross US$ 2bn but trail revised target

Tourism earnings crossed US$ 2bn in August, a month later than in 2025, leaving US$ 2.14bn to be earned in four months to meet the revised US$ 4.2bn target

Leisure travellers made up 70.84 percent of August arrivals, up from 59.22% a year earlier, while visits to friends and relatives rose to 14.09%

Sri Lanka’s tourism earnings crossed the US$ 2 billion mark in August, a month later than in 2025, but at US$ 2,061.1 million for the first eight months the sector remains well short of the pace needed to meet the revised US$ 4.2 billion annual target, as per Central Bank data.

Cumulative earnings for the period were 10 percent below the US$ 2,290 million recorded a year earlier, a shortfall of US$ 228.9 million, although arrivals declined by a comparatively modest 2 percent to 1,535,122.

Last year, earnings had already reached US$ 2,031.1 million by the end of July. As a result, estimated earnings per visitor slipped to about US$ 1,343 from US$ 1,462, a decline of 8.1 percent.

In August alone, earnings rose 2.1 percent year-on-year to US$ 264.4 million, even as arrivals fell 3.3 percent to 191,704.

The weaker cumulative performance follows a marked change in the composition of arrivals since the escalation in the Middle East on 28 February, after which arrivals fell 19.8 percent in March and 22.3 percent in April, as per SLTDA data. European arrivals declined 13 percent to 685,968 over the eight months, whereas Asia-Pacific arrivals rose 11.5 percent to 737,296, displacing Europe as the largest source region and coinciding with the decline in spending per visitor.

The same pattern held in August, when Asia-Pacific arrivals rose 5.8 percent to 94,729 while European arrivals fell 12.9 percent to 81,396. Australia and Japan recorded gains of 13.6 percent and 15.1 percent to 8,900 and 5,902 respectively, while India edged up 1.7 percent to 47,253 and France rose 3.7 percent to 10,885. In contrast, arrivals from Italy fell 27.5 percent to 8,883, the Netherlands 29.1 percent to 4,312, Spain 16 percent to 7,431 and Germany 8.8 percent to 11,401, while Russian arrivals more than halved to 1,639. Middle East arrivals, meanwhile, climbed 38.8 percent to 4,851.

Leisure travellers accounted for 70.84 percent of August arrivals, compared with 59.22 percent a year earlier, while the share of those visiting friends and relatives rose to 14.09 percent from 10.41 percent and MICE travel edged up to 8.01 percent from 7.51 percent. However, much of this change reflects a fall in unclassified responses to about 3 percent from 20 percent, which limits a direct comparison.

Meanwhile, the 2026 revenue target was lowered in August to US$4.2 billion from US$5.0 billion, alongside a cut in the arrivals goal to 2.7 million. Meeting it will require US$ 2,138.9 million between September and December, an average of about US$ 535 million a month, roughly double August’s receipts. Although arrivals and earnings typically rise in November and December with the onset of the winter season, even SLTDA’s conservative scenario of 2,500,220 arrivals for the year, which implies 965,098 visitors over the remaining four months, would yield about US$ 3.39 billion at August’s spending level, leaving a shortfall of some US$ 810 million.
