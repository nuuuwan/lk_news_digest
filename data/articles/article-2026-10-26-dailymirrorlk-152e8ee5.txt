CBSL reviews lenders’ early settlement fees and parate auctions after complaints pile up

CBSL reviewed early settlement and parate practices in the first half of 2026

The Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) carried out targeted reviews of how lenders charge borrowers for settling term loans early and how banks seize mortgaged property under parate execution, following a “significant volume of consumer complaints” in both areas, the Central Bank revealed.

The thematic reviews, conducted in the first half of 2026, covered the early settlement practices of licensed banks and finance companies on term loans and the parate execution process of licensed banks. However, the Central Bank did not disclose its findings, the number of institutions examined or any corrective action, while the major policy measures it listed from July 2025 to end July 2026 include no new rules on either issue.

Officials of the Central Bank’s Financial Consumer Relations Department wrote in June that complaints relating to fees and charges increased by approximately 40 percent between 2023 and 2025, particularly over items such as processing fees, documentation charges and early settlement fees. Licensed banks accounted for 79 percent of complaints over the period and finance companies for 21 percent, meaning banks drew nearly four times as many. Complaints over high lending rates, by contrast, declined significantly.

The officials pointed out that lenders “may need to strengthen disclosure practices” and “simplify fee structures”.

Early settlement charges fall under the Financial Consumer Protection Regulations No. 01 of 2023, in force since February 2024, which list “requiring payment of un-accrued (future) interest/ early settlement fees on credit facilities, exceeding the levels permitted by the Central Bank, if any” as an unfair business practice.

Meanwhile, parate execution allows banks to auction mortgaged property on a board resolution, without going to court, under the Recovery of Loans by Banks (Special Provisions) Act No. 4 of 1990. After the power was suspended in 2024, the government phased out relief for small and medium enterprises (SMEs) last year, ending it on June 30, 2025 for loans above Rs.50 million, September 30 for loans of Rs.25 million to Rs.50 million and December 31 for smaller loans, for borrowers who had begun talks with their banks by March 31, 2025.

The first half of 2026 was thus the first period since early 2024 without any SME relief window.

Banks maintain the power is rarely used, with the Sri Lanka Banks’ Association stating in July 2025 that it was applied to only about 1 percent of non-performing loans between 2019 and 2023.

Borrowers are also under greater pressure, with the Central Bank raising its policy rate in May 2026 and banks’ Stage 3 loans recording slight increases at the end of both the first and second quarters of 2026, following Cyclone Ditwah, higher fuel prices and the Middle East conflict.
