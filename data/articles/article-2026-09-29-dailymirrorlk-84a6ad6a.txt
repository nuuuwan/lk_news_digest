Former President Sirisena calls hospital explosion his ‘most painful day’

Colombo, Sep. 29 (Daily Mirror) - Former President Maithripala Sirisena has expressed deep sorrow over the recent explosion at the National Nephrology (Kidney) Hospital in Polonnaruwa, describing the institution as a facility he had closely supported and protected during his tenure.

The hospital provides specialised treatment to kidney patients from across Sri Lanka and was established following a request made by former president Sirisena to Chinese President Xi Jinping during a visit to China. Sirisena also commended the hospital’s directors, doctors, nurses and technical staff for their dedicated service since the facility was established.

Sirisena said the opening of the Moragahakanda Reservoir had been among the happiest moments of his life, while the explosion at the nephrology hospital was among the most painful. He expressed condolences over the death of an employee in the incident and wished a speedy recovery to staff members who sustained serious injuries.

Explaining why he did not immediately visit the hospital after the explosion, Sirisena said the situation at the site had initially resembled a “war zone”, as hospital staff were engaged in transferring patients and securing the premises. He said he did not want to interfere with the emergency response and said that the following days coincided with Poson and weekend holidays.

Sirisena said he discussed with President Anura Kumara Dissanayake on Monday morning regarding the situation and recommended that the military be deployed to undertake the reconstruction work rather than relying on private contractors.

According to Sirisena, he cited the experience of using the armed forces to implement major development projects during his presidency. He said President Dissanayake agreed with the proposal and undertook to involve the Army in the reconstruction effort.

Sirisena also said he had met the newly appointed Chinese Ambassador at his residence two days before the explosion, where they discussed the hospital and plans for a joint visit. Following the incident, he said he informed the ambassador and wrote to Health Minister Nalinda Jayatissa requesting urgent action.
