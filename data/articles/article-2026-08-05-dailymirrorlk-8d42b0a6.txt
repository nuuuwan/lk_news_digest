T-bill auction yields ease across board

The secondary market witnessed strong buying interest, resulting in a downward shift in yields across the curve amid elevated trading activity and volumes.

Market participants primarily focused on the 2030 and 2031 maturities.

Driven by robust buying interest, the 15.10.2029 and 15.12.2029 maturities traded within the 10.95 percent to 11.00 percent range. Meanwhile, the 01.03.2030, 15.05.2030, 01.07.2030, 01.08.2030 and 15.10.2030 maturities changed hands between 11.10 percent and 11.40 percent.

The 01.12.2031 maturity was traded at 11.40 percent. In the 2032 segment, the 01.07.2032 and 15.12.2032 maturities traded within the 11.85 percent to 11.90 percent range.

Further along the yield curve, the 15.01.2033 maturity traded at 12.00 percent, while the 15.10.2034, 15.08.2036 and 01.07.2037 maturities were traded at 12.15 percent, 12.60 percent and 12.70 percent, respectively.

Moreover, the PDMO concluded its weekly T-bill auction, successfully raising the full initial offer of Rs.140.0 billion. The three-month and six-month bills raised Rs.72.8 billion and Rs.50.0 billion, respectively.

The 12-month bill raised Rs.17.2 billion, lower than the initial offer of Rs.35.0 billion. The weighted average yields of the three-month, six-month and 12-month bills adjusted down by 9bps, 22bps and 1bp to 9.77 percent, 9.99 percent and 10.19 percent, respectively.

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee slightly depreciated against the US dollar, standing at Rs.335.73/US dollar, compared to Rs.335.72/US dollar seen earlier. Overnight liquidity in the banking system contracted to Rs.165.38 billion, from Rs.204.74 billion recorded previously.
