Bottlenecks and  Inconsistencies in the Application of  Government Policies

On a recent visit to Geneva, I met a Sri Lankan who had undergone a harrowing experience upon his arrival from Geneva at Bandaranaike International Airport. He was subjected to lengthy interrogation at the Immigration Counter, followed by being detained in the office of the Chief Immigration Officer, simply because his passport carried an endorsement stating that it was valid only for entry into Sri Lanka.

The Consular Section of the Sri Lankan Mission had already advised him to obtain a new passport after arriving in Colombo and had never given the slightest indication that he would face any difficulties at the airport.

Another passenger on the same flight witnessed the entire incident and volunteered to assist the distressed traveller, who had been held for a considerable period. When the Chief Immigration Officer failed to provide a clear explanation for delaying the passenger’s entry into the country, the Good Samaritan produced his United Nations identity card and pointed out that the treatment of the passenger was contrary to both the principles of human rights and the government’s stated policy of encouraging expatriate Sri Lankans to return home and contribute through investment and economic development.

The Chief Immigration Officer immediately appeared to recognise the seriousness of the unnecessary harassment and released the passenger, advising him to obtain a new passport before returning to Switzerland. This humane advice could, and should, have been given by the immigration counter staff in the first place, thereby avoiding the needless distress and embarrassment.

I presume this incident is not an isolated case. As an urgent measure, the Government should advise and facilitate Sri Lankan embassies and diplomatic missions abroad to issue new passports to bona fide expatriate Sri Lankans who wish to return home and rebuild their lives with their savings.
