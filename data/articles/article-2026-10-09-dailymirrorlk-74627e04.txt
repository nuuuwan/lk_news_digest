Govt. rushes to amend prison attire rules for monks

Colombo, Oct. 9 (Daily Mirror)  - In what appeared to be a sign of succumbing to public outrage and pressure from the Maha Sangha over the stripping of Ven. Galagoda Aththe Gnanasara Thera of his robe, the Government is rushing to amend the laws and regulations relating to the prison attire of convicts, the Daily Mirror learns.

Ven. Gnanasara Thera, who was convicted of contempt of court, was brought before court in prison attire instead of his religious clothing.

The move triggered an angry reaction and protests from a section of Sri Lanka’s Buddhist community and the Maha Sangha. The Mahanayake Theras of the four main chapters wrote to President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, saying that only they could decide on stripping a monk of his saffron robe after an investigation conducted in keeping with Buddhist traditions and customs.

They highlighted that prison authorities had no such role whatsoever.

Against this backdrop, the President decided to amend the Prisons Ordinance to give more flexibility to the authorities in determining the attire of saffron-clad personalities convicted by a court of law.

An informed source said a committee had been appointed for this purpose. It is expected to meet today and complete the task as early as possible, as the controversy continues to swirl.
