Patrick Stewart, Shakespearean actor turned ’Star Trek’ star, dies at 86

Reuters - Patrick Stewart, who rose from poverty in England to become an accomplished Shakespearean stage actor and then achieved global stardom as the erudite Captain Jean-Luc Picard in TV's "Star Trek: The Next Generation" and the telepathic Professor Charles Xavier ​in the "X-Men" movies, has died at the age of 86.

Stewart, knighted in 2010 by Queen Elizabeth II for his contributions to drama, died at his home in Los Angeles, according to a statement on ‌his X account.

"We announce with great sorrow the passing of Sir Patrick Stewart yesterday at his home in Los Angeles, in the loving care of his beloved wife Sunny, following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer's Disease and Lewy Body Dementia," it said.

Stewart became an actor at age 18 and gained acclaim on the stage as a member of the Royal Shakespeare Company beginning in the 1960s. But it was not until after he was hired at age 47 for "Star Trek: The Next Generation" — a TV series that ran from 1987 to 1994 as a sequel to ​the original 1960s "Star Trek" — that he gained mainstream fame, acting into his 80s.

"Make it so," his character Picard often said, instructing a subordinate to carry out a command, or sometimes deploying the similar catchphrase, "Engage."

It was a role ​he had debated even taking after his longtime friend and fellow Shakespearean Ian McKellen advised him to spurn "Star Trek" and remain in theater. Stewart did take the role and became both ⁠a fan favorite and an indispensable part of a lucrative sci-fi franchise, playing Picard not only in 176 episodes over seven seasons of that series but also in several "Star Trek" films and in the later streaming series "Star Trek: Picard" that began ​airing in 2020 when he was 79.

"As the seven seasons of TNG ("The Next Generation") went by, the distinction between Jean-Luc Picard and Patrick Stewart became thinner and thinner, until it was impossible for me to know where he left off and I ​began. So much of what I believed and felt went into that show," Stewart told the Guardian newspaper in 2022.

Stewart also initially balked at being cast as the wheelchair-using mutant Professor X in the "X-Men" movies, based on the popular Marvel comic book character.

"I said, 'Look, I know this isn't science fiction, but it's fantasy, and I've done that.' But they persuaded me that it wouldn't be like 'Star Trek,' so I did it. And yet again I was proved wrong," Stewart told the Guardian.

He became a critical part of another big-money franchise, starting with the first entry "X-Men" in ​2000 and followed by several more, including the 2002 Marvel movie "Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness." He appeared alongside McKellen, who played the villain Magneto, in some of the "X-Men" films.

Stewart, playing Charles Xavier, took part in the filming of the ​highly anticipated "Avengers: Doomsday" and will appear in the Marvel film that is due to hit theaters in December. Marvel Studios paid tribute to Stewart on Thursday in a statement, calling him "a singular talent, a generous collaborator and a true gentleman."
