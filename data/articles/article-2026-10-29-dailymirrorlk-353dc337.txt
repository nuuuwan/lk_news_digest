Medawachchiya elephant calf shows signs of recovery

Colombo, Oct. 29 (Daily Mirror) - A six-year-old elephant calf that had been lying on the ground for several days due to a neurological disorder in the Wewalketiya area of Medawachchiya is showing signs of recovery following treatment by wildlife veterinarians.

The male calf was initially treated by the Pandulagama Veterinary Unit of the Wildlife Conservation Department before being transferred to the Udawalawe Elephant Transit Home on October 22 for further treatment.

Upon arrival, the calf received urgent medical attention and was kept in an upright position for several days with the assistance of specially designed support structures under the supervision of the transit home's Chief Veterinary Officer, Malaka Abeywardena.

Wildlife veterinarians have reported that the calf is gradually recovering and is now eating and drinking well.

The support structures used to keep the calf upright have since been removed, while veterinary teams continue to provide the necessary medical care. The calf is currently being kept in an enclosure at the Ath Athuru Sevana Elephant Transit Home and will be monitored using a radio-tracking collar.

Meanwhile, the Environment, the Department of Wildlife Conservation Ministry and the Armed Forces have jointly launched a special operation to strengthen the protection of Sri Lanka's wild elephant population.

Under the initiative, military personnel are being deployed to monitor selected tuskers considered a national asset, using drone technology and specialised surveillance methods.

Authorities are also taking steps to monitor wild elephant habitats across the country and take legal action against individuals involved in illegal activities in violation of the Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance.
