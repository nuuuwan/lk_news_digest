112 new Metro buses deployed on seven Western Province routes

Colombo, Sep. 29 (Daily Mirror) - A total of 112 newly introduced Metro buses were deployed yesterday (29) on seven routes in the Western Province, with the aim of easing traffic congestion in Colombo and providing a more comfortable and safer public transport service.

The buses were deployed at a ceremony held at the Thalangama Metro Bus Depot under the patronage of President Anura Kumara Dissanayake.

The modern Metro Bus service was initiated as a proposal in the 2025 Budget, with an allocation of Rs. 3 billion for the procurement of 100 comfortable low-floor buses.

Accordingly, 104 buses received by Sri Lanka and eight buses provided by the Government of Japan have been deployed under the new service.

The buses will operate along seven routes centred on Colombo according to designated routes and timetables, enabling passengers to reach their destinations on schedule.

One of the key objectives of introducing the service is to reduce the number of private vehicles entering Colombo, particularly vehicles carrying only a single passenger.

Transport, Highways and Urban Development Minister Bimal Rathnayake said public transport should be placed on the same level as Sri Lanka’s free healthcare and education systems.

He said the President had emphasised this requirement during a recent progress review meeting and that the President’s encouragement had been a major source of strength in taking forward the Metro Bus initiative.

Rathnayake said the Government had invested approximately Rs. 7.5 billion to establish the Metro service, describing the investment as a symbol of the country’s economic strength.

He said the Government had taken less than a year to complete the initiative while adhering to relevant government circulars.

The Minister said another 50 buses were expected to be introduced before the Sinhala and Hindu New Year and deployed on inter-city services to Jaffna, Kandy and Galle.

He said an urban transport permit system should be utilised as the Metro service expands to help reduce inter-city traffic congestion, while the Government also expects to bring the private sector into the programme.

Rathnayake also requested mayors to establish suitable parking facilities close to relevant bus stops to promote a Park-and-Ride system.

Meanwhile, Lanka Metro Transit (Pvt) Ltd, established to implement the Metro Bus programme, has completed the planning, procurement, infrastructure development and trial-operation stages.

The initial Metro Bus route network comprises seven main routes with a total corridor length of approximately 188.7 kilometres.

A fully digitalised system for Metro Bus operations has also been designed, with the procurement process now under way. The system is expected to support modern fleet management, passenger information, fare collection and operational monitoring.

Plans for Metro Bus depots have also been completed, while construction and infrastructure development work has commenced.

The Road Development Authority and the Colombo Municipal Council are carrying out work to develop bus-stop facilities.

The Government plans to expand the Metro Bus service to several major cities across the country in the future.

Japan has provided eight buses to support the launch of the service, while Rathnayake said the Chinese Government had agreed to provide another 100 buses to the Metro company following discussions held during President Dissanayake’s visit to China.

Deputy Minister of Transport and Highways Dr. Prasanna Gunasena, Deputy Minister of Urban Development Eranga Gunasekara, Western Province Governor Hanif Yusoof, Colombo District Members of Parliament, foreign ambassadors, Ministry Secretary Engineer Asiri Karunawardena, Sri Lanka Transport Board Chairman Sajeeva Nandana Kanakaratne, government officials and officials of Lanka Metro Transit were also present at the launch.
