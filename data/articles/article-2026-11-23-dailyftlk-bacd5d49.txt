Illicit cigarettes cost Rs. 29.5 b in lost taxes in 2025: Oxford Economics

Illicit share of cigarette market falls to 20% from 32% peak in 2024

Legal cigarette value chain paid Rs. 181 b in taxes, 3.6% of Govt. tax revenue

Beedi, outside the excise net, accounts for 73% of tobacco sticks smoked

Illicit cigarettes cost the Government an estimated Rs. 29.5 billion in lost tax revenue in 2025, equal to 17% of the legal industry’s direct tax contribution, even as the illicit share of the market fell to 20%, according to a report by Oxford Economics commissioned by Ceylon Tobacco Company PLC (CTC).

The report, ‘The Economic Impact of the Cigarette Industry in Sri Lanka’, estimates the loss at Rs. 24.6 billion in sales taxes, comprising Rs. 18.6 billion in excise duty, Rs. 5.4 billion in Value Added Tax (VAT), and Rs. 600 million in Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL), plus Rs. 4.9 billion in corporate and labour income taxes. Had illicit products been unavailable, smokers would have bought an extra 303 million duty-paid cigarettes, the report said, based on what illicit smokers told a survey they would switch to.

Illicit Factory Made Cigarettes (FMC), mostly smuggled into the country, accounted for an estimated 470 million sticks, or 20% of FMC consumption, in 2025, down from a peak of 32% in 2024, according to research by consultancy PepperCube. The illicit share stood at 15% in 2018, when it first emerged as a significant problem. The report attributed the decline to a sharp increase in illicit cigarette prices and a growing number of retailers unwilling to stock them due to Government enforcement, as well as a more moderate 5.9% excise increase in 2025, after hikes of 44% in 2023 and 14% in 2024.

The PepperCube survey of almost 11,500 male smokers aged 22-64 found 11.5% consumed illicit cigarettes, with prevalence exceeding 20% in several districts and particularly high in Batticaloa, Puttalam, and Ratnapura. Almost half of illicit smokers cited affordability as a reason. Over 2016-2025, consumer prices rose by 140%, while prices of tier 1 cigarettes rose by about 350% and tier 4 by about 250%, driven by above-inflation excise increases. Legal cigarette consumption halved to 1.9 billion sticks from 3.9 billion over the period.

“Continued efforts to reduce illicit trade could, therefore, help protect an important source of Government revenue,” the report said.

The legal FMC value chain contributed an estimated Rs. 181 billion in taxes in 2025, equal to 3.6% of all Government tax revenue. CTC’s own contribution was Rs. 177 billion, including Rs. 112 billion in excise duty, Rs. 33.4 billion in VAT, Rs. 24.7 billion in corporate tax, Rs. 3.7 billion in SSCL, and Rs. 3.2 billion in Withholding Tax on dividends. Taxes accounted for 82% of the average retail price of cigarettes. The report noted corporate tax on the sector was raised to 45% from 40% in 2024, which it described as one of the highest rates in the world for the tobacco sector.

Cigarette-related sales taxes accounted for 2.7% of Sri Lanka’s total tax and non-tax revenue, against 1.8% in Pakistan, 1.4% in India, and a global median of 1%, based on World Health Organisation

The report said this revenue matters given the fiscal outlook. The Budget deficit narrowed to 2.3% of GDP in 2025 from 11.7% in 2021, but Oxford Economics projects it to widen to 5.2% in 2026 and 5.8% in 2027, following Cyclone Ditwah in late 2025 and the early 2026 Middle East conflict. “Against this background, an unexpected decline in cigarette-related revenues could make fiscal consolidation more difficult,” it said.

Beedi, hand-rolled tobacco wrapped in tendu leaf, poses a larger challenge to the tax base. Beedi consumption, legal and illicit, doubled over 2016-2025 to 6.5 billion sticks, accounting for 73% of all tobacco sticks consumed in 2025, against 22% for legal cigarettes and 6% for illicit cigarettes. Beedi attracts no excise duty as of August 2026, only a tobacco tax of Rs. 3 per stick since April 2025, up from Rs. 2 when introduced in 2023. At Rs. 10-15 a stick, beedi retails at little more than a third of the price of Capstan, the cheapest legal cigarette, with reports of widespread smuggling of beedi-related products.

“Strengthening enforcement and compliance measures could help bring beedies into the tax net and provide an additional source of revenue to the Government,” the report said.

The industry’s total contribution to GDP was estimated at Rs. 74 billion, or 0.2% of national output, comprising a direct contribution of Rs. 61.2 billion, Rs. 6.5 billion from Rs. 8.1 billion in domestic procurement, and Rs. 6.3 billion from wage-funded spending. The direct contribution is comparable to that of cereals (Rs. 59.5 billion) and rubber (Rs. 65.6 billion).

The value chain supported 81,400 jobs, or 1% of national employment. These include 62,700 jobs across 74,600 retail outlets, 14,600 leaf farmers and associated workers, 1,100 jobs in 11 distribution companies, and 301 CTC employees. CTC, the country’s sole legal cigarette manufacturer, produced 1.92 billion cigarettes in 2025 using 1.5 million kilograms of tobacco leaf sourced from 884 contracted farmers and 5,610 sub-growers.

“The analysis does not consider the broader social and health impacts of tobacco consumption, whether legal or illicit,” the report said.
