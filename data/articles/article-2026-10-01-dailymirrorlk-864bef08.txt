Rumesh Tharanga returns with gold—and a message

Colombo, Oct. 1 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lankan javelin thrower and Asian Games gold medalist Rumesh Tharanga Pathirage has returned to the island following his historic victory at the Asian Games, expressing pride in his achievement while calling for improved training facilities and equipment for Sri Lankan athletes.

Speaking upon his arrival, Pathirage reflected on his performance and the challenging conditions he faced during the competition, including heavy rain that affected his efforts to improve his throw.

Pathirage said his main target had been to break the meet record. Although he secured the gold medal, he missed the record by just one metre.

He explained that persistent rain made the competition particularly difficult, with the runway becoming slippery and the wet javelins becoming heavier and harder to grip. Rain also affected his vision during the run-up, making it difficult to execute an optimal throw.

Despite the challenging conditions, Pathirage said the support of Sri Lankan spectators gave him significant motivation. He expressed his gratitude to fans who had travelled hundreds of kilometres to watch him compete, saying the sight of Sri Lankan flags in the stands provided an important boost.

Pathirage also used his return home to highlight what he described as serious shortcomings in Sri Lanka's sports infrastructure.

He called on authorities to provide athletes with proper tracks, training environments and equipment instead of placing the emphasis solely on winning medals.

"Sri Lanka has considerable sporting talent, but many athletes struggle to produce their best performances at international competitions because they have limited exposure to international-standard facilities and track conditions,' he said.

He said that tracks used at major international competitions can differ significantly from those available for training in Sri Lanka, leaving local athletes less accustomed to the faster surfaces and conditions they encounter overseas.

Pathirage said that the country has a large pool of young sporting talent and said better facilities and equipment could help athletes develop that potential and perform at their best internationally.

He expressed hope that the country's sports authorities would take concrete steps to address the infrastructure gaps and provide athletes with the resources required to compete at the highest level.
