Sri Lanka’s 4.2% growth conceals two-speed recovery

Growth slowed from 5.0% in corresponding quarter of 2025, with industry expanding 7.3% and services growing 2.7%

Underlying composition showed recovery becoming heavily reliant on construction, mining, financial services and imports

Construction, credit-supported consumption and other interest-sensitive activities that drove 2Q growth could face increasing resistance as monetary tightening filters through economy

The national economy grew 4.2 percent in the second quarter; however, a surge in imports and construction-linked activity masked the contractions across agriculture and key export industries, which points to an increasingly uneven recovery as the tighter monetary conditions begin to weigh on demand.

Growth slowed from 5.0 percent in the corresponding quarter of 2025, with industry expanding 7.3 percent and services growing 2.7 percent, while agriculture contracted 2.3 percent, according to the Census and Statistics Department.

The underlying composition showed the recovery becoming heavily reliant on construction, mining, financial services and imports. At the same time, apparel, tea, rice, fishing and petroleum refining contracted, while tourism-related activity lost considerable momentum.

Construction expanded 13.9 percent and mining and quarrying surged 17.4 percent, making them two of the strongest contributors to industrial growth.

Manufacturing, which accounts for 16.1 percent of GDP, grew at a more moderate 3.2 percent, slowing from 3.8 percent a year earlier.

The divergence was also evident within manufacturing. Wood products, non-metallic mineral products, furniture and machinery recorded double-digit growth, largely reflecting the revival in construction and domestic investment.

In contrast, textile, wearing apparel and leather manufacturing contracted 1.4 percent, after expanding 9.1 percent in the corresponding quarter of 2025. The apparel export volumes declined 4.0 percent, signalling weakness in one of the country’s largest foreign-exchange-earning industries.

The growth pattern coincided with a sharp acceleration in imports. The total import expenditure increased 47.3 percent to Rs. 2.15 trillion during the quarter, while the import volumes rose 20.5 percent.

The consumer-goods import expenditure climbed 41.4 percent to Rs.515.42 billion, with the volumes surging 44.3 percent.

The export performance was considerably weaker. Although the export earnings increased 19.0 percent in rupee terms, the total export volumes grew only 1.8 percent. The industrial export volumes rose 2.5 percent, while the agricultural export volumes declined 2.6 percent.

The gap affirms that the recovery generated a much stronger increase in the import demand than in the export capacity, raising questions over its sustainability as Sri Lanka resumes foreign debt repayments and seeks to rebuild the external buffers.

The Central Bank had already warned in its July monetary policy review that the external current account had moved into deficit from April, mainly because the higher fuel import costs widened the merchandise trade deficit while the tourism earnings slowed.

It also observed that the demand conditions had strengthened and expected the monetary policy tightening undertaken in May, together with the other measures, to moderate the credit growth and demand pressures over the coming months.

The GDP data provide further evidence supporting that caution. Financial services expanded 7.7 percent during the quarter, with the total deposits and gross loans and advances growing 13.7 percent.

However, the borrowing costs were also higher. The Average Weighted Lending Rate increased to 12.16 percent in 2Q26, from 11.56 percent a year earlier, while the Average Weighted Deposit Rate rose to 7.20 percent, from 6.93 percent.

This indicates that construction, credit-supported consumption and other interest-sensitive activities that drove the second-quarter growth could face increasing resistance as the monetary tightening filters through the economy.

Agriculture presented the sharpest contrast to the industrial recovery, contracting 2.3 percent, after growing 2.5 percent a year earlier.

Rice production declined 15.1 percent, while marine fishing contracted 10.1 percent. Tea declined 1.9 percent, reflecting a 2.0 percent fall in made-tea production and weaker demand from the key markets, including Iran.

The agricultural weakness also carried direct cost-of-living implications. Freshwater fishing and aquaculture contracted 61.0 percent, while the producer and retail prices increased 48.7 percent and 44.9 percent, respectively.

Marine fish production fell 10.0 percent, with the retail prices for coastal and deep-sea fish rising 14.4 percent and 13.2 percent.

The combination of reduced food production and stronger consumer demand adds to the inflation challenge. The GDP implicit price deflator increased 6.9 percent in the quarter, while the National Consumer Price Index rose 5.6 percent.

Services, which account for more than half the economy, expanded 2.7 percent, slowing from 4.0 percent a year earlier.

Accommodation, food and beverage services grew 2.9 percent, down sharply from a 12.0 percent growth in 2Q25.

Tourist arrivals fell to 405,939, from 445,768 a year earlier, a decline of nearly 9 percent, as the conflict in the Middle East disrupted travel and weakened the tourism earnings.

Transportation grew 3.1 percent, supported by an 8.9 percent increase in port cargo and a 12.0 percent expansion in transport-support and warehousing activities. However, air passenger kilometres declined 15.8 percent and water transport contracted 6.1 percent.

Technology remained a bright spot. IT programming and consultancy services expanded 10.0 percent, while the combined export earnings from computer, professional and management consultancy and technical and trade-related services rose 15.9 percent in US dollar terms.

The computer-services earnings alone, however, fell to US $ 195 million, from US $ 216 million, with the overall increase driven by the gains in the other business-service categories.

Overall, the 2Q performance shows the economy is still expanding at a relatively firm pace, however, with the increasingly different fortunes across sectors.

Construction, mining, finance and selected services are driving growth, while agriculture, apparel and tourism remain exposed to the domestic supply constraints and external shocks.
