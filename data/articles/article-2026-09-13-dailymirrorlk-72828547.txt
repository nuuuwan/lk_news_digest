China, India agree to strengthen ties as Xi, Modi meet

Chinese President Xi Jinping and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi have agreed to strengthen bilateral relations, with both leaders describing China and India as partners rather than rivals during talks on the sidelines of the BRICS Summit in New Delhi.

The meeting took place on September 12 during the 18th BRICS Summit, where the two leaders held discussions on bilateral relations, economic cooperation, border issues and global affairs.

Xi said China viewed its relations with India from a strategic and long-term perspective and noted that institutional exchanges between the two countries had resumed, cooperation had expanded and bilateral trade had reached a new high.

He outlined four areas for strengthening relations: maintaining an objective strategic outlook, expanding mutually beneficial cooperation, resolving differences through mutual respect and understanding, and enhancing coordination in multilateral organisations.

Xi called for greater cooperation in areas including people-to-people exchanges and direct flights, while urging both countries to address economic and trade concerns in a balanced manner.

On the border issue, Xi said the two sides should work towards improving bilateral relations while seeking a resolution to the boundary question and maintaining peace and stability in border areas.

Modi said India also viewed its relationship with China from a strategic and long-term perspective and wanted the two countries to be partners rather than rivals.

He stressed that India’s relations with China were not influenced by any third country and said India was ready to strengthen strategic communication, expand mutually beneficial cooperation and deepen people-to-people exchanges.

The two leaders also agreed to enhance coordination through BRICS, the United Nations, the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation and the G20, while working together on global challenges and strengthening cooperation among developing countries.

India also expressed appreciation for China’s support for its BRICS chairship and pledged to support China when it assumes the BRICS chair next year.
