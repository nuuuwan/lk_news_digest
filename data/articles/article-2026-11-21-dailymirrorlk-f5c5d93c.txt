Tankers hit in Strait of Hormuz as Iran tightens grip on shipping

Al Jazeera - British maritime authorities have reported two incidents involving tankers struck by unidentified projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz, as Iran continues to restrict shipping through the vital waterway.

The incidents were reported on Saturday near Oman, amid a war involving Tehran, Washington and Tel Aviv that began with the United States and Israel attacking Iran in late February.

Tehran has since sought to tighten its control over the strait, which carried nearly a fifth of the world’s crude oil supplies before the conflict.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) centre, a British agency that monitors threats to commercial shipping, said one tanker was hit at its rear about 20km (13 miles) north of Khasab, a port town on Oman’s northern coast.

Another tanker was struck in its engine room about 31km (20 miles) east of Khasab, the agency said.

No casualties were reported, but it remained unclear whether the two incidents involved separate vessels.

Iran’s navy said on Saturday that an oil tanker had caught fire after striking a naval mine in a restricted area east of the strait.

In a statement carried by Iranian state television, the navy accused the vessel of ignoring warnings and travelling with its identification and navigation equipment switched off.

The Iranian account could not be independently verified, and it was unclear whether the burning tanker was involved in either incident reported by UKMTO.
