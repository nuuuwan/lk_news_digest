CBSL puts stability at centre of fintech ambitions

Sri Lanka’s ambitions for a more digital financial system will have to advance within firm boundaries protecting the rupee and financial stability, the country’s monetary watchdog stressed yesterday while reiterating that digital assets cannot be used for payments between residents under the existing legal framework.

Central Bank Governor Dr. Nandalal Weerasinghe welcomed the opportunities offered by financial technology but stressed that innovation must safeguard public confidence and the country’s monetary sovereignty.

His remarks added support for greater competition and new financial services with an explicit warning against allowing digital assets to displace the rupee in domestic transactions.

“Under the current legal framework in Sri Lanka, digital assets, also called virtual assets, cannot be used for payments between residents,” Weerasinghe said, addressing the Sri Lanka FinTech Festival in Colombo yesterday.

“This position is important in preventing the substitution of the Sri Lankan rupee in domestic transactions and in safeguarding the country’s monetary sovereignty.”

While acknowledging that digital assets could present opportunities, he said their development required clear regulatory frameworks, appropriate safeguards and careful consideration of the implications for the wider financial system.

The Governor’s remarks reaffirmed the existing legal position rather than announcing a new restriction. He also stressed that regulation must evolve alongside technology while preserving its essential objectives.

“Our approach must be proportionate, risk-based and forward-looking, providing clarity to responsible innovators while maintaining firm safeguards against unacceptable risks,” he said.

The Governor urged Sri Lanka to move beyond restoring macroeconomic stability and focus on raising productivity, attracting investment, creating employment and widening economic opportunities.

Reflecting on the recovery following the 2022 economic crisis, Weerasinghe said difficult reforms over the past three to four years had helped restore stability, rebuild confidence and strengthen resilience.

“However, stability is not the destination; it is the foundation upon which sustainable growth must be built.”

He identified financial technology as a means of strengthening the financial sector’s core functions, from mobilising savings and directing capital towards productive investment to facilitating trade and widening participation in economic activity.

Lower transaction costs, better use of data and new business models could help small businesses expand, connect underserved communities to formal finance and improve the allocation of resources, he said.

“In this sense, FinTech is not merely about modernising financial services; it is about enabling national economic development.”

Weerasinghe highlighted opportunities in open and interoperable financial services, digital identity, data-driven lending, cross-border payment connectivity and investment products.

Sri Lanka had already made progress through mobile and internet banking, digital payments, payment cards and applications, supported by national payment infrastructure, including LankaPay systems, he said. Digitalisation was also expanding access to capital markets and investment services.

However, the Dr. Weerasinghe cautioned that these developments brought risks extending beyond individual institutions and products.

Cyberattacks, operational disruptions, fraud, data breaches, inadequate consumer disclosures and excessive leverage were among the vulnerabilities he identified, alongside the concentration of critical services among technology providers.

Cross-border digital transactions could complicate supervision, while certain digital assets could expose consumers to substantial volatility and facilitate illicit financial activity.

“Financial stability cannot be compromised and must be preserved at all costs,” he said.

The Central Bank remained willing to engage with innovators and support responsible experimentation and regulatory coordination, Weerasinghe said, pointing to its existing FinTech Regulatory Sandbox as a structured environment for testing eligible solutions under safeguards.

“...we welcome innovation, we encourage competition, and we support progress, but always with stability, trust and consumer protection at the centre.”
