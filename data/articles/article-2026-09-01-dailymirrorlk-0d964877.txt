Promises alone would not defeat racism

Given several recent developments, it is timely that President Anura Kumara Dissanayake firmly stated his government would not tolerate racism from any ethnic group—Sinhalese, Tamil, or Muslim.

During the National Milad-un-Nabi or Holy Prophet’s Birthday celebration in Negombo on August 26, the President said his government would not allow another ethnic or religious conflict to break out in Sri Lanka. He made this remark against the backdrop of some individuals and groups making malicious statements against some communities in mainstream media, social media and even in Parliament.

An ugly situation emerged several weeks ago where Tamil and Muslim social media activists traded religious insults against each other after a Muslim man in the east had kicked a dog that had urinated in his garden. Similar to the dog Aloka that accompanied a group of Buddhist monks on a Walk for Peace across the United States and Sri Lanka last year and this year, this dog had accompanied a group of Hindu pilgrims on the way from Jaffna to Kataragama, and was named by them “Subramani.”

The assault on the dog incensed some Hindu devotees and resulted in an ugly exchange of religious insults between some sections of the two communities on social media. Ultimately, a Sri Lankan Tamil youth was arrested in Saudi Arabia on charges of blasphemy and was given five years prison sentence and a huge fine of 3 million Saudi Riyals (approximately LKR 260 million).

Then came the controversy over the uniform meant for  female health assistants at hospitals, following nine Muslim health assistants attached to the Kalmunai North Base-Hospital who wanted to cover their heads and legs along with the standard state-prescribed uniform. This led to another wave of religious insults between  Tamil (Hindu) and Muslim social media users.

Although these developments created ugly situations, they also brought forth courageous individuals – both politicians and ordinary people – who stood for communal harmony. While some Tamil politicians and lawyers voiced for the religious rights of the Muslim health assistants, some Muslim social media activists demanded empathy towards Sivarasa Anojan, the Tamil migrant worker from Kalmunai who was imprisoned in Saudi Arabia.

Subsequently,  a person called Sangeeth Jayasekara from Boralesgamuwa claimed during a press conference that there was an LTTE training camp in the Kannagipuram area of Kilinochchi. However, before this could  instill fear and suspicion among the Majority community towards Tamils, the authorities revealed that the claim was only a hoax, and the person who made this statement was arrested.

In another incident, Transport Minister Bimal Rathnayake accused Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB) MP Dayasiri Jayasekara of racism, claiming that the latter wanted police clearance for the appointees of the Wakf Board, the institution that governs the mosques in the country. He asked whether Jayasekara views all Muslims in the country as terrorists.

The viciousness displayed in  social media posts and the follow up comments in connection with these incidents is a vivid testament that we are enjoying just an illusion of peace, and not  real peace. The communal hatred in our society is a phenomenon that prevails like a ball pressed underwater.

With regard to the issue of the health assistants’ uniform, Health and Media Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa told parliament that the concerned uniform was prescribed by a circular issued in 2019 and employees have to adhere to it until it is changed. This raises the question whether the authorities did not know about the Muslims’ concerns about women’s attire, even after it has been a major topic in Sri Lanka for the past two decades. This seems to be a result of absence of proper ethnic representation at the decision-making entities, including the Cabinet.

The danger of racism – irrespective of which community it come from, is well-known. Also, racism is a powerful weapon in the hands of political parties, irrespective of their ethnicities. Since those political forces are not concerned about the devastating consequences that racism can cause for a nation, they might exploit the situations at the first opportunity they get.

Mere promises not to tolerate racism would not serve the purpose. A concerted strategy must also be put in place to see a united country.
