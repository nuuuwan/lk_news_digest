Wijewardena urges AKD to follow Rostow’s ‘Non-Communist Manifesto’ for take-off

President Anura Kumara Dissanayake (AKD) must now shed the communist leanings with which he began his political life and follow the growth path in American economist W.W. Rostow’s ‘Non-Communist Manifesto’, if Sri Lanka is to achieve economic take-off, former Central Bank Deputy Governor Dr. W.A. Wijewardena insisted.

Delivering the keynote address yesterday at the Budget Highlights 2027 forum organised by the Institute of Certified Management Accountants of Sri Lanka (CMA Sri Lanka), Dr. Wijewardena recalled that Rostow was the hero of economics students when he studied the subject in the 1960s.

Rostow’s ‘The Stages of Economic Growth: A Non-Communist Manifesto’, published in 1960, offered the developing countries a path out of underdevelopment but its influence faded as economic policymaking came to rely on models and quantitative analysis, he noted.

“So, in my view, President Anura Kumara Dissanayake will have to follow this book because it’s a non-communist manifesto. Since he had started his political life as a communist, now it is time for him to shed that and move out of it and go along with recommendations like Rostow’s,” Dr. Wijewardena told the gathering.

Dissanayake rose through the ranks of the Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna (JVP), a party with Marxist roots, before leading the National People’s Power (NPP) to victory in 2024.

To drive the point home, the former central banker recalled a remark by former President J.R. Jayewardene that “if you are in your 20s and 30s and you are not a socialist, you are a fool but if you are still a socialist in your 50s and 60s, you are still a fool”.

Applying Rostow’s framework to Sri Lanka, Dr. Wijewardena argued that the country had already completed the preconditions for take-off, with the support of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and that Budget 2027 would lay the foundation for take-off over the next five years.

The later stages, he explained, would involve consolidating that take-off and sustaining growth until the country reached a higher level of income.

However, he cautioned that the IMF programme would end in March next year, with no assurance of an immediate follow-on arrangement, leaving Sri Lanka to rely on its own economic policy programme for the last nine months of 2027.

Likening the economy to a hybrid vehicle that has been running on both a gasoline engine and an IMF-supplied electric motor, he stressed that once the motor is removed, the president must supply “the torque for take-off” from the domestic engine alone.

“Now it is time for Sri Lanka to move forward with this pragmatic development of the country,” he added. (NF)
