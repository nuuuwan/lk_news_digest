A minimum age limit for trishaw drivers

It was the same concern that necessitated a higher age limit in 2018 as well.

The government is considering to raise the age limit for three-wheeler drivers up to 40 years, according to Deputy Minister of Vocational Education Nalin Hewage. It must be reminded that successive governments have mooted this idea several times before on various grounds and abandoned it for various reasons.

Yet, the proposal for age restrictions for the trishaw drivers have not been static over the years. In 2016, the then Chairman of the National Council for Road Safety (NCRS) Dr. Sisira Kodagoda had said that legal age limit for Three-wheel drivers who provide public transport should be increased to 25.  Two years later, in 2018, the Deputy Minister of Transport Minister Ashok Abesinghe told media that the Government was considering banning the issuing of trishaw driving licence to persons less than 35 years.

Now the National People’s Power (NPP) government is mulling to raise the eligibility age for trishaw driving license up to 40.

The reasons for the increasing the age limit for tuk-tuk drives has also changed over the years. Earlier it was the road safety concerns that prompted the authorities to impose restrictions on younger drivers, citing younger age as a major factor that contributes to more road accidents. Announcing the move to impose ban on drivers below the age of 25 in 2016, NCRS Chairman Dr. Kodagoda also had argued “many of the fatal accidents involving three-wheelers have been caused by drivers below 25.”

It was the same concern that necessitated a higher age limit in 2018 as well, though proposed age limit that year was 10 years higher than the limit proposed two years ago. The so-called Yahapalana government assigned an additional task to the National Council for Road Safety to function as the National Transport Security Commission, especially to find ways and means to minimise road accidents, particularly involving three-wheelers and motor bikes. However, the then Cabinet rejected the proposal by a majority vote.

This time, Deputy Minister Hewage attributes  the move to the lack of interest on the part of the youth to seek vocational training, while pointing out that the age restriction would not be implemented immediately. However, Hewage did not mention anything about the common perception that trishaws are a major contributor to road accidents or the younger drivers are more prone to accidents.

In spite of the decision on the increase of the age limit on the grounds of road safety having public support, it does not seem to be logical when one goes into the details. This might be seen as a wise move mainly as  three-wheelers in the country are invariably viewed as a nuisance and their  drivers  are considered as an undisciplined lot, which is not totally wrong. However, they are not the only category of undisciplined drivers on the roads.

According to the statistics issued by the very NCRS, three-wheelers are not the vehicles  involved in the greatest number of road accidents. Motorbikes top the lists of total road accidents as well as  fatal accidents for the past several years. This factor has been confirmed by the statistics issued by the NCRS as well as the police.

No government can increase the age limit for trishaw drivers without facing outrage from a large part of society that benefits from this industry. The drivers’ families and regular passengers who opt for this mode of transport, in the face of a highly inefficient public transport service,  would be affected by this age restriction. It was  fear of such outrage that compelled the government to rollback its move to impose a ban on younger drivers in 2018.

In fact,  governments in the past considered imposing this age limit ignoring the livelihood of over four million people, as it came without entailing any alternative for  people who relied purely on this income.

The current government seems to have realised this aspect. Deputy Minister Hewage says a vocational training campaign is already been in place, intending to steer young people away from  three-wheeler driving and encourage them to pursue skill-based careers. He has stated that once vocational pathways were firmly established, the Government planned to introduce a minimum age requirement of 40 for three-wheeler drivers.
