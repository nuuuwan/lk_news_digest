SJB MP Dayasiri Jayasekara in an interview with Daily Mirror stated that the President is using the 22nd Amendment to the Constitution as a tool to grab power of the judiciary. He said that when there are many vacancies within the judiciary system and it is impractical to think that the backlog of cases will be reduced by merely extending the retirement age of judges. He further stated that he isn’t satisfied with the role SJB is playing as the opposition and must reach out to the grassroots level more.  Jayasekara, while commending the government for changing the political culture, questions the delay in probing allegations of corruption that occurred under this regime and focusing only on the corruption issues taking place under previous governments.  He emphasised that he did not change political parties under any circumstances for his political gains, but was compelled to do so as he was victimised by Party leaders.

QIt’s been two years since NPP assumed power. What are the positives and failures you see in this government? Do you think that they have changed the existed political culture?

Earlier we had seen politicians making themselves dignified and were kind of celebrities. They were well-known figures. Now there are few known MPs as opposed to the politicians who were in power about thirty years ago who maintained a certain level of class. But in this government it is not so.

They show that they no longer use the kind of vehicles or security like the earlier people in power. Previous governments misused government resources, used security just because they wanted to have two to three vehicles for themselves. But not myself. I have never ever used these facilities for my own purposes.

The present government shows that they use less security and seek less prominence. And yes, I would say this government has created a different political culture.

But regarding corruption, this government is talking big about it, but many incidents of corruption are taking place in ministries and corporations. CIABOC is investigating issues that happened during the earlier governments, but avoids corruption issues happening under this government. For example, the 323 container case, coal case and one billion USD issue which I even raised in the Parliament. Sorry to say, but CIABOC is in a dilemma to act against these issues.

QWhat is your stance about the 22nd Amendment to the Constitution?

It is a tool that the President is going to use to grab the power of the judiciary. When you think about the system of governance there are three pillars. Which are executive, parliament and the judiciary. Now for the first time in history, I think after 1977 one party rule is in the Parliament. They want judiciary also in their hands.

When you go through it (22 Amendment) there is no proper explanation by the government. When you get the document from the Ministry of Justice there are around 1.1 million cases pending in courts. Most of them are in the Magistrate Courts and the District Courts. By extending the judges’ retirement age by one to two years nothing will change.

There are many vacancies in Magistrate Courts, AG’s Department and within Courthouses. Also, in the Government Analyst Department. There are many issues due to lack of qualified officers. And regarding Sri Lanka Police, we are in a shortage of police officers to conduct investigations.

There are many courthouses which do not even have a photocopy machine and there is a shortage of officials in the court system.

By giving two more years for the judges you cannot clear the backlog.

First they wanted to increase the retirement age of the Supreme Court Judges. When the matter was taken up in Parliament they said they are going to extend the term of the judges of the Court of Appeal as well. Later it came to other courts as well. There are four vacancies in the Supreme Court, four vacancies in the Court of Appeal. 17 judges were not appointed to the High Court. There are 25 judges who should be appointed to the court. But nothing will happen.

The President said that they don’t have a problem with the Supreme Court. There were about 5000 cases, but after Justice Surasena became Chief Justice he has reduced the number of cases. And now the President is saying the problem is in the Appeal Court. There are 5000 odd cases, but why can’t you appoint four judges to the Supreme Court. What is the problem?

The government is awaiting some cases to be concluded and plan to put some of the politicians from the opposition and Army officials behind bars.

This is a violation of the Constitution, and the President is violating the constitution. According to Article 107(1) to 107(5) the President is the person who should appoint all these judges. The president is trying to control the judiciary by bringing 22nd Amendment.

QDo you think that SJB is playing an effective role as the Opposition? Do you think you have a strong campaigning resource in the ground level as opposed to NPP?

I am not satisfied with their (SJB) role. They do their best, but much more needs to be done. The SJB isn’t playing the role of the opposition in the parliament and must go to the village level. They must organise protest campaigns in the city. Nothing of that sort is happening. They are not fighters and do not have a fighting spirit. JVP had that kind of a spirit earlier. I think we must all come together and do that.

QIt was announced that the SJB will be forming an alliance with UNP. Do you think this would be a successful union and what is your strategy for the upcoming elections?

The merge of SJB and UNP is essential for the time being. But I do not know how they are going to do. UNP never allows Sajith Premadasa to take over the UNP.

As I see it, they are going to have a coalition. They all must get together and discuss with other parties as well taking to the political platform. This is not to contest in another election, but to have a proper understanding. We must have an understanding as to what we are going to do against this government as this government is not functioning withing democratic norms. It is kind of a dictatorship.

After having a discussion only can we decide on an amalgamation of UNP and SJB. And if other parties wish they can join.

At this point I don’t think there will be any election as one of the MPs from the government stated few days ago that there will be no provincial council elections. By not having election this government is trying to establish the system they want.

At the local government elections we contested separately. We all came together and defeated the government.

QAbout two weeks ago, the Gen. Sec. of SJB stated that SJB and UNP will be forming an alliance. But has this been communicated to the Party members as to how is it going to happen?

Not yet. I think they started the first campaign in Anuradhapura and there were meetings with villages to educate people. In the meantime NPP is also going to conduct District Rallies.

With the 22nd Amendment, the government became unpopular. And, in the villages, if you consider the economic level of the people, their living conditions are appalling. That is why the president is also going to address rallies

QYou were a member of the sectoral oversight committee for health, media and women’s empowerment. In your opinion why have all the parties as a whole failed to work on increasing the percentage of women’s representation in the parliament?

When compared to 7th and 8th Parliaments we can say there is an increase in the number of women representation in parliament. We must implement this quota system in parliamentary level as well. Our village level women are not encouraged to enter politics because of the problems they have. The 25% quota system came in the local government election in 2018 and that’s where the quota system was initiated. We have decided to increase this quota to 33% in the Provincial Council level. The women caucus has already submitted a proposal. The second tier is the provincial council election and the third tier is the next general election. We must introduce a piece of legislation to increase the women’s quota system. If that kind of legislation is introduced, automatically by ten years’ time women’s representation in the system will be increased. Otherwise what would happen is the first time you introduce it to local government elections it will work, but come the second time that quota system won’t continue. This is why I say apply the quota system to all three tiers which are local government, provincial council and the Parliament.

QYou changed your party several times throughout your political career. By doing so did you try to cater to your political survival or did you do it for other reasons?

This question is being asked repetitively. There are many who have changed their parties several times. I have changed my party only twice. First I was in the SLFP and changed my party when I was not in the parliament. At that time I was not even in the Provincial Council. I changed my party as a normal citizen. In 2001, I joined the UNP in 2004 and got elected to the Parliament for the first time. In 2013 I resigned from the Parliament and joined my previous party. That is the only time that I have changed my party. There is criticism levelled at me that I have crossed over. But I have never ever crossed over. I resigned from my Parliamentary seat and contested elections. The reason why I resigned from the UNP was because I fought against the leadership. At a meeting in Ratnapura, when I was addressing the crowd, they stopped my speech. I was asked to leave the meeting. When I asked why, I was told that the Party Leader Ranil Wickremesinghe is coming and he did not want me here. This made me think that if the leadership does not want to sit with me where can I go? I resigned from my position as a member of the parliament and gave it back to the party. I contested again from my previous party. There are coalitions. If you look at JVP, they started in 1965 and contested in 1994 under pragathisheeli Peramuna. After that they contested from the UPFA. I have not crossed over and have always fought for the right cause. As mentioned, I was penalised by the Party leadership. In 1997 I contested for the local government election and got the highest number of votes. But I wasn’t given the position of Chairman by Chandrika Bandaranaike. I never crossed over and remained in the Party. At the 1999 Provincial Council elections, they did not allow me to contest. In the year 2000, I wanted to contest, but even that wasn’t allowed. That’s how I quit Party. I was penalised and harassed by the party leadership. I didn’t do anything for my own benefit.
