1983 communal riots back in political limelight

Colombo, November 3 (Daily Mirror)- The 1983 communal riots, which are widely regarded as the immediate trigger for Sri Lanka’s three-decade-long civil war, have come back into the political limelight, with 50 government MPs seeking a parliamentary inquiry into the incident.

In a motion placed on the Order Paper of Parliament, these legislators, including two Cabinet Ministers, Ramalingam Chandrasekar and Ananda Wijepala, ask for recommendations aimed at ensuring national harmony and providing relief to the affected people.

The MPs argue in the motion that it is the responsibility of the State to provide some just relief or dignified redress to all the people who were affected and aggrieved by the incident.

“The Black July, which took place in 1983, dragged the country back economically, socially and culturally for several decades, and as a result, thousands of valuable lives were lost, massive property damage was caused, and the country suffered. And whereas the most serious and devastating damage that was caused to the country was the escalation of tension, fear and suspicion among the communities rather than the physical damage,” the motion said.

The MPs argue that the loss of lives, damage to property and the psychological and social damage caused to the people by the Black July of 1983, which is considered to be the worst tragedy in the history of Sri Lanka, have not yet been properly assessed.

The motion stresses that the need has arisen to develop a strong legal, social and reconciliation mechanism in order to prevent the recurrence of such racist and crisis-ridden incidents in the future and that it has become essential to take necessary steps to prevent activities that undermine the trust and goodwill existing between different ethnic groups in Sri Lanka

The MPs through the motion has said, factors that led to the occurrence of the said incident and the individuals who led such destructive acts must be uncovered.

The motion has come up despite a remark made by Minister of Justice Harshana Nanayakkara during a recent debate in Parliament that it is more appropriate to appoint a select committee to look into national reconciliation rather than focusing on the July 1983 riots alone.

Daily Mirror learnt that a motion can be taken up for debate even if a motion is signed by five members and the Speaker appoints a select committee if its approved by a majority of MPs in Parliament as per the standing orders.
