Indonesia Cultural Centre opens in Colombo

Colombo, August 13, (Daily Mirror) : The Embassy of the Republic of Indonesia in Colombo inaugurated the Indonesia Cultural Centre (ICC) on Wednesday, marking a new step in efforts to deepen cultural exchange and people-to-people relations between Indonesia and Sri Lanka.

The centre was officially inaugurated by Dewi Gustina Tobing, Ambassador of Indonesia to Sri Lanka and Maldives, with Prof. Hiniduma Sunil Senevi, Minister of Buddhasasana, Religious and Cultural Affairs of Sri Lanka, attending as Chief Guest.

The inauguration ceremony brought together ambassadors and representatives of diplomatic missions, school teachers, university lecturers, students, artists and cultural practitioners.

Established as a platform for Sri Lankans to learn about and experience Indonesian culture, the ICC features a library with Indonesian books and cultural materials, traditional musical instruments including Javanese Gamelan and Angklung, Wayang Kulit and Wayang Golek puppets, a traditional dance practice area, Indonesian language learning facilities and a space for film screenings.

A major highlight of the ceremony was a performance by Gamelan Sekar Laras, a Javanese Gamelan ensemble made up of Sri Lankan musicians who have received training and mentoring from the Indonesian Embassy in Colombo. The ensemble performed the popular Javanese piece "Lancaran Gugur Gunung", showcasing the distinctive sounds and rhythms of Javanese traditional music.

The cultural programme also featured Tari Tor Tor, a traditional dance of the Batak community of North Sumatra. The performance was presented by dancers from the Sanka Samudhi Performing Art Foundation of Kalutara, highlighting Indonesia's rich performing arts traditions.

Adding a further dimension to the programme, embassy staff performed Tari Legong Bapang Saba, a traditional Balinese dance. The graceful movements and distinctive expressions of the dance offered guests an insight into Bali's unique cultural heritage.

Together, the performances from Java, North Sumatra and Bali showcased the cultural diversity of Indonesia. The participation of Sri Lankan musicians and dancers alongside Indonesian diplomatic staff also demonstrated how cultural activities can encourage artistic collaboration and mutual understanding between the two countries.

The performances were warmly received by the audience, with particular appreciation for the involvement of Sri Lankan artists in presenting Indonesian traditional arts.

The ICC is expected to offer a range of programmes for the Sri Lankan public, including Indonesian language courses, traditional Indonesian dance classes and classes on traditional Indonesian musical instruments.

The centre will also organise workshops, exhibitions, traditional arts performances, film screenings and other cultural exchange programmes, providing Sri Lankans with opportunities to engage directly with Indonesian culture.
