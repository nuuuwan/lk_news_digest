Illegal electric traps threaten Rattha and other elephants

Colombo, Nov. 4 (Daily Mirror) - More than 10 illegal electric traps allegedly connected to the main electricity supply have been set up in the Welgala area of Ibbagamuwa, posing a serious threat to wild elephants, including the well-known elephant Rattha, the Society for Conservation of Wild Elephants in Sri Lanka (SCWESL) has warned.

In a letter dated November 2 to the Director General of the Department of Wildlife Conservation, the organisation urged authorities to conduct immediate raids and take legal action against those responsible.

It warned that Rattha, currently roaming the Moragollagama-Pothanegama area in Polpithigama, could move towards Welgala in the coming days.

According to the organisation, the elephant could reach the area through Mahadambe and Hakwatunawa or travel via Dombagamuwa and Nelliya Kanda due to obstacles around the Hakwatunawa reservoir.

The organisation said the alleged electric traps had been set up along natural elephant movement routes, placing Rattha and other elephants at risk of electrocution.

It also warned that illegal connections to the main electricity supply posed a serious threat to human lives.

The group called on the Wildlife Department to coordinate with other relevant authorities, conduct immediate raids, identify and arrest those responsible and take legal action against them.

It also urged the authorities to introduce a special protection programme to identify elephant movement routes and vulnerable areas.

Warning against waiting for another elephant to die before taking action, the organisation stressed the need for immediate intervention to prevent further deaths.

Copies of the letter were sent to the Environment Minister, the Army Commander, the Inspector General of Police and the Commanding Officer of the Police Special Task Force.

The allegations have not been independently verified, and it is not clear whether authorities have inspected the reported traps or made any arrests.
