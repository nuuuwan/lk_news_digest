Rs. 3 billion allocated for compensation over drought-hit crops

Colombo, Sep. 3 (Daily Mirror) - The Government has allocated Rs. 3 billion to provide agricultural insurance compensation to farmers whose crops have been damaged by drought conditions associated with El Niño.

Paddy, maize and several other crops have been affected by prevailing dry weather conditions in districts including Monaragala, Polonnaruwa, Hambantota, Anuradhapura and Ampara.

According to preliminary assessments, around 8,000 acres of farmland belonging to approximately 7,000 farmers have been affected. The reported damage includes 1,401 acres in Monaragala, 1,259 acres in Polonnaruwa, 783 acres in Hambantota, 1,055 acres in Ampara and 1,923 acres in Anuradhapura.

Teams comprising field officers of the Agricultural and Agrarian Insurance Board and Agricultural Research and Production Assistants of the Department of Agrarian Development are currently inspecting affected cultivation areas and assessing the damage.

The Government aims to provide compensation to affected farmers before the commencement of the next cultivation season. Accordingly, the Minister of Agriculture, Livestock, Lands and Irrigation has instructed officials to expedite compensation payments, particularly for damaged paddy cultivation.

Under the scheme, compensation of up to Rs. 100,000 per hectare, subject to a maximum of five acres, will be provided for damage to paddy, maize, big onion, potato, soybean and chilli crops.

Farmers who have not yet reported crop damage have been requested to contact the Agricultural Research and Production Assistants of the Department of Agrarian Development or the Development/Field Officers of the Agricultural and Agrarian Insurance Board as soon as possible.

They can also record the damage in the crop damage report books maintained at Agrarian Development Centres.

Farmers requiring further information can contact the Agricultural and Agrarian Insurance Board on the 1918 short code.
