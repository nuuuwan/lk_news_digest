Yield curve eases following US midterms amid improving geopolitical sentiment

Following the conclusion of the US midterm elections, in which the Democrats secured victories in both chambers of Congress, the global oil prices eased to around US $ 100/bbl, providing some relief on the geopolitical front.

The improved external environment contributed to relatively higher market activity, although investor sentiment remained somewhat mixed amid the lingering uncertainties surrounding the domestic interest rate outlook.  Despite this cautious sentiment, the market rates continued to trend lower, supported by the domestic investor demand. Foreign selling was particularly evident in the 2032, 2033 and 2034 maturities; however, these positions were absorbed by the domestic investors. Within the 2030 maturity segment, 15.05.2030, 01.08.2030 and 15.10.2030 traded within a range of 11.40 percent-11.55 percent. Moving further along the curve, 01.02.2031 traded at 11.60 percent, while 01.12.2031 recorded trades at 11.75 percent.  The 15.12.2032 maturity, which witnessed foreign selling, traded at 12.00 percent. In the 2033 segment, both 01.06.2033 and 01.11.2033 traded at 12.35 percent. Further along the curve, 15.10.2034 traded within a range of 12.49 percent-12.45 percent, while the 15.08.2036 maturity recorded trades within a range of 12.80 percent-12.75 percent. Yesterday, the PDMO concluded its weekly T-bill auction, raising Rs.65.9 billion, below the initial offer amount of Rs.90.0 billion. For the three-month T-bill, Rs.40.0 billion was initially offered, while Rs.53.9 billion was accepted.  The weighted average yield rate (WAYR) increased by 1bp to 9.35 percent. For the six-month T-bill, the PDMO accepted only Rs.6.6 billion against an offered amount of Rs.30.0 billion, with the WAYR remaining unchanged at 9.44 percent. For the 12-month T-bill, Rs.5.4 billion was accepted, which was below the offered amount, while the WAYR remained unchanged at 9.95 percent.

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee stood flat at Rs.332.6/US dollar. Market liquidity in the banking system expanded marginally to Rs.389.73 billion, compared to Rs.389.68 billion seen previously.
