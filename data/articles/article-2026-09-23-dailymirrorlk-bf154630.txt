IMF urges Sri Lanka to stay the course on economic reforms

Colombo, Sep. 23 (Daily Mirror) - An International Monetary Fund (IMF) staff team has concluded a visit to Sri Lanka, with discussions focusing on recent economic developments, progress under the IMF Extended Fund Facility (EFF) programme and policy recommendations under the 2026 Article IV Consultation.

The IMF team, led by Evan Papageorgiou, was in Sri Lanka from September 10 to 23, 2026. At the conclusion of the mission, the IMF said Sri Lanka’s economy had remained resilient despite successive shocks, with economic activity expanding by 4.2% in the second quarter of 2026.

The IMF noted that Sri Lanka had recorded 11 consecutive quarters of economic growth. However, headline inflation rose to 8% year-on-year in August, largely reflecting the impact of higher global oil prices. The Fund said inflation expectations remained broadly anchored.

Sri Lanka’s gross official reserves had increased to US$6.9 billion by the end of August, while the IMF said banks remained well capitalised and profitable. It also noted that fiscal performance during the first half of 2026 was strong and that the country’s debt restructuring was largely completed.

The IMF, however, warned that Sri Lanka continues to face downside risks linked to uncertainty over the duration and intensity of the conflict in the Middle East, global trade policies and the impact of El Niño.

The Fund called for continued efforts to strengthen fiscal and external buffers, maintain price stability and advance governance reforms while strengthening social safety nets for vulnerable groups.

The IMF also recommended developing a medium-term revenue strategy to improve tax collection while making the tax system more efficient and equitable. It called for broadening the tax base, reviewing tax exemptions and incentives, and strengthening revenue administration.

On monetary policy, the IMF said the Central Bank should remain prepared to address inflationary pressures and maintain price stability within the country’s flexible inflation-targeting framework. It also highlighted the importance of greater exchange-rate flexibility to absorb external shocks and support the accumulation of reserves.

The Fund further stressed the importance of maintaining the integrity of Sri Lanka’s anti-corruption framework, noting concerns that certain provisions of recently proposed amendments could weaken transparency and accountability.

The IMF said Sri Lanka’s transition from economic stabilisation to long-term transformation would require continued structural reforms aimed at improving the business environment, attracting investment, liberalising trade, modernising business and labour regulations, expanding access to finance and advancing digitalisation.

During the mission, the IMF team also visited Jaffna and held discussions on the Northern Province’s economic potential. The team identified opportunities for investment and job creation in areas including agriculture, fisheries, tourism and renewable energy, while stressing the importance of connectivity, skills development and social protection.

The IMF delegation held meetings with President and Finance Minister Anura Kumara Dissanayake, Prime Minister Dr. Harini Amarasuriya, senior government officials, Central Bank officials, parliamentarians, private-sector representatives, civil society organisations and development partners.

The IMF said discussions with the Sri Lankan authorities will continue with the aim of reaching a staff-level agreement in the near term, which would pave the way for the timely completion of the Seventh Review under the IMF programme.
