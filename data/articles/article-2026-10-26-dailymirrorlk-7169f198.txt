‘Sententia.ai’ backed by Lankan investment banker, joins NVIDIA Inception Programme

Sententia.ai, an early-stage legal-tech venture backed by Sri Lankan investment banker Sanjana Fernando, has been accepted into the NVIDIA for Startups Inception accelerator.

The move gives the cross-border transaction structuring platform access to the chipmaker’s technical resources, AI ecosystem and global startup network, the company announced.

NVIDIA’s Inception programme supports startups building AI-driven technology and does not take an equity stake in its members. Instead, it offers preferential access to NVIDIA hardware and software, technical training and exposure to its venture capital network. For a pre-commercial venture still validating its first structuring corridor, membership is a useful credential when courting design-partner law firms and institutional investors. Fernando, Managing Director of Melbourne-based STF Corporate Advisory, joined Sententia.ai as Corporate Advisor in September and now serves as Investor and Board Advisor. His career spans HSBC Investment Banking, Dresdner Kleinwort and Goldman Sachs, with his work covering mergers and acquisitions, capital raising and cross-border mandates.

The venture positions itself beyond conventional legal automation. It argues that the future of law and finance lies in systems capable of understanding transaction architecture and navigating regulatory complexity.

“We are building a neural network for undertaking transactions, transforming complex cross-border requirements into structured investment models, fund structures, risk intelligence, and execution-ready workflows,” the company noted.

“Our vision is to make global capital programmable,” it added.

Sententia.ai was conceptualised by Shayant Bhattacharjee, a law student at Maharashtra National Law University (MNLU), Nagpur, whose paralegal work on FDI and cross-border M&A mandates at Tier-1 law firms shaped the founding premise. He is joined by fellow MNLU students Prakhar Mishra and Navya Y., while Sanidhya Jain, who studies at Drexel University, Philadelphia, leads the venture’s AI architecture as Chief Technology Officer. Cambridge-trained corporate and TMT lawyer Rupal Chhaya sits on its advisory board.

Meanwhile, the company said the Inception membership would support its efforts as it continues building what it describes as “the intelligence layer for global transactions.”
