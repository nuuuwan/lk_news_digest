Supply constraints hit essential food commodity market

Essential food commodities, both imported and locally produced, are facing tightening supply conditions, prompting traders to shift sourcing markets, according to suppliers.

Dried chillies and coriander have come under sharp price pressure, while the availability of imported onions and potatoes from India and Pakistan has also varied, according to the latest weekly price list issued by the Essential Food Commodities Importers and Traders Association (EFCITA).

According to the wholesale price schedule submitted by EFCITA yesterday, dried chillies were quoted at Rs. 2,000 per kilogram, with traders reporting a shortage and warning that prices could rise further. Deluxe-grade dried chillies, a premium quality category, were priced at Rs. 2,500 per kilogram.

The price increase follows recent delays in customs clearance at the Colombo Port involving Indian chilli consignments over moisture-content specifications, an importer said. The issue has been attributed to high humidity and El Niño-related weather conditions. Coriander was similarly priced at Rs. 500 per kilogram amid a shortage linked to moisture-related issues.

In the vegetable import market, Indian and Chinese potatoes were unavailable on the wholesale price list, leaving Pakistani potatoes as the main imported variety, at Rs. 160–170 per kilogram. Local potatoes traded at significantly higher prices of Rs. 370–380 per kilogram, while WMDA stocks were quoted at Rs. 320 per kilogram.

With no local big onions available in the market, Indian big onions traded at Rs. 270–300 per kilogram for non-AC stocks and Rs. 220 for AC stocks. Holland-variety Indian onions fetched Rs. 230 per kilogram. Indian red onions were quoted at Rs. 650–660 per kilogram, while Bangalore 'Chipla' red onions were priced at Rs. 300. Local Jaffna red onions traded between Rs. 400 and Rs. 500 per kilogram.

Meanwhile, wholesale prices of other essential food commodities showed mixed trends.

Local rice varieties remained relatively stable, with Nadu trading at Rs. 180–185 per kilogram, White Raw at Rs. 175–180, Red Raw at Rs. 175–185, Samba at Rs. 215–220 and Keeri Samba at Rs. 245–250. Araliya Keeri Samba was quoted at Rs. 330 per kilogram. Imported Ponni and Paal Ponni were priced at Rs. 220 and Rs. 275 per kilogram, respectively.

White sugar traded at Rs. 240–245 per kilogram following a recent Rs. 10 per kilogram increase in the Special Commodity Levy, while red sugar stood at Rs. 285. Prima wheat flour was quoted at Rs. 188–190 per kilogram, while imported flour was priced at Rs. 185. Red dhal ranged between Rs. 240 and Rs. 255 per kilogram.

Among other commodities, garlic traded at Rs. 320–340 per kilogram, tamarind at Rs. 650, mustard at Rs. 450 and goraka at Rs. 380. Indian green moong was priced at Rs. 650 per kilogram, with split green moong at Rs. 550, while urid traded at Rs. 765–780. Local groundnuts were quoted at Rs. 550.

In the edible oil market, local coconut oil and palm oil traded at Rs. 850–860 per kilogram, while sunflower oil was priced at Rs. 810–825.

Industry representatives have cautioned that adverse weather conditions in key source markets, coupled with higher international freight costs and rupee depreciation, could pose further risks to supplies and prices of imported commodities, particularly onions and potatoes, as the country heads into December. However, potato supply is steady from Pakistan, where the crop season has begun.
