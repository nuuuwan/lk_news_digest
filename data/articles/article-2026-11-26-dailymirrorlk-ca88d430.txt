Diesel tanker carrying up to 7,000 tonnes sinks near Ibiza, killing one

BBC - An oil tanker carrying up to 7,000 tonnes of diesel has sunk off the coast of Ibiza, leaving one crew member dead, authorities have said.

The Med Emre, a Malta-flagged vessel, was carrying 17 crew members who were all rescued. However seven were taken to hospital and one was later pronounced dead, Balearic Islands emergency services confirmed.

The six crew receiving treatment reportedly suffered minor injuries, such as hypothermia, Spanish media reports.

The possible environmental damage is being assessed by a plane and Spain's pollution control vessel, Clara Campoamor, which is owned by the country's national maritime rescue service.

"There is still little information available about a possible fuel spill," Balearic Islands' President Marga Prohens wrote on X.

Regional emergencies director Pablo Gárriz said the tanker had not completely sunk, and was still partially submerged.

That made conditions more favourable for carrying out recovery operations, he said, "as we have gone from the possibility of the vessel being at a depth of 1,000m to a situation that allows operations to be carried out on the surface."

The Med Emre sank 25 nautical miles (46 km; 29 miles) from Ibiza's coastline and 57 nautical miles from Valencia. It is unclear what caused the sinking.

The chemical tanker was en route from Algeciras, in southern Spain, to Barcelona in the north-east.

Rescuers in Valencia received a distress call around 22:30 local time (21:30 GMT) on Wednesday, according to Spanish media. Minutes later, the vessel reported a leak and the crew said it was leaning at a 90-degree angle.

The injured crew members have been taken to hospitals in Valencia and Can Misses Hospital in Ibiza, President Prohens said.

Ibiza's marine environment was added to the Unesco World Heritage List in 1999. In particular, it is home to dense prairies of Posidonia oceanic seagrass, which is found only in the Mediterranean basin and plays a vital role in supporting local marine biodiversity.

In 2002, Spain experienced one of its worst environmental disasters when the Prestige oil tanker ran into trouble off Galicia's coastline. It spilled more than 60,000 tonnes of heavy fuel oil into the sea.
