UN resolutions on women’s rights available in Sinhala and Tamil

Colombo, Nov. 2 (Daily Mirror) - United Nations policies on women, peace and security are now available in Sinhala and Tamil following the launch of unofficial translations of relevant UN Security Council resolutions in Colombo.

The launch took place at Waters Edge, Battaramulla, to mark the 26th anniversary of UN Security Council Resolution 1325, adopted on October 31, 2000.

The event was held under the patronage of Minister of Women and Child Affairs Saroja Savithri Paulraj.

Printed copies of the translated resolutions were presented at the event. The translations were prepared by the organisation 'Pathways to Peace' with support from the Government of Canada through the Canadian High Commission in Sri Lanka.

The Women, Peace and Security Agenda is an international plan that aims to give women an equal role in building peace, preventing conflicts and making decisions on security.

It also focuses on protecting women and girls during wars, preventing sexual violence and ensuring that their needs are considered when providing support and rebuilding communities after conflicts.

UN Security Council Resolution 1325, adopted in 2000, highlights the important role women play in preventing conflicts, resolving disputes, taking part in peace talks and building peace.

Speaking at the event, Minister Paulraj said making international policies and decisions available in local languages would help more people understand women's rights and encourage women to take part in important decisions.

"Language should not be a barrier to knowledge. It is our shared responsibility to build a peaceful society through awareness, equality and participation," she said.

Canadian High Commissioner to Sri Lanka Isabelle Martin, representatives of 'Paameta Piyamang' (Stepping Towards Peace) and officials from the Ministry of Women and Child Affairs also attended the event.
