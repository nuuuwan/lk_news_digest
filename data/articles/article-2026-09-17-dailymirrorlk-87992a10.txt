48-hour railway strike begins

Colombo, September 17 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka’s railway trade unions launched a 48-hour token strike at midnight yesterday (16), after talks with the Government failed to resolve their demand for the removal of Railway General Manager Ravindra Pathmapriya.

Transport, Highways and Urban Development Minister Bimal Rathnayake has said he cannot agree to the unions’ demand, leaving the main dispute unresolved ahead of the planned strike.

The industrial action, involving a large number of railway trade unions, is expected to disrupt train services across the country. The unions say they have lost confidence in the administration of the current General Manager and are calling for his immediate removal.

The unions had initially announced the strike based on two main demands: the removal of the Railway General Manager and the withdrawal of a letter seeking an explanation from Sri Lanka Railway Station Masters’ Union General Secretary Kasun Chamara Jayasekara over comments he made to the media regarding problems affecting railway services.

Following discussions with the Transport Ministry on September 14, the unions decided to proceed with the strike after no acceptable decision was reached on the removal of the General Manager.

During the meeting, union representatives raised concerns over a range of administrative and operational issues within Sri Lanka Railways, employee-related matters and disruptions affecting passengers.

They told the Ministry that their confidence in the present administration had completely eroded and called for the General Manager to be removed immediately.

The unions said the dispute had remained unresolved for nearly two years and rejected a request from the Ministry Secretary for additional time to reach a final decision.

They said that the strike was not aimed at opposing the Government but at rebuilding the Railway Department, improving operational efficiency and restoring a reliable service for passengers.

According to the unions, continuing train cancellations and delays, overcrowding, technical failures, inadequate passenger facilities and other operational shortcomings have highlighted the difficulties facing the railway service.

They also accused the administration of failing to adequately engage with trade unions representing railway employees.

Union representatives said several groups within the Department, including station masters, controllers, drivers, cabinmen, shunting staff, workshop employees and station staff, had sought discussions on issues affecting both employees and passengers but had not been given adequate opportunities to raise their concerns with the General Manager.

The Railway Controllers Union has separately backed the strike, saying its demands include the immediate and unconditional withdrawal of the request for an apology from the Station Masters’ Union secretary and the removal of the General Manager.

The Railway Technological and Operating Service Union has also announced that it would join the joint trade union action, citing concerns over the administration of the Department and delays in processing railway employees’ pension matters.

The union said the head of the Railway Department should possess expertise suited to a highly specialised engineering and operational institution, warning that continued administrative experimentation could further affect the service.

It said it would participate in the strike to draw the attention of President Anura Kumara Dissanayake to the issues faced by railway employees and the Department.

Meanwhile, Minister Bimal Rathnayake has maintained that he cannot agree to the demand for the removal of the General Manager.

The dispute has also begun affecting train operations ahead of the formal commencement of the strike. Reports yesterday said six train journeys scheduled for last night were due to be cancelled, including the 7.00 p.m. Colombo Fort-Batticaloa service, the 8.00 p.m. Colombo Fort-Kankesanthurai service and the 9.30 p.m. night mail train to Batticaloa. However, the Railway Department and transport authorities had not officially confirmed all the reported cancellations at the time.

The unions have warned that further and more prolonged industrial action could follow if their concerns are not addressed.

They maintain that their objective is to restore Sri Lanka Railways as an efficient and dependable public transport service while addressing the concerns of railway employees and commuters.

With the Government yet to accept the unions’ principal demand and the unions maintaining their decision to strike, commuters are likely to face disruptions to train services during the planned 48-hour industrial action.
