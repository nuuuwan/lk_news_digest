Over 200 kg of fish caught using dynamite ordered destroyed

Colombo, Sep. 8 (Daily Mirror) - More than 200 kilograms of fish seized following an illegal fishing operation involving dynamite have been ordered destroyed by court order after authorities determined that the stock was unfit for human consumption.

The Sri Lanka Navy arrested a suspect and seized a fishing boat during a special operation conducted in connection with the illegal use of dynamite for fishing.

Fishing using dynamite or other explosives is prohibited in Sri Lanka due to the serious damage it can cause to fish stocks and the wider marine ecosystem. The practice can kill large quantities of fish indiscriminately, including juvenile fish and other marine organisms, while also damaging the seabed.

Following the seizure, Fisheries officials and a Public Health Inspector jointly inspected the fish stock and found that it was not in a condition suitable for human consumption.

Authorities subsequently took legal action over concerns that fish caught using explosives could pose a risk to public health if they entered the market or were consumed.

The seized fish stock was produced before court, and the court ordered that the entire stock, weighing more than 200 kilograms, be destroyed rather than released for human consumption.

Authorities stressed that preventing fish caught through illegal methods from entering the market is important not only to protect marine resources but also to safeguard public health.

The operation also highlighted the importance of coordinated action by the Navy, Fisheries officials and the Public Health Division in tackling illegal fishing and ensuring that potentially unsafe food does not reach consumers.

Illegal fishing methods such as dynamite fishing pose a dual threat, damaging Sri Lanka’s marine environment while potentially creating public health risks when contaminated or unsafe fish enter the food supply. Continued enforcement is therefore essential to protect both marine resources and consumers.
