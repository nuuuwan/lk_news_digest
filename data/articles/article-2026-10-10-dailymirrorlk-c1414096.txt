Madu Ganga boat operations to face stricter controls

Authorities have decided to introduce stricter controls on tourist boat operations in the Madu Ganga to protect its sensitive aquatic ecosystem from environmental damage caused by excessive boat traffic.

The decision was taken at a special meeting held at the Balapitiya Divisional Secretariat yesterday (9), chaired by Deputy Minister of Environment Anton Jayakody.

Officials from several state institutions, including the Department of Wildlife Conservation, Central Environmental Authority, Department of Forest Conservation, Coast Conservation Department, Irrigation Department and Sri Lanka Police, attended the meeting.

It was revealed that around 327 boats operated by 70 tourist boat operators currently use the Madu Ganga, an internationally recognised Ramsar wetland. The high number of boats has contributed to riverbank erosion, mangrove degradation and disturbances to fish breeding grounds and other aquatic life.

The Deputy Minister stressed that exceeding the carrying capacity of a natural ecosystem could cause irreversible environmental damage, adding that tourism activities must be managed with a long-term vision rather than focusing solely on short-term profits.

Until a dedicated legal framework is finalised by the Legal Draftsman’s Department and published in the Government Gazette, authorities will enforce interim guidelines approved by the District Coordinating Committee.

The proposed measures include restricting permits to boats fitted with engines of 15 horsepower (HP) or less, requiring the exclusive use of petrol for boat engines and limiting operating hours to between 6 a.m. and 5 p.m.

All tourist boats operating in the Madu Ganga will also be required to register with the Department of Wildlife Conservation.

Authorities will determine the maximum number of boats permitted to operate on the river based on scientific assessments of its ecological carrying capacity.

It was also decided to obtain the full support of the Sri Lanka Police to enforce the regulations and establish a joint monitoring committee comprising the relevant authorities.i9

The initiative aims to protect the biodiversity of the Madu Ganga while ensuring that tourism activities in the area remain environmentally sustainable.
