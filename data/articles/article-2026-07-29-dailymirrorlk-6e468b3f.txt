Australia’s Gold Rush, Seales’ heroics, Essex history & UEFA oppose FIFA plan

July.29 (Mirror Sports) - From medal dominance to cricket heroics, historic milestones and football controversies, the world of sport produced another action-packed day. Australia, West Indies, Essex Cricket and UEFA all made headlines in the latest global sports roundup.

Australia continues to dominate the Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games, leading the medal table with 35 gold, 18 silver and 27 bronze medals as of 29 July.

The Australian team has built a strong advantage across multiple sports, securing the top position ahead of its Commonwealth rivals. With more events still to come, Australia remains on course to extend its lead and strengthen its position as the most successful nation at the Games.

Jayden Seales claimed five wickets as West Indies defeated Pakistan by 90 runs in the first Test at the Brian Lara Cricket Academy in Trinidad. The victory gave the hosts a 1-0 lead in the two-match series.

Chasing 211 for victory, Pakistan were bowled out for 120 after a late fightback from Babar Azam, who remained unbeaten on 58. Seales finished with figures of 5-20, while Justin Greaves also played a key role with his brilliant bowling performance.

West Indies captain Roston Chase dedicated the win to the late Sir Garfield Sobers, saying the team wanted to honour the legendary cricketer with a memorable performance. The second Test will begin in Port of Spain on August 2.

Essex Cricket have appointed Rachel Lewis as the club’s first-ever female chair of the board in their 150-year history. Lewis replaces Jason Gallian, who stepped down from the role after “careful consideration” last week.

The former Olympic volleyball player has served as chairwoman of Essex Cricket in the Community for the past three years and becomes the club’s fifth chair in five years.

Lewis said she was excited to lead the club into a new era, with Essex focused on ground redevelopment and continued success for both their men’s and women’s teams. Gallian will remain involved as a board member.

UEFA and Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham have criticised FIFA’s plan to bring private investment into its competitions, including the FIFA World Cup. FIFA said the move aims to boost football development funding beyond $10 billion through a new commercial subsidiary, FIFA Forward Enterprise.

The proposal would allow third-party investors to take minority stakes in the venture, with US firm Thrive Eternal expected to lead the investor group. The plan still needs approval from FIFA’s 211 member associations.

UEFA said the move “crosses a line”, while Burnham warned that football should not be controlled by investors. He insisted the game belongs to fans and communities rather than commercial interests.
