Mirissa harbour to get fisheries-tourism upgrade

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - South Korea has agreed in principle to support a programme to develop Mirissa Harbour as a fisheries and tourism port, while also exploring cooperation with Sri Lanka in fisheries technology, aquaculture and marine biotechnology.

The preliminary agreement was reached during bilateral discussions between Sri Lanka's Secretary to the Ministry of Fisheries, Aquatic and Ocean Resources, Dr. B.K. Kolitha Kamal Jinadasa, and South Korean representatives during his visit to Busan.

Dr. Jinadasa attended the 2026 Korea International Cooperation Conference on Oceans and Fisheries (KICCOF-2026) at the invitation of South Korea's Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries. The conference, organised by the Korea Maritime Institute (KMI) under its Best Achievements in Oceans and Fisheries Official Development Assistance (BADA) programme, was held under the theme “A New Path for Ocean and Fisheries ODA: Connecting Technology, Communities, and People”.

The event brought together nearly 100 government officials and experts from 11 countries to discuss cooperation in ocean and fisheries development.

During the bilateral discussions, Sri Lanka focused on attracting South Korean investment to modernise its fisheries industry, promote local fisheries and develop fisheries tourism. The proposed development of Mirissa Harbour is expected to combine fisheries-related activities with tourism while addressing marine litter.

The two sides also discussed the possibility of introducing artificial intelligence-based forecasting technology to Sri Lanka's fisheries sector by 2028, with a view to improving forecasting capabilities and supporting the industry.

Meanwhile, South Korean investors were invited to explore investment opportunities in Sri Lanka's aquaculture, marine bioprospecting and blue biotechnology sectors.

Addressing the conference, Dr. Jinadasa highlighted the longstanding relations between Sri Lanka and South Korea and the close cooperation between Sri Lanka's Fisheries Ministry and South Korean institutions, including the Korea Maritime Institute and the Korea Institute of Ocean Science and Technology (KIOST).

He said such international partnerships had helped create new employment opportunities for Sri Lankan youth in the fisheries sector.

The conference also provided an opportunity to identify future areas of cooperation by linking maritime technology with the development needs of coastal communities.
