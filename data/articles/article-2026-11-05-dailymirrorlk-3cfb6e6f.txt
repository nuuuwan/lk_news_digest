JAAF calls for evidence before Colombo Port charging rules are changed

Apparel industry says a US $ 50mn forex leakage claim needs to be tested and that exporters need cost certainty

The Joint Apparel Association Forum (JAAF) is calling on the authorities to test a reported claim that Sri Lanka “could be losing” more than US $ 50 million a year in foreign exchange through the Colombo Port charging arrangements before any changes are made to the rules that the exporters rely on.

The claim was made by the Ceylon Freight and Logistics Association (CEYFFA), whose General Secretary Inam Caffoor raised it at a press conference, reported in the Sunday Island, on October 31, 2026.

The report gives no calculation, period, container volume or transaction data and its wording is “could”, not “is”.

“The apparel exporters ship every day and they plan their costs around the freight charges they are quoted,” said JAAF Secretary General Yohan Lawrence.

“A figure of this size deserves scrutiny. But the rules that give the exporters predictable pricing should not be rewritten on a claim that has yet to be backed by evidence.”

For the JAAF’s members, the stakes are practical. Under Gazette No. 2334/26 of May 31, 2023, the terminal handling charges form part of all-inclusive freight, which can be recovered only from the party contractually bound to pay it and no other charges may be added for exporters. That framework gives the shippers a single, transparent price.

The National Chamber of Exporters has noted that before 2013, the importers and exporters faced nearly 50 separate charges and it has warned against going back to that. The timing adds to the concern. The apparel exports for January to September 2026 stood at US $ 3,588.46 million, a decline of 5.52 percent from the same period last year, even after September’s 3.47 percent rise.

n a year when the industry is working hard to hold on to orders, cost certainty and reliable logistics matter.

The JAAF also sees gaps in the leakage claim itself. The port’s own charge is set by tariff and the Sri Lanka Ports Authority has reportedly collected its charges from the shipping lines in US dollars since June 2022.

The money paid to a Sri Lankan port or terminal operator is a foreign-exchange inflow, not a leak. The rest of the freight rate is the shipping line’s payment for carrying cargo by sea, which is earned overseas on every international shipment. A legitimate freight payment to a foreign carrier is not automatically an unlawful outflow.

A simple test of scale shows why the detail matters. Colombo handled about 1.10 million import and export containers (TEUs) between January and October 2025, so a US $ 50 million loss spread across that cargo would equal roughly US $ 38 to US $ 45 per TEU. That arithmetic proves nothing by itself but it shows that a credible estimate has to start from actual transactions.

The JAAF is asking for five questions to be answered: Who is losing the money? Who is receiving it? Which transactions are involved? What is the legal basis? How was the figure calculated?

It also urges the Director General of Merchant Shipping, Sri Lanka Ports Authority, Central Bank of Sri Lanka and Sri Lanka Customs to request the underlying evidence from the CEYFFA, carry out a formal examination and publish the findings.

The JAAF supports the CEYFFA’s call for a consultative mechanism that includes all stakeholders and believes the exporters should have a seat at that table.

“If a loss exists, we should find it and fix it,” said Lawrence.

“If it does not, the public should be told that too. Our members need stable, transparent costs to compete and that is best protected by following the evidence.”
