Sri Lanka to launch oil, gas licensing round today

Colombo, Aug. 25 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka will launch today a new licensing round for offshore oil and gas exploration this year, with the Petroleum Development Authority of Sri Lanka (PDASL) preparing to invite international energy companies to explore and develop the country’s petroleum resources.

The authority said the licensing round is aimed at attracting global energy companies to explore and produce oil and natural gas offshore Sri Lanka. It said exploration carried out to date has confirmed viable natural gas accumulations off the western coast, with the development of these resources expected to strengthen energy security and support industrial growth.

The PDASL is expected to announce further details of the licensing process on Tuesday at a press conference.

The latest move follows a long-running effort to develop Sri Lanka’s offshore petroleum resources. Exploration in the country dates back to the late 1960s, while significant progress was made in the Mannar Basin in 2011 when Cairn Lanka drilled two wells and discovered natural gas in the Barracuda and Dorado fields.

The government plans to seek investment proposals for exploration and production in four blocks of the Mannar Basin. Cabinet approval for the licensing round was granted in May 2025, while the PDASL subsequently began work to market the blocks to potential international investors.

The process had previously been delayed by legal proceedings. However, recent reports indicate that the legal obstacles have been cleared, allowing authorities to move ahead with the international bidding process.

Sri Lanka has also established a legal framework for petroleum exploration and production through the Petroleum Resources Act No. 21 of 2021. A national policy on natural gas was gazetted in 2020, setting out measures to develop domestic demand and provide operators with options to commercialise offshore gas.

The Mannar Basin has previously been estimated to contain substantial hydrocarbon resources. A 2021 parliamentary estimate valued the potential oil and gas resources in the basin at around US$267 billion, although such figures represent resource potential rather than proven commercially recoverable reserves.
