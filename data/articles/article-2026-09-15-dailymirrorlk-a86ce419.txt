Strong remittances offer FX cushion as rupee comes under pressure

The workers’ remittances remained robust in August, providing a key foreign exchange cushion as the rupee continued to face depreciation pressure.

The remittance inflows reached US $ 748.6 million in August 2026, up nearly 10 percent from US $ 680.8 million a year earlier, according to the latest Central Bank data.

The strong year-on-year performance comes as the rupee has weakened against the US dollar, recording a 5.7 percent year-to-date depreciation as of September 11.

While the August inflows moderated by 3.7 percent from US $ 777.6 million in July, they remained at a relatively high level, reinforcing the remittances as an important source of foreign currency for the economy.

The Central Bank noted that the Brent and WTI crude prices surged during the week ended September 11, on concerns over the prolonged Middle East conflicts and potential supply disruptions. Both benchmarks had moved above US $ 100 per barrel by the end of the period.

The higher oil prices could increase Sri Lanka’s import bill and foreign exchange demand, making the steady inflows from the remittances increasingly important in supporting the balance of payments and easing the pressure in the domestic foreign exchange market.

Meanwhile, the gross official reserves were provisionally estimated at US $ 6.905 billion at end-August 2026, inclusive of the proceeds from the People’s Bank of China swap arrangement.

The workers’ remittances, together with the export and tourism receipts, remain among Sri Lanka’s principal sources of foreign exchange earnings.
