Japan provides Rs. 600 million for Kelani River water quality project

Colombo, October 26 (Daily Mirror) - Japan has pledged over Rs. 600 million for a five-year project to monitor and improve water quality in the Kelani River basin, with the initial consignment of water testing equipment handed over to Environment Minister Dr. Dammika Patabendi today (26).

The project, implemented through the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA) and the Japan Science and Technology Agency (JST) under the SATREPS programme, aims to establish a comprehensive data platform to monitor the aquatic environment of the Kelani River basin from 2026 to 2030.

The initiative seeks to address pollution in the Kelani River, a major source of drinking water for Colombo and surrounding areas, by strengthening water quality monitoring and supporting evidence-based government policymaking.

The equipment was handed over to the Minister by Japanese representatives at a meeting of the project's steering committee held at the Central Environmental Authority (CEA) auditorium today.

The equipment was subsequently distributed among the CEA, the University of Moratuwa and General Sir John Kotelawala Defence University for use in the project.

The Kelani River stretches approximately 145 kilometres, with a basin covering around 2,300 square kilometres. Approximately one-fourth of Sri Lanka's population lives within the basin, which serves as a vital source of drinking water.

The basin includes 14 major water treatment and distribution facilities and more than 5,400 industries, including those operating in the Biyagama and Seethawaka industrial zones with CEA authorisation.

However, the river faces significant pollution from industrial wastewater, untreated sewage, agricultural waste and sand and gem mining. Climate-related events, including floods and droughts, also pose challenges to water quality.

The absence of specific groundwater quality standards is another concern, particularly as groundwater is a major source of drinking water for many households in the Western Province.

The project will establish a basin-wide monitoring network and a comprehensive aquatic environment data platform to collect and analyse water quality information. It will also focus on developing policy frameworks, training personnel, engaging technical experts and publishing research findings.

The Aquatic Environment Data Operations Centre at the University of Moratuwa will play a key role in implementing the programme.

The project's steering committee operates under the leadership of Environment Ministry Secretary K.R. Uduwawala, while Professor So Kasama of Tohoku University in Japan serves as the principal investigator.

The committee meeting was attended by representatives of JICA, CEA Chairman Professor Tilak Hewawasam, CEA Director General Kapila Rajapaksa, Professor Lalith Rajapaksa of the University of Moratuwa's Department of Civil Engineering, the Kaduwela Municipal Commissioner and officials from the Environment Ministry.

Representatives of the Department of Irrigation, the Water Resources Board and the Clean Sri Lanka Secretariat are also involved in the initiative.

The project is expected to establish a scientific foundation for improving water quality management across the Kelani River basin, with the ultimate goal of ensuring safer drinking water for the public by 2030.
