Urgent call to curb aggravating wildlife crimes

There needs to be a holistic approach to protect elephants from farmers and people in elephant dominated areas. Recently, a joint operation in the Galgamuwa area uncovered a site allegedly used to manufacture hakka-patas (improvised explosive devices). Several suspects were arrested with deer skins and hakka-patas. Hakka-patas or jaw bombs are frequently used to chase away elephants. Galgamuwa is an area frequented by wild elephants and is the home ground of the magnificent tusker Kavantissa. Recently Kavantissa was shot it in its left hind leg and wildlife officials made immediate interventions to treat the iconic tusker. The fact that people are illegally manufacturing hakka-patas in such a sensitive area speaks volumes about the conflict. Minister Lal Kantha recently said that animals protected by the state should be managed and the wildlife department doesn’t have the resources to manage wild animals. He said that domestic animals such as cattle when sent into national parks create more issues due to competition for grass among herbivores. It is quite not clear as to what the government’s stance is with regards to protecting already protected animals such as elephants. But strict enforcement is required to curb aggravating wildlife crimes.
