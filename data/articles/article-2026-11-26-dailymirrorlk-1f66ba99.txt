World Bank launches $10 million rupee financing facility for Sri Lanka

Colombo, Nov. 26 (Daily Mirror) - The World Bank Group has introduced a new financing facility in Sri Lanka, aimed at expanding access to long-term rupee-denominated funding, reducing foreign exchange risks for businesses and encouraging private investment in renewable energy and infrastructure.

Under the initiative, the World Bank Group Treasury has arranged a six-year, fixed-rate Sri Lankan rupee borrowing line between the International Finance Corporation (IFC) and HSBC Sri Lanka, equivalent to US$5 million.

The facility marks the IFC’s first borrowing line in Sri Lanka and, together with additional local currency funding, will support a US$10 million, 10-year rupee-denominated loan to one of the country’s largest renewable energy companies.

The investment marks three significant milestones for the IFC, including its longest-tenor Sri Lankan rupee loan to date, its first rupee-denominated infrastructure loan and its first infrastructure investment in Sri Lanka since the country’s economic crisis.

By raising funds in local currency, the World Bank Group aims to provide businesses with financing in the same currency in which they generate revenue, helping them reduce exposure to exchange rate fluctuations.

“Access to long-term local currency financing can help businesses invest, grow and create jobs without taking on foreign exchange risk they may not be able to manage,” said Keshav Gaur, Director of Treasury Client Solutions and Structuring at the World Bank Group.

The World Bank Group said it intends to build on the facility to expand access to long-term local currency financing in Sri Lanka, with the aim of supporting private sector investment and economic growth.
