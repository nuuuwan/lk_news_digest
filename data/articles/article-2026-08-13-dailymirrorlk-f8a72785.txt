UN Global Compact launches coalition to take sustainability into SL boardrooms

The UN Global Compact Network Sri Lanka has launched a new private-sector coalition, aimed at integrating sustainability, stronger governance and responsible business practices into corporate boardrooms and executive decision-making.

The Responsible Business Coalition will bring together the business and professional institutions to promote the UN Global Compact’s Ten Principles across Sri Lanka’s corporate sector through advocacy, technical capacity-building, strategic engagements and shared learning.

Its founding members include the Sri Lanka Bankers’ Association’s Sustainable Banking Initiative, Sri Lanka Institute of Directors and Centre for a Smart Future.

The Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) and The Ceylon Chamber of Commerce are supporting the initiative and working towards founding membership, according to the UN Global Compact Network Sri Lanka.

The coalition is intended to move responsible business practices beyond the companies’ dedicated sustainability functions and integrate them into board-level governance, executive leadership and professional development.

The initiative was launched during UN Global Compact CEO and Executive Director Sanda Ojiambo’s visit to Sri Lanka.

The launch coincided with a ‘Ring the Bell for Responsible Business’ ceremony at the CSE, where Ojiambo joined the exchange’s senior leadership to ring the opening bell.

Addressing the event, Ojiambo said responsible business was increasingly becoming a strategic requirement for competitiveness, trust and long-term value creation rather than merely a corporate responsibility exercise.

“The private sector has a critical role to play in shaping economies that are resilient, inclusive and sustainable. As businesses navigate a rapidly changing global landscape, responsible business is increasingly central to competitiveness, trust and long-term value creation.”

Ojiambo said the stock exchanges and listed companies were positioned to accelerate the transition by strengthening corporate governance, mobilising sustainable finance and supporting progress towards the Sustainable Development Goals.

She said the UN Global Compact’s Ten Principles provided the companies with a framework to convert the sustainability commitments into action. The principles cover human rights, labour, environment and anti-corruption.

CSE CEO Rajeeva Bandaranaike said responsible corporate conduct, transparency and strong governance were fundamental to investor confidence and resilient economic growth.

“The Responsible Business Coalition provides an important platform for bringing together businesses and professional institutions around a shared commitment to sustainability and responsible corporate conduct,” he said.

The coalition will seek to use the reach of its participating business and professional associations to provide current and future corporate leaders with knowledge, tools and partnerships to improve responsible business practices.

The bell-ringing event forms part of an international initiative involving the UN Sustainable Stock Exchanges Initiative, UN Global Compact, International Finance Corporation and World Federation of Exchanges.
