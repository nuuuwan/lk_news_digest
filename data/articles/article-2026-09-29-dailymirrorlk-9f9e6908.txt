Tissamaharama Base Hospital faces severe staff, facility shortages

Colombo, Sep. 29 (Daily Mirror) - Serious shortcomings in infrastructure, medical and nursing staff, and critical care facilities at the Tissamaharama Base Hospital have created difficulties in maintaining patient care services, President of the Government Medical Officers’ Forum (GMOF) and Consultant Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa said.

He said the hospital, which serves a large population in the Hambantota District, is currently facing a major problem due to the failure of its sewage disposal system.

He said the malfunctioning system has caused a foul odour from the hospital toilets to spread throughout the premises, causing severe discomfort to patients and healthcare workers.

Although the Medical Superintendent had raised the matter at a recent Hambantota District Development Committee meeting, no solution had been provided so far, Dr. Sanjeewa said.

He also highlighted a severe shortage of medical and nursing staff. The hospital requires nearly 100 doctors, but only around 50 are currently serving, while the required nursing staff cadre is approximately 176, compared to only around 80 currently on duty.

The hospital also lacks an administrative officer and an accountant to carry out essential administrative functions, he said.

Dr. Sanjeewa said that despite nearly 15 years having passed since the hospital was upgraded to a Base Hospital, it still lacks an Intensive Care Unit (ICU) and an emergency unit equipped to provide specialised medical care.

He said the absence of these facilities had created serious difficulties for specialists in providing treatment, while a significant number of specialists are serving in an acting capacity. Patient care services have also been affected by a shortage of anaesthesiologists, he added.

Another issue is the lack of permanent official quarters for specialists and the Medical Superintendent, Dr. Sanjeewa said.

As a result, accommodation in the private sector is being rented temporarily at a cost of nearly Rs. 180,000 per month, which he described as an unnecessary expenditure of public funds that could be avoided if proper official quarters were provided.

Dr. Sanjeewa said the difficulties faced by the hospital were ultimately placing an additional burden on residents of the area, who are compelled to travel to the Hambantota or Matara Base Hospitals to obtain certain medical services.

He urged the provincial political and health authorities, as well as officials of the relevant ministry, to intervene without delay and address the longstanding shortcomings at the Tissamaharama Base Hospital.
