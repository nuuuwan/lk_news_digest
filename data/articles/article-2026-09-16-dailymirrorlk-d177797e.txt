LAWASIA raises serious concerns over procedure in Supreme Court hearings on 22nd Amendment

Colombo, Sep. 16 (Daily Mirror) - The Law Association for Asia and the Pacific (LAWASIA) has expressed serious concern over the procedure followed by the Supreme Court of Sri Lanka in the constitutional challenges to the proposed 22nd Amendment to the Constitution, which seeks to extend the retirement ages of Judges of the Supreme Court and the Court of Appeal.

In a statement issued dated September 16, 2026, LAWASIA reiterated its longstanding commitment to the rule of law, judicial independence and the proper administration of justice. The organisation referred to its earlier statement of 30 June 2026, in which it had already flagged concerns about the implications of the ad hoc reform for judicial independence and the need for transparency, consultation and stakeholder engagement.

LAWASIA observed that it had taken cognisance of the Commonwealth Lawyers Association’s (CLA) report dated 7 September 2026, prepared after the CLA President visited Sri Lanka to observe the Supreme Court proceedings.

The LAWASIA stressed that its concerns relate solely to the procedure adopted in the hearings and that it does not express any view on the merits of the constitutional challenges or the substantive arguments advanced by the parties.

The association observed that applications seeking the recusal of the Chief Justice and requesting that the petitions be heard by a Full Court comprising all sitting Judges of the Supreme Court were declined. According to the CLA report, no reasons were provided for the dismissal of these applications other than that the Court had jurisdiction to hear the matter. The recusal application had been made on the basis that the Chief Justice would be the first Supreme Court Judge who could benefit from the proposed amendment.

LAWASIA also highlighted concerns regarding the composition of the five-member Bench that heard the petitions. With the exception of the Chief Justice, the four Judges immediately senior to Justice Achala Wengappuli namely Justice Yasantha Kodagoda, Justice A.H.M.D. Nawaz, Justice Shiran Gooneratne and Justice Janak De Silva were not included on the Bench.

While acknowledging that seniority is not, of itself, determinative of the constitution of a Bench and without questioning the competence of the Judges who sat, LAWASIA stated that in proceedings of such exceptional constitutional significance, the basis upon which a Bench is constituted “should be transparent and discernible and capable of maintaining public confidence in the institutional impartiality of the Court.”

A further serious concern related to procedural fairness. After the oral submissions of the Interveners and the State, the Petitioners were not afforded an opportunity to make oral submissions in reply, although they were permitted to tender written replies by 3.00 pm the following day. LAWASIA observed that the opportunity to respond meaningfully to material submissions made against a party’s case is an important aspect of procedural fairness.

“Scrutiny of judicial procedure should not be regarded as an attack upon judicial independence. On the contrary, judicial independence must be safeguarded both in fact and in public perception, and procedural fairness, impartiality and transparency are essential to maintaining public confidence in the Judiciary,” the statement said.

The statement was issued under the signature of TL Yap, President of LAWASIA.
