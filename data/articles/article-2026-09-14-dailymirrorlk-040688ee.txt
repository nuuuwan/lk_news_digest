Gnanasara Thera reportedly missing after pardon annulled

Colombo, Sep. 14 (Daily Mirror) - Bodu Bala Sena (BBS) General Secretary Galagoda Aththe Gnanasara Thera is reportedly untraceable after the Supreme Court annulled the presidential pardon granted to him in 2019, requiring him to serve the remaining portion of his prison sentence.

Prison officials had reportedly visited the Thera’s temple in Nawala, Rajagiriya, to take steps to return him to prison. However, monks at the temple had informed the officials that they were unaware of his current whereabouts.

A prison official said prison authorities could not take further action unless the Thera surrendered to the police or was taken into custody by the police.

Meanwhile, a senior BBS official told the Daily Mirror that ethnic tensions in the country had declined following the identification of Zahran Hashim as the leader of the National Thawheed Jama'ath (NTJ), which the official alleged was responsible for the coordinated Easter Sunday bomb attacks.

The BBS official also claimed that Gnanasara Thera had never attempted to create ethnic conflict in the country.

The Supreme Court recently ruled that the presidential pardon granted to Gnanasara Thera by then-President Maithripala Sirisena in May 2019 was null and void. The Court held that the decision was arbitrary and violated public trust and principles of natural justice.Gnanasara Thera had been sentenced to six years in prison for contempt of court and had served around nine months of the sentence before receiving the presidential pardon.

Following the Supreme Court ruling, he is required to serve the remaining portion of his sentence, subject to applicable legal and prison procedures.

The development comes amid renewed discussions on ethnic harmony and hate speech. Representatives of the All Ceylon Jamiyyathul Ulama (ACJU), during a recent meeting with President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, expressed appreciation to the Government for maintaining ethnic harmony over the past two years.

The ACJU representatives also called for action against hate speech being disseminated through social media.
