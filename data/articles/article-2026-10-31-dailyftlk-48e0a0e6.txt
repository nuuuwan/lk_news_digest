Mayor of Colombo joins British High Commission and delegation of European Union to launch joint solar initiative

Mayor of Colombo Vraie Cally Balthazaar today joined the  British High Commission Colombo and the Delegation of the European Union to Sri Lanka to  launch their joint solar energy initiative, marking a practical partnership for more sustainable operations.

The Mayor joined British High Commissioner Andrew Patrick and EU Ambassador-designate Sylvia Hartleif and Deputy High Commissioner Theresa O’Mahony for a ceremonial switch on and a showcase of the High Commission’s wider greening initiatives.

Approximately 240 kWp of installed solar capacity across the High Commission’s office estate is projected to generate 365,000 kWh of electricity annually, avoiding an estimated  193 tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions each year.

British High Commissioner Andrew Patrick, subject to approval: “The UK and the European Union share a commitment to a cleaner future and a  belief that we achieve more together. This joint solar initiative puts that commitment  into practice here in Colombo, generating renewable energy and reducing our  environmental footprint. It is a practical partnership we can build on.”

The solar partnership forms part of a wider effort to reduce the High Commission’s  environmental footprint. More efficient cooling, lighting and building systems, alongside  solar water heating, offer estimated energy savings of 79,200 kWh a year.

Other measures include 10,000 litres of rainwater storage capacity and a vehicle fleet that is 25% plug-in hybrid. Waste segregation and safe disposal, including recycling and  composting, account for approximately 1.13 tonnes on an annualised basis.

EU Ambassador-designate Sylvia Hartleif noted: “Transitioning to cleaner energy requires ambition, partnership and practical action.  This joint solar initiative demonstrates how we can turn our shared climate  commitments into tangible results, reducing emissions while making our operations  more sustainable. It also reflects the European Union’s broader support for Sri  Lanka’s Green Recovery, advancing renewable energy, fostering an inclusive and  climate-resilient energy transition, and contributing to greater access to affordable,  sustainable energy.”
