Heritance Ahungalla celebrates 45 years of architectural legacy with ‘45 to Infinity’

A celebration of Geoffrey Bawa’s timeless vision, four and a half decades of hospitality and a horizon of possibilities

The Heritance Ahungalla marks its 45th anniversary on 23 November 2026 with ‘45 to Infinity’, a celebration honouring the architectural legacy of Geoffrey Bawa, the people who have shaped the property’s journey and its enduring place in Sri Lanka’s hospitality landscape.

More than a milestone, the anniversary reflects four and a half decades of evolution, during which the property has remained deeply connected to its natural surroundings and architectural identity.

Originally opened in 1981 as the Triton Hotel, Heritance Ahungalla was conceived as a distinctive coastal retreat by Geoffrey Bawa, Sri Lanka’s pioneering architect and a leading figure of Tropical Modernism. At a time when resort design often placed buildings at the centre of the experience, Bawa envisioned a property that embraced its environment, allowing the ocean, tropical gardens, natural light and coastal breezes to shape its character.

One of the most defining expressions of this vision is the property’s iconic infinity pool, designed to create a seamless visual connection between the pool, the Indian Ocean and the sky. Its disappearing edge captures Bawa’s philosophy of dissolving the boundaries between architecture and nature, an experience that remains as striking today as it was when the hotel first opened. The anniversary theme, ‘45 to Infinity’, draws inspiration from this architectural landmark, connecting the property’s 45-year heritage with an endless horizon of possibilities.

Throughout Heritance Ahungalla, Geoffrey Bawa’s design philosophy can be discovered across 21 distinctive architectural features. From the iconic Triton sculpture and the 600-foot Sakman Maluwa pathway to the open-air lobby, the Mirror Lounge, the 03 Blues infinity pool and carefully framed ocean views, these architectural details tell a story of harmony between nature, culture and design. Natural materials, open spaces, tropical landscaping, reflecting pools and the interplay of light and shadow create an environment where architecture and landscape exist as one.

The property’s architectural significance extends beyond its visual appeal. It represents an important chapter in Sri Lanka’s design heritage and continues to attract guests, architects and design enthusiasts eager to experience Bawa’s approach to Tropical Modernism. Through its anniversary celebrations, Heritance Ahungalla will bring this legacy to life with architectural storytelling, guided experiences and a dedicated content series exploring the distinctive design elements found throughout the property.

Beyond its architecture, the story of Heritance Ahungalla is equally one of people, relationships and shared experiences. Over the past 45 years, the property has welcomed generations of travellers, celebrated personal milestones and offered individuals the opportunity to build lasting careers in hospitality. Its team members, including those who have dedicated decades of service to the hotel, remain an integral part of its identity.

Aitken Spence Hotel Holdings PLC Chairperson Stasshani Jayawardena said: “Heritance Ahungalla represents an important part of Sri Lanka’s architectural and hospitality heritage. For 45 years, it has stood as a testament to Geoffrey Bawa’s remarkable vision and to the enduring value of creating experiences that are deeply connected to their surroundings. This milestone is also an opportunity to recognise the generations of dedicated team members, guests and partners who have contributed to its journey. As we celebrate this legacy, we remain committed to preserving its distinctive identity while looking towards the opportunities that lie ahead.”

Aitken Spence Hotel Holdings PLC Managing Director Susith Jayawickrama said: “The 45-year journey of Heritance Ahungalla is a story of vision, resilience and the people who have brought that vision to life. From its beginnings as the Triton Hotel to its continued recognition as a landmark of Geoffrey Bawa’s architectural philosophy, the property has maintained a distinctive place within Sri Lanka’s hospitality landscape. As we mark this anniversary, we extend our appreciation to our teams, guests and partners who have been part of this journey. The ‘45 to Infinity’ campaign reflects our commitment to honouring this remarkable heritage while continuing to create meaningful experiences for generations to come.”
