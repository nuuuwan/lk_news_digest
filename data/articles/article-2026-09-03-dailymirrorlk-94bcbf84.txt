Police told not to issue charges for missing digital insurance cards

Colombo, Sep. 3 (Daily Mirror) - Traffic police officers have been instructed not to issue charge sheets to motorists solely for failing to produce a digital vehicle insurance card, Deputy Inspector General of Police (DIG) in charge of the Traffic Control and Road Safety Range W.P.J. Senadheera said.

Addressing a media briefing today (3), DIG Senadheera said officers have been instructed to verify the validity of a vehicle’s insurance coverage using the driver’s mobile phone or the officer’s own mobile phone.

He said causing inconvenience to motorists by failing to follow instructions issued by the Inspector General of Police regarding digital insurance cards would amount to a serious dereliction of duty.

The DIG said complaints had been received that several officers attached to the Padukka and Gampaha Police Stations had harassed members of the public by refusing to accept digital insurance cards.

Accordingly, the IGP has instructed all police stations across the country to officially accept digital insurance cards issued by 14 leading insurance companies, which have been approved by the Insurance Regulatory Commission under an additional message dated May 5, 2026, issued under the RTM 93 Act.

DIG Senadheera said digital insurance cards have full legal recognition under the Electronic Transactions Act No. 19 of 2006.

He said motorists can also verify their insurance coverage through several free methods if they do not have a digital insurance card.

He said instructions have been reissued to lower-ranking officers through the Officers-in-Charge (OICs) of all 607 police stations across the island.

The public has been urged to lodge a complaint with the nearest police station or contact the Traffic Headquarters' special hotline on 070 475 5600 if an officer refuses to accept a valid digital insurance card.

DIG Senadheera said strict disciplinary action would be taken against officers who violate the instructions.
