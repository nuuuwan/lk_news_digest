Imported food prices may rise due to El Niño effects in India, Minister warns

Prices of certain imported food items could increase due to weather conditions in India caused by El Niño, Trade Minister Wasantha Samarasinghe said.

However, importers have agreed to explore alternative markets and ensure an uninterrupted supply of essential goods during the upcoming festive season, he said.

The Minister made these remarks following a discussion held at the Ministry with importers to address possible price increases of essential food items during the festive period.

The Minister said that importers had assured the government that all imported goods, except dried chillies, could be supplied to the local market without disruption.

Explaining the situation surrounding dried chilli imports, the Minister said the main issue was the high moisture content in certain consignments arriving from India.

Under Sri Lanka Standards Institution (SLSI) regulations and food safety requirements, imported dried chillies must have a moisture content below 12 per cent to be cleared. However, heavy rainfall and El Niño-related weather conditions in India have resulted in some consignments recording moisture levels of between 13 and 14 per cent, delaying their release.

He said that the situation did not amount to an actual shortage of dried chillies in the market, but was a clearance issue arising from the need to comply with quality and safety standards.

As further shipments could also be affected if the weather conditions persist, importers have requested discussions to establish an interim mechanism that would remain in effect until next March, he said.

Minister Samarasinghe warned that the Government would intervene if traders attempted to exploit supply-related difficulties to increase prices unfairly.

While the Government expects prices to be determined by market supply and demand, without unnecessary intervention, action would be taken through the Consumer Affairs Authority and the Consumer Services Council, including the imposition of controlled prices, if traders seek to exploit consumers, he said.
