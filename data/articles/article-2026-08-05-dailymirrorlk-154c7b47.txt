Sri Lankans working in India can continue Sri Lanka EPF contributions

Sri Lankan workers temporarily employed in India will be able to continue contributing to the Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF) administered by the Sri Lankan Department of Labour while being exempted from contributing to the Indian Employees’ Provident Fund under a proposed Social Security Agreement between Sri Lanka and India.

The agreement is expected to address key social security concerns faced by Sri Lankan workers employed in India and prevent them from having to make duplicate contributions to two separate social security systems.

The fourth round of bilateral talks between the governments of Sri Lanka and India on the proposed agreement concluded successfully in Colombo on July 21 and 22 this year, with both sides reaching an agreement on outstanding issues related to the draft and finalising clauses to be included in the pact.

Negotiations on the agreement began nearly a decade ago, with the latest discussions marking a significant step towards strengthening social security protection for workers of both countries.

Under the proposed arrangement, eligible Indian workers temporarily employed in Sri Lanka will also receive similar benefits, allowing them to continue contributions to their respective social security systems without being required to make additional contributions in the host country.

The agreement is also expected to provide relief to Sri Lankan workers who have previously contributed to India’s Employees’ Provident Fund but have been unable to withdraw their savings.

Under existing Indian EPF regulations, members are eligible to withdraw their benefits after reaching the age of 58. However, many Sri Lankan workers have faced difficulties in accessing their funds due to practical issues, including challenges in maintaining or operating Indian bank accounts.

Once the agreement comes into force, eligible Sri Lankan workers will be able to access their accumulated EPF benefits through an established mechanism.

The Indian delegation at the talks was led by P. S. Gangadhar, while the Sri Lankan delegation was headed by Commissioner General of Labour Nadeeka Wataliyadda. Senior officials from the Ministry of Labour, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Foreign Employment and Tourism participated in the discussions.
