Tax hikes risk pushing consumers to illicit liquor: DCSL

Distilleries Company of Sri Lanka PLC (DCSL) has warned that further increases in alcohol taxes could drive more consumers towards illicit products, undermining the legitimate manufacturers and depriving the government of revenue.

The country’s largest spirits producer said successive adjustments to the excise duties, combined with a higher corporate income tax rate for the alcoholic beverage industry, had increased pressure on profitability and consumer affordability.

“The unregulated segment of the market remains a structural drag on the legitimate industry,” DCSL said in its annual report for 2025/26.

The company said every litre sold outside the excise system generated no revenue for the state while allowing the illicit producers to undercut the tax-compliant manufacturers on the price. Unregulated alcohol also posed public health risks, it added.

DCSL identified further changes to the corporate tax or excise regime as the largest external variable facing the group in the 2026/27 financial year. The pricing and portfolio decisions would be calibrated once any such changes were confirmed, it said.

Sri Lanka’s alcohol industry has long argued that the steep tax-led price increases can reduce the legal sales without delivering a corresponding rise in government revenue, as the price-sensitive consumers shift towards the cheaper unregulated alternatives. DCSL did not provide the market-wide data quantifying the scale of the illicit alcohol consumption.

The company’s payments to the state climbed 20.4 percent to Rs.116.57 billion during the year ended March 31, 2026, from Rs.96.86 billion a year earlier.

The total was equivalent to about 71 percent of DCSL’s standalone gross turnover of Rs.164.38 billion. The excise duties alone rose 20.7 percent to Rs.102.90 billion, from Rs.85.26 billion.

The gross turnover increased 18.3 percent, while profit before tax rose at a slower 4.6 percent to Rs.30.35 billion. The income tax expenses increased 18.2 percent to Rs.13.50 billion, contributing to a 4.3 percent decline in standalone profit after tax to Rs.16.84 billion.

At group level, profit after tax rose 16.3 percent to Rs.17.07 billion, supported by the contribution and continued integration of DCSL Breweries Lanka Ltd.

DCSL said the improved macroeconomic conditions, including lower inflation and a more stable rupee, had yet to translate fully into stronger household purchasing power. Consumer spending remained cautious, due to the high cost of living and repeated tax adjustments, particularly affecting the discretionary and price-sensitive products such as alcohol.

The company called for a balanced fiscal and regulatory framework that protects the government revenue while allowing the legitimate producers to compete with the illicit market.

For 2026/27, DCSL plans to focus on brewery integration, automation at its Seeduwa facility and supply-chain improvements to contain the costs as the tax and excise pressures continue.
