Nearly 10,000 snakebites reported annually in Sri Lanka

Colombo, Oct. 2 (Daily Mirror) - Nearly 10,000 snakebite cases are reported in Sri Lanka each year, with hundreds of people losing their lives, as local and international experts gathered in Kandy yesterday for the world’s first international conference dedicated to snakes.

The “1st World Congress on Snakes – 2026,” held under the theme “Snakes, Science, Species Survival & Sri Lanka,” commenced yesterday at The Grand Kandyan Hotel with the participation of more than 400 scholars, researchers, environmentalists and field experts representing 42 countries.

Snakebite and its impact on human health are among the key issues being addressed at the four-day congress.

Globally, approximately 138,000 people die each year from snakebites, while many survivors suffer permanent disabilities, including limb loss, disfigurement, kidney failure and severe psychological trauma.

Sri Lanka also faces a significant snakebite burden, with nearly 10,000 cases reported annually. A considerable number involve envenomation, while hundreds of victims die as a result.

However, experts at the congress are also highlighting an important distinction: the vast majority of Sri Lanka’s snake species are harmless to humans, with only a very small proportion posing a serious medical threat.

The issue is particularly significant as Sri Lanka is home to 112 of the 411 snake species recorded worldwide. Of these, 63 species are endemic to the island.

At the same time, snakes face growing threats from habitat loss and fragmentation, agricultural expansion, urbanisation and climate change.

Recent extreme weather situations have also increased encounters between wildlife and human communities, potentially raising the likelihood of human-snake encounters.

The four-day congress aims to bring together global expertise on snake biology, diversity, conservation and medical research, while examining possible future measures based on scientific data.

Environment Minister Dammika Patabendi, who was the patron of the the inaugural session, said the conference should go beyond academic discussions and produce knowledge that could be translated into practical action.

He said institutions affiliated with the Ministry were working to implement national biodiversity priorities under the National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan for 2026–2030.

The Minister stressed that the knowledge generated through the conference should ultimately contribute to saving both human and wildlife lives.

Sri Lanka, the United States and Australia are serving as key partners of the congress, with experts from countries including India, Australia, Germany, Pakistan, Poland and Malaysia taking part.

Eight new books on snakes are also scheduled to be launched during the congress, while the Department of Posts plans to issue a commemorative stamp to mark the first World Snake Conference.
