Have courage to face your opponents at the Polls: Dayasiri tells Government

Colombo, November 21 (Daily Mirror)- The government should have the courage to face its political opponents at elections, as those it seeks to suppress may ultimately have the last laugh, MP Dayasiri Jayasekera told Parliament today.

Jayasekera made these remarks during the Committee Stage debate on the Budget, referring to the arrest of MP Namal Rajapaksa.

He warned that a country could retain its Parliament, judiciary and electoral system while gradually moving towards one-party rule, stressing that the intimidation of trade unionists, citizens and political opponents could undermine democratic freedoms.

He also cautioned that the independence of the judiciary was essential to protecting citizens, particularly when they felt threatened by those in power.

"An independent judiciary is the last place to which that citizen can turn. If he comes to see the court as an extension of the executive, where do we expect him to go?" he asked.

The MP further argued that using the criminal justice system against political opponents to prevent them from contesting elections reflected a lack of confidence in the government's ability to secure another mandate from the people.

He urged the government to defend its record before the electorate rather than restrict the choices available to voters.

Referring to Sri Lanka's political history, Jayasekera recalled how former President J.R. Jayewardene's government stripped former Prime Minister Sirimavo Bandaranaike of her civic rights in 1980, preventing her from contesting the 1982 Presidential Election.

He said the move not only affected Bandaranaike but also deprived the public of the opportunity to exercise their democratic choice.

Addressing the government benches, Jayasekera warned that those who used the law to exclude political opponents from electoral contests would eventually be held accountable by future generations.

He called on the government to allow the people to decide the political fate of their opponents through elections.

Recalling that Sirimavo Bandaranaike returned to power as Prime Minister for the third time in 1994, he concluded by reminding the government that she had ultimately had the last laugh.
