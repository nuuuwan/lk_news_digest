Sri Lankan, Malaysian university students join hands to clean Mount Lavinia beach

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - University students from Sri Lanka and Malaysia joined hands in a beach clean-up campaign at Mount Lavinia yesterday (18), in a move to promote environmental conservation and strengthen international student exchange cooperation.

The programme was held under the leadership of Deputy Environment Minister Anton Jayakody, with the support of the Ministry of Environment.

The initiative was organised alongside the “Kembara Eksplorasi Asia @ Colombo, Sri Lanka 2026” international student exchange programme, jointly conducted by Universiti Malaysia Pahang Al-Sultan Abdullah (UMPSA) and Sri Lanka’s University of Vocational Technology (UoVT).

The event brought together university students, academics, representatives of Malaysian universities, officials from the Ministry of Environment and the Department of Coast Conservation and Coastal Resource Management, as well as representatives of the Malaysian High Commission.

Addressing the gathering, Deputy Minister Jayakody said Sri Lanka’s proposal titled “Enhancing Meaningful Participation of Youth in Environmental Processes and Environmental Education” had been adopted by consensus at the seventh session of the United Nations Environment Assembly (UNEA-7), held in Nairobi, Kenya, in December 2025.

He also said the Cabinet of Ministers had granted special approval on November 17 to facilitate greater youth participation in environmental conservation and sustainable development.

The Deputy Minister stressed that protecting marine ecosystems was not the responsibility of a single country and said the joint effort by Sri Lankan and Malaysian students could inspire young people across the region to take a more active role in protecting the environment.
