Seafood exporters seek easier import rules in push for US $ 680mn by 2030

Sri Lanka’s seafood exporters have called for simpler rules on importing raw materials for processing and re-export, saying continued access to overseas supplies will be critical to reach US $ 680 million in annual export earnings by 2030.

The Seafood Exporters’ Association of Sri Lanka (SEASL) said the value-added products generated US $ 64 million of US $ 246.12 million earned from seafood exports in the first nine months of 2026. That lifted their share of seafood export earnings to 26 percent, from 4 percent in 2022.

“The priority now is implementation. We must ensure that the exporters can access these incentives, that the raw-material imports for value-added processing continue without unnecessary restrictions and that sustainable fisheries management remains a national priority. With the right policies, Sri Lanka can achieve US $ 680 million in seafood export earnings by 2030,” said Dilan Fernando of SEASL.

The association said the proposed overhaul of the Temporary Importation for Export Processing scheme, together with the Customs digitalisation and National Single Window for Trade, could simplify the procedures and cut costs.

It said access to imported raw materials helps the processors keep the factories running and expand production for overseas markets.

Budget 2027 proposes loans of up to Rs.500 million, at 5 percent annual interest for qualifying large-scale fisheries enterprises. The SEASL said the financing could support investment in processing plants, equipment and cold-chain infrastructure.

A proposed 200 percent enhanced capital allowance for qualifying Board of Investment-approved export manufacturing projects worth at least US $ 3 million could also encourage the processors to expand capacity and develop higher-value products, the association said.

The budget proposes Rs.5 billion for fisheries infrastructure in the Northern province, including the Pesalai Harbour and fishery anchorages, to improve the landing and handling facilities.

A further Rs.1 billion is earmarked for fisheries resource management, including scientific stock assessments, habitat mapping and monitoring.

The SEASL identified shrimp farming as another source of export growth and called for closer links between the aquaculture farmers, processors and exporters. It cited the Marine Stewardship Council certification of Sri Lanka’s North-Western Blue Swimming Crab Fishery as an example of how recognised sustainability standards can help the producers access premium markets.
