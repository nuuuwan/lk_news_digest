Hemas eyes Kenya as springboard for foreign expansion, pursues potential acquisitions

Targets 15% int’l revenue share by 2030, using Kenya as gateway to 330 million-strong East African market

Plans phased rollout of high-margin stationery lines to scale Twiga’s portfolio and profitability over the next two years

Reassures 80-85% of capital investment will stay in Sri Lanka despite dedicated US$ 100 mn M&A war chest

Hemas Holdings PLC is eyeing Kenya as an operational springboard for its expansion across the East African Community, alongside a pipeline of potential acquisitions following its recent buyout of Twiga Stationers and Printers Limited.

Backed by a dedicated acquisition war chest, the conglomerate aims to lift its international revenue share to 15 percent by 2030 — though group leadership stressed that regional growth will not come at the expense of domestic investment.

Hemas Holdings Group CEO Ashish Chandra said the 15 percent target does not depend solely on acquisitions.

“Our focus is to reach about 15 percent of the total, which can actually happen from the current portfolios that we have,” he said.

“Earning from US$ does not only come from buying the company, it also comes from the exports.” The group said at a media roundtable in Colombo this week that no further acquisitions are currently at an advanced stage, even as it continues to evaluate partnerships and joint ventures.

The Kenya move marks a milestone in the group’s 75-year history, following three years of market study and relationship-building.

“Hemas was started by my great-grandfather in 1948,” said Hemas Consumer Brands Managing Director Sabrina Esufally. “I don’t think in his wildest dreams he would have imagined that what was conceived as a small local company would now become a regional player.”

Kenya’s appeal lies in its position as gateway to the East African Community’s roughly 330 million-strong market.

“We are from a 22 million population, I get an access to 330 million population there,” Chandra said. Twiga already exports to 11 countries in the region, the company said, from its 290,000-square-foot Nairobi facility - giving Hemas an existing distribution base to build on.

Basic exercise books currently account for about 85 percent of Twiga’s revenue. Hemas plans to apply Atlas Axillia’s design, R&D and LEAN manufacturing expertise to move Twiga up the value chain, adding higher-margin categories - ballpoint pens, pencils, colour products and art supplies - which the company estimates represent a US$ 300 million adjacent opportunity in Kenya. The first year will focus on stabilising operations and consolidating share in exercise books under Twiga’s “Kasuku” brand, which the company said commands 91 percent brand recall in Kenya; the higher-margin categories are due to roll out in years two and three. Cost synergies are also expected from joint raw material procurement, given Twiga’s paper consumption is nearly three times that of Atlas.

The deal was structured as a US$ 22.0 million acquisition of a 75 percent stake, funded partly through a US$ 16.1 million foreign-currency borrowing facility over three and a half years. Twiga reported revenue of US$ 38 million — approximately Rs. 12.6 billion at current exchange rates — at an 8 percent normalised EBITDA margin for FY2025. The transaction lifts Hemas’s international revenue contribution from 3 to 10 percent of group turnover; group executives said the deal is expected to become earnings-accretive from FY2029.

Hemas Holdings Chief Strategy and Growth Officer Rizny Faisal drew a comparison to the group’s Atlas Axillia turnaround eight years ago. “We’ve almost doubled the turnover, we’ve done almost three times, 3.2 times to be exact, in terms of the bottom line,” he said. “So this is something similar that we’re looking at replicating with this particular business unit as well.” Twiga’s founding promoter will retain a 25 percent stake and serve as Chairman for two years; Atlas Axillia CEO Asitha Samaraweera has taken over as Twiga’s CEO to lead integration, while Hemas retains the existing local workforce and management, layering in its Sri Lankan retail distribution model over the medium term.

Chandra reaffirmed that domestic investment remains the priority. “Our more than 80 to 85 percent investment continues to be in Sri Lanka,” he said. “Because that’s where we believe is our home market, that’s where we have a right to win. And once we perfect the product and the way to do that capabilities in Sri Lanka, only then we will take it out.” US$ 100 million is available for possible acquisitions over the next three years, separate from a further US$100 million earmarked for hospital machinery upgrades — keeping the group primed to expand across consumer goods, healthcare and regional supply chains.
