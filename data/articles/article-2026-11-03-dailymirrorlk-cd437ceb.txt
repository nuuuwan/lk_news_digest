Custodial deaths are inimical to national interests

Two custodial deaths  have rocked the National People’s Power (NPP) government, amidst allegations by opposition parties and local and human rights activists that the government is giving increased powers to the police to detain individuals for anextremely long duration through new laws.

In one incident, 29-year-old Ishara Harsha Kumara, a resident of Kiriwaththuduwa, died under suspicious circumstances while in the custody of the Homagama Police Station on October 25, 2026.

The medical report on the suspect who died while in Homagama Police custody has revealed that he had three broken ribs and bleeding

Western Province Senior DIG Sajeewa Medawatte said investigations have revealed that the young suspect was assaulted during a clash between two groups on October 24, a day before his arrest. The suspect had claimed he was hospitalised after falling off a bicycle. However, police claim that investigations revealed he had been assaulted and later left hospital without medical approval.

What is the relevance of these claims about falling from a bicycle and assault during a clash? Do the police attempt to attribute the death of the youth to those incidents? However, the very action by the police clearly negates those attempts. It is common knowledge that hardly a man would be able to walk into a police station or at least to his home with three broken ribs and

Police admitted the man to Homagama hospital on the day after he was arrested. It also negates the validity of the claims about  falling from the bicycle and assaulted during a clash.

After the death of the youth three police officers attached to the Homagama police —including two Sub-Inspectors  of the Crime Division and the Anti-Vice Raids Unit, alongside a Police Constable on duty—were suspended from service on October 26. This is not the normal procedure police follow in respect of civilians, especially in connection with a death. They will arrest the suspects immediately if they are civilians. This special treatment to three of their colleagues raises a, serious doubt whether the victim’s family would be meted out justice.

Eventually, as social media mounted its criticism the OIC of the Crimes Division was arrested on October 28 and remanded till November 12 in connection with the incident.

In the second incident, villagers of Beddegama area in Kobeigane apprehended a 41-year-old man who was suspected of multiple burglaries in the area tied him to a tree, and assaulted him on October 25 – on the same day Homagama man was arrested. Kobeigane Police took the injured suspect into custody and held him at the station without immediately providing medical treatment. Although he was eventually hospitalised, he succumbed to his injuries the following day. Two police sergeants and five residents who initially apprehended the deceased and handed him over to police have also been arrested by Kobeigane Police following the death in custody.

Although assaulting the suspects - leave alone torturing - in police custody is illegal, it is common knowledge that it is a norm in police stations. Conversely. even the most innocent law breaker would not voluntarily reveal or accept his culpability for an offence, unless some sort of pressure is exerted on him. Suspects in high-profile cases  reportedly refuse to reveal the passwords of their computers which are believed to contain information crucial to national security. Needless to say, this a tricky issue for police officers.

Nevertheless, deaths in custody are absolutely unacceptable, notwithstanding the gravity of the allegations against the suspects in question or their notorious track records. The Human Rights Commission of Sri Lanka said following the death in Homagama:  “Between January 2020 and 31 March 2025, there were 49 reported deaths in police custody and 30 deaths resulting from confrontations with the police following arrest.”

Morality apart, human rights in individual countries are closely linked to the economic cooperation within the international community. Issues related to GSP+ and labour rights are cases in point. This is highly important for a country like Sri Lanka, which is recovering from a brutal economic crisis through formidable sacrifices made by the people, especially the downtrodden.
