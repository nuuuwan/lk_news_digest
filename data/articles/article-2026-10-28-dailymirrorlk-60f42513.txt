Over 5,000 tonnes of battery acid dumped annually in Sri Lanka

Colombo, Oct. 28 (Daily Mirror) - More than one million tonnes of lead slag have reportedly accumulated across Sri Lanka, while over 5,000 tonnes of battery acid are allegedly dumped on the ground annually, raising concerns over environmental pollution and public health.

Ceylon Waste Management (Pvt) Ltd Chairman, Sudesh Nandasiri revealed these figures during a webinar held to mark International Lead Poisoning Prevention Week of Action from October 18 to 24.

He said unsafe battery recycling and informal smelting activities were major sources of pollution, with significant quantities of lead slag reportedly found in the Puttalam District.

Nandasiri warned that toxic waste left exposed to rain and sunlight could contaminate the environment, while fumes from unsafe smelting could threaten workers and nearby communities. He called for unregulated smelting to be halted and used batteries to be processed at approved facilities with proper pollution-control systems.

He also stressed the need to collect and treat battery acid instead of dumping it on the ground.

Meanwhile, environmental campaigners warned that lead exposure could cause serious health problems, particularly among children, affecting brain development, learning and behaviour. Adults could also suffer damage to their kidneys, heart, blood and reproductive system.

Speakers at the webinar called for stronger enforcement of environmental regulations, improved battery collection systems, regular health checks for recycling workers and proper monitoring of hazardous waste.

They emphasised that although lead-acid battery recycling enables valuable materials to be recovered and reused, unsafe practices could turn the process into a serious threat to the environment and public health.
