July tea output falls 1% as medium-grown production drops

Sri Lanka’s tea production fell 1.05 percent in July from a year earlier, as the declines in the medium and low-grown teas offset the stronger high-grown output, the data compiled by Forbes & Walker Research showed.

Production totalled 21.26 million kilogrammes during the month, down from 21.49 million kilogrammes in July 2025. The output was also 8.33 percent below the 23.19 million kilogrammes produced in July 2024.

The medium-grown production recorded the sharpest year-on-year decline, falling 11.10 percent to 3.24 million kilogrammes, while the low-grown output slipped 1.20 percent to 13.58 million kilogrammes.

The high-grown production rose 8.24 percent to 4.19 million kilogrammes, while the green tea output increased 10.69 percent to 246,114 kilogrammes.

For the first seven months of the year, Sri Lanka produced 153.14 million kilogrammes of tea, down 2.62 percent, from 157.26 million kilogrammes in the same period last year.

The decline was driven largely by the low-grown production, which fell 3.60 percent to 90.23 million kilogrammes and the medium-grown output, which dropped 5.34 percent to 27.03 million kilogrammes.

The high-grown production increased 1.89 percent to 34.32 million kilogrammes during January-July, while the green tea production rose 9.21 percent to 1.56 million kilogrammes.

Despite the year-on-year decline, total production for the seven-month period remained 1.32 percent above the 151.15 million kilogrammes recorded in the corresponding period of 2024.
