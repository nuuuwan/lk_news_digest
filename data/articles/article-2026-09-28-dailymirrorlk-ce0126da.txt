TIN made mandatory for account opening, asset registration and share transfers from Nov. 1

Individuals will be required to furnish a valid Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) when opening any account at a bank or financial institution, registering motor vehicles, land or businesses, obtaining building plan approvals or credit cards, and transferring shares from November 1, the Finance Ministry announced.

The requirement, introduced through the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 11 of 2026, applies to both the transferor and the transferee in share transactions. A public notice issued by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) on September 22 also lists the renewal of motor vehicle licences among the covered transactions.

Officials responsible for processing these transactions have been directed through circulars to verify that a valid TIN has been submitted before completing them.

“All those intending to carry out any of the specified transactions should ensure they hold a valid TIN and, if not, obtain one,” the ministry advised, adding that a printout of the IRD’s online verification showing the holder’s NIC number and TIN would be accepted in lieu of the TIN certificate.

However, the compliance window appears narrow. The IRD noted in June that more than 13 million TINs had been issued, leaving roughly four million of the estimated 17 million adults required to hold one, or close to a quarter, yet to register, with around five weeks now remaining.

The measure revives a 2024 initiative, under which a Rs.50,000 penalty was announced for adults without a TIN, before the deadline was declared no longer operational in April that year amid public pressure. The current framework is considerably broader in scope, extending the banking requirement from current accounts to all accounts and bringing credit cards, business registration and share transfers within its ambit.
