Ampara human-elephant conflict: 14 new control units to be established

Colombo, Sep. 21 (Daily Mirror) - The Government has taken a series of measures to address the escalating human-elephant conflict in the Ampara District, including the establishment of 14 new Elephant Control Units in high-risk areas.

The decisions were taken at a special meeting of the Ampara District Human-Elephant Conflict Management Committee held yesterday (20) under the chairmanship of Environment Minister Dr. Dammika Patabendi.

The initial phase of establishing the 14 Elephant Control Units has already commenced, with officials instructed to expedite their completion.

The meeting also discussed introducing a Waste-to-Energy project as a long-term solution to the environmental issues surrounding the Addalaichenai garbage dump, which has been attracting wild elephants.

Attention was also given to strengthening field operations by increasing human and material resources. Accordingly, 1,463 Civil Security Department personnel and 552 Multi-Purpose Development Task Force officers have been deployed to support the Department of Wildlife Conservation.

The Wildlife Department has also received 38 motorcycles and two double-cab vehicles, while nine additional single-cab vehicles are scheduled to be provided to wildlife offices within the next two weeks.

The committee further decided to establish village-level committees that will meet monthly to strengthen community participation in managing the conflict.

These committees will assist in maintaining electric fences and newly installed spring gates, while legal and administrative measures will also be taken to prevent the unauthorised entry of cattle into forest reserves and remove existing unauthorised cattle herds from protected areas.

Deputy Minister Vasantha Piyathissa, Ampara District MPs, the District Secretary, senior officials from relevant government institutions, farmer representatives and local residents participated in the meeting.
