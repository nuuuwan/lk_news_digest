Parliamentary committee calls for wider tax net, faster digitalisation

Sri Lanka must accelerate the digitalisation of tax administration and bring more informal economic activity into the tax net, to sustain the recent improvement in government revenue, a parliamentary committee has said.

The Committee on Ways and Means called for stronger institutional capacity, better taxpayer compliance and closer data-sharing among the state agencies as part of the efforts to improve the efficiency of the tax system.

The recommendations followed a review of the government’s 2026 tax plan and revenue collection performance up to June 30.

The committee also examined the revenue estimates, performance across tax categories, size of the existing tax base and progress in reforming tax administration.

The officials from the Finance Ministry and Inland Revenue Department briefed the committee on the Revenue Administration Management Information System (RAMIS), taxation of digital services and introduction of technology to simplify the tax processes.

The key challenges identified included integrating the data held by different state institutions, upgrading the outdated technology and developing the specialised workforce required for modern tax administration.

The committee also called for further improvements to the RAMIS and expansion of electronic taxpayer registration, return filing and payment facilities.

The meeting was chaired by MP Wijesiri Basnayake and attended by the representatives of the Finance Ministry and Inland Revenue Department, alongside the deputy ministers and members of Parliament.
