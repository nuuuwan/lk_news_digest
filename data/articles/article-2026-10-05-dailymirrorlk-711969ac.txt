13,000 families live in unsafe houses in Sri Lanka

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Claiming that some 13,000 families live in unsafe houses in Sri Lanka, Housing, Construction and Water Supply Minister Susil Ranasinghe said the government’s priority is to provide safe housing for these 13,000 families currently living in unsafe conditions across Sri Lanka.

Speaking to the media, the Minister said figures from the 2024 Census of Population and Housing indicate that approximately 13,000 families are living in unsafe houses.

As part of the government’s efforts to address the issue, 5,000 houses constructed with government assistance will be handed over to beneficiaries today in conjunction with World Habitat Day.

He said the main ceremony to mark the occasion will be held at Temple Trees, with Prime Minister Dr. Harini Amarasuriya attending as the chief guest.

Of the 5,000 houses, 681 have been constructed in the Kurunegala District, 200 in Kandy, 694 in Jaffna, 341 in Gampaha, 402 in Ampara and 348 in Anuradhapura, with the remaining houses distributed among other districts.

The Minister said the houses were constructed with financial assistance of Rs. 1 million each from the National Housing Development Authority (NHDA). The authority also provided housing plans and technical assistance to beneficiaries.

In addition, houses constructed with Rs. 2 million in financial assistance from the Ministry’s resettlement unit for people displaced by the war in the Northern and Eastern provinces will also be handed over.

Responding to a question on housing villages, Minister Ranasinghe said the government does not plan to establish such projects, noting that previous housing village initiatives had not been successful.

He pointed out that many such projects had been developed on land belonging to the Forest Department or the Department of Wildlife Conservation, creating various difficulties.

Meanwhile, the Minister said the NHDA has commenced work on 10,000 new houses this year. A further 2,500 houses are being constructed through the Ministry’s resettlement unit, while the Samurdhi Department is working on another 1,250 houses.

These initiatives are in addition to housing programmes being implemented for people affected by Cyclone Ditwah.
