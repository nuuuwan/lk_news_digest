CA orders state authorities to prevent elephant deaths caused by train-elephant collisions

A team of counsel from Colombo Law Alliance successfully supported and argued two connected public-interest writ applications before the Court of Appeal of Sri Lanka, seeking urgent judicial intervention following the tragic collision between the Batticaloa–Colombo “Meenagaya” Express and a herd of wild elephants, which resulted in the deaths of seven elephants.

The two applications (CA (Writ) Application Nos. 318/2025 and 319/202) arose from the same incident and raised substantially overlapping issues concerning wildlife protection, railway safety and the duties of public authorities. Accordingly, they were supported and argued together before the Court. The Petitioners relied on several newspaper articles published in the Daily Mirror and Sunday Times Newspapers as material in support of the fact that despite the tragic incidents of wild elephant deaths on certain railroads in Sri Lanka time and time again, no meaningful steps have been taken by the authorities and officials concerned to prevent or mitigate such incidents, which was gradually taking its toll on the wild elephant population of Sri Lanka.

On 10 September 2026, the Court of Appeal (Dr D. F. H. Gunawardhana J.) delivered judgment in CA (Writ) Application No. 318/2025, granting writs of mandamus and issuing a series of important directions intended to prevent further infrastructure-related wildlife deaths.

A particularly significant feature of CA (Writ) Application No. 318/2025 was the inclusion of the Second Petitioner, “Elephant X” (Elephas maximus maximus), a survivor of the collision, represented by the Centre for Eco-Cultural Studies as its next friend.

The Court considered comparative jurisprudence from Ecuador, India, Argentina and Pakistan concerning the recognition of animals and nature as rights-bearing subjects. In a notable development in Sri Lankan environmental jurisprudence, the Court held that it was under a duty to protect the Second Petitioner.

The Court granted writs of mandamus and directed, among other matters, The Director-General of Wildlife Conservation to prepare or implement a national plan and an appropriate management plan for National Reserves addressing wildlife deaths caused by infrastructure and related human activity, and to report to Court on the steps taken; and the General Manager of Sri Lanka Railways to report to Court on the training provided to locomotive pilots and guards, including simulator-based training, the regulation of train speeds at identified elephant crossings, and the other measures adopted to minimise such collisions.

Recognising that continuing action is required from the Respondents, the Court directed that the matter be called again in three months to ascertain the progress made in implementing its orders.

Dr Asanga Gunawansa, P.C., with Swasha Fernando, Chayangi Kannangara and Sayuru Kariyawasam; and Mr Dilshan Jayasuriya with Dushinka Nelson and Anish de Silva. Both teams were instructed by Paul Ratnayeke Associates. Senior State Counsel Nayomi Kahawita appeared for the Respondents.
