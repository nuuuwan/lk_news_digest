President seeks mechanism to educate parents on drug threat

Colombo, Sep. 30 (Daily Mirror) - President Anura Kumara Dissanayake has instructed officials to establish a mechanism to actively involve parents in protecting children from the growing drug menace, stressing that strong parent-child relationships are more effective than legal measures alone.

He said parents should be directly educated on the seriousness of the drug threat and trained to identify and monitor changes in their children’s behaviour.

The President issued the instructions at a meeting of the “A Nation United” National Steering Committee established to eradicate the drug menace, held today at the Presidential Secretariat.

He also stressed the need for a trusted person to guide parents when their children are at risk of being exposed to drugs. Noting that parents often turn to teachers or religious leaders for assistance, he directed officials to develop a systematic programme to provide reliable guidance.

He said teachers involved in such a programme should receive special training.

The President also called for further strengthening measures to dismantle drug distribution networks.

The meeting was informed that the Government Analyst’s Department had increased its daily output of forensic reports following the expansion of facilities and improvements in efficiency. Around 8,000 drug-related forensic reports were completed during September alone, with Navy personnel assisting in data entry.

Officials said the faster processing of forensic reports had enabled a significant number of suspects remanded for drug-related offences to obtain bail, helping to ease prison overcrowding.

Dissanayake instructed officials to refer the new drug control Bill currently before Parliament to the relevant committee during the first week of October, obtain its approval and take steps to have it passed by Parliament by the third week of October.

Discussions were also held on establishing a centralised digital system linking the Police, Government Analyst’s Department, Department of Prisons and the judicial system.

The President instructed officials to discuss the practical issues involved with the Defence Ministry Secretary and other relevant officials and take necessary action.

He also inquired into measures to enable expert reports and Government Analyst’s reports to be submitted directly to courts by email under Act No. 7 of 2025.

Progress in filling vacancies at the Legal Draftsman’s Department was also reviewed, along with programmes conducted in international schools to combat drug use and their effectiveness.

The President further stressed the need for social media campaigns targeting young people to raise awareness of the dangers of drug use.

The meeting also discussed the drug-related challenges emerging in the Northern and Eastern regions following the war and measures being implemented to address them.

Dissanayake emphasised the importance of maintaining the 1818 hotline for information on drug trafficking, distribution and other related illegal activities. He called for greater publicity on action taken in response to information received through the hotline to strengthen public confidence in the service.

The progress of the drug rehabilitation programme, including measures to increase the capacity of rehabilitation centres, was also reviewed.
