Squabbling over judges amid starvation and indebtedness

According to Numbeo, a user-generated cost-of-living statistics website, Sri Lanka is the second most expensive country in South Asia to live in

With problems faced by the people increasing, the government’s popularity today is at a new low. The ruling party too,  has seen ‘the writing on the wall.’

However, let’s get back to our tale of woes. As of  January 1, 2026,  ‘the national minimum wage in Sri Lanka has been LKR 30,000/- per month and LKR 1,200/- per day.

Our people are hungry. Our people are angry. Our people have too little cash to clothe themselves with, and our wages are inadequate to provide a family of four (father, mother and two children) with two square meals a day. Worse. the price of fuel is on the rise, and there is little likelihood of wages rising to meet the cost of living.

On the plus side,  the government has been largely successful in bringing to book politicians and bureaucrats involved in corruption. Former Ministers, deputy ministers and a variety of local government politicians have been charged with fraud and corruption. They are in the process of being brought to justice. Among them were former President Mahinda Rajapaksa’s younger son, a former Minister of Health, Deputy Ministers and a variety of local and provincial council officials.

The Commission to Investigate Allegations of Bribery or Corruption (CIABOC) has not released a single cumulative census explicitly combining all past and present figures of former ministers, local politicians, and bureaucrats under one total count. However, enforcement data shows that 67 public officials have been arrested on bribery and corruption charges in 2026 alone, alongside active grand-scale probes into approx. 300 major complaints targeting former high-ranking officials and their political affiliates.

However, let’s get back to our tale of woes. As of  January 1, 2026,  ‘the national minimum wage in Sri Lanka has been LKR 30,000/- per month and LKR 1,200/- per day’. At the same time at August 2026, the cost for a family of four having 2 nutritious local meals (rice and curry with vegetables and a protein source like fish or chicken) a day ranged between Rs. 1,000/- to R. 3,000/-.

With a basic family comprising around four persons (father, mother and 2 children) an average family needs around Rs. 60,000/- per month, to merely provide two nutritious meals per day, per family alone! Even if both father and mother work, their total monthly income averages Rs. 60,000/-, a sum just sufficient for two daily meals.

In other words, parents are often short of sufficient funds for children’s education, health, clothes, medical or travel needs. This has led to malnutrition among children and anemia among women. UNICEF reports 17.3% of children in our country under five suffer from stunting, with rates reaching up to 32% in tea estate areas. While anemia affects approximately 8.5% to 18.5% of women of reproductive age in this land.

Additionally we have a massive external debt hanging over our heads. Former President Wickremesinghe, warns Lanka could fall into a situation of bankruptcy once again if we are unable to repay foreign creditors in a timely fashion.

Our main source of foreign exchange earnings is tied to the countries of West Asia which bring in our highest share of foreign exchange earnings via expatriate workers. Expatriate workers in West Asia provide roughly 50% of Sri Lanka’s total worker remittances. Total annual remittances hit a record US$ 8.076 billion in 2025, the combined estimated earnings sent back from the Middle East amounted to approximately US$ 4.04 billion for that year.

Today US President Trump and his ally Israel are engaged in an undeclared war in Iran. The continuing war has resulted in the closure of the Strait of Hormuz leading to a rapid rise in the cost of fuel and gas. The result is a continuing world-wide rise in the cost of fuel and gas as well as all other goods and services.

If the war in Iran expands to other countries in West Asia,  it could easily create a situation similar to that which we faced during the COVID epidemic. In turn it could lead to the West Asian job market closing down. In the event of the West Asian does market close down, it will lead to an exodus of expatriate workers from the region and we could once again face a financial crisis and leave the country facing bankruptcy.

We have still not learnt lessons from the time the COVID epidemic struck. The outbreak of the epidemic led to the repatriation of over a million of our expatriate workers and a loss of a major source of foreign earnings. Neither the government nor the political Opposition have made any move to seek new sources of income.

We still remain dependent on remittances of expatriate workers to meet costs of imports as well as to repay our external debt by 2028. Government’s failure to fulfill many of its post-election promises has taken toll of its popularity among the masses. Today we are beginning to hear even drivers of three-wheeled vehicles (among the government’s main propagandists earlier) now berating the President and his men in the language of Billingsgate.

While the majority of our countrymen and women are burdened with heavy financial problems, the government has recently approved plans to give Members of Parliament (MPs) a ‘small rise’ for fuel allowances. Cabinet Spokesman Minister Nalinda Jayatissa responding to a question, claiming  MPs had not received a salary increase. Rather the take home cash they received were linked to changes in a fuel allowance,  he said.

The Minister assured us citizens his government had not paid themselves any salary increase. It was,  the Minister said, merely an entitlement of a specified quantity of fuel, with the corresponding allowance calculated based on revised fuel prices.

Unfortunately,  this entitlement has not been made available to the rest of us ordinary citizens.

With problems faced by the people increasing, the government’s popularity today is at a new low. The ruling party too,  has seen ‘the writing on the wall’. The refusal to hold the constitutionally required Provincial Council election in a timely fashion is a clear indication government is afraid to expose the drop in its popularity.

What is most awkward however is the present government appears to be trying to deceive big brother across the Palk Straits that they intend to speedily hold Provincial Council Elections.  The reality is that the present government has taken no practical steps to implement that promise.

While the country is facing this plethora of mega problems, our politicians of the political opposition and the government, like the Roman Emperor ‘Nero’ of old, are fiddling while our country is sinking further and further into a mess.

Our country and its people are drowning in poverty. Its children are stunting and women are anemic; but parliament, is agog with excitement over a government plan to raise the retirement age of judges in the country. By increasing the retirement age of judges,  it is being argued by politicians in the parliamentary opposition, the independence of the judiciary would be  compromised.

Sadly no big campaigns have been launched to eradicate hunger malnutrition and anaemia in the country. Why is Lanka’s Opposition trying to create a mountains out of a mole hill? How does the issue of increasing the retirement age of judges in our country compromise the independence of the judiciary?

Another question arises:  Why has the Commonwealth Lawyers Association  stridently raised its voice in support of their colleagues in the Lankan Bar Association. Some countries of the EU too,  have taken up cudgels against the government’s proposal.  Even a UN Special Rapporteur has spoken out against government’s plan.

What a storm in a tea cup the government’s proposal to increase the retirement age of Judges has created. Some analysts have noted that the controversy surrounding judicial independence could even become a rule-of-law compliance test under the EU’s stricter GSP+ requirements.

Wow! All these international organisations taking up cudgels against this simple matter of the retirement age of judges in Lanka. In Gaza, the  state of Israel is openly committing genocide against the Palestinian people. Since October 2023,  Israel has kept up a relentless bombing campaign which has killed over 73,000 Palestinians. The UN reports over 21,000 Palestinian children have been killed in Israeli strikes. Another 44,000 children have been injured and maimed.

Israeli forces have killed more than 1,700 Palestinian healthcare workers in Gaza since October 2023, according to reports from humanitarian organisations and the Palestine Ministry of Health. According to United Nations agencies,  at least 34 to 38 hospitals in Gaza have been hit, damaged, or put out of service by Israeli military operations since October 2023, leaving the vast majority of the territory’s medical infrastructure non-functional.

Israel has also launched attacks on Lebanon, Syria and Iraq and occupied parts of those countries too. In a similar manner,  Israel together with the US has attacked Iran. It murdered over 140 children in their classrooms and killed the leader of that country.

Strangely,  neither these European countries nor the Commonwealth Lawyers Association have  come out so strongly against the atrocities which are being carried out in plain sight in Palestine. Is  this difference of attitude based on skin colour,  or due to the fact the State of Israel is backed by the Israeli lobby in the US which ‘helps’ many Non-Government Organisations (NGOs).

Or could it be these organisations, our parliamentary Opposition, particular political parties and NGOs in Lanka sincerely believe that extending the retirement age of Sri Lankan judges is more life-threatening than the genocide being committed in Gaza?

If not, why are we not all raising our voices to condemn Israeli genocide and support justice for Palestine?
