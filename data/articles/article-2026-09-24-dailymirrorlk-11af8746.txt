Another Damper for Aussie Dream of Lankans

The Australian dream, especially for thousands of Sri Lankan students and their families, received another major blow recently.

This was triggered by the hard-right “One Nation Party led by Pauline Hanson”, which is now leading the polls ahead of the 2028 election.

They promised to slash temporary migrants by 750,000 in three years. Specifically, they proposed to cut international students from 590,000 to 350,000, cut post-study graduate work visas from 270,000 to just 40,000, ban all family members accompanying students and temporary workers, and cap total net migration at 130,000 per year. They blame migrants for the housing and cost-of-living crisis.

Fearing this populist wave, the ruling Labour Government’s Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke announced immediate tough measures to curb migration. From now on, international students will not be allowed to bring partners or children. Visitor visa holders cannot apply for partner visas onshore, a ballot system will be introduced for backpackers extending working holiday visas, and “course hopping” to extend visas will be banned.

While Australia has every right to manage its borders, these back-to-back announcements create deep uncertainty. For middle-class Sri Lankan families who invest their life savings to send a child to Australia, often with the hope that a spouse can work legally to support them, this is a damper.  This is a very sad plight for parents who saw Australia as final destiny for their children.
