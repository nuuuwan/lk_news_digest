Whither UN  sanctity when Trump and Netanyahu take the podium?

The busiest week in the international political calendar turned out to be its most appalling.

As expected, Iran, Palestine, Yemen, Sudan, and Ukraine figured prominently during the ongoing debate, yet none expected the speakers to perform a miracle and spring a peace surprise that would end more than 50 wars happening around the world today. The sessions, as a matter of routine, simply offer world leaders an opportunity at the world’s largest diplomatic forum to express their views on war, peace, and global concerns such as poverty, climate change, artificial intelligence, and nuclear proliferation.

Decorum, however, demands that world leaders recognise the UN as a sanctum sanctorum, since it was established with the hallowed intention of preventing wars. Yet, world leaders have repeatedly desecrated its sanctity.

With fabrications and false narratives, Israel’s Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has used the platform to demonise Iran and Palestinians to justify illegal wars, genocide, and annexations. On Thursday, he was once again using the UN platform to play victim and defend his crimes. It was the height of deception.

His speech was replete with melodrama, theatre, twists, turns, spin doctoring, and the distortion of truth. Nary a word was said about the more than 75,000 Palestinians killed over the past three years. Yet what happened, or did not happen, during the October 7, 2023, Hamas raid was very much part of his act. He repeated the lies that he and his Zionist allies uttered to justify unleashing a genocide on the people of Gaza. According to him, Hamas terrorists burned babies, beheaded children, raped women, and massacred 1,200 Israelis, some of whom, he said, were kind enough to provide Palestinians with jobs and help them obtain medical treatment for cancer in Israeli hospitals. Independent investigations have found little evidence to support the claims that Hamas fighters burned or beheaded children, or raped women. They have also concluded that a significant number of Israelis were killed by Israeli Defence Forces fire.

On Iran, he was equally deceptive. Semantics can be satanic. Netanyahu was at his satanic best when he delivered his speech, which analysts say was also aimed at propping up his image during the ongoing election campaign, where the opposition has been flaying him over corruption allegations.

That is Netanyahu. Equally preposterous was Trump. He desecrated the pacifist forum on Tuesday and used it to threaten Iran, a nation of 92 million people, with annihilation. Unprecedentedly shocking, indeed.

Surely, a leader of an English-speaking country must know the meaning of the word “annihilation”. According to the Merriam-Webster dictionary, “annihilation” means “the state or fact of being completely destroyed or obliterated: the act of annihilating something or the state of being annihilated.”

Does Trump want to be counted among the wicked—in the company of Adolf Hitler, Genghis Khan and Pol Pot? The America that its Founding Fathers envisioned does not. But that America has long been lost. In the context of modern warfare, annihilation cannot be carried out except with a nuclear weapon. If Trump is implying that he would nuke Iran, then it is the moral duty of Congress to stop him.

To be fair to Trump—even though he is rarely fair to anyone, not even himself—he qualified his outrageous statement. He said he would annihilate Iran if no deal was reached to end the war, while adding in the same breath that an agreement could come soon.

“Will a deal be made with Iran that lets them rebuild and create a far greater country than it ever was before?... Or do I annihilate the Islamic Republic and do it quickly, never giving them a chance to kill and destroy people and countries again?” he said.

A nuclear strike is easier said than done. No world leader with a modicum of rationality would ever resort to it. But Trump being Trump, nothing can be ruled out. Moreover, he is said to suffer from narcissistic personality disorder. Its latest manifestation was his decision to ban CNN, MS Now, and Politico journalists from covering his events—a form of punishment for their criticism of his administration. On Thursday, a federal judge issued a temporary ruling blocking White House officials from barring journalists from Trump events. The embarrassing blow came when Trump was according the visiting Chinese President Xi Jinping a welcome that he had not given any world leader before.

As Trump delivered his UN speech, the Iranian delegate in the audience appeared unmoved and defiant. Within hours, Iran responded, declaring that Tehran was in “full readiness to inflict more severe, crushing, and unpredictable blows on the aggressors.”

Amid the erratic nature of Trump’s politics, US officials—assisted by the shuttle diplomacy of Qatari mediators—held talks with Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi at the UN to explore ways to end the war.

Trump claimed the talks US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held with Araghchi made progress, but reports indicated Iran insisted that the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding be revived and its provisions honoured, adding that peace could be just days away.

The talks offered welcome relief to a world struggling to cope with high energy costs and commodity prices as a result of the war. However, Trump maintains that whatever happens will occur after the November elections, warning Iran not to mess around with him as he has two and a half years left as president, regardless of the midterm outcomes.

Displaying diplomatic courage—symbolising a walk into enemy territory—Iran’s President Masoud Pezeshkian and his delegation arrived in the very country with which their nation has been at war for the past seven months. Trump, in his speech, blasted Iran as a terrorist state and the biggest threat to world peace. Pezeshkian—covered by the UN host country agreement—hit back. In his UNGA speech on Wednesday, he turned the tables on Trump, describing the US as the true bully and terrorist in West Asia. Vowing Iran would never bend the knee to the US, he said, “It must be understood by President Trump and those who seek to bully us that we are ready for dialogue and diplomacy and negotiations, but without the language of force.”

As Iran remains defiant, on the sidelines of the UNGA sessions, Trump held a meeting with Middle Eastern leaders without the elephant in the room—Israel. At the UNGA session, some of these leaders, particularly Jordan’s King Abdullah and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, made thundering speeches critical of Israel. But fiery speeches count for nothing if they do not walk the talk.

On that note, the sessions were not short of walkouts. The Israeli delegation staged a walkout on Tuesday when Erdogan delivered his speech, accusing Israel of being driven by an “insatiable genocidal mindset”. On Wednesday, when Iran’s President Pezeshkian took the podium, the US delegate walked out. A mass walkout—similar to the one last year— took place when Netanyahu took to the UNGA podium, to unleash lies, damn lies and fabricated statistics. Netanyahu called the delegates who staged the walk out moral cowards, but they symbolised the segment of international community that honour the sanctity of the UN and want to be on the right side of history. Sri Lanka was reportedly among them.

The new UN Secretary-General, assuming office in January, would do well to inscribe on the podium a simple reminder: ‘Speak the truth and promote peace and justice.’
