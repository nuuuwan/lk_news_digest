Mixed sentiment in secondary market amid subdued trading activity

The secondary market experienced mixed sentiment yesterday, with the overall yield curve remaining largely unchanged amid moderate activity and subdued trading volumes.

While the foreign selling pressure was evident in the longer tenures, particularly in the 2033 and 2034 maturities, this was partially offset by the buying interest from the domestic investors.

Among the actively traded maturities, the 15.10.2030 bond traded at 11.63 percent, while the 01.12.2031 and 15.12.2032 maturities recorded yields of 11.90 percent and 12.10 percent, respectively.

Further along the curve, the 01.11.2033 maturity traded at 12.38 percent. Within the 2034 segment, the 15.06.2034, 15.09.2034 and 15.10.2034 maturities traded within a yield range of 12.50 percent to 12.55 percent.

Yesterday, the PDMO concluded its weekly T-bill auction, raising Rs.86.5 billion, below the initial offer amount of Rs.100.0 billion. For the three-month T-bill, Rs.50.0 billion was initially offered, while Rs.68.1 billion was accepted.

The weighted average yield rate (WAYR) increased by 2bps to 9.34 percent. For the six-month T-bill, the PDMO accepted only Rs.9.3 billion against an offered amount of Rs.25.0 billion, with the WAYR remaining unchanged at 9.44 percent. For the 12-month T-bill, Rs.9.0 billion was accepted, which was below the offered amount, while the WAYR remained unchanged at 9.95 percent.

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee depreciated against the US dollar, standing at Rs.332.41/US dollar, compared to Rs.332.31/US dollar seen earlier. Market liquidity in the banking system contracted to Rs.389.55 billion, compared to Rs.391.50 billion recorded previously.
