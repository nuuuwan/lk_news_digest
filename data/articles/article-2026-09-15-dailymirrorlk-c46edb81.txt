No ‘dog tax’ under new Rabies Act: Vet

Colombo, Sep. 15 (Daily Mirror) - There is no new “dog tax” and no provision to jail people for feeding stray dogs under Sri Lanka’s newly gazetted Rabies Eradication and Dog Registration Act, Veterinary Surgeon and Animal Welfare Coalition (AWC) Executive Member Dr. Chamith Nanayakkara said.

Speaking at a press conference, Dr. Nanayakkara said several claims circulating on social media about the new legislation were misleading and had created unnecessary public alarm.

He said dog registration was not a new requirement introduced by the Act, adding that local authorities already imposed nominal registration fees. The fee is currently Rs. 100 in the Colombo Municipal Council area and Rs. 300 in Anuradhapura, he said.

Dr. Nanayakkara said arrangements were being made to conduct dog registration alongside free rabies vaccination programmes conducted in villages, eliminating the need for owners to make separate visits to local authority offices.

He also clarified that feeding a stray dog does not automatically make a person its legal owner.

“Feeding a stray animal on a single occasion does not make a person its legal owner. However, if someone regularly feeds and interacts with an animal, there is a social responsibility to ensure that it receives necessary care, including rabies vaccination,” he said.

He said genuine stray dogs with no caretaker would remain the responsibility of local authorities, which would be required to arrange vaccination and sterilisation.

The new legislation also introduces stricter provisions against the arbitrary killing of dogs, he said.

According to Dr. Nanayakkara, the powers previously available under the 1894 Rabies Ordinance to kill animals found on the streets have been abolished.

“A dog cannot simply be killed because it has bitten someone or is suspected of having rabies,” he said.

Such dogs should instead be captured from public areas and transferred to holding centres, where they must be observed by a government veterinary surgeon.

Euthanasia would only be permitted where a government veterinarian officially confirms that the dog has rabies and determines that it is necessary for public safety, he said.

The Act also requires sterilisation of stray dogs to be carried out by veterinarians. Dogs undergoing sterilisation must be cared for until they recover and then returned to the location from which they were captured, with an identifying mark to prevent them from being captured again.

Dr. Nanayakkara said people who abandon dogs in public places, including near temples, could face fines of Rs. 10,000 or more and imprisonment ranging from three to six months.

The legislation also introduces regulations for commercial dog breeders, requiring breeding establishments to obtain licences and provide adequate food, water, shelter, ventilation, lighting, exercise and veterinary care.

Sri Lanka has an estimated 2.1 million dogs, although only around three per cent are believed to be genuinely ownerless street dogs, Dr. Nanayakkara said.

He identified irresponsible waste disposal and abandonment of animals as major contributors to the country’s stray dog problem.

He said sterilisation and vaccination were essential to addressing the problem and achieving the goal of eliminating rabies by 2030.

Dr. Nanayakkara claimed that funds allocated for sterilisation had not been fully utilised, with around Rs. 100 million of the Rs. 200 million allocated in 2024 being spent, while only about Rs. 48 million had been utilised from the 2025 allocation.

He said arrangements were also being finalised for the Government and private sector to provide free rabies vaccination and sterilisation services for both stray and owned dogs.

Another major development would be the introduction of a new rabies testing method in January, which would eliminate the need to decapitate suspected animals for testing, he said.

Under the new system, samples could be obtained from the neck area, while rabies testing facilities would be expanded to 25 centres across the country.

Dr. Nanayakkara urged the public to read the actual legislation rather than relying on sensational claims circulating on social media.

“There is no provision for a ‘dog tax’, nor does feeding an animal automatically make a person its legal owner,” he said.
