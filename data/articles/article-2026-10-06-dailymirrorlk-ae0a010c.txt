Abolish private fuel companies if they fail public, union tells Govt

Colombo, Oct. 6 (Daily Mirror) - The Sri Lanka Podujana Progressive Employees' Union-Petroleum (SLPPEU-P) has called on the Government to abolish private petroleum companies operating in Sri Lanka if they fail to ensure a fair fuel supply to the public.

SLPPEU-P Chief Secretary Bandula Saman Kumara alleged that private petroleum companies deliberately slow down fuel sales when prices are reduced, while ensuring uninterrupted sales when prices are increased.

Speaking to the media, Kumara said that under the existing fuel pricing mechanism, revised fuel prices come into effect on the first day of each month, with the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) and private petroleum companies required to sell their fuel stocks at the revised prices.

He claimed that the fuel stocks required by private companies had already been stored at facilities in Kolonnawa and Sapugaskanda and, therefore, they were required to release those stocks at the revised prices.

“Whenever fuel prices are increased, these companies have no difficulty selling their stocks. But when the Government announces a lower or break-even price, their sales slow down,” he alleged.

The union has therefore urged the Government to reconsider the presence of private petroleum companies in the local market and allow the CPC to provide fuel to consumers while offering concessions to the public when necessary.

Kumara claimed that the privatisation of the petroleum sector was initially introduced with the objective of reducing the country's foreign exchange burden.

“Private petroleum companies were allowed to sell fuel in the local market and make profits. However, when they slow down their sales, the CPC has to spend more foreign exchange to import additional fuel to fill the gap. These companies do not spend their own money to bring the required fuel into the country,” he alleged.

He further alleged that multinational petroleum companies were primarily focused on maximising profits and repatriating those profits to their respective countries.

“This has become an impractical situation for Sri Lanka. Ultimately, it is the people who have to suffer,” Kumara said.

Bandula claimed that previous governments had managed to provide greater fuel concessions to the public, whereas the current Government had failed to provide similar concessions.

He alleged that even the Government was unable to cover the cost of unloading fuel.

Kumara also claimed that the CPC had never incurred losses and had earned a profit of Rs. 28 billion over the past eight years. He said the CPC had recorded a profit of more than Rs. 32 billion last year alone.

“If the CPC has earned such a profit, people can figure out how much profit private petroleum companies could be making,” he said.

He claimed that private petroleum companies had entered the local market with the aim of creating competition and, therefore, should be able to sell fuel at competitive prices.

The union called on the Government to review the existing fuel market structure and take steps to ensure that fuel supplies are maintained regardless of fluctuations in retail prices.
