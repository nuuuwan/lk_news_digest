Sri Lanka holds 21st in Asia Power Index as tourism lifts cultural clout

Sri Lanka’s standing in Asia strengthened this year to its best level since the 2022 economic crisis, as the improved tourism indicators lifted its cultural influence, although the island stayed 21st among 27 countries and territories and continued to wield less influence than its resources would suggest, the Lowy Institute’s Asia Power Index 2026 showed.

The Sydney-based think tank placed Sri Lanka’s comprehensive power score at 8.2 out of 100, up 0.4 points or 5.1 percent when compared to 7.8 points in the 2025 edition.  This year’s gain alone exceeded the 0.3 points the island added across the two previous editions combined, when its score crept up from 7.5 to 7.7 and then to 7.8. However, the improvement was not enough to lift Sri Lanka up the table, leaving it 21st for a fourth consecutive edition. The island slipped from 20th to 21st in the 2023 edition after its score fell 1.1 points to 7.5, its lowest in any edition, a decline the institute linked to the economic and political crises of 2022.

The latest score is still 0.4 points below the 8.6 points Sri Lanka recorded in the 2021 edition, before that fall.

The institute credited the uplift chiefly to cultural influence, noting that the island’s tourism indicators had improved. Sri Lanka’s cultural influence score climbed 1.0 point or 14.9 percent to 7.7, lifting it four places to 16th. That made it the only one of the index’s eight measures in which the island changed rank and it is now Sri Lanka’s strongest measure.

The index uses cultural influence to capture how far a country can sway international opinion through its cultural appeal and people-to-people contact.

Diplomatic influence recorded the island’s largest score increase, rising 1.1 points to 22.3, yet Sri Lanka remained 22nd on the measure, its lowest ranking across all eight.

Its diplomatic influence score sat 15.5 points below the regional median, the widest of its shortfalls and the index found the country scored below the median on every measure in 2026.

Defence networks improved 0.9 points to 5.5 and resilience gained 0.7 points to 25.3, with the island holding 20th and 18th place on the two measures, respectively.

Its scores for economic capability, military capability, future resources and economic relationships were unchanged at 1.9, 6.3, 2.6 and 2.5, leaving it 21st, 19th, 19th and 21st on those measures. The index’s power gap measure, which compares the influence a country exerts with what its resources would predict, put Sri Lanka at minus 2.6, an improvement on minus 2.9 a year earlier.  “Its influence still falls short of its resources, though the shortfall narrowed slightly,” the think tank observed. Meanwhile, Sri Lanka closed some of the distance to Bangladesh, which held the 20th place, with 8.9 points, down from 9.0 points. The gap between the two South Asian neighbours narrowed to 0.7 points, from 1.2 points a year earlier.

Sri Lanka also widened its margin over the country ranked directly below it. Myanmar, which climbed to 22nd on 7.2 points, trails the island by 1.0 point, when compared to the 0.6-point lead Sri Lanka held over 22nd-placed Laos in 2025.

Elsewhere in South Asia, Pakistan held the 16th place after its score rose 0.6 points to 15.0, just clearing the 15-point threshold the 2026 edition sets for middle powers. The institute put the gain down to shuttle diplomacy that gave Islamabad a brief role in the US-Iran ceasefire talks, though it cautioned the boost was unlikely to last.  India, ranked third, consolidated its major power status with 40.5 points, up 0.5 points.

India accounted for 18.5 percent of Sri Lanka’s trade in 2025, followed by China at 13.8 percent, the European Union at 12.5 percent, United States at 8.8 percent and United Arab Emirates at 6.2 percent, as per the index’s bilateral trade data. Together, the two Asian giants made up nearly a third of the island’s trade.

The dependence runs largely one way, as Sri Lanka made up just 0.5 percent of India’s trade, although that was still the highest reliance on Sri Lanka among the region’s countries. At the top of the table, the United States remained Asia’s most powerful country, with 78.9 points, though China, on 73.6 points, narrowed the gap between the two to 5.3 points, from 6.8 points a year earlier.  Launched in 2018, the index ranks 27 countries and territories, using 131 indicators across eight measures.
