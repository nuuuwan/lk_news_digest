Prosecuting a social activist for stating what WHO said?

The reported prosecution of social-service activist Mr. Chirantha Amarasinghe over his assertion that COVID-19 does not spread through water raises serious questions about the proportionality of such legal action.

His position was not an isolated or inexplicable claim. WHO itself stated that available evidence indicated that the Corona virus did not spread through water. . WHO also specifically stated that water or swimming does not transmit COVID-19, while identifying close contact and respiratory transmission as the principal routes.

If Mr. Amarasinghe’s statement reflected the scientific evidence and WHO guidance available at the relevant time, why should expressing that view alone warrant criminal proceedings?

Public debate, particularly during a health emergency, must allow room for citizens to express views based on available evidence. Legitimate criticism or disagreement should not automatically become a criminal matter.

I therefore request  the Attorney General’s Department to review the prosecution in the light of the relevant scientific evidence and legal circumstances and, where appropriate, reconsider continuing the proceedings. I also appeal to President Anura Kumara Dissanayake to ensure that the matter receives due consideration within the framework of the law and justice.
