Venue shortage cost Sri Lanka an Indian film festival:  Deputy Tourism Minister

MICE share of arrivals up from 5% in 2024 to around 10-11%, figures estimated

Sri Lanka recently lost a major Indian film festival after the organisers’ preferred venue, the Nelum Pokuna, proved unavailable, underlining the venue and accommodation gaps that continue to hold back the country’s push for international business events, Tourism Deputy Minister Prof. Ruwan Ranasinghe said on Tuesday.

“Very recently one top group from India for a film festival met me and they came for the Nelum Pokuna but it was not available and they cancelled,” Prof. Ranasinghe revealed at the media briefing for Sri Lanka MICE Expo 2026.

He cited connectivity, room inventory and the availability of suitable venues and logistics as key constraints facing the meetings, incentives, conferences and exhibitions (MICE) segment.

However, the government has opened Temple Trees for events, while the developments in Port City Colombo are expected to add capacity and a process is underway to bring the facilities in Mattala into MICE use.

Sri Lanka Convention Bureau (SLCB) Chairman Dheera Hettiarachchi put the MICE share of total tourist arrivals at about 5 percent in 2024, when the current leadership took charge, a figure that has since doubled to 10 percent.

“Our expectation is to grow it to 11 percent by the end of this year,” he said.

Prof. Ranasinghe placed the current share at around 11 percent but acknowledged the limits of the data.

“Of course, these statistics are some approximations, where we don’t have the exact statistics pertaining to MICE business. But there is an increase, a clear increase,” he explained.

Globally, MICE is a US $ 1 trillion business, accounting for around 20 percent of the tourism market, rising to 23 percent in Asia Pacific, according to Prof. Ranasinghe. While those figures measure MICE’s share of the tourism business rather than of arrivals, making a direct comparison is difficult; he conceded that “there is a long way ahead that we need to go”.

“If we take the top 10 MICE destination cities globally, except Sydney and Melbourne, all the other eight are in the Asian region. So, Sri Lanka faces strong competition in this business as well,” Prof. Ranasinghe warned.

He also disclosed that the SLCB, a team of just nine members, operates on an annual budget of Rs.360 million, of which more than Rs.110 million is being invested in this year’s expo. That is nearly a third of its yearly allocation or roughly Rs.1 million for each hosted buyer.

Meanwhile, Hettiarachchi observed that the sixth edition had drawn more than 600 applications from MICE professionals and media representatives worldwide.

“Since its beginnings in 2021, the expo has evolved from a virtual initiative into an international platform,” he added.

The figures presented by SLCB Marketing Manager Malkanthi Welikala showed that the bureau received 587 hosted buyer applications from 49 countries. With more than 700 buyers already hosted across previous editions, only new applicants were considered and after 81 repeat applications were excluded, 104 buyers from 24 countries were selected from the 506 evaluated, roughly one in five.

“India is the main market for MICE tourism and because of that reason, we have selected 40 percent from India,” Welikala pointed out.

A further 17 journalists from 11 countries were chosen from 79 media applications, bringing the international contingent to participants from 27 countries.

The delegates will arrive on October 4 or early on October 5, with the B2B meetings scheduled for October 6 and familiarisation tours of the Cultural Triangle and other areas on October 7 and 8, before the programme closes on October 9.

Hettiarachchi expects the expo to facilitate more than 10,000 B2B meetings between the hosted buyers and over 100 local MICE operators. With the meetings confined to a single day, that target would require each buyer to hold close to 100 meetings.

Welikala highlighted Sri Lanka’s compactness, which allows the delegates to attend a conference and experience a different climate on the same day, along with its year-round appeal and online visa facilities for 40 countries.

“This is value for money, not a budget. We provide a good service with competitive rates,” she stressed.

Prof. Ranasinghe emphasised that a single large event benefits a wide chain of stakeholders, from airlines, tour operators, venues, photographers to logistics suppliers.

“This is not just a single business for one or two groups,” he remarked. (NF)
