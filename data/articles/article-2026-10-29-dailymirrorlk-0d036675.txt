Court says pension authorities cannot decide marriage validity

Colombo, Oct. 29 (Daily Mirror) - The Court of Appeal has ruled that pension authorities cannot assume the jurisdiction of a competent court to declare a registered marriage invalid, while ordering the Department of Pensions to recognise a retired municipal employee’s wife as eligible for benefits under the Widows’ and Orphans’ Pension Fund Ordinance.

The Court of Appeal two-judge-bench comprising Justices Adithya Patabendige and Sasi Mahendran, held that although the employee’s first marriage remained legally subsisting when he contracted his second marriage, the subsequent dissolution of the first marriage had to be considered in determining pension entitlement under the relevant legislation.

Justice Adithya Patabendige, delivering the judgment, emphasised that the authority to determine pension entitlement does not confer jurisdiction to determine the validity of a marriage under general matrimonial law.

The court issued a writ of certiorari quashing the decision of the Director of Pensions to deny recognition to the petitioner’s wife on the basis that their marriage was invalid. It also issued a writ of mandamus directing the relevant pension authorities to recognise her entitlement to benefits under the Ordinance and take the consequential steps required by law.

The petitioner, a retired Machine Minder of the Colombo Municipal Council, had challenged the pension authorities’ decision concerning his wife, in the second marriage.

The petitioner had previously married and subsequently instituted divorce proceedings in the District Court of Gampaha on the ground of malicious desertion.

On July 5, 2000, the District Court entered judgment in his favour and directed that a decree nisi be made absolute upon payment of Rs. 50,000 as permanent alimony. The petitioner completed the payment on April 2, 2001.

However, before the decree nisi was made absolute, he contracted his second marriage on May 12, 2001. The decree nisi was ultimately made absolute on August 24, 2001.

Following his retirement on December 25, 2021, a dispute arose over his wife’s entitlement to pension benefits. The pension authorities refused to recognise her on the ground that the first marriage remained legally subsisting when the second marriage was contracted.

The petitioner was represented by Attorney-at-Law Nilanga Perera, instructed by Neminda G. Kariyawasam. Deputy Solicitor General Zuri Zain appeared for the respondents.
