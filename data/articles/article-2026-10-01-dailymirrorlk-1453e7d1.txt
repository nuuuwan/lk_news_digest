Fuel companies to get Rs. 70 per litre diesel subsidy

Colombo, Oct. 1 (Daily Mirror) - The government will provide Rs. 70 per litre of diesel as part of a Rs. 41 billion fuel subsidy approved by the Cabinet to cushion consumers and fuel distributors from the impact of surging global oil prices, Energy Minister Anura Karunathilaka said.

The subsidy is intended to prevent the full impact of rising international fuel prices from being passed on to consumers.

Karunathilaka said fuel distribution companies have reported varying levels of losses under the current pricing structure amid the sharp increase in global fuel prices.

According to the Minister, companies have cited losses ranging from Rs. 120 to Rs. 247 per litre of diesel, with figures of Rs. 135, Rs. 247, Rs. 192 and Rs. 120 per litre reported by different companies.

The government will release the Rs. 41 billion subsidy over three months, allocating Rs. 15 billion in October, Rs. 13.5 billion in November and Rs. 12.5 billion in December.

The subsidy will be calculated based on the volume of diesel sold by each fuel distribution company during the respective month.

Karunathilaka acknowledged that the Rs. 70-per-litre subsidy will not fully cover the losses reported by fuel companies, meaning distributors will continue to absorb part of the increased costs.

He said the measure was aimed at easing pressure on the fuel market and preventing a sharp price shock for consumers.

The Minister said some fuel distribution companies had begun restricting diesel supplies, placing additional pressure on the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC).

Despite the increased burden on the CPC, Karunathilaka expressed confidence that the corporation would not record an overall financial loss by the end of the year.

He said revenue from the CPC’s wider operations, including petrol, diesel and aviation fuel sales, as well as refinery operations, would help offset the additional costs.

Karunathilaka said the government will review current fuel prices at the end of the month.

A decision on revised prices for petrol and diesel was to be announced yesterday, taking into account the impact of the newly introduced subsidy.
