Stamp marks London Vihara centenary

A century of Buddhist heritage in Britain: The Sri Lanka Philatelic Bureau of the Department of Posts issued a Rs. 70 postage stamp yesterday (17), to commemorate the 100th anniversary of the London Buddhist Vihara, founded in 1926 by Sri Lankan Buddhist revivalist Anagarika Dharmapala. Recognised as the first Theravada Buddhist monastery established outside Asia, the Vihara has played a pioneering role in spreading Buddhist teachings, meditation and practice in Britain. The institution was initially established at Foster House in Ealing with the support of American benefactor Mrs. Mary Foster Robinson before moving to larger premises in Gloucester Road in 1928. Its activities were interrupted during World War II, but the Vihara was re-established in 1954 at Ovington Gardens, Knightsbridge, continuing its role as a centre for Buddhist study and spiritual development.
