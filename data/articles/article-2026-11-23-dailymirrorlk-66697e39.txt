CHC plots 50-hotel EKHO push from wayside rest houses

BRAND RECAST FOR YOUNGER TRAVELLERS, WITH ELLA, BELIHULOYA AND KITHULGALA FORMING NEW ADVENTURE CLUSTER

MGT. CONTRACTS TO CARRY EXPANSION BEYOND CHC’S OWN REST HOUSE PORTFOLIO

Ceylon Hotels Corporation PLC (CHC) is setting its EKHO Hotels and Resorts brand on course for 50 hotels within five years, reinventing its historic wayside rest houses for a new generation of travellers and taking on properties under management contracts to reach the target, according to a senior official.

“The whole idea for us is to convert more of the rest houses into the EKHO brand, but the long term is to get 50 EKHO hotels,” CHC Marketing and Communications Manager Sherin Hamid said.

However, the ambition marks a sharp acceleration for a brand launched in 2016 through the conversion of properties such as the Polonnaruwa Rest House, now EKHO Lake House. EKHO is taking its count to seven as Belihuloya and Kithulgala join its hotels in Bentota, Tissamaharama, Sigiriya, Polonnaruwa and Ella. Reaching 50 would mean adding close to nine hotels a year, roughly 12 times the pace at which it assembled its first seven over the past decade.

With CHC running just three other rest houses with accommodation, in Dambulla, Pussellawa and Ambepussa, even a full conversion would lift the brand to only 10 hotels. The balance is set to come from properties EKHO will operate under management contracts, an asset-light route that lets the brand scale without carrying the full cost of ownership.

The target caps a renewed push behind EKHO over the past four years, during which CHC has brought in seasoned hospitality professionals to drive the brand. Among them are Hamid, who joined CHC in 2024 after two years as Marketing Manager at boutique hotel group Uga Escapes, and naturalist and self-taught wildlife photographer Gayathri Tissera, formerly of Aitken Spence. CHC’s group revenue rose 42 percent to Rs.2.37 billion in the financial year ended March 2026 from Rs.1.67 billion a year earlier, with CHC Chairman Sanjeev Gardiner crediting continued investment in the EKHO brand as a key driver.

The conversions are aimed squarely at a younger audience. “The reason for us to convert these rest houses into the EKHO brand is basically to gain more traction with the younger market and the younger audience,” Hamid explained.

Yet CHC is careful not to erase the memories that made these properties landmarks on the island’s highways, where families once pulled in for breakfast and a rice and curry lunch.

“While keeping that nostalgia going, we wanted to just give it a refreshed look,” Hamid added.

EKHO’s chief asset, she pointed out, is its footprint. “The main selling point we have is the location. We have amazing locations all across the country,” Hamid remarked.

That footprint underpins a destination-led approach, in which EKHO markets the surrounding region as much as the hotel itself. ‘ “Through our channels, we want to promote the destination, and along with that, we promote our property,” she noted.

Meanwhile, the clearest expression of the repositioning is a new EKHO Adventure cluster. It draws together the hiking trails of Ella, the waterfalls and river valleys of Belihuloya and the white-water rapids of Kithulgala, and CHC is pitching it to travel agents specialising in adventure travel.

“We’re looking at EKHO as a different repositioning, if you will,” Hamid stated.

For now, Belihuloya has yet to draw the market the brand is courting.

“Right now we don’t really get the younger crowd coming over here,” Hamid admitted, adding that CHC is working closely with the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA), which has its own plan to uplift Belihuloya as a destination.
