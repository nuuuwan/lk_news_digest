UK regularly raises accountability, disappearances with Sri Lanka- Foreign Office

Colombo, Sep. 14 (Daily Mirror) - Britain has said it regularly raises issues of accountability, reconciliation and enforced disappearances with the Sri Lankan Government, while urging a credible and transparent investigation into the Chemmani mass graves that meets international standards.

Parliamentary Under-Secretary at the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office Chris Elmore said last week that the UK was closely monitoring developments relating to the Chemmani mass graves and continued to engage with the Sri Lankan authorities on the need for a credible and transparent investigation.

He said the investigation should include robust forensic safeguards and meaningful engagement with the families affected by the disappearances.

“We have encouraged consideration of international assistance as excavations continue,” Elmore said in a written parliamentary answer.

He said the UK had regularly raised accountability, reconciliation and enforced disappearances with the Sri Lankan Government.

The former UK Foreign Secretary discussed these issues with the Sri Lankan Prime Minister during her visit to the UK in May, while the former Deputy Prime Minister raised them during his visit to Sri Lanka in February 2026, he said.

More recently, the British High Commissioner in Colombo discussed enforced disappearances with Sri Lanka’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs and met families of missing persons in Jaffna.

Elmore said the UK remained committed to supporting truth, justice and accountability in Sri Lanka through its leadership of the Core Group on Sri Lanka at the UN Human Rights Council and its support for ongoing UN accountability mechanisms
