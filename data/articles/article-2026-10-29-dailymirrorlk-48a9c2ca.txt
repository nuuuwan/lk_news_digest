112 Interpol Red-Notice suspects identified in 13 countries

Colombo, Oct. 29 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lankan authorities have identified 112 individuals wanted under Interpol Red Notices who are believed to be hiding in 13 countries, with urgent measures underway to bring them back to Sri Lanka, Police Spokesman ASP F.U. Wootler said.

Addressing a media briefing to outline progress under the “Ratama Ekata” national operation, he said authorities expect to repatriate the suspects by 2027 and take legal action against them upon their return.

ASP Wootler said two specialised police divisions are conducting round-the-clock investigations into assets allegedly acquired through illegal drug trafficking and other criminal activities. The assets identified for confiscation include gold jewellery, land and property, vehicles and cash.

According to the figures presented, the value of assets targeted for state acquisition amounted to Rs. 1,490 million in 2024, Rs. 1,549 million in 2025 and Rs. 1,656 million so far in 2026, as of October 28.

Meanwhile, police reported significant drug seizures over the past two years up to October 2026, including 4,483 kilogrammes of heroin valued at Rs. 107.942 billion, 7,129 kilogrammes of ICE valued at Rs. 121.64 billion, 33,277 kilogrammes of cannabis valued at Rs. 5.293 billion and 333 kilogrammes of cocaine valued at Rs. 14.306 billion.

The spokesperson also warned that, under existing law, possession, trafficking, trading or production of more than five grammes of heroin, ICE or cocaine can carry the death penalty or life imprisonment following proceedings before the High Court.

Reviewing the first year of the “Ratama Ekata” operation, launched in October 2025, police said they had seized 2,835 kilogrammes and 682 grammes of heroin, 3,550 kilogrammes and 750 grammes of ICE, 297 kilogrammes and 135 grammes of cocaine, and 11,167 kilogrammes and 569 grammes of cannabis during the past 12 months.

Police also arrested 5,743 individuals allegedly linked to various crimes during the period, while investigations into suspected illicit assets are ongoing against 450 of them.
