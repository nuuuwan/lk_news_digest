Iranian Vessels: U.S. embassy says no comment on private diplomatic communications

Colombo, Sep. 3 (Daily Mirror) - In response to a question whether the U.S. had any communication with the Sri Lankan authorities over the Iranian vessels anchored off the coast of Sri Lanka, an embassy spokesperson said that the substance of private diplomatic conversations would not be commented on

“We do not comment on the substance of private diplomatic conversations. Information regarding U.S. sanctions, including designated vessels, is publicly available through the U.S. Department of the Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control,” the spokesperson said.

There were reports that nearly 20 Iranian fuel tankers, including around 13 Iranian-flagged vessels, were gathered off Sri Lanka’s coast. Several other vessels have reportedly been identified as part of a so-called “shadow fleet” allegedly used to circumvent international sanctions.

According to Article 58 of the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), even though a coastal State has special rights in its Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ), other States—including landlocked States—still have certain freedoms there. Article 87 of the UNCLOS says the high seas are open to all States, whether coastal or land-locked.

However, any coastal state has sovereign jurisdiction over its territorial waters extending up to 12 nautical miles from its baseline. Sri Lanka can act in case any of these vessels enter its territorial waters.
