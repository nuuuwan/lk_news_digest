Sri Lanka calls for regional action against drug trafficking, maritime threats

Colombo, Nov. 26 (Daily Mirror) - Defence Secretary Air Vice Marshal Sampath Thuyacontha (Retd) has called for stronger regional cooperation to address shared security challenges, including drug trafficking, illegal fishing, threats to critical maritime infrastructure and natural disasters.

He made these remarks while addressing the 8th National Security Advisors’ (NSA) Meeting of the Colombo Security Conclave (CSC), held in Malé, Maldives, yesterday (25).

The Defence Secretary highlighted the importance of strengthening cooperation among regional countries in an increasingly interconnected and complex security environment, noting that the Colombo Security Conclave had evolved into an important platform for addressing common security concerns through dialogue, cooperation and collective action.

Emphasising the Conclave’s five pillars of cooperation, he called for enhanced collaboration in maritime safety and security, countering terrorism and radicalisation, combating trafficking and transnational organised crime, cybersecurity and the protection of critical infrastructure and technology, and humanitarian assistance and disaster relief.

He also outlined Sri Lanka’s “Ratama Ekata” National Mission, aimed at combating drug trafficking through enhanced law enforcement, disruption of supply networks, preventive measures, public awareness and rehabilitation initiatives.

Addressing maritime security concerns, Thuyacontha stressed the need for greater regional cooperation to combat illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing through information sharing, coordinated surveillance and effective enforcement.

He also highlighted the importance of protecting submarine communication cables, describing them as critical infrastructure requiring enhanced maritime domain awareness and coordinated regional responses.

Referring to the recent experience of Cyclone Ditwah, the Defence Secretary underscored the need to strengthen regional disaster preparedness, early-warning systems, emergency response coordination and humanitarian assistance. He also called for greater sharing of knowledge and best practices on climate change adaptation and resilience.

He reaffirmed Sri Lanka’s commitment to the objectives of the Colombo Security Conclave and expressed the country's readiness to work closely with participating nations to strengthen practical cooperation and address shared regional security priorities.

The meeting brought together representatives of CSC member countries to discuss regional security priorities and emerging challenges in the Indian Ocean Region.

The Sri Lankan delegation also included the Chief of National Intelligence, Additional Secretary for South Asia and SAARC of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs Varuni Muthukumarana, and Senior Assistant Secretary of the Ministry of Defence Chinthaka Abeykoon.
