Is retirement age extension issue aimed at a particular judge?

Opposition parties are not really concerned about the independence of the judiciary, given their track record

The government, after its announcement on the Cabinet decision, cannot deny the allegation that it did not consult the stakeholders on the matter

Experience matters when it comes to efficiency in any sector, but it is not something that the government is going to infuse into the judiciary by extending judges’ retirement age

Disregarding weeks-long agitations by the Opposition parties and a section of the legal fraternity against its move to extend the retirement age of the judges, the National People’s Power (NPP) government at last has clarified its stance on the matter.

The Cabinet had on Monday approved a proposal to go ahead with the move, allaying speculations on the existence of such a move and whether it concerns judges of all courts or only the superior courts.

Health and Media Minister Dr. Nalinda Jayatissa stated that the purpose of the proposal was to accelerate the disposal of the large backlog of court cases using the experience of senior judges.

Certain individuals of the Opposition and the media outlets aligned to them started the debate on this matter weeks ago, accusing that the proposed Constitutional amendment is aimed at giving service extension to a particular individual, namely  Chief Justice Preethi Padman Surasena. Their whole protest campaign seemed to have been based on this premise which they never substantiated.

They initially questioned as to why the government was interested in extending only the retirement age of the judges of the Supreme Court and the Court of Appeal -- instead of judges of all courts. The leaders of the government were tightlipped until the opposing groups built a considerable public opinion in favour of their premise. The government’s deafening silence on the matter created suspicion even in the minds of its loyalists.

Interestingly, however, when Minister Jayatissa told the journalists  that the Cabinet has decided to increase the retirement age of all judges, the opposing groups were not in a position even to reconsider their position, as they had already reached a point of no return from their assertion.

The government, after its announcement on the Cabinet decision, cannot deny the allegation that it did not consult the stakeholders on the matter. Before the announcement it was only an idea just mooted, when there were opportunities to consider other views.

Yet, neither the government’s main contention that extension of the judges’ retirement age would help accelerate the disposal of the aforementioned backlog,  nor the opposing groups’ argument that  judicial independence would be threatened by such an extension, is very convincing.

Experience matters when it comes to efficiency in any sector, but it is not something that the government is going to infuse into the judiciary by extending judges’ retirement age. Experience already exists. The backlog of cases was gathered while that experience was in practice.

Experience of the judges, especially that of the superior court judges would help accelerate disposal of cases when it is coupled with new strategies implemented with new resources pumped into the system. Even then, newcomers who are not so experienced would take over a part of the job again, once the extension period of the current judges is lapsed, i.e. new judges would be promoted to the superior courts from the lower courts.

On the other hand, independence of the judiciary would not be harmed by such an extension of the retirement age, unless it is aimed at a particular person, as the opposing groups allege. The government may or may not want to retain a particular judge, may be the Chief Justice, for two more years by this Constitutional amendment. However, the onus is in the opposing groups including the Opposition parties to prove their allegation.

If they fail to prove their allegation, it would backfire – meaning it is they who want to prevent a particular judge from being given an extension. This is an allegation that would prevent such extensions in the future as well, as it could be levelled at any time against any government that attempts to do the same. However, it is too late for the government to allay that fear as the Opposition has already sold that idea to a considerable segment of the people.

Opposition parties demand a referendum to approve the government’s move. However, the Article 83 of the Constitution clearly specifies the circumstances when people’s approval has to be sought through a referendum. It provides for the President to hold referendums to amend the Articles of it concerning the name of the country, nature of the state, sovereignty of the people, national flag, National Anthem, National Day, place of Buddhism, religious freedom, freedom from torture, term of the President and the term of the Parliament.

Besides, it is obvious that  Article 83 if itself  requires a referendum to amend,  those articles also need a referendum to be amended.

Also, Bills or provisions of Bills that are certified by the Cabinet or determined by the Supreme Court as requiring the approval of the people, and those matters which, in the opinion of the President, are of national importance have to be put to the people through a referendum. The president too,  can get a Bill that is already rejected by Parliament passed through a referendum.

It is not clear under which of these categories, according to the Opposition, the judges’ retirement age issue falls. However, it is not against the law if the government refers the matter to the people through a referendum, considering it as a “matter which, in the opinion of the President, is of national importance.”

Opposition parties are not really concerned about the independence of the judiciary, given their track record. The United National Party (UNP) government led by leaders like J.R. Jayewardene, Ranasinghe Premadasa and Ranil Wickremesinghe once in the 1980s justified  attacks by thugs on houses of Apex Court Judges who gave a judgement against their government. President Mahinda Rajapaksa ignored a Supreme Court ruling on oil prices and appointed superior court judges without the approval of the Constitutional Council in 2006. He used his party’s majority in Parliament to impeach Chief Justice Shirani Bandaranayake in 2013 for not giving judgements favourable to him.

Gotabaya Rajapaksa facilitated his brother Basil to contest elections while being a US citizen through the 20th Amendment. President Ranil Wickremesinghe ignored the Supreme Court order to hold local government elections in 2023. His government attempted to summon the judges who gave this ruling before the Privilege Committee of the Parliament. He called a ruling by the Supreme Court “judicial cannibalism.”

Minister Jayatissa says that a Parliamentary subcommittee consisting of members of all parties in the House, including the current opposition parties,  had recommended the extension of judges’ retirement age in 2016.

Hence, the Opposition parties are not genuinely committed to the independence of the judiciary. They are only attempting to build a public opinion against the government using the extension of the judges’ retirement age.

However,  it seems to be a futile exercise to hang onto  a subject which the ordinary people are not interested in.
