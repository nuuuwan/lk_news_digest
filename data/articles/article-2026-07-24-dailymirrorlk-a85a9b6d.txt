US tariff parity averts competitive hit to apparel sector: JAAF

Sri Lanka securing a 10 percent US tariff rate has averted a potential competitive disadvantage for the country’s apparel industry, the Joint Apparel Association Forum (JAAF) said, noting that even a 2.5 percentage point tariff gap could determine where the global buyers place orders.

Sri Lanka initially expected to face a 12.5 percent tariff but has now been placed at 10 percent, on par with key apparel competitors Bangladesh, India, Pakistan and Cambodia. “Even a 2.5 percentage point difference in tariff treatment can be the difference between winning and losing an order to a rival sourcing destination,” the JAAF said. The industry body said tariff parity protects the competitiveness of Sri Lanka’s largest export industry at a time when the sector is competing in an increasingly crowded global sourcing market. The JAAF, which actively pushed for parity, credited the sustained government-industry engagement for the outcome and thanked President Anura Kumara Dissanayake and the officials involved in the negotiations with the US authorities. It said the outcome demonstrated the importance of government-private sector collaboration in protecting Sri Lanka’s export competitiveness.
