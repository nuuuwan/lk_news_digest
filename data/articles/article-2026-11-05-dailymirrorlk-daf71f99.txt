Cancer radiation machine tender faces conflict of interest allegations

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The procurement of cancer radiation therapy machines for the Apeksha Cancer Hospital and the Galle National Hospital has come under fresh scrutiny following allegations of conflicts of interest involving suppliers and government officials.

Consultant Physician Dr. Chamal Sanjeewa has called for an independent investigation into the tender process, claiming that medical physicists involved in evaluating the bids had attended a promotional event organised by a company linked to one of the suppliers just nine days before the scheduled opening of tenders.

The procurement involves Linear Accelerator (Linac) machines funded through an Asian Development Bank (ADB) loan scheme.

Dr. Sanjeewa has also questioned the proposed purchase of a 17-year-old Linac model, warning that Sri Lanka could end up with outdated equipment for cancer treatment.

According to information presented by Dr. Sanjeewa, the Government Medical Physicists' Association (GMPA) held its Annual General Meeting at the Taj Samudra Hotel in Colombo on June 14, 2026.

An international representative of the company manufacturing the radiation equipment in question had delivered a promotional presentation at the meeting. The event was sponsored by the company's local agent.

The presentation took place just nine days before the scheduled opening of tenders on June 23 for cancer treatment equipment for the Apeksha Cancer Hospital and the Galle National Hospital.

Dr. Sanjeewa claimed that medical physicists expected to serve on the Technical Evaluation Committees (TECs) for the same tenders had also attended the meeting.

He questioned the participation of officials responsible for evaluating the bids in a promotional event organised by a company linked to a potential supplier while the tender process was underway.

He said such contact could affect the independence and fairness of the procurement process.

Dr. Sanjeewa also said procurement guidelines, including those applicable to ADB-funded projects, required strict controls on contact between bidders and officials involved in tender evaluations.

He warned that such incidents could create opportunities for suppliers to influence technical decisions and could result in Sri Lanka purchasing outdated equipment instead of modern cancer treatment machines.

He has requested the Health Ministry to launch an independent investigation into the June 14 meeting and examine whether procurement rules had been violated.

He has also called for the removal of medical physicists who attended the promotional event from the relevant Technical Evaluation Committees until the allegations are investigated.

Dr. Sanjeewa warned that failure to address the alleged conflicts of interest could lead to legal disputes and further delays in purchasing the machines, putting the lives of cancer patients awaiting treatment at greater risk.
