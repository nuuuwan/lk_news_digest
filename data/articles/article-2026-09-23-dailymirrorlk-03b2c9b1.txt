CBSL orders asset freeze on six companies

Colombo, Sep. 23 (Daily Mirror) - The Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) has imposed freezing orders on six companies and their directors, restricting them from transferring, disposing of or otherwise dealing with their properties and assets.

The orders were issued by the Director of the Department of Supervision of Non-Bank Financial Institutions under Section 44 of the Finance Business Act, No. 42 of 2011. The Colombo High Court has subsequently confirmed and extended the freezing orders.

The companies named in the orders are Kasagala Green Plantation (Private) Limited, Ceylon Green Life Plantation (Private) Limited, Singhe Capital Investment Limited, Pro Shop Advertising Holdings (Private) Limited, Athens International Education Centre (Private) Limited, and Eyon Lanka Investment & Film Production International Company (Private) Limited.

According to the CBSL, Kasagala Green Plantation and Ceylon Green Life Plantation are currently under investigation under Section 42 of the Finance Business Act. The central bank said investigations into the two companies are ongoing.

The CBSL said it has already determined that the other four companies accepted deposits or carried out finance business in violation of Section 2 of the Finance Business Act.

The central bank has meanwhile urged the public to deposit money only with institutions that are legally authorised to accept public deposits. It said institutions licensed under the Banking Act, No. 30 of 1988, or the Finance Business Act, along with specifically exempted entities, are permitted to accept deposits from the public.

The CBSL warned that placing funds with unauthorised institutions could expose members of the public to the risk of losing their money. The regulator also said it is investigating several other individuals and institutions following complaints concerning suspected unauthorised financial activities.

The public has been urged to report individuals or institutions suspected of illegally accepting deposits to the CBSL. The central bank said such information would assist its ongoing investigations into unauthorised financial operations.
