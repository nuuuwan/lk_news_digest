Parents urged to nurture child development during first five years

Colombo, Nov. 18 (Daily Mirror) - The first five years of a child's life are crucial for brain development, language acquisition, emotional control, social interaction and learning abilities, highlighting the importance of parental involvement in early childhood development.

The home environment plays a significant role in shaping a child's physical, emotional, intellectual and social development, with parents serving as the child's first teachers.

Early Childhood Development should therefore not be considered a process confined to preschool education, as it must begin at home through love, affection, protection, proper nutrition, meaningful communication and activities that stimulate learning.

Against this backdrop, the Ministry of Women and Child Affairs has launched awareness videos on Home-Based Early Childhood Care and Development (HB-ECCD) under the ‘Senehe Thatakaya’ programme.

The videos were launched recently at the Ministry under the patronage of Deputy Minister Dr. Namal Sudarshana.

Implemented by the National Secretariat for Early Childhood Development, the programme aims to empower parents and caregivers with the knowledge and skills needed to create a nurturing environment for children at home.

It also seeks to strengthen family nutrition and food security and ensure the early identification of vulnerable children and children with special needs so that they can be referred to appropriate services.

The programme is aligned with the National Policy on Early Childhood Care and Development and the National Multi-Sectoral Strategic Action Plan for 2025–2029.

Ministry Secretary Tharangani Wickramasinghe, Additional Secretary (Development) Nilmini Herath, Community Specialist Dr. Asiri Hewamalage of the Family Health Bureau, Professor Duminda Guruge of Rajarata University, Senior Lecturer Sakunthala Ranasinghe of the National Institute of Education and Western Province Director of Preschools Randika Rajapaksha attended the launch.

Representatives of Save the Children, officials of the National Secretariat for Early Childhood Development and medical professionals were also present.
