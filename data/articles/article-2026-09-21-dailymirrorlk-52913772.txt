Prime lending rate edges up despite cuts by most banks

The AWPR rose 3 basis points to 10.76%, although nine of the 13 domestic banks tracked by the CB lowered their prime rates during the week

The weekly Average Weighted Prime Lending Rate (AWPR) edged up 3 basis points to 10.76 percent in the week ending 18 September, even though most of the country’s larger commercial banks lowered their prime rates during the week, as per Central Bank data.

The increase partly reversed a 19 basis point decline a week earlier, when the rate fell to 10.73 percent from 10.92 percent, but left the AWPR 269 basis points above the 8.07 percent recorded a year ago. The Overnight Policy Rate, meanwhile, remains at 8.75 percent, 100 basis points higher than a year earlier.

Among domestic banks, Sampath Bank recorded the steepest reduction, lowering its prime rate by 79 basis points to 11.76 percent, followed by Seylan Bank and NDB Bank, which cut their rates by 66 and 59 basis points to 13.23 percent and 10.83 percent respectively. People’s Bank, DFCC Bank and Commercial Bank also reduced their rates to 11.79 percent, 10.78 percent and 10.93 percent, while Bank of Ceylon, Hatton National Bank and Amana Bank recorded marginal cuts, leaving Bank of Ceylon and Seylan Bank with the highest prime rates in the market at 13.23 percent. In contrast, Pan Asia Banking Corporation raised its rate by 44 basis points to 11.74 percent, while Union Bank and Nations Trust Bank recorded smaller increases. Cargills Bank, which kept its rate unchanged at 10.50 percent, offered the lowest prime rate among domestic banks. Since the AWPR is weighted by loan volumes, the overall increase indicates that a larger share of prime lending during the week was extended at higher rates.

Among foreign banks, HSBC lowered its prime rate by 76 basis points to 11.63 percent and Standard Chartered by 67 basis points to 10.46 percent, while Habib Bank recorded the highest rate in the foreign bank segment at 13.06 percent.

State Bank of India offered the lowest rate among banks that extended prime loans during the week at 9.65 percent. Citi Bank, MCB Bank and Bank of China did not grant prime loans during the week, with their latest available rates carried forward.

Treasury bill yields also moved higher at the weekly auction, with the 364-day yield rising 15 basis points to 9.18 percent, 161 basis points above its level a year earlier, while foreign holdings of government securities declined 3.4 percent to Rs.206.1 billion. The rise in lending rates comes as private sector credit expanded 26.4 percent year-on-year in July.
