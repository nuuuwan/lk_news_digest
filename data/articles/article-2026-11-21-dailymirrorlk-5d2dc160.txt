25bps policy rate hike triggers a modest rise in yields across curve

The Monetary Policy Board of the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL), at its meeting held on November 19, 2026, decided to increase the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) by 25bps to 9.00 percent, from 8.75 percent, following a careful assessment of the evolving domestic and global economic conditions and the overall outlook.

Further, the CBSL raised the Statutory Reserve Ratio (SRR) by 100bps to 3.00 percent, marking the first adjustment to the ratio since 2023.

Following the monetary policy announcement, the secondary market initially witnessed a sharp overreaction, with some selling pressure during the early hours of trading, pushing yields higher by approximately 35bps.

However, as the day progressed, the buying interest gradually emerged, offsetting part of the initial upward movement in the yield curve. Consequently, the yields eventually settled around 10-20bps higher compared to the previous day.

Among the traded maturities, at the shorter end of the curve, 15.09.2029 traded at 10.85 percent. Further along the curve, 01.03.2030 traded at 11.10 percent, while 15.10.2030 traded higher between 11.25 percent to 11.20 percent. 01.12.2031 traded at 11.35 percent.

Within the 2032 segment, 01.10.2032 traded at 11.55 percent, while 15.12.2032 traded at 11.65 percent to 11.60 percent. In the 2033 segment, 15.01.2033 traded at 11.80 percent, while 01.11.2033 traded between 11.95 percent to 11.90 percent.

In the 2034 segment, both 15.06.2034 and 15.10.2034 traded at 12.05 percent. Further along the curve, 15.08.2036 traded at 12.30 percent.

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee depreciated against the US dollar, standing at Rs.332.82/US dollar, compared to Rs.332.76 recorded previously. Market liquidity in the banking system contracted to Rs.444.19 billion, compared to Rs.451.08 billion seen previously.
