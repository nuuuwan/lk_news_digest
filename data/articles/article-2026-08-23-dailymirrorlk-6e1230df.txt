Prime lending rate eases to 10.86% as banks diverge on pricing

Sri Lanka’s benchmark prime lending rate edged down during the week ending August 21, 2026, breaking recent upward momentum even as individual commercial banks displayed contrasting pricing strategies for their prime corporate borrowers.

According to data released by the Central Bank of Sri Lanka, the Average Weighted Prime Lending Rate (AWPR) declined by 9 basis points to 10.86 per cent, down from 10.95 per cent recorded in the preceding week. Despite the slight week-on-week easing, prime borrowing costs remain up 279 basis points from 8.07 per cent a year earlier, reflecting cumulative monetary adjustments and broader market rate realignments over the past year.

A granular breakdown of bank-wise lending rates revealed divergent trends across domestic private, state, and foreign commercial banks. Among state-owned lenders, the country’s largest bank, Bank of Ceylon, trimmed its prime lending rate by 17 basis points to 10.89 percent from 11.06 percent. In contrast, People’s Bank raised its prime rate by an equivalent 17 basis points to 11.02 percent from 10.85 percent a week earlier.

Key domestic private commercial banks predominantly experienced modest upward adjustments during the week. Commercial Bank of Ceylon saw its AWPR increase to 11.27 percent from 11.02 percent, while Hatton National Bank climbed to 11.36 percent from 11.18 percent. Sampath Bank and Seylan Bank recorded rates of 11.45 per cent and 11.39 percent, respectively, both firming from 11.25 percent in the previous week.

Bucking the upward trend among private peers, Union Bank of Colombo posted a substantial rate reduction of 131 basis points, bringing its AWPR down to 12.31 per cent from 13.62 percent.

DFCC Bank and Nations Trust Bank also recorded slight contractions to 11.55 per cent and 10.91 per cent, respectively, while National Development Bank (NDB) and Pan Asia Banking Corporation held their prime rates flat at 12.00 per cent and 12.50 percent. Amana Bank posted an increase of 95 basis points to settle at 13.36 percent.

Foreign banks operating in the country continued to anchor the lower band of prime lending rates, though the picture was complicated by thin trading among some lenders. Citi Bank recorded the lowest rate in the sector at 8.25 percent, though the Central Bank noted the bank had not extended fresh loans to prime customers during the week, meaning the figure reflects its last available transaction rather than current pricing — a caveat that also applies to Cargills Bank, MCB Bank and Bank of China this week.

Among banks with active pricing, State Bank of India offered the most competitive terms at 9.88 percent, followed by Deutsche Bank at 9.89 per cent. HSBC and Standard Chartered Bank eased their prime rates to 10.09 percent and 10.26 per cent, respectively, whereas Indian Bank recorded the highest rate in the banking system at 13.82 percent.

The AWPR’s dip came against a backdrop of ample, if narrowing, systemic liquidity. Outstanding market liquidity stood at a surplus of Rs. 278.51 billion by August 21, down from Rs. 290.69 billion a week earlier.

Primary market Treasury bill yields eased across short-term tenures over the same period, with the 91-day, 182-day, and 364-day yields falling to 9.22 per cent, 9.60 percent, and 9.91 percent, respectively. The Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) remained steady at 8.75 per cent, while the Average Weighted Call Money Rate (AWCMR) ticked up marginally to 8.81 percent.
