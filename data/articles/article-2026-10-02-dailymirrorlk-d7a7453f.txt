Labour and material shortages curb construction momentum

The local construction sector secured new orders at a faster pace in August, largely from government-funded projects, although shortages of bitumen, skilled workers and raw materials constrained activity and slowed the industry’s expansion.

The Central Bank’s Construction Purchasing Managers’ Index, measured by the Total Activity Index, eased to 59.4 in August from 61.4 in July. The reading remained above the neutral threshold of 50, signalling continued month-on-month expansion at a slower rate.

The New Orders Index rose to 62.5 from 57.1, pointing to stronger growth in incoming work even as construction activity lost momentum.

“Many survey respondents highlighted the ongoing bitumen shortage, which continued to constrain road construction progress, limiting the overall growth momentum of the construction industry,” the Central Bank said in its latest survey release.

The findings highlight supply constraints facing the sector as government-funded projects drive new business. Respondents also continued to report persistent shortages of skilled labour and raw materials as key obstacles to growth.

Employment and material purchases increased during August, although both expanded at a slower pace than in July.

The Employment Index eased to 59.7 from 61.8, while the Quantity of Purchases Index fell to 59.4 from 64.7. Both remained above 50, indicating that firms continued to add workers and purchase materials.

Supplier delivery times remained extended, reflecting prevailing workloads among suppliers. The Suppliers’ Delivery Time Index edged down to 59.4 from 60.0, indicating that delivery times lengthened at a slower rate.

Expectations for construction activity over the next three months remained positive, supported by newly awarded projects. However, the Central Bank said bitumen shortages and fluctuations in material prices continued to pose challenges.

The survey targets the highest-graded local construction companies registered with the Construction Industry Development Authority and covers around 35 responses to track monthly developments.
