Sri Lanka urges world to put humanity before weapons

Sri Lanka has called for strict adherence to international law and international humanitarian law amid the continuing conflicts in West Asia, while reaffirming its support for the Palestinian people's right to self-determination.

Addressing the international community, Sri Lanka said the conflicts in West Asia and elsewhere were having direct security, economic and social implications for the country, particularly given its geographical position in the Indian Ocean and the large number of Sri Lankan migrant workers in the region.

Sri Lanka said it had continued to uphold its international commitments and protect human life and dignity despite the difficult geopolitical environment.

Referring to an incident in March this year, Sri Lanka said it had rescued sailors from a vessel belonging to a party to a conflict after the ship ran into distress close to Sri Lankan territorial waters.

“While safeguarding our neutrality, we placed humanity above all else,” Sri Lanka said, recalling how the country mobilised its limited resources to rescue and care for wounded and sick sailors and subsequently rescued hundreds more.

Sri Lanka said the decision was guided by international law and humanitarian principles.

“As President Disanayaka has reiterated, ‘we will never hesitate to protect humanity. If there are actions that must be taken to protect human lives, we will not hesitate to take them under any circumstance’,” it said.

Sri Lanka said it wished to present itself to the world as a small island nation that believed there was always space for humanity, even amid conflict and deep divisions.

“We need a world where our capacity to uphold humanity grows faster than our capacity to destroy it. That is the transformation we should seek,” Sri Lanka said.

Expressing particular concern over the situation in Gaza and the Occupied Palestinian Territories, Sri Lanka said it was “pained” by the continuing developments.

It said the global security environment had deteriorated significantly in recent years, questioning the continued spending of vast resources on weapons while children faced starvation and lacked access to medicine and education.

“Sri Lanka remains deeply concerned by the conflicts in West Asia and by the heavy cost being borne far beyond the region,” it said.

Sri Lanka reiterated that international law, including International Humanitarian Law, must be respected by all parties, stressing that dialogue remained the only viable way forward.

The country also reaffirmed its support for the inalienable rights of the Palestinian people, including their right to self-determination.

Sri Lanka said it was among the countries that had recognised Palestine as a distinct State in the 1980s and reiterated its support for an independent and sovereign Palestinian State existing alongside Israel in peace, within the pre-1967 borders.

It also welcomed the UN Security Council resolution calling for a ceasefire in Gaza and urged all parties to uphold it.

“Humanitarian assistance must reach civilians without obstruction,” Sri Lanka said, adding that the recovery and reconstruction of Gaza should be treated as an urgent priority.
