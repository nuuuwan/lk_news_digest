Titanic hero’s postcard to sister sells for £32k

BBC - A postcard written by the Titanic's senior wireless operator has sold for £32,000 at auction, well over the expected price of up to £20,000.

Jack Phillips, 25, who came from Surrey, wrote the card to his sister Elsie who lived in Godalming, while the ship was docked in Southampton on 6 April 1912.

Phillips, who died in the disaster alongside about 1,500 others, remained at his post sending wireless distress calls after the TItanic had struck an iceberg.

His postcard, which was among the lots at auction on Saturday in Wiltshire, reads: "Thanks very much for your letter. Having glorious weather, went to Cowes yesterday. Will write later before we sail."

Phillips was described by auctioneer Andrew Aldridge, of Henry Aldridge and Son Ltd in Devizes, as "one of the true heroes of the disaster".

He said: "After abandoning ship when water flooded around his feet, he ended up on an overturned collapsible lifeboat where he later died of exposure to the severe cold."

While more than 1,500 people died in the Titanic, 705 people were rescued on to the RMS Carpathia, which was sent thanks to Phillips' messages.

The wireless operator had learned Morse code while working in his first job at his local Post Office.
