Sri Lanka advances regional trade framework with full SLAB digitalisation

Sri Lanka receives official invitation to join 5 sponsoring nations for a proposed UN General Assembly resolution

Sri Lanka has taken a definitive step towards modernising its national quality infrastructure and expanding its footprint in regional trade through the complete digitalisation of the Sri Lanka Accreditation Board (SLAB).

The landmark initiative was officially launched during a high-level evening gathering hosted for the Asia Pacific Accreditation Cooperation delegation at The Kingsbury Hotel in Colombo this week.

The week-long capacity-building programme brought together international experts and delegates from the member economies across the Asia-Pacific region to exchange best practices and elevate technical competencies in conformity assessment.

The digital transformation, officially inaugurated by Trade, Commerce, Food Security and Cooperative Development Minister Wasantha Samarasinghe, encompasses the automation of all the SLAB accreditation schemes, administrative mechanisms and accounting workflows. Alongside the systemic automation, the SLAB unveiled its newly redesigned official website, engineered to provide a secure, user-friendly digital interface that aligns with the evolving global digital and cybersecurity standards.

The event, which included key addresses by Ministry Secretary K.A. Vimalenthirarajah and SLAB Acting Director and CEO Chanditha Ediriweera, highlights the state’s strategic focus on leveraging automated compliance frameworks to boost national economic competitiveness.

A significant milestone of the international engagement was an official invitation extended to Sri Lanka to become one of the only five sponsoring nations—alongside Australia, Panama, Tunisia and Italy—for a proposed United Nations General Assembly resolution. The resolution seeks to formally establish June 9 as the United Nations International Day for Accreditation. This global invitation, extended during the discussions between the state officials and Global Accreditation Cooperation Incorporated (GLOBAC ACI) Chair Brahim Houla, signals the growing international recognition of Sri Lanka’s active contributions to the global quality assurance community.

Addressing the delegates, Houla commended Sri Lanka’s long-standing dedication to regional compliance architectures, recalling that the institution had hosted the joint APLAC and PAC meetings in the exact same location 11 years prior.

“Today we return not only with fond memories of this milestone but with renewed confidence in Sri Lankans and your economy, recognising your continuous adaptations and achievements in the past as well as the responsibility for the future,” Houla stated.

He further noted that the rapid advancements in artificial intelligence, digital product passports, sustainability reporting and carbon border adjustment mechanisms are fundamentally reshaping modern assessment paradigms, transitioning accreditation from a technical function into a “strategic national asset”.

The high-level forums also focused on long-term national growth, detailing strategic collaborations to upgrade Sri Lanka’s laboratory frameworks, food safety systems, public health protection and export reliability.

The evening concluded with a vote of thanks by SLAB Chairman Air Vice Marshal (Retired) Prasanna Ranasinghe. By aligning local conformity protocols with international benchmarks, Sri Lankan products and services stand to gain frictionless entry and heightened trust across highly competitive global markets.
