Over half of Sri Lankans cannot independently use a computer

Colombo, Nov. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Nearly 62 per cent of Sri Lankans aged between five and 69 are unable to independently use a computer, despite the growing use of digital technology, the latest government survey has revealed.

According to the Department of Census and Statistics (DCS), only 38.1 per cent of the population in this age group are computer literate.

The Computer Literacy Statistics Bulletin for the first six months of 2026 found that although only 38.1 per cent of Sri Lankans are computer literate, 72.5 per cent have digital literacy, suggesting that many people can independently use smartphones and other digital devices but not necessarily computers."

The survey revealed a slight gap between male and female computer literacy, with 38.9 per cent of males and 37.3 per cent of females able to use a computer independently.

Young people aged between 15 and 19 recorded the highest computer literacy rate at 75.2 per cent, followed by those in the 20–24 age group at 71.2 per cent. However, the rate dropped sharply among older people, with only 18 per cent of those aged 50–59 and 9.2 per cent of those aged 60–69 being computer literate.

Education was also found to have a significant influence on computer literacy. Those with G.C.E. Advanced Level qualifications or higher recorded the highest rate at 77.7 per cent, while only 1.5 per cent of those with no schooling were computer literate.

The survey also found that computer literacy was considerably higher among people who could read and write English, with 74.7 per cent of this group able to use a computer independently.

Meanwhile, only 20.8 per cent of households in Sri Lanka owned a desktop or laptop computer, meaning that more than four out of every five households did not have either device.

Computer ownership was highest in the urban sector at 36.2 per cent, compared to 18.6 per cent in rural areas and just 2.2 per cent in estate areas.

The Western Province recorded the highest household computer ownership at 32.1 per cent, while the Eastern Province reported the lowest at 12.8 per cent.

The urban sector also recorded the highest computer literacy rate at 51.6 per cent, followed by the rural sector at 36.3 per cent. The estate sector recorded the lowest rate at 15.8 per cent.

Among the provinces, the Western Province had the highest computer literacy rate at 47.6 per cent, while the North Central Province recorded the lowest at 27.5 per cent.

Despite the relatively low computer literacy rate, internet usage has continued to increase, with 63.9 per cent of the population aged between five and 69 using the internet at least once during the preceding 12 months. This was an increase from 60.4 per cent in 2025.

People aged between 20 and 24 recorded the highest internet usage at 91.8 per cent, followed by those aged 25–29 at 90.6 per cent.

However, only 21.9 per cent of the population used email during the same 12-month period.

The findings also highlighted the growing reliance on smartphones to access the internet and email. Smartphones accounted for 80.9 per cent of devices used for these purposes, while desktop and laptop computers accounted for 17.1 per cent.

Schools and universities were identified as the main sources of computer knowledge, accounting for 63.5 per cent of computer-literate respondents. Self-learning was the second most common source at 41 per cent, followed by family members at 28.1 per cent.

The survey was conducted between January and June 2026, covering a nationally distributed sample of 12,500 households and individuals aged between five and 69.
