US announces new tariffs of up to 20% on China, the EU, and 12 other nations

yahoo finance - The Trump administration on Wednesday proposed new tariffs of up to 20% on China, the European Union, and 12 other major US trading partners to take effect next year, following an 8-month investigation into excess manufacturing capacity.

The duties could be a shock to the global economy, with China a main target.

The proposed 20% tariff on many Chinese goods was described by the Trump administration as a "deal rate" that could go higher depending on how ongoing talks progress. The new tariff would be stacked on top of at least some preexisting tariffs, resulting in a total rate over 40% on some goods.

The new tariffs won't go into effect until after additional hearings and a public comment period and "probably will be in January at the earliest," according to a US official who briefed reporters ahead of the announcement.

An existing trade truce with China is set to expire on Jan. 10.

Several other Asian nations are also in line for higher duties, with a new 20% tariff proposed for Vietnam. Cambodia, Indonesia, and Malaysia are in line for 18% rates. India would have a 17% rate but only, the administration said, if ongoing talks produce a deal.

The US official said the rates could go even higher depending on how the countries respond, and added that the investigation had concluded that a new 40% tariff on China and a 35% rate on Vietnam was "warranted," even as a lower rate is being proposed for now due to ongoing talks

On Wednesday, the White House listed an array of "Proposed Rates" that were higher across the board, ranging from 20% to 40%.

The effects elsewhere could be more muted, with new duties designed not to overstep rates negotiated in recent trade deals. That meant trading partners, including the European Union, South Korea, and Japan would all see tariff rate of 15%, which is in line with what they negotiated with the Trump administration in recent months.

The new duties exclude key products such as semiconductors, as well as automobiles, steel, and aluminum, which are subject to a separate tariff authority. Other goods the Trump administration has carved out in recent months, including certain energy and food products, will also be exempted.

The investigation was conducted under the authority of Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 on Bangladesh, Cambodia, China, the European Union, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, and Vietnam.

Norway and Mexico were also under investigation. Those findings are still being reviewed.

The new duties are formally aimed at what the Trump administration charges are illegal subsidies by foreign nations to create excess manufacturing capacity that the US found "to be unreasonable and burden or restrict US commerce."

"This is not a new issue, unfortunately," the US official said. "It's created severe imbalances globally, and it's harmed US commerce in particular."

The duties are also meant to replace tariffs struck down by the US Supreme Court in February.

In some cases, the new tariffs will supplant a previous set of tariffs that went into effect in July of 10% to 12.5% on 60 of the top US trade partners over the issue of forced labor.

The excess capacity duties, which are higher, will supplant the forced labor tariffs in nations that were investigated for both issues.

The Yale Budget Lab and other trade trackers didn't offer an immediate read on how the new tariffs would affect overall tariff levels, but the group calculated earlier this month that the effective US tariff rate sits at about 11.3% across all trading partners. That same measure was 13.86% just before the Supreme Court struck down Trump's blanket tariffs in February and was above 15% for much of 2025.

In a note to clients ahead of the announcement, Ed Mills of Raymond James described the move "as an effort to restore the previous tariff structure" that existed before the Supreme Court's intervention.

This investigation has long been expected to wrap up by the end of the year, but the unveiling this week was notable amid growing concerns in Washington over semiconductor exports, Nvidia, and questions about "mass smuggling" of advanced chips to China.

The announcement also came on the heels of a trip to Asia where President Trump had a trade signing ceremony with Thailand and announced what he called a trade deal with the Philippines but left many of the major trade issues between the US and China unresolved.

That meeting ended with no apparent agreement to extend the truce that has lowered tariffs between the US and China that expires on Jan. 10.
