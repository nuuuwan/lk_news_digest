Prison population falls, but remand inmates still pack jails

Colombo, Oct. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Sri Lanka’s prison population has declined gradually in recent months, with the total number of inmates standing at 36,364 as of October 3, 2026, according to official statistics.

The latest figures show that the prison population comprises 10,768 convicted prisoners, including 10,493 males and 275 females, while 25,596 are unconvicted or remand inmates, comprising 23,963 males and 1,633 females.

The total prison population stood at 39,516 inmates as of August 1, indicating a reduction of more than 3,000 inmates over the period.

Despite the decline, unconvicted inmates continue to account for more than 70% of the total prison population, highlighting the pressure created by prolonged remand periods.

Recent updates also show that 101 convicted prisoners were released, while 11 unconvicted inmates were granted bail.

Last week, Justice and National Integration Minister Harshana Nanayakkara said extensive measures were being taken to address chronic overcrowding and bottlenecks in the judicial process.

He said 17,506 Government Analyst reports related to drug cases had been expedited and issued during August and September, with 9,061 reports released in August and a further 8,445 in September.

Delays in obtaining Government Analyst reports have previously resulted in suspects remaining in remand custody for periods ranging from 18 months to two years.

The Parliament Sectoral Oversight Committee on Governance, Justice and Civil Protection, chaired by MP Dr. Najith Indika, has also examined a range of legal, administrative and infrastructure measures to address prison overcrowding.

Among the proposals is the use of community service or work camps for minor offenders sentenced to less than four years or those unable to pay minor fines, through expedited judicial procedures.

The committee has also discussed amendments to enable persons arrested for minor drug offences or drug use to be directed to rehabilitation centres following medical assessments, instead of being imprisoned.

To reduce the security and administrative burden associated with transporting thousands of inmates to courts, the committee has called for the rapid introduction of remote video conferencing facilities for virtual court proceedings.

Measures have also been proposed to facilitate the release of elderly, fully disabled and chronically ill inmates based on recommendations by medical boards. A special judicial mechanism is also being considered to expedite cases involving mothers imprisoned with children below five years of age.

Meanwhile, authorities have stressed the need to renovate deteriorating prison buildings, complete the new Millewa-Horana prison complex and make better use of existing prison space by transferring inmates from severely overcrowded facilities.

Commenting on recent unrest in prisons, Minister Nanayakkara said the incidents were not spontaneous and had been influenced by both external factors and internal tensions.

The Minister also said the misconduct of a small number of prison officers should not undermine the reputation of the entire prison service, reiterating the Government’s commitment to protecting the welfare and dignity of inmates.
