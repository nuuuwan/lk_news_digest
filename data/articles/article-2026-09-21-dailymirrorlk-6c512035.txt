Softlogic to bring Wow! Momo, Tiong Bahru Bakery and Mad Over Donuts to Sri Lanka

Softlogic restructures Rs. 59.8bn of loans, with Rs. 18.1bn due in 2026/27

Group still seeks buyers for its hotels, while ODEL Mall needs Rs. 6.1bn to complete

Ashok Pathirage-controlled  Softlogic Holdings PLC plans to open three new international restaurant franchises in Sri Lanka by January, including India’s Wow! Momo and Singapore’s Tiong Bahru Bakery, as the debt-laden conglomerate works to convert a strong operating recovery into sustainable profits.

The group disclosed the plans in its annual report for 2025/26, in which it posted a marginal pre-tax profit of Rs. 24 million, compared with a pre-tax loss of Rs. 7.49 billion a year earlier. However, Softlogic still recorded a loss of Rs. 8.63 billion attributable to shareholders for the year ended 31 March 2026.

According to the report, the restaurant business will introduce Tiong Bahru Bakery, the Singaporean artisan bakery and café, along with India’s Wow! Momo and doughnut chain Mad Over Donuts.

Speaking to Mirror Business, Softlogic Chairman and Managing Director Ashok Pathirage revealed that Tiong Bahru Bakery will open at Arcade Independence Square, which is managed by the group, with a target launch in December or January. He added that Mad Over Donuts and Wow! Momo are expected to open around January at One Galle Face Mall. Founded in 2012, Tiong Bahru Bakery is known for its French-inspired handmade pastries and croissants, and operates around 20 outlets in Singapore. The brand made its international debut  last year, opening its first overseas outlet in Manila through the Philippines’ Jollibee Group.

Wow! Momo, which began as a single outlet in Kolkata in 2008, has grown into one of India’s fastest-growing quick service restaurant chains with a network of around 850 stores. Mad Over Donuts, also founded in 2008, operates more than 150 outlets across India and is known for its freshly made, eggless doughnuts. Softlogic’s restaurant arm operated 43 outlets as at 31 March 2026, comprising 22 Burger King, seven Baskin-Robbins, six Popeyes, four Délifrance, three Subway and one Crystal Jade outlet. The group described the business as one of its successful consumer operations. It opened Délifrance and Burger King outlets in Thalawathugoda in July and scheduled a new Popeyes outlet at One Galle Face for this month, further expanding its presence at the mall. The group also intends to expand its GLOMARK supermarket chain, with two to three new stores targeted for the current financial year and an average of four to five stores annually thereafter, including its smaller-format concept.

Group revenue rose 24 percent to Rs. 129 billion during the year, while EBITDA grew 43 percent to Rs. 17.8 billion and operating profit increased 83 percent to Rs. 11.6 billion. Finance costs eased to Rs. 13.7 billion from Rs. 16.1 billion, helping the group narrow its loss after tax to Rs. 3.59 billion from Rs. 11.09 billion.

Despite the improvement, the balance sheet remains under severe strain. Shareholders’ funds stood at a negative Rs. 66.4 billion, and the current ratio was 0.48 times. Net interest-bearing borrowings declined only marginally to Rs. 115.9 billion as at 31 March 2026, from Rs. 117.6 billion a year earlier. The group also slipped back into a pre-tax loss in the first quarter of 2026/27, which management attributed to prevailing economic conditions and financing costs.

To ease its liquidity pressures, the group has signed debt restructuring offer letters for loans amounting to Rs. 47.5 billion up to 2025/26 and a further Rs. 12.2 billion after the year end, securing grace periods of six to 18 months on capital repayments. Under the rescheduled terms, Rs. 18.1 billion falls due in 2026/27 and Rs. 41.8 billion thereafter. Restructuring requests for another Rs. 15.7 billion are pending approval from lenders.

Pathirage noted that only a small fraction of the group’s debt still needs to be restructured, with negotiations with lenders currently underway.

Softlogic is also seeking approval at extraordinary general meetings on 21 September to extend the conversion date of 121.6 million warrants, issued with its 2024/25 rights issue, to 7 October. Its major shareholders, Pathirage and Dubai-based Samena Capital’s Samena Ceylon Holdings, have already exercised their warrants. The company has received Rs. 498 million so far, with a further Rs. 777 million expected, and will use the funds to settle debt and meet working capital requirements.

“The same entrepreneurial conviction that built Softlogic over more than three decades is today being applied with greater financial discipline, sharper capital allocation and a clear focus on sustainable returns,” Pathirage told shareholders in the annual report, acknowledging that considerable work remains ahead.

The flagship ODEL Mall project at Ward Place remains the group’s largest exposure, with work-in-progress of Rs. 18.5 billion as at 31 March 2026. The auditors flagged its carrying value as a key audit matter, noting it represents 10 percent of group assets, and the project recorded a Rs. 370 million impairment during the year. Management has scrapped the original apartment component in favour of office space and prioritised Phase I, retaining around 100 perches of land for future development. The company intends to raise Rs. 6.1 billion through office space pre-sales and external equity to complete construction. Meanwhile, a bank consortium led by Hatton National Bank is evaluating options to restructure the existing syndicated loan.

In the leisure sector, the Board is still in discussions with potential buyers and investors and intends to continue these efforts during 2026/27. This is despite the group’s two hotels moving to Accor management in August as the Pullman Colombo City Centre and Pullman Bentota Resort & Spa. Asked about the planned divestment, Pathirage remained noncommittal. “If we get the right price, then we will see,” he told Mirror Business.

Leisure and property revenue rose 5 percent to Rs. 3.1 billion, while sector EBITDA improved to Rs. 303 million from Rs. 74 million.

The automobile sector delivered one of the sharpest turnarounds following the relaxation of vehicle import restrictions. Sector revenue climbed to Rs. 3.5 billion from Rs. 409 million, and the business posted a profit after tax of Rs. 231 million against a loss of Rs. 111 million a year earlier. The recovery came from the return of Ford vehicles and Suzuki motorcycles, along with the group’s entry into the passenger and commercial three-wheeler market through ATUL Auto.
