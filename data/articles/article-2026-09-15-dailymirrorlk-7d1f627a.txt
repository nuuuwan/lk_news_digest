CSE extends decline as elevated oil prices keep sentiment cautious

The Colombo Stock Exchange closed lower for a second consecutive session on 15 September 2026, with both benchmark indices declining amid continued cautious investor sentiment and elevated global oil prices.

The ASPI fell 51.79 points, or 0.24%, to 21,262.13, while the S&P SL20 declined 17.66 points, or 0.29%, to 5,976.67. The ASPI weakened steadily through the session and closed near the day’s lows, with Brent crude remaining elevated around USD 105.68 per barrel and keeping energy-cost concerns in focus.

Market turnover reached LKR 2.23 billion, with 55.82 million shares traded. The Food, Beverage and Tobacco sector led activity with LKR 1.20 billion in turnover, driven mainly by Lanka Milk Foods, which recorded LKR 872.57 million.

A notable feature of the session was the large LMF.N block, where 10.00 million shares changed hands at LKR 84.50 for LKR 845.00 million. Crossings accounted for around 52% of total turnover, meaning headline activity was significantly boosted by block transactions rather than broad market participation.

Market breadth remained weak, with 71 gainers against 132 decliners, while foreign investors recorded a net outflow of LKR 63.05 million.

Dialog Axiata was the largest negative contributor to the ASPI, followed by Ceylinco Insurance, Lion Brewery, Melstacorp and John Keells Holdings. Sampath Bank provided the strongest positive contribution.

Overall sentiment remained cautious, with weak market breadth, continued foreign selling and elevated oil prices weighing on risk appetite. Despite higher turnover, the concentration of activity in the LMF.N crossing suggests underlying market participation remained relatively soft.
