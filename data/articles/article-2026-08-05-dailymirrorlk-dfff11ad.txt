Elephant corridors to be restored under new Human-Elephant Conflict strategy

Colombo, August 5 (Daily Mirror) - The Government is planning to restore blocked elephant migration routes and establish elephant enrichment zones as part of a new national strategy aimed at addressing Sri Lanka’s long-standing Human-Elephant Conflict (HEC).

The Environment Ministry said measures were underway to clear obstructions along traditional elephant corridors and improve habitats within forest ecosystems through the "Vanaspathi" national programme.

Officials said the loss of natural habitats and disruption of elephant movement patterns due to rapid development activities, deforestation and changes in land use have contributed significantly to the increasing conflict between humans and elephants.

The proposed National Action Plan on Human-Elephant Conflict will focus on long-term ecological solutions, including restoring elephant habitats, protecting migration routes and creating safer conditions for both wildlife and communities living in conflict-prone areas.

The preparation of the action plan is being carried out with the participation of Members of Parliament representing districts most affected by human-elephant conflict, along with government officials and environmental experts.

A monitoring committee overseeing the preparation of the plan has been established, with discussions focusing on developing a community-participatory approach to managing the conflict.

The Government has also identified the need to strengthen the management of Managed Elephant Reserves by ensuring proper implementation of existing laws and regulations.

Officials said the new strategy will move beyond temporary measures and introduce a scientific approach aimed at promoting coexistence between humans and elephants while safeguarding Sri Lanka’s natural ecosystems.
