Manufacturers seek power and tax relief in Budget 2027

Sri Lanka’s main industrial chamber called for relief on electricity tariffs and corporate taxes, saying Budget 2027 would expand access to credit for the small businesses while leaving the domestic factories under pressure from the operating costs.

Ceylon National Chamber of Industries (CNCI) Chairman Pradeep Kahawalage said the budget lacked “broad-based relief on industrial power tariffs or corporate taxes to ease the operational pressures on the domestic factories”.

The CNCI also pointed to the absence of large industrial or construction projects that could attract private-sector investment and generate orders for local manufacturers. It called for tighter oversight of public investment, from planning through execution, to ensure the funds are used on time.

Kahawalage welcomed the allocation of Rs.67.7 billion for interest subsidies and refinancing, which the CNCI said was expected to unlock Rs.174 billion in concessional loans for entrepreneurs through state and private banks.

He said the financing would support the micro, small and medium enterprises, which the government has placed at the centre of its plans to expand production.

The CNCI also backed the plans to develop the Valachchenai Industrial Estate and fund feasibility studies and preliminary planning for an estate in Puliyankulam.

The budget seeks to accelerate work in Ingiriya, Dambulla, Raigama, Millaniya and Katunayake and upgrade the infrastructure at the existing estates. Kahawalage urged the officials to ensure timely spending across the government’s wider investment programme including transport, highways, railways, water supply, irrigation, health, education, housing, urban development, energy, digital infrastructure, ports and regional projects.

He also backed the push to digitise public administration, saying it could improve the efficiency and transparency of the government operations.

The CNCI welcomed the budget’s emphasis on preserving macroeconomic stability, strengthening economic resilience, reviving the rural economy and keeping energy markets stable.

Kahawalage cited President Anura Kumara Dissanayake’s aim to deliver a budget that “strengthens the production economy, protects the small and medium-scale industries, makes room for investment, connects knowledge and technology to the economy through digital economic activities, protects the environment and ensures social justice”.

The CNCI said it would continue to work with the government on its economic programme.
