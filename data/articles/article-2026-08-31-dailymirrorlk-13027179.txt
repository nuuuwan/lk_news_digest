Sri Lanka closely monitors Iranian-flagged vessels near EEZ

Colombo, Aug. 31 (Daily Mirror) - The Sri Lankan Government is closely monitoring several Iranian-flagged vessels anchored off the country’s coast near the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ), while authorities have clarified that the ships remain outside Sri Lanka’s territorial waters and are therefore not subject to intervention under the country’s jurisdiction.

The spokesman for the Sri Lanka Navy said the Navy and Sri Lanka Air Force are maintaining close surveillance of the vessels. However, he stressed that the ships are located outside Sri Lanka’s sovereign territorial waters, meaning the Government is neither responsible for them nor providing port facilities or other assistance.

According to maritime tracking service TankerTrackers, nearly 20 Iranian fuel tankers, including around 13 Iranian-flagged vessels, are currently gathered off Sri Lanka’s coast. Several other vessels have reportedly been identified as part of a so-called “shadow fleet” allegedly used to circumvent international sanctions.

The vessels are reportedly empty and have been unable to return to Iranian ports amid an ongoing U.S. naval blockade affecting Iranian ports.

The presence of the vessels has attracted increased attention amid concerns over maritime security, particularly following the sinking of the Irish-flagged Dena vessel approximately 19 nautical miles off Sri Lanka’s coast in March.

Commander Chaminda Walakuluge said that foreign vessels operating beyond the country’s territorial waters are entitled to navigate international sea lanes in accordance with international maritime law.

He said the vessels in question remain beyond the 12-nautical-mile territorial limit and within areas where Sri Lanka has limited jurisdiction.

He pointed to the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), particularly Articles 58 and 87, which protect freedom of navigation in the EEZ and on the high seas. Under these provisions, vessels operating lawfully outside territorial waters generally remain under the jurisdiction of their respective flag states.

Despite having no legal basis to arbitrarily stop or obstruct the vessels, the Sri Lanka Navy continues to maintain 24-hour Maritime Domain Awareness and surveillance across the country's maritime zones using naval patrol craft, the spokesman said.

The spokesman also said that Sri Lanka's enforcement powers in the contiguous zone are limited to specific matters, including customs, fiscal, immigration and environmental regulations, as well as certain unlawful activities such as illicit ship-to-ship transfers. He said no illegal activity or regulatory violation involving the vessels has so far been detected or reported.

Sri Lankan authorities have also clarified the country's position regarding international sanctions. Commander Walakuluge said Sri Lanka is legally bound by sanctions imposed by the United Nations, but unilateral sanctions imposed by individual countries do not automatically create a legal obligation for Sri Lanka to take action against foreign vessels.

In the absence of a violation of UN sanctions or any unlawful activity occurring within Sri Lankan jurisdiction, the spokesman said there is currently no legal requirement or basis for Sri Lanka to intervene against the vessels.
