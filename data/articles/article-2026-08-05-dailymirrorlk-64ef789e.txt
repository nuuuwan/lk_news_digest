Court seeks IRD clarification before ruling in national cricketers’ tax dispute

Colombo, Aug. 5 (Daily Mirror) - The Court of Appeal today sought clarification from the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) on whether the recently enacted amendments to the Inland Revenue Act would affect the tax dispute involving Sri Lanka’s national male and female cricket captains, before delivering its judgment.

The writ applications, filed by the national cricket captains, were taken up before a bench comprising President of the Court of Appeal, Justice Rohantha Abeysuriya PC, and Justice K. Priyantha Fernando.

When the matter was called, the Court noted that Parliament had recently amended the Inland Revenue Act to specifically classify “sports persons” as Independent Service Providers (ISPs), a category that does not attract Advance Personal Income Tax (APIT).

The bench sought clarification from Deputy Solicitor General Manohara Jayasinghe, appearing for the IRD, on whether the department would reconsider the decision being challenged in light of the legislative amendment.

Responding to the Court, the Deputy Solicitor General maintained that the new amendment had no bearing on the present dispute. He argued that national cricketers are employees because they are engaged under contracts with Sri Lanka Cricket and submitted that the Court should proceed to deliver its judgment.

Appearing for the two male national captains, Counsel Nishan Sydney Premathiratne referred to the relevant Parliamentary Hansard and argued that the amendment had been introduced specifically to address areas of uncertainty and dispute under the tax law.

He submitted that Parliament had now clearly categorised sports persons, including cricketers, as Independent Service Providers alongside professionals such as doctors, lawyers, architects, videographers and singers.

Counsel further argued that for more than 15 years national cricketers had been recognised as ISPs rather than employees, and that the IRD had arbitrarily reclassified them as employees, solely for the purpose of imposing APIT.

He contended that the latest amendment reflected Parliament’s clear legislative intent that sports persons should not be treated as employees for tax purposes, but should instead remain subject to the withholding tax regime applicable to Independent Service Providers.

The petitioners also informed the Court that professional payments due to the players for the past seven months had been withheld as a result of the dispute, causing them significant hardship.

The Court informed the parties that although it had already prepared its judgment, it had postponed delivering the decision in order to obtain clarification from the IRD regarding the effect of the recent legislative amendment.

The bench granted both parties one week to file written clarification submissions and fixed the matter for judgment on August 31, 2026.

Counsel Nishan Sydney Premathiratne appeared with Shenali Dias and Sidath Gajanayaka on the instructions of Gamindu Karunasena for the petitioners representing the male cricketers.

President’s Counsel Dr. Harsha Cabral with Vikum Jayasinghe and Manith Dasanayake on the instructions of Gamindu Karunasena appeared for the petitioners representing the female cricketers.

Deputy Solicitor General Manohara Jayasinghe appeared for the Attorney General representing the Inland Revenue Department.

President’s Counsel Kuvera de Zoysa appeared with Sajana de Zoysa on the instructions of Sanjaya Fonseka, representing the former office bearers of SLC.
