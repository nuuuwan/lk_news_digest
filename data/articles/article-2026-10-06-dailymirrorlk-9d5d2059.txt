CSE ends flat as early recovery fades

The Colombo Stock Exchange attempted a modest recovery on 6 October 2026 following the previous session’s sharp decline, although early buying momentum faded as selling pressure returned during the afternoon.

The ASPI gained just 5.97 points, or 0.03%, to close at 20,650.23 after trading above 20,740 during the morning. The S&P SL20, however, declined 2.74 points, or 0.05%, to 5,844.28, reflecting the mixed nature of the session.

Market turnover moderated to LKR 1.07 billion, with 43.09 million shares traded. The Food, Beverage and Tobacco sector led activity with LKR 243.40 million in turnover, while Watawala Plantations generated LKR 86.31 million within the sector.

Crossings accounted for around 27% of total turnover, led by CDB.N with LKR 99.00 million, followed by WATA.N, Commercial Bank and Ceylon Cold Stores.

A notable development was trading in AMSL.N, which recorded the highest individual counter turnover at approximately LKR 156.42 million as around 15.50 million shares changed hands, while the share price declined sharply by 21.88%.

Market breadth remained negative, with 87 gainers against 108 decliners, while foreign investors continued to be net sellers with an outflow of approximately LKR 29.28 million.

GREG.N was the strongest positive contributor to the ASPI, followed by Ceylinco Insurance, Hemas Holdings, LOLC Finance and Piramal Glass. Dialog Axiata remained the largest negative contributor, followed by Commercial Bank, Hatton National Bank, SMB Finance and Lion Brewery.

The inability to retain the stronger morning recovery, together with negative breadth and continued foreign selling, suggests buying conviction remains weak. Although the ASPI managed a marginal positive close, broader participation will need to improve for a more convincing recovery to emerge.
