Cabinet approves national committee to address wildlife hazards in aviation

Colombo, Oct. 27 (Daily Mirror) - The Cabinet of Ministers has approved the establishment of a National Wildlife Hazard Management Committee for Aviation Safety to strengthen coordination among relevant agencies in addressing the risk of aircraft-wildlife collisions in civil aviation.

The decision was taken following a proposal submitted by the Minister of Ports and Civil Aviation, paving the way for a national-level mechanism to coordinate wildlife hazard management at and around aerodromes across Sri Lanka.

Sri Lanka, as a contracting state to the Convention on International Civil Aviation, also known as the Chicago Convention, is required to implement international standards and recommended practices to minimise the risk of aircraft colliding with wildlife.

Under paragraph 9.4 of Volume I of Annex 14 to the Convention, Sri Lanka must maintain procedures to record and report aircraft-wildlife collisions, collect information on wildlife that could pose a threat to aviation, and continuously assess the risk of such incidents at or near aerodromes through suitably qualified personnel.

Information on wildlife collisions must also be submitted for inclusion in the International Civil Aviation Organization’s Bird Collision Information System.

The decision is also in line with the organisation’s Asia and Pacific regional guidelines, which recommend establishing a Wildlife Hazard Management Committee or Forum to facilitate coordinated action at the national level.

The new committee is expected to improve cooperation among agencies responsible for managing wildlife hazards and strengthen aviation safety by supporting more effective monitoring, reporting and assessment of risks posed by wildlife to aircraft operations.
