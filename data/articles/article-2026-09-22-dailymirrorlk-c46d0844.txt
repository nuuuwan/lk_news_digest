Govt. commits to boat manufacturing zone; EDB to pick site

EDB targets over US $ 500mn in marine exports, over 9 times 2025 boat export earnings

Only 10 to 12 of 60 to 70 boatbuilders are established exporters

The government has committed to establishing a dedicated boat manufacturing zone, with the Export Development Board (EDB) in the process of selecting a site from the locations proposed by the industry, the speakers said at the fourth Annual General Meeting of the Chamber of Marine Industries of Sri Lanka (CMISL), held at Amari Colombo last week.

Outgoing CMISL Chairman Indhra Kaushal Rajapaksa revealed that the chamber had put forward the Oruwella Harbour and Kapparathota Harbour Marina as potential locations, following a recent meeting with the Industry and Fisheries Ministers, at which the government agreed to set up the zone.

He explained that the industry’s main difficulty lies in shipping out the completed vessels rather than in building them.

“We find it very difficult not to make boats but to export the finished boat,” Rajapaksa told the gathering, adding that the Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Ministry had agreed to set up a one-stop shop within the ministry to help the boat exporters resolve their problems.

Newly appointed CMISL Chairman Ricky Barnett observed that a dedicated zone would lower the production costs for the local builders and could attract foreign direct investment by improving the feasibility of projects in the sector.

The zone marks a second attempt at such a facility, as Industry and Entrepreneurship Development Ministry Secretary J.M. Thilaka Jayasundara acknowledged that an earlier effort with the Sri Lanka Ports Authority to set aside a separate industrial zone for maritime, boatbuilding and shipbuilding activities had collapsed.

“That failed because of some policy and regulatory issues but I feel we can reidentify the requirement of establishing a separate industrial zone,” she remarked, stressing that the government’s aim went beyond piecemeal infrastructure.

“Instead of just establishing facilities, we really want to develop a separate zone with all facilities.”

Separately, Jayasundara disclosed that the first inter-agency meeting on a marine hub at Kapparathota would be held this week, bringing together the relevant ministries, including the Industry and Fisheries, alongside the EDB and Board of Investment.

According to Jayasundara, the EDB has already surveyed the site and development is expected to commence by 2027.

Jayasundara noted that the boat manufacturing industry earned US $ 53.7 million in export revenue in 2025, while the earnings for the January to July 2026 period stood at around US $ 25 million. If sustained, that pace would bring full-year earnings to roughly US $ 43 million, below the 2025 level. Against this backdrop, she pressed the industry to define its share of the government’s US $ 36 billion export target for 2030.

“So, where is the gap and what is the way forward?” she questioned, before asking the chamber, “Out of that US $ 36 billion, what is your portion? What is your contribution?”

EDB Chairman Mangala Wijesinghe urged the industry to rethink how it sees itself, arguing that “the marine-based industry should no longer be viewed simply as a traditional maritime sector”.

“We are closely working with the relevant chambers to fulfil the identified gaps and are targeting more than US $ 500 million in export revenue from this sector,” Wijesinghe noted.

The target is more than nine times the boat industry’s 2025 export earnings, though it would still account for only about 1.4 percent of the national export goal. Citing the UN Trade and Development data, he pointed out that the global ocean economy generates approximately US $ 2.2 trillion in exports annually and supports more than 600 million jobs.

Meanwhile, Barnett, who takes over the chair, with Adityavikram More continuing as Secretary and Akalanka Rathnayaka serving as Treasurer, outlined a governance overhaul at the chamber. “Thirteen of the 15 directors resigned and we decided to create a very focused executive committee,” he announced, noting that the new council would be built around four pillars, namely marine and offshore, boatbuilding, nautical tourism and marine sustainability and ESG. He also highlighted the narrow base of the country’s exporting builders. “Today, the marine industry has about 60 to 70 boatbuilders but only about 10 to 12 of them are topclass international exporters. You can see the gap,” Barnett told the members, meaning roughly one in six builders currently competes globally.

To narrow that gap, the chamber will host an EDBsponsored Boat Building Industry Technology and Standardisation Symposium on October 30 and 31, which is expected to draw about 100 participants. On the existing infrastructure, Rajapaksa credited the ministry with completing the Beruwala boat launching ramp, which Jayasundara confirmed had been handed over to the association and Ceylon Fisheries Harbour Corporation, with a memorandum of understanding on its maintenance to be signed with the CMISL shortly. With the first slipway at Beruwala now in place, he requested that the two remaining slipways planned for the location be completed, while also appealing for facilities at Welipatanwila and Negombo, pointing out that the boat manufacturers in the South and in Negombo were facing considerable difficulties without them. Responding to those requests, Jayasundara indicated that the feasibility studies for the Negombo and Galle locations would be carried out with the support of the CMISL, with the development targeted within five years.
