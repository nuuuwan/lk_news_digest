Buying sentiment remains strong in secondary market

The buying sentiment remained firm in the secondary market, with robust trading activity observed yesterday.

The heightened demand continued to support the market, resulting in yields edging lower across the curve.

In the 2028 segment, 15.02.2028, 15.03.2028 and 01.05.2028 traded between 10.30 percent and 10.20 percent. In the 2029 category, 15.10.2029 and 15.12.2029 traded within the range of 10.75 percent–10.65 percent.

Moving towards the mid-end of the curve, 01.03.2030, 15.05.2030, 01.08.2030 and 15.10.2030 traded between 11.10 percent and 10.85 percent. Further along the curve, 01.02.2031 and 15.05.2031 traded between 11.25 percent and 10.95 percent.

In the 2032 segment, 15.12.2032 traded at 11.40 percent. In the 2033 segment, 15.01.2033 and 01.11.2033 traded between 11.80 percent and 11.60 percent.

Moving towards the tail end of the curve, 15.10.2034 traded between 11.95 percent and 11.82 percent. Finally, in the 2035 segment, 15.03.2035 traded between 12.00 percent and 11.85 percent, whilst 15.08.2036 traded at 12.30 percent.

Moreover, the PDMO concluded its weekly T-bill auction, successfully raising the full initial offer of Rs.140.0 billion. The three-month, six-month and 12-month bills raised Rs.55.0 billion, Rs.50.0 billion and Rs.35.0 billion, respectively, in line with the amounts initially offered.

The weighted average yields on the three-month, six-month and 12-month bills declined by 33 bps, 21 bps and 18 bps, respectively, to 9.44 percent, 9.78 percent and 10.01 percent.

On the external front, the Sri Lankan rupee appreciated against the US dollar, standing at Rs.334.79/US dollar, compared to Rs.335.20/US dollar seen earlier. Market liquidity in the banking system expanded to Rs.299.5 billion, from Rs.274.0 billion recorded previously.
