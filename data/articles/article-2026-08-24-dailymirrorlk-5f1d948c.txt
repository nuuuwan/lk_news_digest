The controversial 22nd Amendment to the Constitution

The much talked of 22nd Amendment to the Constitution was tabled in Parliament on  August 18 2026 by the Minister of Justice. According to the government, the bill itself is designed to roll back the concentration of unchecked executive powers granted under the 20th Amendment (during the presidency of Gotabaya Rajapaksa) and restore key parliamentary checks and balances.

The 22nd Amendment to the Constitution speaks of Trimming Executive Presidential Powers. Under the proposed Amendment,the President can no longer arbitrarily remove the Prime Minister or the Cabinet. The President must act on the Prime Minister’s advice when appointing or dismissing Ministers.

It bars Dual Citizens, (individuals holding dual citizenship) from contesting elections or holding public office (e.g., as MPs or President).

It seeks to re-establish the Constitutional Council to oversee key state bodies and appointments (such as the Elections Commission, Bribery Commission, Human Rights Commission, and Police Commission).

It places the appointment of the Governor of the Central Bank of Sri Lanka under the approval of the Constitutional Council and Strengthens Anti-Corruption Safeguards via enhanced financial and constitutional oversight to enforce greater government accountability.

However what comes across very loudly are voices from the parliamentary Opposition, saying that the bill is all about the extension of dates for the retirement age of judges in the country. The parliamentary Opposition tells all of us citizens’ of Lanka, that the government simply wants to lengthen the tenure of the present Chief Justice (CJ) of the country.

The CJ, as  certain quarters claim,  is more compliant to the wishes of the present regime,  and therefore the government wants him to continue in office for a further period of two years. The Bar Association of Sri Lanka (BASL) too,  vehemently opposes 22A on this basis. They claim the government’s move to extend the retiring age of judges will infringe on the independence of the Judiciary.

To strengthen their call for rescinding  the 22nd Amendment to the Constitution,  the Association has called for the support of the Commonwealth Lawyers Association. This Association too,  has rushed in to the support the local lawyers’ association. In our country, we are unable to settle any of our internal problems within our borders, irrespective of whether we are Sinhalese, Tamil, Muslim or Burghers or of any other ethnicity.

During the initial days,  when members of the Tamil community felt they were discriminated against, they first attempted dialogue locally, But,  when these efforts did not succeed both the Sinhalese and Tamils called for Indian help. The Indians were not able to solve our problems. They worsened it. It ultimately led to the Lankan President demanding that the Indian army leave our shores,  and the  Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE) assassinating the Indian Prime Minister.

As we did not make a serious effort to settle problems within our shores, our country was burdened with a near three decade long civil insurrection. Over a hundred thousand of our people were killed and the scars of that battle still remain.

Rather than call in international organisations to help settle our problems,  we need to look within ourselves and identify why we still cannot look on ourselves as Sri Lankans rather than viewing ourselves through the prism of race, profession etc. The minority communities within our nation state have very definite grievances.

While we have accepted Sinhalese and Tamil as our national languages,  English is recognised as a link language. Yet all official communication is mainly in Sinhala only. If a Tamil or Muslim person were to attempt making a statement at the local police station, they are mostly unable to do so in either their mother-tongue or in the link language. They face similar problems when applying for identity cards or passports.

Rather than taking offence at minor slights,  we need to discuss problems rather than escalate them.

The 22nd Amendment in its whole undoes a number of unlawful privileges a past president gave unto himself. Let’s not throw the baby out with the bathwater.
