Tanker owners continue fuel deliveries after CPC agreement

Colombo, Oct. 2 (Daily Mirror) - The Ceylon Petroleum Private Tanker Owners Association (CPPTOA) has decided to continue fuel transportation services after the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC) agreed to meet its demand for revised fuel transportation commission charges.

CPPTOA Chairman A.M.H. Adhikari said the decision was taken following discussions held this evening with the CPC Chairman, two professors from the University of Moratuwa and representatives of the association.

He said the CPC had agreed to provide the transportation commission charges demanded by private tanker owners, prompting the association to continue fuel deliveries as usual.

The association had called for a revision of the fuel transportation formula to ensure tanker owners receive a commission of at least 20%.

Adhikari said the CPPTOA first requested a revision of transportation charges in 2025, with discussions held to introduce the revised rates from January 2026. However, he said the revised rates had not been implemented despite several rounds of discussions with relevant authorities.

He also said the recent increase in fuel prices had significantly raised the operating costs of private fuel bowsers.

According to Adhikari, the prices of spare parts, tyres, tubes and vehicle servicing had increased by more than 50%, further increasing the financial burden on tanker operators.
