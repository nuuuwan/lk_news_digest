Multi-day fishing vessels urged to leave Andaman sea areas amid rough weather

Colombo, Sep. 21 (Daily Mirror) - The Department of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources has urged all multi-day fishing vessels operating in deep-sea areas around the Andaman Islands to immediately leave the area and move towards safer waters as strong winds and rough seas are expected in the coming days.

Department Director General Susantha Kahawatte said the low-pressure system currently developing in the region, east of Sri Lanka and near the Andaman Islands, is expected to gradually intensify and move towards India.

Speaking at a media briefing yesterday, he said the system could affect sea conditions for several days and urged fishing vessel owners and crews to take immediate precautions.

He said multi-day fishing vessels operating around the Andaman Islands and the northern Bay of Bengal had been instructed to move southwards towards Sri Lanka to distance themselves from the developing weather system.

However, several vessels were still engaged in fishing activities in the affected areas, he said, warning that continuing operations could expose both vessels and crews to serious risks from strong winds and rough seas.

Meanwhile, small-scale fishermen in other sea areas around Sri Lanka have also been advised to closely monitor weather and sea conditions before venturing out to sea.

Kahawatte said no decision had been taken to suspend fishing activities across the country. The Department is closely monitoring the situation and will issue further instructions based on weather advisories from the Meteorological Department.

He urged fishermen to strictly follow weather warnings and safety instructions and to wear life jackets when going to sea, while engaged in fishing and when returning to shore.

He said lives had been lost in the past during adverse weather due to fishermen failing to follow safety instructions and weather warnings and not wearing life jackets.

The Department has contacted owners of multi-day fishing vessels operating in the affected areas and informed them of the developing situation. Crews have also been alerted through radio communication systems and instructed to leave the affected areas immediately.
