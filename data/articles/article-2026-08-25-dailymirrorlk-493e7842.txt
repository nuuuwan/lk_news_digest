BASL raises gratification concern over judges’ tenure extension

Colombo, Aug. 25 (Daily Mirror) - The Bar Association of Sri Lanka (BASL), in a Special Determination petition filed before the Supreme Court challenging the constitutionality of the proposed Twenty-Second Amendment to the Constitution, has raised concerns over whether the extension of the tenure and related benefits proposed for sitting judges could amount to a “gratification” under the Anti-Corruption Act.

The petitioners, BASL President Rajeev Amarasuriya and its Secretary Nalin De Silva stated that a serious question arises as to whether the proposed extension of the tenure and attendant benefits granted to sitting judges could constitute a “gratification” within the meaning of the Anti-Corruption Act, No. 9 of 2023, as amended.

The BASL said this issue assumes particular significance because the same judges may be required, in their judicial capacity, to determine the constitutionality of the Bill seeking to grant such an extension.

However, the petitioners stressed that they were not alleging any wrongdoing or casting aspersions on the judges. They said the relevant provisions of the Anti-Corruption Act were cited only to draw the Supreme Court’s attention to the serious risk of public perception arising in the circumstances.

The petitioners also informed that it may be argued that legislative and judicial acts could be immune from the application of the relevant provisions of law.

The BASL specifically referred to Section 93(b) of the Anti-Corruption Act, which applies to Supreme Court and Court of Appeal judges, among others.

The provision states that a Supreme Court or Court of Appeal judge, judicial officer or other person exercising judicial or quasi-judicial power who solicits or accepts any gratification as an inducement or reward for doing or refraining from doing any act in their judicial capacity commits an offence of bribery.

According to the provision cited in the petition, a person convicted of such an offence may be fined up to Rs. 1 million and sentenced to rigorous imprisonment for a period of up to seven years.

The BASL also raises a potential conflict-of-interest issue, noting that the sitting Supreme Court judges who would determine the constitutionality of the Bill could themselves benefit from the proposed extension of their tenure.

The petitioners are seeking a declaration that the proposed “Twenty Second Amendment to the Constitution” requires approval by the People at a referendum, arguing that the Bill threatens the independence of the Judiciary and the sovereignty of the People.

The BASL has also requested the Chief Justice to constitute a full bench comprising all sitting Supreme Court judges to hear the matter, citing the grave, exceptional and urgent constitutional issues arising from the Bill and an alleged conflict of interest.

The Bill, published in the Gazette on August 7 and placed on Parliament’s Order Paper on August 18, seeks to amend the constitutional provisions governing the retirement age of superior court judges.

Under the proposed amendment, the retirement age of Supreme Court judges would be increased from 65 to 67, while that of Court of Appeal judges would be increased from 63 to 65. The Bill also provides a special rule for the Chief Justice, whose retirement would occur either upon reaching 67 or completing six years from appointment as Chief Justice, whichever comes first.

The Bill further proposes to increase the maximum number of other judges of the Court of Appeal from 19 to 24, effectively allowing five additional judges to be appointed.

According to the petition, extending the tenure of sitting judges could create both an actual and perceived risk to judicial independence, as the judges would receive an additional two years in office together with the associated salary, privileges and other benefits.

The petition further argues that the amendment could damage public confidence in the Judiciary because decisions involving the Government could subsequently be viewed through the perception that judges had benefited from an extension granted by the legislature.
