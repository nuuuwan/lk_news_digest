Exporters seek support to meet new EU food and packaging rules

The Sri Lankan exporters are seeking technical assistance and training to meet the new European Union (EU) food safety and packaging requirements, with limits on certain mineral oil contaminants in food, scheduled to apply from January 2027.

The National Chamber of Exporters of Sri Lanka (NCE) yesterday said it was working with the other stakeholders to secure support for the businesses preparing to comply with the requirements.

A key concern is the EU’s new maximum levels for mineral oil aromatic hydrocarbons (MOAH) in food. A European Commission statement set January 01, 2027 as the application date for most of the limits.

Sri Lanka’s Commerce Department has also begun assessing the country’s readiness. It convened a stakeholder meeting on September 24 to examine the preparations for the proposed MOAH regulation and highlighted the need for the exporters to respond to the evolving European market requirements.

The NCE council also discussed the EU’s Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, which has implications for the businesses supplying the European market.

The regulation generally began applying on August 12, 2026 and covers packaging placed on the EU market, including imports from Sri Lanka. An EU-supported seminar held in Colombo in July sought to help the food exporters, packaging manufacturers, printers and other stakeholders prepare for the requirements.
