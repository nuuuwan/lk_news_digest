Ravi K seeks details on El Niño’s economic impact

Colombo, Aug. 5 (Daily Mirror) - Opposition MP Ravi Karunanayake has questioned whether the government has assessed the likely economic impact of the forecast strong El Niño event expected to affect Sri Lanka during 2026/2027, including its impact on inflation, export crops, government revenue, disaster relief expenditure and the country’s Gross Domestic Product (GDP).

Raising the matter in parliament, Karunanayake asked whether the government had quantified the likely impact on inflation, the effect on export crops, additional expenditure required for disaster relief, revenue losses to the government and the expected GDP loss under both moderate and severe El Niño scenarios.

Citing scientific forecasts warning of a strong El Niño event, the MP noted that Sri Lanka could face prolonged droughts, floods, food insecurity, shortages of drinking water, reduced hydroelectric power generation, adverse public health outcomes and economic disruption, requiring coordinated action by multiple ministries and government agencies.

Karunanayake also sought to know whether the government had prepared and approved a National El Niño Preparedness and Response Plan, when it was approved, whether it had been made public and the key measures contained in the plan.

He further questioned which ministry or agency had been designated as the lead coordinating authority and what mechanisms were in place to coordinate the Department of Meteorology, Disaster Management Centre, the Ministries of Agriculture, Irrigation, Health, Water Supply, Power and Energy, the Mahaweli Authority and provincial administrations.

The MP also requested a sector-wise breakdown of funds allocated under the 2026 Budget for El Niño preparedness and response, including allocations for agriculture, irrigation, disaster management, drinking water, public health, electricity generation and emergency relief, together with any allocations expected to continue into 2027.

Karunanayake further sought the government’s assessment of the expected duration and severity of the El Niño event, including its anticipated commencement, peak period, projected duration and the districts likely to be most affected.

He also asked what contingency measures had been implemented to safeguard food production, irrigation, water resources, electricity supply, healthcare services and disaster response, including maintaining adequate reserves of food, seed, medicines, drinking water and emergency relief supplies.

In addition, the MP questioned what public awareness and preparedness programmes had been implemented, which districts had been identified as being at the highest risk, whether Sri Lanka had sought technical or financial assistance from international organisations including the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), the World Bank and the Asian Development Bank (ADB), and why the government had not obtained natural disaster insurance similar to the arrangements made during the 2016/2017 El Niño event.
