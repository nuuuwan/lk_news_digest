Gold may not have peaked yet as geopolitical risks rise: AIIB Treasurer

Gold prices may have further room to climb as heightened geopolitical uncertainty strengthens the precious metal’s appeal as portfolio insurance, visiting Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) Treasurer Domenico Nardelli said, arguing that the market may not yet have reached its peak.

Nardelli observed that gold had gained increasing traction as investors sought greater diversification in response to a more fragmented and uncertain global environment.

“Gold has intrinsic value and, as such, can be viewed as an insurance for any portfolio in almost any market situation, in particular when conflict and uncertainties are on the rise,” he said.

“If that is true, I believe we may not have seen the peak in gold prices yet.”

He shared his views while speaking at the inaugural session of the Reserve Management Conference 2026, held earlier this month.

According to Nardelli, gold’s traditional attributes, including the absence of credit risk, reasonable liquidity and its role as a hedge against inflation, continue to make it attractive to reserve managers and other institutional investors.

However, he pointed out that the geopolitical backdrop was becoming an increasingly important driver of demand for the precious metal, as investors looked to reduce concentration across assets and jurisdictions.

Reserve managers globally are reassessing portfolio allocations against elevated geopolitical tensions and increased volatility across financial markets.

Nardelli noted that after a relatively uneventful decade between 2010 and 2020, gold began a pronounced rally following the COVID-19 pandemic, with the momentum gaining significant strength over the past two years.

He explained that the shift towards diversification was occurring as investors questioned the risks of concentrating assets within a single jurisdiction in an increasingly divided geopolitical landscape.

Despite making the case for gold, Nardelli cautioned that its appeal did not come without risks, pointing to sharp price swings that could pose challenges for treasury portfolios.

He noted that gold had experienced periods of pronounced volatility, including significant declines during the first half of 2026, illustrating the potential mark-to-market impact for institutional investors.

For treasury managers whose portfolio performance feeds directly into institutional earnings, such fluctuations could make substantial gold exposure difficult to accommodate, he added.

Nardelli revealed that AIIB itself does not hold gold in its portfolio, although the question of whether it should do so surfaces periodically in discussions with management. He recalled a 2024 meeting with a Middle Eastern central bank that held 50 percent of its assets in gold, a level of exposure that he said had surprised him at the time. While the subsequent rally would have benefited such a position, Nardelli said recent volatility illustrated the trade-off confronting portfolio managers seeking both diversification and liquidity.

He asserted that growing geopolitical fragmentation would continue to strengthen the case for diversification across both asset classes and jurisdictions, although investors would also have to contend with the costs and operational complexities that come with a more diversified portfolio. (SAA)
