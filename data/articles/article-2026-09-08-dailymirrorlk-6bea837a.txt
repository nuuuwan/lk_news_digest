Sri Lanka-Japan kick off work on export-oriented industrial corridor

Sri Lanka and Japan have begun work on developing an export-oriented industrial corridor, according to the Board of Investment (BOI), opening a new avenue for bilateral economic cooperation focused on investment and export-led industrial development.

The first meeting of the Sri Lanka-Japan Joint Working Group on Export-Oriented Industrial Corridor Development was held recently, alongside the fifth working-level meeting of the Sri Lanka-Japan Business Environment Committee, according to the BOI.

The BOI, issuing a statement, described the inaugural working group meeting as an important milestone that is expected to steer the economic ties between the two countries in a new strategic direction.

However, the BOI did not disclose the proposed location of the industrial corridor, the sectors expected to be accommodated, the scale of investment envisaged or a timeline for its development.

Details were also not provided on the respective roles of the Sri Lankan and Japanese governments or whether the proposed corridor would involve new Japanese private-sector investment.

The establishment of the working group nevertheless signals an effort to broaden the economic engagement between the two countries towards export-oriented industrial activity.

The discussions were held with the participation of the representatives from the Embassy of Japan, Japan External Trade Organisation (JETRO), Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), Japan Chamber of Commerce and Industry (JCCI), Japanese companies operating in Sri Lanka, BOI and other government institutions.

The BOI said it would continue to function as the principal liaison between the investors and Sri Lankan government, with the broader engagement aimed at strengthening coordination and deepening economic cooperation with Japan.

Meanwhile, the Japanese companies operating in Sri Lanka raised operational and business concerns across five broad sectors, bringing the issues directly before the relevant government agencies through a bilateral investor forum.

The concerns covered manufacturing and industrial production, consumer and health products, value-added trade, environmental services and infrastructure, logistics and energy, according to the BOI.

The issues were raised at the fifth working-level meeting of the Sri Lanka-Japan Business Environment Committee, a quarterly platform established specifically to address the challenges encountered by the Japanese investors operating in the country.

The government agencies participating in the meeting provided on-the-spot clarifications and solutions to the concerns raised by the individual companies, the BOI said.

However, the BOI did not disclose the specific difficulties identified by the companies, the number of issues raised or how many were resolved at the meeting.
