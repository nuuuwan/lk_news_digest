‘Become un-purchasable or leave the uniform’: President warns corrupt Police

Colombo, September 03 (Daily Mirror) - President Anura Kumara Dissanayake has issued a stern warning to police officers colluding with drug traffickers and organised criminal groups, telling them to overcome their fear, remain beyond the reach of criminals or leave the police service.

Addressing the 160th Police Day commemoration at the Thimbirigasyaya Field Force Headquarters, President Dissanayake said the foremost responsibility of the Sri Lanka Police today was to eradicate the drug trade and organised armed crime.

He described the drug menace and armed criminal networks as a serious threat spreading through society and expressed concern that a small number of police officers tasked with combating these crimes were themselves becoming involved with criminal elements.

“I invite both these groups: immediately cast away your fear. Become an officer who cannot be bought. If not, leave the uniform,” the President said.

He said some officers had become involved with criminal networks because of fear, while others had been influenced through financial or other inducements.

President Dissanayake warned that such conduct damages the dignity of the police uniform and undermines public trust in the institution.

He said that if corrupt officers did not leave voluntarily, the disciplinary authorities of the Police must take action to remove them from the service following proper investigations and due process.

The President said that the conduct of a small group of corrupt officers should not undermine the reputation of the majority of police personnel who are performing their duties with dedication.

Meanwhile, he commended the Police for their efforts against drugs and organised crime over the past two years.

According to figures cited by the President, authorities have seized 525 kilograms of heroin, 1,996 kilograms of ICE, 44,684 kilograms of Kerala cannabis and 16.2 million narcotic pills during operations against the drug trade.

He said the seizures had prevented significant quantities of narcotics from reaching young people and schoolchildren.

President Dissanayake also praised intelligence-led operations that resulted in the return of 70 fugitive criminals from overseas, while noting that efforts were underway to bring back several more suspects.

He further highlighted the recovery of approximately 3,850 illicit firearms, including T-56 assault rifles, pistols and revolvers, from criminal networks.

The President expressed his appreciation to police officers engaged in operations against organised crime, saying their efforts were vital to protecting society and restoring public security.
