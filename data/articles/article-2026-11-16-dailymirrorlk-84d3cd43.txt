Parliamentary Committee mulls GPS, satellite collar to curb human-elephant conflict

Colombo, Nov. 16 (Daily Mirror) - The Sectoral Oversight Committee on Environment, Agriculture, and Resource Sustainability has held extensive discussions on mitigating human-elephant conflict, mapping wild elephant migration routes, and adopting modern technologies for elephant conservation.

The matters were reviewed during a recent committee meeting in Parliament chaired by MP Hector Appuhamy.

During the session, members identified habitat loss, the obstruction of traditional elephant corridors, and the encroachment of wild elephants into human settlements and farmlands in search of food as primary drivers behind the escalating conflict. To address this, the committee emphasized the importance of deploying GPS and satellite collar technology to track and study elephant movement patterns.

Several community-focused proposals were also tabled to reduce clashes. These include establishing local-level joint committees comprising wildlife officers, Grama Niladharis, and residents, alongside strengthening community participation and setting up early warning communication networks.

Additionally, the committee examined the operational challenges faced by veterinary surgeons of the Department of Wildlife Conservation when treating sick or injured elephants. Discussions highlighted the inherent risks of anaesthetizing wild elephants and the urgent need for specialized field equipment—such as mobile veterinary units and portable X-ray machines—to deliver timely medical care on-site.
