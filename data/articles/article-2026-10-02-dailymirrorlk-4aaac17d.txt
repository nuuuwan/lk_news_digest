Easing sovereign risks lift BOC’s credit rating

Fitch Ratings has upgraded Bank of Ceylon’s (BOC) long-term foreign- and local-currency ratings to ‘B-’ from ‘CCC+’, with a Stable Outlook, citing easing sovereign risks and stronger prospects for Sri Lanka’s largest bank.

The October 1 rating action follows Sri Lanka’s sovereign upgrade to ‘B-’ with a Stable Outlook on September 22, reflecting the close link between the bank’s credit strength and the government’s financial position.

Fitch also raised BOC’s Viability Rating, which assesses its standalone credit profile, to ‘b-’ from ‘ccc+’, and its Short-Term Issuer Default Rating to ‘B’ from ‘C’.

The bank’s National Long-Term Rating of ‘AA-(lka)’ with a Stable Outlook was not considered in the review.

Fitch said structural reforms had eased Sri Lanka’s external financing risks and improved the economy’s resilience to shocks, supporting greater financial-market stability and the bank’s business and financial prospects.

“The sovereign credit profile is a key constraint on the bank’s ratings,” the agency said.

However, BOC’s substantial exposure to the state remains central to its risk profile. Fitch estimated that sovereign bonds and loans to state-owned entities accounted for about 60 percent of the bank’s total assets at the end of the first quarter of 2026, including Treasury securities held for liquidity purposes.

The improved sovereign credit profile prompted Fitch to raise its assessments of BOC’s risk profile, asset quality and capitalisation, reflecting reduced risks from these exposures. It also expects credit risks outside the sovereign portfolio to ease as stronger economic conditions feed through to borrowers.

BOC’s market position remained a rating strength. The bank accounts for around 22 percent of banking-sector assets and deposits, while its extensive branch network and deposit franchise provide an advantage in generating business at acceptable risk, Fitch said.

The agency raised its assessment of the operating environment for Sri Lankan banks to ‘b-’ from ‘ccc+’, citing easing direct and indirect sovereign risks. Nevertheless, the sector remains closely tied to the domestic economy through government securities holdings and wider public-sector lending.

Fitch expects stronger economic resilience to reduce BOC’s earnings volatility over the medium term. Near-term profitability could face pressure from moderately higher funding costs and lower interest income from government securities.

Domestic funding and liquidity conditions should also become more stable, while access to foreign-currency funding is expected to widen gradually.

Despite the upgrades, Fitch affirmed BOC’s Government Support Rating at ‘ns’, or “no support”, reflecting constraints on the state’s capacity to provide extraordinary assistance.

The agency said the government’s weak finances and modest foreign reserves continued to limit that capacity.

Further upgrades to BOC’s long-term ratings would most likely depend on an improvement in Sri Lanka’s sovereign credit profile and the banking sector’s operating environment. A sovereign downgrade, a significant deterioration in operating conditions or renewed foreign-currency funding and liquidity constraints could trigger a downgrade.
