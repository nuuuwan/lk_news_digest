2,700 women-headed families in Mullaitivu

Mullaitivu District Secretary A. Umamaheswaran said, at a recent event organised by the Viluthu Foundation for Women to support women’s livelihood and entrepreneurship, among the 47,000 families in Mullaitivu District, 2,700 families are entirely run by women, and more than 6,000 women are widows according to records.

A participatory research on the definition of women heads of household conducted by Viluthu, the civil society organisation that supported in implementation of this livelihood effort found 11 main categories, and over 58 subcategories and more than 100 further sub divisions of women under the women heads of households banner- showing a lot more has to be done to understand their lived realities.

A conservative estimate, without taking into consideration the actual definitions women have recommended suggests, there are at least 67,000 women-headed households across the Northern Province. They carry this responsibility in one of the poorest districts in the country, where household incomes sit well below the national average. The women and government social workers in the North and East estimate that if real inclusive definitions are used that there may be nearly 100,000 to 150,000 women heads of household in the North and East region alone. Mr. Umamaheswaran said how it is “especially the women’s hard work that keeps families afloat. Primarily MSMEs in the district are run by women and it is their innovation and determination and effort that help their families, this community and this district.”

Mr. Umamaheswaran remarked on his long awareness of Viluthu’s establishment over 23 years, starting from his own experience collaborating with founder and social visionary Shanthi Sachithanandan in 2003 in Veruhal in the Eastern Province. He commended her daughters for continuing the worthy cause initiated by their parents, Shanthi Sachithanandam and Manoranjan Rajasingam. In this initiative, by working in close coordination with the District Secretariat, The Viluthu Foundation for Women was able to identify the most suitable candidates from the 6 Divisional Secretaries and focused on pairing the right equipment with her real needs and existing skills. Importantly, by including all the DS Divisions, it brought together Tamil, Muslim and Sinhala women on the common goals of promoting women’s economic participation and livelihood enhancement through this support of the Foundation reaching them all.

The Foundation hopes to build on this initial support over time with connecting her to the markets, mentoring and collective support that keep an enterprise alive. Speaking on the initiative, Mr. Shivananth explained “the premise of Her Rise is simple: when she rises, communities rise. A woman who leads her household alone is already carrying her family's survival on her shoulders. Give her the tools to earn with dignity, and the return does not stop with her, it reaches her children's schooling, her family's nutrition, and her community's recovery. Helping one woman can help reach 100,000” he said, referring to the Foundation’s goal of helping 100,000 vulnerable women to become economically independent and stable. The Durrai family from Singapore has already supported 182 women in Kilinochchi and now approximately more than 130 women in Mullaitivu, and hope to personally support raising funds for a total of 1000 women as part of their family pledge to the Foundation. These efforts stand testament to the important role that private donors have in Sri Lanka and the change creation for poverty alleviation that they can be part of.

The Viluthu Foundation for Women is a holistic and integrated organisation that was initiated by Maithreyi and Aaranya Rajasingam to take forward the transformative work over three decades by their mother, social activist and philanthropist Shanthi Sachithanandam. With 13 years of experience working with women and communities affected by war, poverty and economic crisis, the founders hope to support 100,000 women in Sri Lanka, building on lessons learned and best practices in Sri Lanka and globally to ensure that individual and corporate donors have the right channel through which to invest in people, helping to make a difference that can turn the tide for generations to come. Speaking at the event, Maithreyi thanked the Durrai family and S. Raman for their support and remarked, “Donors like them show us how we can partner with communities not just to help in hardship but to also to be part of women’s collective successes. We co-designed the programme with the beneficiaries themselves so that they feel the ownership and motivation to be able to carry the work forward with us. We hope that events such as these encourage others here in Sri Lanka and abroad, individuals and companies alike, to come forward, to look closely at a need most have moved past, and to partner with organisations such as ours that turn goodwill into lasting, measurable change.” The Viluthu Foundation for Women looks forward to continuing this impact-driven work throughout the whole of Sri Lanka.
