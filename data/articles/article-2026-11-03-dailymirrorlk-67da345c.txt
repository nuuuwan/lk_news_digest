Seylan’s quarterly profit tops Rs.3bn

Seylan Bank’s group profit rose 6 percent to Rs.3.08 billion in the September quarter, with an impairment reversal supporting the earnings as the higher funding costs squeezed the interest income growth and the expenses outpaced the revenue.

The profit attributable to the equity holders increased 6.2 percent to Rs.3.07 billion, from Rs.2.89 billion a year earlier, while the earnings per share improved to Rs.4.82, from Rs.4.54.

The group recorded an impairment reversal of Rs.965.3 million, against a charge of Rs.226.7 million in the corresponding quarter, delivering a favourable year-on-year swing of nearly Rs.1.2 billion.

This helped offset the pressure on the operating performance. The group operating income was virtually unchanged at Rs.11.82 billion, while the operating expenses rose 12.2 percent to Rs.6.32 billion. The operating profit before impairment and taxes consequently fell about 11 percent to Rs.5.50 billion.

At bank level, the quarterly profit after tax increased 6.95 percent to Rs.3.04 billion, while the profit before income tax grew 8.74 percent to Rs.4.75 billion. The interest income rose 21.7 percent to Rs.25.12 billion but the interest expenses increased at a faster 39.5 percent to Rs.15.83 billion. Consequently, the quarterly net interest income edged down to Rs.9.29 billion, from Rs.9.30 billion. The fee income provided support, with the net fee and commission income increasing 13.5 percent to Rs.2.46 billion.

The bank reported a net trading loss of Rs.2.31 billion for the quarter, compared with a loss of Rs.97.6 million a year earlier. The net other operating income increased to Rs.2.26 billion, from Rs.295.5 million, substantially offsetting the trading loss.

Total bank operating income grew just 0.26 percent to Rs.11.71 billion, while the operating expenses rose 12.2 percent to Rs.6.27 billion. The personnel expenses increased 13 percent to Rs.3.33 billion.

For the nine months ended September 30, the group net profit increased 9 percent to Rs.9.12 billion. The profit attributable to the equity holders rose 9.1 percent to Rs.9.09 billion, with the earnings per share improving to Rs.14.30, from Rs.13.11.

The bank’s nine-month profit after tax increased 9.47 percent to Rs.9.12 billion, while the profit before income tax rose 9.64 percent to Rs.14.04 billion.

The bank net interest income grew 6.62 percent to Rs.28.85 billion, supported by the balance-sheet expansion. However, the reported net interest margin narrowed to 3.99 percent, from 4.50 percent for 2025, with the bank attributing the decline to the higher market interest rates.

The net fee and commission income increased 18.19 percent to Rs.7.11 billion.

“This growth was mainly attributable to the increased fee income from the cards, remittances, trade, loans and other financial services,” the bank said in its accompanying statement.

The nine-month operating income increased 8.87 percent to Rs.37.25 billion, while the operating expenses grew 13.54 percent to Rs.18.56 billion. The personnel expenses rose nearly 13 percent, mainly due to the annual salary revision.

“The bank continues to implement targeted cost optimisation initiatives to manage the overhead cost effectively,” it said. The reported cost-to-income ratio nevertheless increased to 49.81 percent, from 44.52 percent for 2025. A nine-month impairment reversal of Rs.232.7 million, against a charge of Rs.645.4 million a year earlier, helped support the earnings.

The bank’s total assets rose 10 percent from end-December to Rs.1.013 trillion. The net loans and advances increased 14.86 percent to Rs.688.92 billion, while the customer deposits expanded by about Rs.78 billion to Rs.811 billion. Its current and savings account ratio stood at 26 percent.

The impaired loans ratio remained broadly steady at 1.02 percent, compared with 1.03 percent at end-2025, while the stage three provision coverage stood at 84.98 percent.

The capital ratios declined during the period. The bank’s common equity Tier 1 ratio fell to 11.12 percent, from 12.39 percent at end-2025, while its total capital ratio decreased to 15.14 percent, from 17.89 percent.

The return on equity stood at 14.44 percent, against 15.89 percent for 2025.
