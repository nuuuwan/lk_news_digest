August tea output dips 3.7%

Sri Lanka’s tea production fell 3.7 percent year-on-year in August, as the sharp declines in the high and medium-grown categories outweighed an improvement in the low-grown output, the industry data showed.

Production declined by 720,000 kilogrammes to 19.00 million kilogrammes, from 19.72 million kilogrammes in August 2025, according to the data compiled by Forbes and Walker Research.

The high-grown production contracted 16.2 percent to 3.02 million kilogrammes, while the medium-grown output fell 14.8 percent to 2.70 million kilogrammes.

The low-grown production, which accounted for nearly 69 percent of the monthly total, rose 2.6 percent to 13.09 million kilogrammes. The green tea output edged up 0.2 percent to 184,634 kilogrammes.

Compared with August 2024, the total production was down 11 percent or 2.34 million kilogrammes, from 21.34 million kilogrammes.

The cumulative production during the first eight months of 2026 declined 2.7 percent to 172.21 million kilogrammes, compared with 176.98 million kilogrammes in the corresponding period last year.

The year-to-date contraction was driven mainly by the low-grown production, which fell by 2.99 million kilogrammes or 2.8 percent to 103.37 million kilogrammes. The medium-grown production declined 6.2 percent to 29.75 million kilogrammes.

The high-grown output recorded a marginal 0.1 percent increase to 37.35 million kilogrammes, while the green tea production rose 8.2 percent to 1.74 million kilogrammes.

Compared with the first eight months of 2024, the cumulative production was marginally lower by 280,000 kilogrammes or 0.2 percent. The high-grown and green tea production remained above the 2024 levels, while the medium and low-grown output continued to lag.
